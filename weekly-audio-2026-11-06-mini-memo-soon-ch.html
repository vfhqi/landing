APM Mini-Memo. Sonova. The sixth of November 2026.

Sonova is new this week at rank fourteen on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. The one-line view. Sonova is a well-run leader of the hearing-aid oligopoly, with real self-help on top of a strong product cycle. The story depends on holding share through the crowded launch window of late this year, and on buying the shares cheaper than today's two hundred and eighty-eight francs.

First, what the company does. Sonova is a Swiss hearing-care group. Sonova makes hearing aids under the Phonak, Unitron and Hansaton brands, runs more than three thousand of its own clinics, and makes cochlear implants. Sales for the year to March were about three point six billion francs, up six percent in local currencies. The market value is about fourteen and a half billion euros. Wholesale is about half of sales, at operating margins above thirty percent. Retail is about forty percent of sales, at mid-teens margins. The loss-making Sennheiser consumer business, about five percent of sales, is being sold. Five makers hold over ninety-four percent of the world's hearing-aid wholesale market. Sonova leads with a quarter of it. Platform launches every two years support prices, and prices held when volumes softened last year.

Second, the thesis. The thesis is self-help on top of a product cycle. A completely new leadership team was in place by December last year. That team narrowed the company to medical hearing care, moved to a four-region model in April, and launched a savings programme worth ninety million francs a year by year four. The team also set a growth algorithm: five to ten percent sales growth in local currency, and seven to twelve percent growth in core operating profit, towards six billion francs of sales by the financial year ending in March twenty thirty-one. Underneath that, the Infinio and Sphere artificial-intelligence platforms are winning share. Wholesale grew eleven percent in local currency in the second half of last year, against a market growing two to four percent. Sonova's unit share at the United States Veterans Affairs reached fifty-four percent in July, up four and a half points in a year. The route to earnings per share is simple. Sales growth of about six and a half percent a year, plus margin gains of about two percent a year, compound earnings per share at about nine percent a year in francs. That is the Investment Analyst's central path, from about ten francs forty last year to about thirteen francs forty by March twenty twenty-nine. Growth is faster in local currency. Earnings per share rose sixteen percent in local currency last year but were flat in francs, because the strong franc took over two hundred million francs off sales. The sources of upgrades are specific: the smaller Sphere device, year-two savings, the consumer exit worth about two hundred basis points of group margin, and cheaper components. Consensus has risen four to five percent on every horizon in six months, with no cuts. Three drivers to track. Wholesale share through the launch window, read monthly from the Veterans Affairs series. The drop-through of savings to a margin of about twenty-four percent. And the franc against the dollar and the euro, which translates both into reported earnings.

Third, the Target Setup. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. The Investment Analyst's memo of the eleventh of October rated Setups' Fit/Crispness B. The leadership change is complete and its four big choices were acted on within a year. But the outputs reported so far belong to the product cycle more than to the corporate change, so the pattern fits crisply on inputs and less crisply on maturity. My own reading adds a Demand-driven EPSU alongside, from the artificial-intelligence upgrade cycle that patients pay for themselves. The memo's secondary pattern, a cause for concern clearing in a high-quality business on a low multiple, fitted in May at one hundred and ninety-three francs and sixteen times earnings. That pattern has since been priced out.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The Investment Analyst's memo of the eleventh of October rated Case Riskiness C, on a scale where A is simple and low-risk. The case is simple where it counts: one bet, three drivers and monthly indicators. The fundamentals carry no red flag, no deceleration and no estimate cuts. The exposure is on price. What needs to go right is the launch window of late this year, the most crowded in years. Four of the five majors launch premium platforms at once. Demant's Oticon Zeal has already taken the custom category, where Sonova's value share fell from sixty-one to forty-eight percent. Sonova must ship the smaller Sphere in time for the Veterans Affairs procurement window in November, and hold wholesale share through it. The trigger to re-run the case is monthly Veterans Affairs unit share falling below forty-five percent. The reassuring sign would be the compact Sphere recovering custom share. A miss costs a de-rating. A lost window would take the multiple from about twenty times towards seventeen times, or the sell-side's bear multiple of fourteen times. That is a fall of seventeen to thirty-one percent on unchanged earnings. Two headwinds sit beside this, and Sonova controls neither. The franc: about fifteen percent of costs but one percent of sales are in francs, hedges run one to six months, and this year's guidance carries a two to three point profit headwind. And channel consolidation: Amplifon's purchase of GN Hearing takes about sixty million francs of revenue from late in the twenty twenty-seven year. The cochlear implant unit is small, fell seventeen percent in the second half, and awaits a new processor. Treat its recovery as an option.

Fifth, business quality. This is a high-quality business with a lead that must be re-earned every two years. The Investment Analyst rated Foundations' Robustness B on the eleventh of October. Gross margin is seventy-four percent. The normalised operating margin is twenty-two and a half percent, and reached twenty-five percent in the second half. Return on capital employed is nineteen percent, cash conversion is above ninety percent, and leverage is one point one times. The moat is more than the chip. Audiologists' familiarity with the fitting software, custom moulds, the Roger ecosystem in schools, in-house silicon and the clinic network kept total share rising through the very quarter in which the custom category was lost. Capital allocation is the swing factor. The six billion franc ambition needs about eight hundred million francs of retail acquisitions at mid-teens margins. The one and a half billion franc buyback is paused to fund them, so group returns on capital fall even as sales grow. Against the new team's speed stand a profit warning in twenty twenty-two, the failed consumer acquisition, and a product quality rate that worsened last year.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The return is an F at today's price. At two hundred and sixty-five francs, the Investment Analyst's memo of the eleventh of October put the central return at eight and a half percent a year over eighteen months, and just over nine percent a year over three years. Re-based by the weekly routine to two hundred and eighty-eight francs today, the TSR/Valuation rating reads under three percent a year over eighteen months and six and a half percent over three years. That is an F against the twenty percent bar. The memo had pinned a B on the multiple alone. The bull branch, with the window won and the market paying twenty-three times, gives about twenty-six percent a year, at odds of about one in five. Where does the case clear the bar? The shares re-rated from about sixteen times to twenty times two-year-forward earnings between May and October. They rose forty-eight percent in the six months to October and sit two percent below the fifty-two-week high. The central path clears the bar below about two hundred and twenty-seven francs on eighteen months, and below about two hundred francs on both horizons. That is where the shares traded between May and August. The consensus target of two hundred and forty-five francs sits below the price, and only a quarter of analysts say buy, so the sell-side has not yet followed the market. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of the fifth of November is B, and the weekly Technical Momentum rating of the sixth of November is B.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros. Artificial-intelligence sound processing drives a premium upgrade cycle that the patient pays for, and Sonova owns the silicon, the software and a clinical channel. The Sphere took fifty-two percent of rechargeable receiver-in-canal aids at the Veterans Affairs within months, at a ten percent premium. Hearing-aid makers were the Sector's strongest cohort, up forty-one percent in the twelve months to late October. Demant raised its growth guidance in October. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of the sixth of November is B, up from the D pinned in the memo. The cons. A launch premium lasts as long as the technical lead, and that lead is twelve to eighteen months. So the Sector Digest judges the hearing story known and in the price, and better bought after a fall. Retail consolidation is turning Sonova's biggest customers into competitors, as Amplifon absorbs GN Hearing and Demant integrates nine hundred German clinics. And part of the Sector's rise may be money leaving technology, which can leave again.

The one-line view. Sonova is a well-run leader of the hearing-aid oligopoly, with real self-help on top of a strong product cycle. The story depends on holding share through the crowded launch window of late this year, and on buying the shares cheaper than today's two hundred and eighty-eight francs.
