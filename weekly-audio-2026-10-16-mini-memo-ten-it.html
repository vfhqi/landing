APM Mini-Memo. Tenaris. The sixteenth of October 2026.

Tenaris sits at rank two on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The view is bullish, as a working hypothesis. This is the world's largest oil and gas pipe maker, self-funded with three thousand three hundred and twenty-two million dollars of net cash, at an earnings trough with dated recoveries ahead. But no Investment Analyst memo exists, the share price sits near its high, and the open question is who keeps the rent.

First, what the company does. Tenaris makes steel pipe for the oil and gas industry: seven point eight million tons of capacity across twelve seamless and twenty-five welded mills in about twenty countries. Tubes are ninety-five percent of sales and North America is forty-six percent of revenue. Revenue was just under twelve billion dollars in twenty twenty-five, at a twenty-four point two percent margin of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. The shape that matters is an oligopolist that turned a commodity into a service. Tenaris holds a thirty-five percent global share and thirty-seven percent of premium connections. Its Rig Direct model delivers from mill to wellsite and manages the customer's inventory; it carries seventy percent of global sales and ninety percent of United States sales.

Second, the thesis. Earnings are at a trough and dated recoveries sit ahead. The second quarter of twenty twenty-six, on the Earnings Review of the eleventh of September, showed revenue of two thousand nine hundred and sixty-seven million dollars, down four percent sequentially, a twenty-one point nine percent margin and a nine percent beat on net income. The Strait of Hormuz closure deferred about one hundred and thirty million dollars of high-margin shipments. Management guided the second half flat assuming the Strait stays shut through year-end, so that volume is upside, not base case, and Tenaris has beaten consensus earnings per share in eighteen of the past twenty-one quarters. The Change Forces report of the fourth of September names five drivers. PipeLogix index recovery is the largest at about fifteen percent of earnings impact, as United States seamless pricing rose about ten percent year to date behind Section two thirty-two tariffs. Deepwater volumes in Brazil, Guyana and West Africa are about twelve percent, and the highest conviction. United States onshore rigs are about ten percent, Hormuz reopening about eight, and Vaca Muerta about five. Redburn projects margins widening from twenty-two point nine to twenty-five point six percent by twenty twenty-seven. The drivers to track are Baker Hughes rig counts, the import monitor, PipeLogix, the Strait, and working capital days.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up, Stock-driven ratings, and this read is mine. The Target Setup is Demand-driven EPSU. The step-up is a volume and price recovery across deepwater, the Middle East and United States onshore. I considered CfC in quality cyclical at trough-on-trough, leading to reversion to normal. Earnings are at a trough, but the price is not: it sits five and a half percent below its fifty-two week high after a sixty percent gain over twelve months. The earnings half of that setup is present; the valuation half is not.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. There is no Case Riskiness rating. The business is simple and the case is not. One product, one service model and net cash make the operating story easy to follow. The layers around it are harder: a geopolitical event in the Strait; a policy variable that cuts both ways, since Section two thirty-two lifts pricing and costs one hundred and forty to one hundred and fifty million dollars a quarter; Argentine capital controls; and a controlling shareholder with four independent directors of ten.

Fifth, business quality. Strong, and not the contested variable. The Business Foundations report of the fourth of September gives a Foundation Verdict of strong: a margin premium of about nine hundred basis points over the sector median and one hundred percent conversion of net income to free cash flow. The Rig Direct lock-in is organisational: a customer that outsources procurement has dismantled the function it would need to rebuild to leave. The cost of that moat is working capital: one hundred and forty-three days in June twenty twenty-six, up from one hundred and twenty-eight a year earlier, against Vallourec's eighty-six and a historical commitment below one hundred and thirty.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The price was twenty-six euros thirty on the sixteenth of October, a market value of about twenty-five point four billion euros, on a current price to earnings of thirteen times, the fifty-fifth percentile of its history. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of the sixteenth of October is C, a valuation pin only, with no Modal return on file. The script's plausible return calculation of the fifteenth of October averages forty-six and a half percent a year, but it gets there by moving the multiple from thirteen to twenty-three point two times, the ninetieth percentile of ten years, on consensus earnings growth of only six point seven percent a year. I would not lean on that re-rating. Earnings growth of about seven percent plus a dividend yield of about six point eight percent gives roughly thirteen to fourteen percent a year without it. Sell-side targets run from twenty-six euros at Redburn to thirty-two at Jefferies.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of the sixteenth of October is A, environment only, with the stock in the Overweight services row. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the fifteenth and its Technical Momentum reading of the sixteenth are both B. The pros: a multi-year deepwater cycle, protectionism tightening the domestic market, and imports down thirty-four percent year on year. The cons: United States land distributors hold seven months of pipe, welded imports jumped eighty-one percent month on month in July, and Chinese mills now target Saudi Arabia and Qatar.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. The pre-mortem's winning profile is False Friend. A flattering print on seamless pricing masks onshore volume contraction and working-capital friction. The doubled dividend and paused buyback obscure cash conversion pressure: Redburn cut its twenty twenty-six free cash flow forecast by thirty-four percent on receivables. Tariffs impose a five to six percent structural drag on processing margins, and a prolonged Hormuz closure keeps margins below twenty-five percent. Governance caps what a minority holder is paid: four hundred and fourteen million dollars of related-party dividends in a half-year, and a free float that could force a Milan delisting by twenty twenty-seven. The bear case is fifteen to sixteen euros against twenty-six thirty.

The one-line view. Bullish, as a working hypothesis: the world's largest oil and gas pipe maker, self-funded and at an earnings trough with dated recoveries ahead, but with no memo yet, a price near its high, and the question of who keeps the rent still open.
