Week of Friday 2026-11-13. Components 4 to 6 of the Portfolio Fitness for Fighting judgement: how fit the live portfolio is, the named gaps to the optimal portfolio, the changes worth considering and the Lists’ tilt.

Suggestions for Richard’s consideration, not instructions to trade

The week in brief

  • The portfolio rates 64 out of 100, grade C, up from 60. It is getting fitter because of what was sold, not because of what is owned: the weakest holding left, several weak charts repaired, and no holding was added in the rows the target most wants.
  • Gap 1, the shortage of Overweight rows outside consumer staples, is unchanged and matters most. The portfolio owns no large pharma, insurer, integrated utility or energy name.
  • Gap 2, a portfolio too small and too fragile for a target that is Double Underweight Fragility, is unchanged. Eleven of fifteen holdings are in the bottom 41% of the universe by size.
  • Gap 3, weight in rows the target holds Underweight, widened because the target moved. Electrical equipment went to Underweight beneath Arteche, and the largest position, Helios Towers, already sits in an Underweight row.
  • At the portfolio level, the limit that deserves most attention is a cap on total weight in Underweight rows (amendment 12, strengthened). Three limits are weaker: the gross exposure review, the cap on rate-sensitive rows and the cap on weak charts. There is no new amendment.
  • The environment read is less certain than a week ago, which cuts both ways for the portfolio. The rate-sensitive and small-cap exposures that the target counts against the portfolio are the exposures a confirmed easing by the Federal Reserve would reward first.
  • Stock-specific: Theon, Xvivo, D'Ieteren and Bally's Intralot fight the environment on most measures, while Arteche and Volex have good cases in the wrong place. In the MI Pool, large pharma and the integrated utilities close Gap 1 and Gap 2 together.
  • The Lists' tilt holds at 0.625, Overweighting Fitness for Fighting. The tilt goes to 0.50 if the weak-breadth signal ends.

The gaps

Gap 1: the portfolio is short of every Overweight row except consumer staples. Unchanged, and the largest gap

  • The portfolio's only exposure to a row the target Overweights is its three staples holdings: Carlsberg, Fevertree and Hilton Food, 11.86 points.
  • The missing rows are the ones the target expects to hold up if the rates squeeze persists.
  • The energy part of the gap shrank this week. The Energy industry went to Neutral, so energy is a view on three sectors and no longer the first thing the portfolio lacks. Large pharma and hospitals, insurance and integrated utilities are now the core of the gap.
  • Counter-case: the market has been moving to every one of these rows for three months, and the defensive lead narrowed this week. Filling the gap now risks buying the crowded end of a rotation in the week it starts to fade, and the three staples holdings already give the portfolio its defensive leg.

Gap 2: the portfolio is small and fragile where the target wants large, simple and proven. Unchanged

  • Eleven of fifteen holdings, 72% of invested weight, sit at or below the 41st percentile of the universe by size, while Fragility is Double Underweight.
  • Five (Hilton Food, Xvivo, Hexatronic, Sdiptech and, at the 13th, CVS Group and Fevertree) are in the bottom eighth or close to it. Avanza, Carlsberg, D'Ieteren and IMCD are the only holdings above the 60th percentile.
  • The size shortfall is the portfolio's design as much as a gap: a concentrated portfolio of smaller companies will always read short of size. The regime makes it a gap, because Richard's own line is that investors sell "small, niche, weird, unproven, risky, complex".
  • Counter-case: the Fragility row rests on credit evidence three weeks old, and breadth has broadened for three weeks, which is smaller stocks recovering. The target lifts Fragility one notch if 200-day breadth reaches 65% with the index within 1% of its high (63% today), and Gap 2 would then narrow without a trade.

Gap 3: weight in rows the target holds Underweight, concentrated in the largest position. Wider this week, by a tilt change

  • Five holdings, 18.51 points or 31% of invested weight, now sit in rows held at Underweight or Double Underweight. Helios Towers, D'Ieteren, Bally's Intralot and Theon are 14.83 points, and the move of Electrical equipment to Underweight adds Arteche.
  • More than two fifths of the 18.51 points is one position: Helios Towers, in Towers, which the target holds Underweight because the sector is valued off a yield and financed with debt.
  • Counter-case: Gap 3 is the gap most exposed to the environment read being wrong.
    • If the Federal Reserve is easing, as two weeks of undated headlines say, Towers is among the first rows to lift, and Helios Towers' own readings say the market is already paying for the case.
    • Arteche has the strongest bottom-up case in the portfolio, 91.4.

Proposed amendments to portfolio-level risk management

Thirteen amendments are carried from last week and none is new; four have a changed footing.

Amendment 12: a cap on the portfolio's total weight in rows the target holds at Underweight or Double Underweight. Status: strengthened

  • What it would do: holding more than a set share in Underweight rows would become an explicit decision, taken holding by holding, with a written reason that the case outruns its row. Arteche is the model exception: a top bottom-up case in a row the market is selling.
  • Why it is stronger: the weight is 14.83 points, from 15.13, and rises to 18.51 with this week's tilt change beneath Arteche. Last week the amendment would have fired on a trade (the addition to Helios Towers); this week it would fire on a judgement, which is the other case it was written for.
  • Counter-case: amendment 12 overlaps amendment 3 on three of the five names, and a cap that tightens whenever the Assistant Portfolio Manager moves a letter hands the tilt table a power over the portfolio that the tilt scorecard has not yet earned.

Amendment 3: duration and leverage limits. Status: weakened on both the cap and the floor

  • What it would do: a cap on exposure to the Underweight rows valued off a yield or financed at the margin, with additions above the ceiling an explicit decision, and a floor on the yield-beneficiary rows.
  • The cap: the capped set holds 11.31 points, from 11.67 (Helios Towers 7.92, D'Ieteren 2.49, Bally's Intralot 0.90), with Helios Towers larger again.
  • The floor: the floor rows hold 11.86 points, all staples, and the floor loses its energy leg at the industry level this week.
  • Why it is weaker: the direction of rates is no longer established. The last dated read is two weeks old and the headlines say the Federal Reserve is easing.
  • Counter-case, now stronger than the case: if easing is confirmed, amendment 3 is the limit a relief rally punishes most.

Amendment 1: review gross exposure against the environment read, with a downward bias. Status: weakened, partly delivered

  • Why it is weaker: invested weight fell to 59.71 points from 65.60, so the portfolio moved the way the review pointed. The price evidence for a downward bias also softened: the index is at its 52-week high with breadth broadening.
  • Why it stays: the weak-breadth combination is still present.
  • Counter-case: a portfolio 60% invested at an index high has already paid for its caution if the rally broadens.

Amendment 13: a cap on the share of invested weight in holdings graded Technical Momentum D or F. Status: weakened, largely delivered

  • Why it is weaker: the share is 29%, from 54%, after Alfen's exit and repaired charts, so the shape the cap was written against has halved without the cap. Arteche, Volex, D'Ieteren and Bally's Intralot grade D; Theon and Xvivo grade F.
  • Why it stays: as a limit on additions, set above today's level.
  • Counter-case, unchanged: the Momentum row is Underweight, so the target itself says not to chase strength, and the cap must never force an exit.

The Lists' tilt

  • How large the gap is: Gaps 1 and 2 did not close and Gap 3 widened. The portfolio's better rating came from selling weak names, which removes unfitness without adding fitness.
  • Why not 0.75: the environment read is less certain than a week ago. The causes of the regime were unread for a second week and the price signal weakened, so the target the Lists would chase harder is itself held with lower conviction.

Endnotes: how this note is made

Who decides. This note proposes; Richard decides capital.

How the rating is computed.

  • The measured reading is the position-weighted mean of the Top Down/Exogenously-driven composite across 15 of 15 holdings, computed on last week's carried-forward tilts at a Lists' tilt of 0.625. The reading already includes each holding's fragility adjustment, so size is not deducted again.
  • Grades follow the scorer's bands as they appear on the holdings (78.2 grades B, 73.0 grades C, 47.7 grades D).
  • The earlier readings in the track that carry a correction note are compared on their measured starts.
  • Weights are points of the whole portfolio; shares are of the 59.71 points invested.

What could not be read this week.

  • Last week's per-holding sizes and readings are known only where last week's amendments and reasons quoted them, so the split of the rise between the portfolio changing and the ratings moving is an estimate.
  • The holdings file does not say whether a change in a position's weight was a trade or a price move; "cut", "raised", "fell" and "rose" describe the weight.
  • The holdings file does not say what the uninvested 40.29 points are held in.
  • Small-cap growth against large-cap yield has no European series, so Gap 2 is measured by the size percentile alone.
  • Revenue by country is not in the holdings file, so country concentration is by listing only.
