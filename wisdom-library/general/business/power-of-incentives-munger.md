# Power of Incentives (Munger)

## Definition
Munger's super-claim, sharpened: *"Never, ever, think about something else when you should be thinking about the power of incentives."* The economic, reputational, psychological, and career-stake compensation structure of the actors in a system explains 80%+ of the behaviour you observe. When a behaviour looks irrational, the incentive that explains it has been misidentified, not absent. *Cui bono* (who benefits) is the analytical first move.

This is the deeper, harder version of "Incentives Drive All Behaviour" (Gold). That entry frames the principle. This entry sharpens the **operational discipline** Munger insisted on: the incentive lens is not a one-time check; it is the FIRST tool reached for in every analytical situation, including the ones where you didn't think it applied.

## Why It Matters
The single biggest source of analytical error among smart investors is taking stated rationale at face value. Stated rationale is what actors say; revealed incentive is what they actually optimise. The two are routinely orthogonal. Sell-side ratings, management guidance, regulator commentary, expert-call testimony — every channel of information has an incentive-shaped lens that distorts it. The discipline is to map the incentive of each information source BEFORE weighing the content.

It is also the most reliable ⚡ outlier-detector: the deliberately weird signal usually traces back to an incentive nobody named.

## Application
- **Universal first-question:** for every information artefact (memo, expert call, sell-side note, press release), name the incentive of the source. If you can't name it, your read of the content is unreliable.
- **Management assessment:** map compensation drivers (revenue / margin / ROIC / share price / equity grant timing / tenure). Behaviour will track the dominant driver, not the strategy slide.
- **Information-source weighting (RESEARCHER):** before extracting from sell-side or expert-call sources, write one line on the source's incentive. Surface this in §10/§11/§12 sceptical-lens treatment.
- **Edge detection partner:** when a deliberately-weird signal appears, ask "who is incentivised to produce this signal, suppress it, or amplify it?" Frequently surfaces the actor whose M/M/O is about to crystallise.
- **ACH discipline:** for each competing hypothesis, identify the actor whose incentives the hypothesis serves. The hypothesis serving the most plausible incentive structure rarely wins on evidence weight alone, but it does lift the prior.
