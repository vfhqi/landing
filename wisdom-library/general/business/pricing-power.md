# Pricing Power

## Definition
The ability to raise prices without losing volume. The single most important attribute of a great business (Buffett). Demonstrated in market, not theoretical. Visible through: actual price increases, customer retention post-increase, margin stability in inflation.

## Why It Matters
Pricing power is the ultimate test of competitive advantage. If a company can't raise prices, it doesn't have a moat — it has a market share that can be competed away.

## Application
For every business assessment: has the company actually raised prices? What happened to volume? Was pricing passed through in inflation? If pricing power is theoretical ('they could raise prices'), downgrade the moat assessment.

## Examples from Track Record
- Fevertree: demonstrated pricing power in spirits category.
- Inflationary environment lesson: not just pricing power but raw material cost sensitivity. Companies slow to catch on, then over-react.
