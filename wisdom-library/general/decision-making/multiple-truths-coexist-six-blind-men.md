# Multiple Truths Can Coexist (Six Blind Men and the Elephant)

## Definition
The Buddhist parable: six blind men each touch a different part of an elephant — leg, tusk, trunk, ear, side, tail — and each declare the elephant is "like a pillar / spear / snake / fan / wall / rope" respectively. Each is correct *about the part they touched*. None is correct about the whole. The parable's edge: their disagreement is not a sign that one is right and the others are wrong; it is a sign that the truth is composite and each of them holds a partial, complementary fragment.

Concept B's CRITICAL ACTION 3: *multiple truths can coexist*. When two evidence-based views appear to contradict each other, the most common analytical situation is that BOTH are partially correct — and the synthesis (the elephant) is what the analyst needs to construct. The wrong move is to pick one and discard the other. The right move is to ask: *what shape is the whole that contains both?*

## Why It Matters
Most "contradictions" in financial analysis are not contradictions; they are the analyst encountering the elephant from two angles. Sell-side BUY ratings + revisions falling = both are evidence about the elephant (rating reflects long-horizon view + analyst incentive structure; revisions reflect short-horizon view + earnings momentum; both are real, both are signal). Treating them as a contradiction to resolve is the analytical error; treating them as two facets of a composite truth is the discipline.

This is also the structural antidote to the binary-answer reflex that LLMs (and analysts under time pressure) fall into. The instinct is to choose; the discipline is to *integrate*. The integration is harder, slower, and produces better answers.

Pairs operationally with **Concept B as a whole**: go to the most-different viewpoint (CRITICAL ACTION 1), stew until uncomfortable (CRITICAL ACTION 2), let the multiple truths coexist (CRITICAL ACTION 3). The three together are the cognitive discipline that turns disconfirming evidence from a threat into a structural input.

## Application
- **When two information sources contradict, synthesise before resolving.** Write a paragraph that names the elephant: what is the underlying composite truth that contains both partial views? Often the resolution is "they are both right about different timeframes / different segments / different scenarios".
- **For sceptical-lens treatment:** the "and yet" bullet is the structural operationalisation of multiple-truths-coexist. State the lead view; then state the complementary view; then state the composite.
- **For PRO/CON memo structures:** these are dangerous if treated as a binary. Use them as elephant-touching exercises: what is the shape that contains both the PRO and CON?
- **For peer-cohort analysis:** when peer dispersion is wide, don't average it away. The dispersion IS the signal — different peers are touching different parts of the cohort elephant.
- **For thematics work:** the entire AI-thematic project (UWB-6) is operationally an elephant-touching exercise. Each sub-theme is a partial truth; the whole-theme view requires synthesis.
- **In the AI-Dunning-Kruger declaration:** explicitly state the partial truth that the leading view *misses*. Not as a counter-argument, but as a complement.
- **Universal anti-binary discipline:** before concluding "X is true and Y is false", check whether "both X and Y are true under different conditions" is the better-fitting hypothesis. Often it is.
