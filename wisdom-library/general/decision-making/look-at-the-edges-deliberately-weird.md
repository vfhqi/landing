# Look at the Edges / Deliberately Weird

## Definition
The most consequential change in a complex system shows up first at the edges, the cross-roads, and the outliers — not in the consensus middle. Train the eye to spot what's *deliberately weird* — the small detail that doesn't fit the prevailing narrative. The internal cue is "things that make me go *hmmmm*"; that prickle is signal, not noise. Coined in spirit by Neal Stephenson (cross-disciplinary edge-walkers see what specialists miss) and reinforced operationally by the Munger discipline of inverting and looking for what others have decided not to look at.

## Why It Matters
Consensus information is already priced. By the time something is well-known, the incremental analytical return has compressed to zero. Edge signals are where unmodelled variance lives. They are also asymmetric: most edges turn out to be nothing, but the ones that turn out to be something tend to be regime-shifting (means/motive/opportunity for the next leg). For an investor whose job is to find non-consensus truth before the cohort, the discipline of staring at edges and tolerating weirdness is the structural source of edge.

The opposite habit — explaining anomalies away with the prevailing narrative — is the single most reliable way to miss the next inflection. "Boiling frog" failures almost always trace back to an edge-detector that was trained out of the operator.

## Application
- **Triaging and ESA work:** explicitly catalogue what doesn't fit. Maintain a "things-that-make-me-go-hmmmm" register per name. Don't try to resolve weirdness too quickly — let it stew.
- **Cross-roads detection:** name the boundary the company is on (regulatory, technological, demographic, competitive). The biggest moves come at boundary crossings.
- **CEO/operator screening:** look for non-standard biographies. The WH Smith CEO who came from a different industry and asked the "wrong" questions is the archetype. Operators who do the unconventional thing — within reason — produce the unconventional outcome.
- **Outlier amplification:** when a data point is >2 sigma from peers, treat the data point as the lead, not the footnote. Most analysis filters outliers; the edge-detector elevates them.
- **Memo discipline:** every Triaging/ESA/DD memo has a dedicated **⚡ Outliers / Deliberately Weird** sweep that surfaces (a) statistical outliers vs cohort, (b) qualitative oddities the operator hasn't explained, (c) cross-roads exposures the consensus is ignoring.

## Examples from Track Record
- WH Smith CEO archetype (illustrative): non-traditional background → asked the unconventional retail-format questions → travel-retail pivot was the cross-road that re-rated the business.
