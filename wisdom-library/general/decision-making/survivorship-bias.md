# Survivorship Bias

## Definition
Studying only survivors (successful companies, winning strategies) gives a distorted picture because it ignores the many failures with similar characteristics. The successful fund manager may have the same strategy as 99 failed ones — the difference was luck.

## Why It Matters
Critical for evaluating any 'lessons from success' framework. Without understanding the failure rate, lessons from winners are potentially meaningless.

## Application
When drawing lessons from successful stocks/strategies: how many stocks with similar characteristics failed? Is the success pattern genuinely predictive or is it survivorship bias?

## Examples from Track Record
- Mauboussin: skill vs luck decomposition. How much of the track record is skill vs the right market regime?
- Track record analysis: 57% win rate means 43% of months lose money. The wins must be large enough to compensate.
