# Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH — Heuer)

## Definition
A structured analytical method developed by Richards J. Heuer Jr. at the CIA to combat confirmation bias and force discipline on judgement under uncertainty. The procedure: (1) generate a *complete* set of mutually exclusive competing hypotheses, including the unlikely ones; (2) list all evidence; (3) build a matrix of evidence × hypothesis, scoring each cell for *consistency / inconsistency / not applicable*; (4) the winning hypothesis is **not** the one with the most supporting evidence — it is the one with the **least disconfirming evidence**; (5) identify the diagnostic evidence (the items that discriminate hardest between hypotheses) and prioritise gathering more of it; (6) re-test as new evidence arrives.

The crucial inversion: ACH is a falsification engine, not a confirmation engine. It picks the hypothesis hardest to kill, not the hypothesis easiest to support.

## Why It Matters
Investing under uncertainty is hypothesis-management at heart. The default analytical reflex is to build the case *for* the leading view; ACH forces the structural discipline to build the case *against* every view simultaneously, then choose the survivor. It is the operational engine of "Strong Views, Weakly Held" — the matrix is what makes the views revisable.

ACH is the explicit alternative to the analyst's most common failure mode: **anchoring on the first plausible story and recruiting confirming evidence**. Heuer's empirical claim — borne out across the CIA's analytical track record — is that the hypothesis with the fewest hard inconsistencies wins more often than the hypothesis with the most consistencies, because consistency is cheap (almost any story can be made consistent with some evidence) but inconsistency is expensive (a single hard contradiction is fatal).

## Application
- **In Pre-Mortem (Q19):** ACH is the structural spine. Generate ≥3 plausible bear hypotheses + the leading bull hypothesis + the "neither / null" hypothesis. Build the matrix. Identify the diagnostic items. Surface the lowest-disconfirming-evidence survivor.
- **In Analytical KQ work:** every analytical KQ should be structured as a multi-hypothesis ACH, not a single-hypothesis defence. Default to ≥3 hypotheses.
- **In KD Assessment (Q14 §10):** apply ACH across drivers. Each driver is a sub-hypothesis. The cross-driver matrix surfaces which drivers are mutually-exclusive vs. mutually-reinforcing.
- **In sceptical-lens treatment (every memo §10/§11/§12):** name the second-and-third hypothesis explicitly. The sceptical bullet is the ACH sketch.
- **In the AI-Dunning-Kruger check:** the question "have I generated a counter-hypothesis with at least one piece of disconfirming evidence that, if true, would invalidate the leading view?" is the minimal-form ACH test.
- **As outlier-validation:** when a ⚡ deliberately-weird signal appears, run mini-ACH: which competing hypothesis would the signal most strongly discriminate? Most outliers are diagnostically sharp.

## Examples from Track Record
- DCC pre-mortem: ACH-style enumeration of the conglomerate-discount, credit-cycle, regulatory, and operator-quality hypotheses produced the diagnostic insight — the conglomerate-discount hypothesis had the fewest hard inconsistencies.
- XVIVO complexity-beyond-competence problem: a proper ACH would have made the "case is unanalysable at this stage" hypothesis a contender, surfacing earlier.
- Premortem framework already embedded in WL (`pre-mortem`) — ACH is the disciplined structural form that pre-mortem becomes when matrix-ised.
- 10 INVALIDATION ACHs (Richard's live-case rules): the *Hard Bright Lines* are an applied ACH — each one is a single disconfirming-evidence trigger. ACH formally codifies the discipline already operating in Richard's investment process.
