# Top Decile / Top Quartile Grading (A-F Bell Curve)

## Definition
The simple framework Richard uses to find outlier-best investments: **target the top decile, only accept the top quartile.** Operationalised as a 5-letter A-F grading scale applied to every meaningful metric, attribute, or finding:

| Grade | Percentile | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Top 10% (top decile) | Outlier excellence — what we are trying to find |
| **B** | 75-89% (top quartile, ex-A) | Acceptable threshold for our investments |
| **C** | 50-74% (2nd quartile) | Above median but unremarkable |
| **D** | 25-49% (3rd quartile) | Below median — concerning |
| **F** | Bottom 25% | Bottom-of-class — likely disqualifying |

## Why It Matters
A concentrated 5-15 position long-only portfolio cannot afford C-grade businesses. The mathematics of concentration require A-grade conviction with B-grade fallback tolerance. Applying the grading every time forces the analyst (or Watson) to take a position rather than describe.

The grading is also the same scale as the FCS rating system + APM IC/BB family ratings — keeping it consistent across RESEARCHER (per-metric grading) and APM (composite ratings) means the language compounds across the workflow.

## Application
- Every metric, attribute, or finding gets a grade where the comparison is defined
- Inline marker: prepend `[A]`, `[B]`, `[C]`, `[D]`, or `[F]` to the verdict word/phrase
- Always state which base rate the grade is against: *"[A] vs sector"*
- Composite metrics get the analyst's judgement informed by components — say so
- No `+` / `-` modifiers from RESEARCHER (per APM A&J SOP v2.1 R18) — APM only
- If you cannot grade, say so and flag

This is operationalised as **Communication Principle #2** in `memory/skills/communication-principles/SKILL.md`.

The grading also encodes the **investment philosophy**: *target the top decile, accept the top quartile*. If the case as a whole averages C or below across pillars, the case fails the conviction test regardless of narrative.

## Examples from Track Record
- **Diploma (track record):** ROCE 28% — `[A] vs distribution sector` (median 13%, only 3 of 47 peers >25%). This single grade carried huge weight in the conviction-building.
- **BFF (Feb-26 cockroach lesson):** When the metrics started slipping from B to C to D, Richard held — endowment bias. Had the D ratings been called out clearly with the `❌` prefix, the exit signal would have been clearer earlier.
- **WH Smith (Apr-26):** Travel division `[B] vs UK travel retail` carried the case; UK High Street `[F] vs UK retail` — clear bifurcation. Made the focus-on-Travel thesis obvious.
- **CARLB (Apr-26 Q1 beat):** Volume +5.3% — `[B] vs European brewers` (median 0-2%, only Heineken at 4%). The grade made the beat actionable not anecdotal.

## Boundary Conditions
- The grade is only as good as the comparison set. Garbage in = garbage out. State the peer set explicitly.
- Avoid grade inflation. If three of five pillars grade B+, the case should not be presented as A.
- Grades change over time as comparison sets shift — re-check every 6 months for live positions.
