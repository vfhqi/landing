# Means / Motive / Opportunity

## Definition
A three-leg causal triangulation borrowed from the investigative / forensic tradition: a step-change has happened (or is about to happen) only when an actor has the **means** (capability, resources, capital), the **motive** (incentive, pressure, pay-off), AND the **opportunity** (window, structural permission, unblocked path). Two legs without the third produces inertia, not change. All three legs aligned produces inflection.

## Why It Matters
Most edge-signals are ambiguous on their own. Means/Motive/Opportunity is the discipline that converts an interesting anomaly into a falsifiable hypothesis. It also forces the analyst to name *who acts*, *why*, and *when the window opens* — three questions consensus narratives routinely skip. When the answer to any of the three is "no one really" or "not yet", the edge-signal is probably not actionable, and the discipline tells you to park.

It is the structural complement to "Look at the edges" — the edge-detector spots the anomaly; M/M/O tests whether the anomaly has the causal architecture to actually move.

## Application
- **For each ⚡ outlier flag:** force the M/M/O test. Who is the actor? What capability do they have? What incentive is pulling them? What unblocks the opportunity right now (not in five years)?
- **For management/operator change cases:** map M/M/O on the operator. Has the new CEO got the means (mandate from board, balance-sheet flexibility), the motive (compensation re-set, equity grant, reputation play), and the opportunity (industry inflection or competitor disruption opening a window)?
- **For competitive disruption / fulcrum drivers:** map M/M/O on the disruptor and the incumbent. The classic miss: incumbent has motive but no means; disruptor has means and motive but the opportunity hasn't opened (regulatory lag, customer-switching cost not yet eroded).
- **For pre-mortem and ACH:** treat each competing hypothesis through M/M/O. Hypotheses that fail one of the three legs drop out faster.

## Examples from Track Record
- DCC pre-mortem: bear-case fragmentation was the *motive* of the management team to break the conglomerate, but the *opportunity* was gated by the credit cycle — explained the timing puzzle.
- Carlsberg-DK: post-acquisition capacity rationalisation needed all three (means: balance-sheet headroom; motive: ROIC recovery; opportunity: regulatory window in EM markets).
- FEVR US fulcrum: the disruptor's M/M/O was complete in 2024; the consensus was still pricing inertia. M/M/O test would have surfaced this earlier.
- Semicap WFE recovery signal: the customer (foundry) had means (capex up-cycle), motive (node transition), and opportunity (tool delivery slots open) — three-leg confirmation lifted conviction.
