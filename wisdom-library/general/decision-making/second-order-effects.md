# Second-Order Effects

## Definition
First-order effects are direct and obvious. Second-order effects are indirect and often counterintuitive. Most people stop at first-order. Second-level thinking asks: 'And then what?' Third-level: 'And then what after that?'

## Why It Matters
Second-order thinking is where investing edge lives. If everyone can see the first-order effect, it's priced in. The edge comes from seeing what happens next.

## Application
For every thesis driver: trace the chain 2-3 levels deep. Iran conflict → oil price → European chems input costs → margin compression → earnings downgrades → SP impact. Each level reduces certainty but increases edge.

- Iran analysis (Mar-26): Richard's masterclass in multi-order thinking. 1st/2nd/3rd order effects traced across sectors.
- Howard Marks: 'Second-level thinking is what separates great investors from ordinary ones.'
