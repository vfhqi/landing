# Regression to the Mean

## Definition
Extreme outcomes tend to be followed by more average outcomes. Applies to earnings growth, margins, returns on capital, and stock returns. Not because of a mystical force — because extreme outcomes often have a luck component that doesn't persist.

## Why It Matters
Prevents extrapolation of extreme performance (positive or negative). When a company delivers 50% growth, the base rate says it's more likely to deliver 20-30% next year than 50% again.

## Application
For any extreme metric: ask what the sustainable run-rate is. If current performance is 2x the historical average, expect regression. Size accordingly.

## Examples from Track Record
- Attendo: 'Leading share with mediocre economics is never as good/bad as peaks/troughs imply.'
- Avanza: lazy extrapolation of cyclical high activity = ignoring regression.
