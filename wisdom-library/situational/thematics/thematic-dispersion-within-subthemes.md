# Thematic Dispersion Within Sub-Themes Is Where Alpha Lives

## Definition

Within any major thematic, the *between-sub-theme* analytical question is usually consensus-priced (the market understands which sub-themes are exposed). The *within-sub-theme* dispersion — which specific names within a sub-theme have genuinely durable economics versus those with apparent-but-fragile moats — is where consensus is undifferentiated and where bottom-up analysis adds the most value.

## Why It Matters

Thematic baskets capture some of the upside but also drag in the losers. A concentrated investor with bottom-up analytical capability extracts alpha by being right on within-sub-theme dispersion, not by being right on which sub-themes matter. The latter is largely consensus.

## Evidence Base (AI Thematic, 3-May-26)

The Q1 2026 European software selloff treated all reference-data and industrial-software incumbents as uniformly disrupted — single-event repricing of an aggregate concern. Within-sub-theme analysis revealed:

- **Most defensible:** RELX (legal/scientific), Wolters Kluwer (tax/regulatory), SAP S/4HANA core, Nemetschek (REVERSED from initial classification when AS evidence on AEC client conservatism + 54.7% subscription growth surfaced).
- **Mid-tier defensibility:** Hexagon metrology (high integration in factory workflows), Dassault manufacturing-integrated PLM customers.
- **Most exposed:** Sage SMB (low integration for smallest customers, low data moat), Dassault CAD-only customer segment, long-tail European specialist-vertical software.

The dispersion within "European software" is enormous; the average is meaningless.

## Application Rules

1. **Reject basket exposure to thematics.** Identify the highest-edge dispersion question within each sub-theme; concentrate analytical work there.
2. **The discriminating questions are usually job-to-be-done + integration depth + data proprietariness.** Apply consistently across names within the sub-theme.
3. **Single-event aggregate selloffs in a thematically-exposed sub-theme are often dispersion opportunities.** If the market repriced every name in a sub-theme by similar magnitude on a single catalyst, the market got the question wrong at the aggregate level — bottom-up dispersion analysis can find the misclassified protected names.
4. **Update classifications as new evidence arrives.** AS Q2 evidence reversed the initial Nemetschek classification (Mid-tier → Most defensible). Within-sub-theme positioning is dynamic, not static.

## Limits / Counter-Evidence

- For very long-cycle disruption, all incumbents within a sub-theme eventually compress. Within-sub-theme dispersion provides 18-36 month alpha but not necessarily 5+ year alpha.
- When a sub-theme is genuinely systemically disrupted (typewriters, video rental), dispersion converges to zero. Don't apply this principle to terminal-disruption sub-themes.
