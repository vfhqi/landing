# Bear Case Lives in Qualitative Framing, Not Quantified Sell-Side Models

## Definition

When researching a contested thematic, the sell-side bull case is typically modelled and validated with explicit quantitative scenarios; the bear case is typically articulated as qualitative framing without quantified base-case models. This asymmetry is a meta-signal about positioning and where the actual edge lives.

## Why It Matters

Equity-investor positioning is influenced by what is modelled, not by what is qualitatively articulated. When the bear case lives only in qualitative framing, the market positioning will skew bullish even if the qualitative bear arguments are persuasive. This creates asymmetric multiple-compression risk — the market hasn't priced the bear scenarios because no model says it should.

For an investor doing bottom-up work, this asymmetry is exploitable: you can take the qualitative bear case seriously and position for it before the sell-side updates its models.

## Evidence Base (AI Thematic AS Q3, 3-May-26)

For the hyperscaler-capex-retrenchment failure path:
- **Bull case:** Modelled by GS, Morgan Stanley, JPM, BofA, Bernstein with explicit quantitative trajectory. Capex revised UP to consensus $527bn 2026, $600bn 2027.
- **Bear case:** Articulated qualitatively (capex/OCF approaching 90% historically marks late-cycle behaviour; OpenAI/Anthropic still cash-burning at scale; ROI timeline unclear). **Quantified moderation models with explicit probability weights DO NOT SURFACE in AS evidence.**

The asymmetry means the market is positioned for the bull case in size; an actual hyperscaler capex moderation would produce sharper-than-modelled multiple compression because the position rebalancing has nowhere to land.

## Application Rules

1. **In any contested thematic, audit the bull/bear evidence base for asymmetry.** Is the bull case modelled? Is the bear case modelled? If one is and the other isn't, that is a positioning signal.
2. **When the bear case is qualitative-only, the asymmetric trade is to position for the bear case fractionally,** even if you believe the bull case more strongly. The reward-to-risk on the bear hedge is improved by the absence of model-based positioning support.
3. **Use the asymmetry to inform pre-mortem stress testing.** If you can articulate a bear case that no sell-side model has quantified, you have potential edge. If your bear case mirrors quantified sell-side bear models, the edge is more limited.
4. **Watch for transitions.** The first quantified bear-case model to surface from a credible sell-side analyst can trigger position rebalancing that exceeds the change in fundamentals.

## Limits / Counter-Evidence

- Sometimes bear cases live in qualitative framing because they are weak and the modelling work hasn't been done because no analyst takes them seriously. Distinguish "bear case is qualitative because it's hard to model" from "bear case is qualitative because no one believes it."
- Sell-side incentives systematically bias toward bull-case modelling (sell-side analysts get paid for buy ratings). The asymmetry partly reflects this; not all of it is fundamentals-driven positioning.
