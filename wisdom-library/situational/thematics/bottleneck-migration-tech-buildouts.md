# Bottleneck Migration in Tech Buildouts

## Definition

In any large multi-stage technology buildout, the binding constraint shifts predictably over time as supply responds to demand at each stage. The capital cycle relieves one bottleneck, which transfers the binding constraint upstream or downstream. Disproportionate value accrues to the owners of *the current* binding constraint; predicting the *next* binding constraint is where forward edge lives.

## Why It Matters

For an 18-month investor in a multi-year buildout (AI, energy transition, electrification), the binding constraint is unlikely to shift within the window — but for a 24-36 month investor, the constraint shift is the dominant strategic question. Riding the current bottleneck owners is the cleanest beneficiary trade; positioning for the next bottleneck is the contrarian trade.

## Predictable Migration Pattern

In tech buildouts, constraints tend to migrate in this order:

1. **Capital** (initially binding when demand visible but uncertain)
2. **Land / siting permission** (when capital plentiful but locations specific)
3. **Physical infrastructure** (grid connection, water, road access) — typically the longest-binding constraint because lead times are 5-10 years
4. **Heavy equipment** (transformers, gas turbines, cables) — manufacturing capacity expansion lags demand by 3-5 years
5. **Skilled labour** (engineering, technical, operational) — this is the constraint that emerges in the middle phase
6. **Ancillary specialised inputs** (cooling fluids, specialised materials, advanced packaging) — late-cycle bottleneck
7. **Application-layer integration** (software, workflow, change management) — terminal constraint

## Application to AI Buildout (3-May-26)

- **Currently binding:** Grid connection capacity (7-10 year queue across Western Europe). Heavy power equipment (transformer lead times 5+ years). HVDC cables (combined ~€35bn backlog through 2028+).
- **Next binding (likely 18-24 months out):** Skilled engineering labour for installation, commissioning, operations. Specialised cooling capacity. Advanced packaging (HBM, hybrid bonding).
- **Investment implication:** Current bottleneck owners (Siemens Energy, Schneider, Prysmian/Nexans/NKT, ABB) capture early-cycle rents. Specialised engineering services (Spie, Bilfinger, Vinci energies, Eiffage) and back-end semis (BESI, ASM International, Soitec) capture mid-cycle rents.

## Application Rules

1. **Identify the current binding constraint** in any tech buildout you analyse. Ask: where does additional demand actually translate into delivered capacity? The slowest step is the bottleneck.
2. **Concentrate beneficiary exposure on current bottleneck owners,** not on names that look like beneficiaries but operate at non-binding constraints.
3. **Predict the next binding constraint** by mapping the supply response. Where is capital flooding in fastest? That is where the next bottleneck will be.
4. **Time the rotation.** Constraint migration typically happens over 18-36 months as supply response delivers. For an 18-month investor, the current bottleneck dominates; for a 36-month investor, the next bottleneck matters more.

## Limits / Counter-Evidence

- Bottleneck migration can stall if a single constraint becomes structural (e.g., no labour pool exists; no cooling-fluid capacity at all). In such cases the constraint sticks longer than the predictable pattern suggests.
- Buildouts can be cancelled or de-scoped before the constraint migrates. Capital-cycle retrenchment short-circuits the migration pattern.
