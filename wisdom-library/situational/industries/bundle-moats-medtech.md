# Bundle Moats in Fragmented Med-Tech Sub-Sectors

## Definition

In med-tech sub-sectors with 2-3 dominant players (radiation oncology = Varian/Elekta; surgical robotics = Intuitive/Medtronic; orthopaedics = Stryker/Zimmer), the player with multi-product BUNDLING capability progressively wins share against single-product specialists, regardless of point-product quality. Bundle moats are structural and accumulate at 1-2pp/year — invisible quarter-to-quarter, decisive over 3-5 years.

## Why It Matters

The smaller bundle-disadvantaged player almost always under-prices this dynamic in re-rating cases. The share loss is gradual but cumulative. By the time it shows in headline financials, the structural disadvantage has hardened. APM analysis that focuses on point-product technical comparison (Evo vs Halcyon, da Vinci vs Hugo) misses the bundle dynamic that determines hospital-system tender outcomes.

## Application

When evaluating a med-tech case in a fragmented oligopoly:

1. Identify the bundle player and the bundle composition (e.g., Varian = linac + diagnostic imaging + ARIA OIS; Intuitive = da Vinci + service network + training)
2. Test whether the case-target competes at point-product level OR at bundle level
3. Check 3-year share trajectory in regions with hospital consolidation (US > Europe > Japan)
4. If point-product specialist + share losing + hospital consolidation accelerating: structural disadvantage rating, not cyclical

The bundle-moat test is independent of product quality. Elekta's Evo is technologically competitive with Varian's Halcyon, but Evo competes at point-product while Halcyon plugs into the Siemens diagnostic-imaging bundle. Different selling motion; different win rates.

## Examples from Track Record

- **EKTA (May-26):** ESA confirmed Varian-Siemens bundle moat as structural competitive disadvantage. US share erosion 20% → <15% over 3 years validates pattern. Drove BB#2 Foundation Quality C → D rating revision.
- **Intuitive vs Medtronic Hugo (training reference, 2020-24):** Intuitive bundle (da Vinci + service + training network + reusable instruments + surgeon community) kept Medtronic Hugo at <5% share by 2024 despite 2018 launch and competitive technical specs.
