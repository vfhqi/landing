# R&D Capitalisation Policy Unwinding — Persistent Earnings-Quality Drag

## Definition

When a company shifts R&D capitalisation policy DOWNWARD (e.g., COMET shifting from 60% capitalised to 40%), the result is a **100-200bp persistent reported-EBIT drag for 2-3 years** as previously-capitalised projects continue to amortise while new R&D flows fully through OpEx. The drag is non-cash, doesn't affect FCF, and represents REAL accounting-quality improvement — but it depresses reported margins in a way most consensus models miss.

## Why It Matters

Both bull and bear narratives typically misframe this:
- **Bears** cite the drag as evidence of "prior earnings inflation" — overstates the case (the prior accounting was within GAAP norms, just at the aggressive end)
- **Bulls** dismiss as "non-cash, ignore" — overstates the case (reported margins do drive multiple compression while consensus catches up)

The right framing is **three distinct layers**:

1. **Accounting change** = positive (cleaner reporting; closer to economic reality)
2. **Reported margin impact** = negative for 2-3 years (consensus models often slow to adjust)
3. **Underlying ROIC** = unchanged (same R&D dollars, just classified differently)

APM judgement should price in the margin headwind to consensus while NOT downgrading underlying business quality. Different conclusions depending on which layer dominates the case.

## Application

When evaluating any R&D-heavy business (semi-cap, software, pharma, biotech, defence):

1. Check disclosed R&D capitalisation rate over the last 3-5 years
2. If rate has shifted DOWN by >10 percentage points: estimate the persistent margin drag using R&D quantum
   - Drag estimate = (old % - new %) × R&D / Revenue × amortisation period adjustment
   - For COMET: (60% - 40%) × ~SEK 1.3bn / SEK 4.6bn revenue ≈ 5.6% × amortisation pacing ≈ 100-150bp/yr
3. Consensus EBIT margin model: subtract the drag from any management-target margin
4. CMD or guidance update: watch for management explicitly addressing the drag (positive signal — they're being transparent) vs implicitly absorbing it (negative signal — they're hoping investors don't notice)

If consensus already prices the drag (lower target multiple): no incremental APM advantage. If consensus has NOT priced it (typical 12-18 months after policy change): explicit downside not yet in the share price.

## Examples from Track Record

- **COMET (May-26):** Shifted 60% → 40% capitalisation in FY25. ~150bp persistent EBIT drag in Q3 FY25/26. Management 14% EBIT target requires the drag to be offset by operating leverage + Evo pricing — narrows the path. Consensus models partially pricing this; bears (Morgan Stanley) explicitly citing it; bulls (DNB) downplaying.

## Boundary Conditions

- Different from one-off impairments / write-downs (which DON'T persist)
- Different from operating-lease ASC 842 changes (which are balance sheet, not P&L)
- Pharma / biotech R&D usually 100% expensed already — no shift dynamic
- Software industry varies (US GAAP IFRS divergence creates capitalisation discretion)
