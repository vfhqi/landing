# Cockroach Pattern at M&A Integration Level

## Definition

The standard Cockroach Pattern (one bad operational signal usually means more) extends specifically to M&A integration failures: a single capital-destruction M&A bet (acquired-then-closed, write-down of acquired entity, integration failure) within a serial-acquirer business model has a **30-50% probability** of being followed by a SECOND such case within 12-18 months. Reason: M&A integration failures often share root causes (deal-sourcing discipline, integration playbook, cultural mismatch, post-acquisition incentive structure) — fixing one doesn't fix the others on the IN-FLIGHT pipeline.

## Why It Matters

Serial-acquirer business models (Hexatronic, Halma, Diploma, Spirax, Bunzl, Indutrade) depend on a high success rate (~85-90%) on M&A integration. A single visible failure isn't fatal IF the failure rate stays at the 10-15% expectation. But because M&A integration failures CLUSTER (share root causes), the conditional probability of a second failure given a first is materially higher than the unconditional rate.

The case-killer scenario in serial-acquirer cases is "Weterings + a second Weterings within 12-18 months" — this confirms the failure was systemic, not idiosyncratic, and forces a re-rating of the whole M&A engine.

## Application

When evaluating a serial-acquirer / M&A-driven EPSU case:

1. Identify any visible M&A integration failure in the prior 24 months (write-down, acquired-then-closed, leadership change at acquired entity)
2. If a Weterings-type cockroach is present: do a forensic walk of the LAST 5 acquisitions minimum
   - Check each for: integration milestone slippage, EBITDA dilution, leadership turnover at acquired entity, any visible cultural/operational friction
3. Assess whether the failure was idiosyncratic (specific deal characteristics) or systemic (deal-sourcing discipline, integration playbook, incentive structure)
4. Apply the conditional probability: presume 30-50% probability of a second cockroach within 12-18 months
5. Add the second-cockroach trigger as an explicit invalidation criterion (D.II.1 ACH#11 candidate)
6. Monitoring: the second cockroach typically surfaces 12-24 months post-deal (impairment review timing)

The cost of skipping this check is materially higher than the time spent. A serial-acquirer case rated B at C-conviction can be retroactively invalidated by a second cockroach showing 6-12 months later.

## Boundary Conditions

- Applies specifically to serial-acquirer business models (≥3 acquisitions/year average over a 5-year window)
- Single-acquisition cases or transformational-deal cases follow different patterns — single-deal failure doesn't predict a second
- A failure at 12+ months post-acquisition is strong cockroach signal; a failure at <12 months may be due-diligence noise (less informative)
- Sufficient time-since-CEO-arrival matters: a cockroach surfacing during new CEO's "kitchen-sink" cleanup is less informative than one surfacing 18+ months into stable leadership
