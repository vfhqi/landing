Key Themes of the Middle Innings Lists in the Week of the sixth of November 2026.

This is Watson with the Key Themes. Where the Overview said which stocks to investigate, this script reads the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection and the Lists for what they would do to the shape of the portfolio: its industries, countries, size and valuation, what is missing from them, and how this week's Portfolio Fitness for Fighting judgement shaped them.

The headline.

My overall reading is that the Lists describe a portfolio close to the mirror image of the one you hold. The book is small, Swedish and industrial, cheap on our valuation reading, and more than half of it sits on weak charts. The Ideas Bench is large, spread across nine countries, led by healthcare with an energy hedge attached, fairly priced rather than cheap, and mostly on grade-A charts. That contrast is not an accident: the target portfolio drew it. Fragility at Double Underweight lifted the large names, Momentum at Underweight stopped the chart-only names rising further, and the Overweight rows for pharma, energy, staples and integrated utilities put their best candidates on the Bench. What the shape lacks is cases: the memos park the energy, and the healthcare leaders are mostly unwritten. And the Lists are shut to three groups: financials, the Pool's largest; industrials, the book's largest; and consumer discretionary, where the target table itself says its tilt is most likely wrong.

The first theme: healthcare would become the book's centre of gravity, and the target table has deliberately stopped short of saying so.

The data supports this at every step. Healthcare is eight per cent of our universe, thirteen per cent of the Pool, a fifth of all eighty list places, and forty per cent of the Ideas Bench, eight of twenty stocks, nine if you count Sectra, which our files class as software. The live portfolio holds two healthcare names among sixteen. So the scoring concentrates healthcare threefold from the Pool to the Bench, and the Bench would take the book from a light healthcare weight to healthcare as its largest industry.

The Portfolio Fitness for Fighting judgement considered lifting the whole healthcare industry to Overweight this week and chose not to, for reasons that matter to how a position would be built. An industry letter would add the same weight to clinical-stage biotech and capital-cycle medical equipment as to large pharma; the sector rows already carry the view, with pharma at Overweight; eleven of the top forty on the raw score are already healthcare; and no source spoke to the industry this week. The tape is ahead of the letter in medtech and pharma manufacturing supplies, which read A and B against a Neutral row, and the judgement's own trigger for a lift is a healthcare read from a source while relative strength holds A or B.

For the shape, the Bench offers healthcare in three forms: large pharma in Genmab, Orion, Merck and Bavarian Nordic, hearing-care medtech in Demant and Sonova, and supplies in SCHOTT Pharma, with Bonesupport the small one. Three additions from that set are one bet on the rotation if they share a driver and three bets if they do not. What I will prepare is a view of which drivers the leading healthcare cases share, so that a first healthcare addition is sized knowing what a second and third would add. The sentence to carry: healthcare is where the Lists would put the book's weight, so the first question is whether three healthcare cases are one bet or three.

The second theme: energy is the gap the Lists fill most and the memos fill least.

Energy is six per cent of the universe, sixteen per cent of the Pool, the largest single group across all eighty list places at twenty-two per cent, and three of the twenty Bench stocks. The live portfolio holds none. The target holds energy downstream at Double Overweight and upstream and services at Overweight, and of its six Overweight rows the book holds only staples, about twelve points, with nothing in energy, insurance, pharma or integrated utilities. That is the first gap the live-portfolio judgement names, and the Lists answer it most heavily in energy.

Yet the memos refuse. Every memo-backed major is parked: a central-case return of about eleven per cent a year at TotalEnergies against the twenty per cent bar, a re-entry price below today's at Eni, a bearish tilt on entry at BP and a bearish verdict at Repsol. Energy's relative strength has slipped to B and energy services to D, the one oil headline this week had the price dipping on peace hopes, and no dated oil reading was taken. The cap I proposed on any one sector's share of the Bench and Shortlist was weakened this week because energy fell to eight of the top forty from fifteen as Neste and Galp left the Pool; healthcare is now the largest group there with eleven.

For the shape, the energy hedge cannot be reached through a stock case this week, so it is a size and timing decision on the environment. One large major closes energy and size at once; two closes them further and adds Norway or France, where the country rows are Overweight on an oil price not re-read and Double Underweight on a bond premium not re-read. The level that removes the reason for the Overweights is written down: Brent under eighty-five dollars. What I will do is get a dated Brent reading and the BP print into next week's pack, so the energy rows are tested rather than carried. The sentence to carry: the energy hedge is a portfolio decision the table can make and no memo will, and its price is a year of return if the memos are right.

The third theme: the Lists would take the book up in size and out of Sweden, before any rule has to do it.

The book's median holding is worth about one and a half billion euros, ten of the sixteen holdings sit under two billion, none is above fifty billion, and seventy-seven per cent of invested weight sits at or below the forty-first size percentile. The Bench's median is about seven billion euros, nearly five times the book's; only two of its nineteen measured stocks are under two billion and three are above fifty billion. The Pool's median is five billion, so the scoring lifted size again from Pool to Bench. Fragility at Double Underweight did the lifting: its modifier moves a score by up to seven and a half points, pulling small no-memo names such as Bonesupport and Bavarian Nordic down and large ones, Shell, Kerry and Merck, up.

Country tells the same story. The live portfolio sits in seven countries, with Sweden thirty per cent of invested weight in four names, three of them very small, and the Netherlands third by count. The Bench spans nine countries: six United Kingdom names, three Danish, three Swedish, two German, two Swiss, and one each from Poland, Finland, Ireland and France. No Dutch or Norwegian name made it. Denmark would enter the book through Demant, Genmab and Bavarian Nordic, and Switzerland, an Overweight country row, through Sonova and Clariant. The United Kingdom share would stay near thirty per cent, on a country row whose re-test against Underweight could not run because the Bank of England's decision was not read.

For the shape, the Sweden cap and the cap on leveraged, long-duration rows that I proposed were both strengthened this week because the week's additions to the book went the wrong way for them. The Bench would move the book toward those caps' intent by selection alone: the three Swedish Bench names, Sectra, Bonesupport and Sinch, are mid-sized or small, so the large additions come from elsewhere. Fourteen of the sixteen holdings sit outside the Pool today, which says how far the book's shape and the Pool's have drifted apart. The sentence to carry: the Bench is five times the book's median size and spread across nine countries, so adding from it eases the size and Sweden concentration before any cap is set.

The fourth theme: the Lists are fairly priced, not cheap, and the premium over the book is the price of the rotation.

Our valuation reading runs from A, the cheapest, to F. The live portfolio reads A on forty-four per cent of holdings, which is cheap. The Pool reads F on a third of its stocks and A on only eight per cent, the most expensive slice of the universe, because the Pool selects the regime's winners after the market has re-rated them. The Bench sits between: half of it reads B, a tenth reads A, and no Bench stock reads F. So the scoring screened the paid-for names out of the top twenty, and what remains is fair rather than cheap. On the target-row letter, three quarters of the Bench sits in rows the target holds at Overweight or better, against about half of the Pool and of the universe.

The risk in that shape is the one the tilt scorecard shows. Every one of the eight target tables now reads negative, meaning the Overweight rows have lagged the Underweight rows on every reading since September. Eight weeks is noise and no row moved on it, but the direction ran against the tilts for a second week. The sentence to carry: the Bench buys fair prices in favoured rows, and the premium over the book is what you pay to own the rotation rather than wait for it.

The fifth theme: what is absent, and why.

Three groups the book or the Pool holds heavily are missing from the Bench. Financials are the Pool's largest group at seventeen per cent, twenty-nine stocks, and take eleven list places, all banks and insurers with parked memos or none; none reaches the Bench, and banks are held at Neutral. The one financial row the target Overweights, insurance, is represented by Hiscox alone on the Shortlist, with two of its three engines softening. Industrials are the book's largest group at five of sixteen holdings, seven per cent of the Pool, and two list places: Bravida, held back by an industry relative strength that would meet a Sell Criterion if bought, and DiscoverIE, leaving the Pool. Consumer discretionary is thirteen per cent of the universe and eight per cent of the Pool, and takes one list place, because the target holds the industry at Double Underweight on a consumer read it could not re-test, against four of its sectors at A or B on the tape. The judgement names that row the likeliest reason the scorecard is negative, and the condition to lift it is written: a European consumer reading that is not falling, or a second industry reading at C or better with hotels and online retail holding A. Five industries are absent from the Pool altogether, telecoms, defence, infrastructure, media and real assets, so the telecoms downgrade this week touches no stock on the Lists.

For the shape, the Lists would rotate the book away from industrials without offering a financial or a consumer name in exchange, and would leave the insurance Overweight unfilled. The sentence to carry: the Lists cannot give you a bank, an industrial or a consumer name this week, and the last of those is by a judgement the table itself doubts.

The sixth theme: the Lists are a book of strong charts in a regime that is not yet rewarding strength.

A new rule I proposed this week would cap the share of invested weight in holdings graded D or F on Technical Momentum, because today that share is fifty-four per cent of the book and no Sell Criterion acts on the aggregate. At least nine of the twenty Bench stocks read A on Technical Momentum, so on paper the Bench repairs the book's chart mix. But Momentum is held at Underweight for a second week on its own half-failed condition: the share of stocks above the two-hundred-day average is sixty-one per cent, which passes, while the three-month defensives line is above six points when the condition wanted under two, which fails harder. The recovery is led by defensives, so a strong chart alone buys a memo, not capital. The sentence to carry: the Bench's charts would fix the book's weak-chart share, and the regime says not to pay for the chart alone yet.

The counter-case.

The strongest case against my reading is that the Lists buy a two-month-old rotation the market has already paid for, shaped by tilts that have lagged on every table since September, and shut to the one industry where the tape says cheap good businesses are accumulating. Healthcare's relative strength at A is a two-month reading; the Pool is a third F on valuation; energy's strength is slipping while its rows stay Overweight; and breadth recovering for two weeks with the index at its high is how broadening rallies start. If a dated source confirms the headlines of the Federal Reserve and the Bank of England cutting next year, the leveraged, long-duration rows the book already holds rally hardest and the defensive Overweights lag.

My reply has three parts. First, the Bench is not the expensive end of the rotation: it carries no F valuation readings and half B, and the parked energy memos are the system refusing to pay, so the Lists already discount the already-paid argument. Second, the healthcare cases that lead are product cycles with dated tests, Demant's statement, Kainos's results, Orion's re-based return, not bets on the tape; a pass is company evidence whatever the rotation does. Third, the book already holds the other side of the trade, a third of its weight in leveraged and capital-cycle rows the regime is selling, so taking from the Lists is a complement to what you own, not a doubling down. Where I would be wrong is if Brent under eighty-five dollars and a confirmed cutting path arrive together; then a quarter of the Lists move down at once and the book's present shape is the right one for a quarter. The conditions to move Momentum, consumer discretionary and the energy rows are all written, and the first dated readings will test them.

The recap.

The Lists would rebuild the book around the regime's defensives: healthcare as the largest industry, an energy hedge the table wants and the memos will not price, holdings five times the book's median size, nine countries instead of seven with Sweden diluted, fair prices in favoured rows, and strong charts in a regime not yet paying for them. What they cannot give you is a bank, an industrial or a consumer name, and the last absence rests on the tilt most likely to be wrong.

Two next actions. For you: settle how large an energy hedge would have to be to matter against a third of the book in the rows the regime is selling, so that when the Brent reading arrives it answers a question you have already framed, and listen for whether the healthcare cases you like share one driver or several. For me: build a what-if of the top ten Bench names on the book's industry, country, size and chart mix, and get the missing dated readings, Brent, rates and the Bank of England's decision, into next week's pack so the energy and consumer rows are tested rather than carried.

That is the end of the summary.
