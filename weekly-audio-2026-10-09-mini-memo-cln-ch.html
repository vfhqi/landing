APM Mini-Memo. Clariant. The ninth of October 2026.

Clariant enters at rank thirteen on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The view is neutral, with medium conviction. At ten point three three Swiss francs this is a fair holding rather than a crisp one, because the self-help and the half-cleared legal overhang have both been paid for already, and what is left to earn is decided by a German court and a Middle East refill cycle that management does not control.

First, what the company does. Clariant is a Swiss-listed pure-play specialty chemicals producer with three businesses. Sales were just under four billion Swiss francs in the twenty twenty-five financial year: Care Chemicals is the largest unit at a little over two billion, Catalysts about eight hundred million on a twenty percent margin in normal cycles, and Adsorbents and Additives about one billion. It serves more than fifteen thousand accounts with none above ten percent of sales, and has SABIC on the register at thirty-one and a half percent since twenty twenty.

Second, the thesis. It is self-help on margin and cash bolted onto a stalled top line, with the re-rating supplied from outside the company. The margin before exceptional items has risen from fourteen point six percent in twenty twenty-three to eighteen point two percent in the second quarter of this year, and cash conversion from thirty-two percent to fifty-two percent. The mechanism to earnings per share has three parts: a cost programme raised to one hundred million Swiss francs of run-rate savings on the thirty-first of July, with sixty-nine million already banked; Catalysts normalisation, worth thirty to forty million of profit once refills return; and Lucas Meyer Cosmetics, on margins above forty percent. Consensus earnings per share then recover about twenty-three percent a year for two years and six percent after that, all of it on revenue that has fallen every single year since twenty twenty-two. Four things to track: German court steps in Munich and Dortmund; Middle East catalyst utilisation, which you can read early through force majeure declarations and Hormuz shipping volumes; savings realisation; and the third-quarter print on the third of November.

Third, the Target Setup. The Target Setup is Major cause-for-concern Clearing in a medium-quality non-cyclical or better business on a bottom-quartile multiple, leading to reversion to normal, with Big Corporate Change-driven earnings per share step-up combining underneath it as the mechanism. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twenty-sixth of September rated Setups' Fit and Crispness C. Four of the five conditions passed on dated evidence: the Amsterdam District Court dismissed Shell's one billion euro claim on the twenty-ninth of July, consensus had been cut hard across all three forecast years, the top line had been rebased, and the price had touched a fifty-two week low in July. The condition that has lapsed is the price. At eleven point three times two-year-forward earnings the shares sit at their own five-year median, not at the bottom quartile of nine point one four times, so a setup that was crisp at seven to eight francs in June is a C today.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. This is the weakest link, and it is honestly set. The same memo of the twenty-sixth of September rated Case Riskiness D, where A means simple and low-risk. The asymmetry is the reason. A full German clearing is worth about one and a half to two francs a share on the sell-side's own numbers, while an adverse ruling is about six francs a share gross on Bernstein's estimate. Simplicity is no better: ten company-specific concerns are named, and two of the three key drivers cannot be tracked monthly from the company's own disclosure.

Fifth, business quality. Medium quality, well run, and still earning below its cost of capital. That same memo rated Foundations' Robustness C and the Thesis's Change Forces' Strength C, with operator quality a B inside the Foundations. Return on invested capital of six point nine percent sits below a seven point eight percent cost of capital, and the adjusted operating margin is about ten percent against the framework's twenty percent test. Against that, cash conversion is in the top five percent of the peer group. The script's Technical Momentum reading of the ninth of October is A, and its Sell-side Momentum reading of the ninth of October is B.

Sixth, returns and valuation. Two readings disagree, and the disagreement is the case. The script's TSR and Valuation reading of the ninth of October is B on a valuation pin at ten point three three francs, a valuation reading only, with no modal return in it. Its plausible return reading of the eighth of October averages twenty-six point one percent a year, and it gets there on the multiple moving from eleven point three times towards the peer median of fourteen times. The Investment Analyst's modal path, written at ten point six seven francs, holds the multiple near twelve times and gets about ten percent a year. The gap is the multiple, which is to say the German courts. At about nine point three five francs the modal path clears the twenty percent bar on its own, and that is where the memo of the first of October puts re-entry.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The script's Thematic Fit and Momentum reading of the ninth of October is B, with the exposures derived from tags. The pros are specific and slow: tightening rules on fluorinated chemicals pull demand into halogen-free additives, and record biofuel blending lifts adsorbents for biodiesel and aviation fuel. The cons are structural: European gas at three point three times the United States price on an unintegrated cost base, Chinese producers moving up into specialty additives, formula contracts that hand deflation straight back, and less than one percent of revenue in Swiss francs against ten percent of costs.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. It is the False Friend reading, and the Investment Analyst concedes every fact in it. The second-quarter margin was flattered by an eight million franc inventory revaluation, without which Care Chemicals was nineteen point nine percent, not twenty point seven. The three percent price rise sits beside a three point three percent volume fall, which is customer attrition as much as pricing power. And the legal relief is premature while six point four billion euros sits unprovisioned in German courts that are not bound by the Dutch ruling, with a fresh Dow Europe claim filed in July. On that reading the trough is five to six francs.

The one-line view. Neutral, with medium conviction: at ten point three three Swiss francs Clariant is a fair holding rather than a crisp one, because the self-help and the clearing so far are already paid for, and the rest is set by a German court and a Middle East refill cycle.
