APM Mini-Memo. TotalEnergies. The first of October 2026.

TotalEnergies is rank nine on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, promoted since last Friday. My view is neutral, held as a working hypothesis. This is the lowest-cost, least-indebted European oil major, with real volume growth and a well-covered dividend, but its earnings per share over the next eighteen months to three years ride on oil, gas and refining margins that are currently lifted by disruption, so the predictability you want is not there.

First, what the company does. TotalEnergies is a French multi-energy major with five segments: exploration and production, integrated liquefied natural gas, integrated power, refining and chemicals, and marketing and services. Revenue was one hundred and eighty-two billion dollars in twenty twenty-five, and production grew nearly four percent to two point five three million barrels of oil equivalent a day, across about fifty countries. Exploration and production is the cash engine, at fifteen point six billion dollars of operating cash flow last year. The shares closed at seventy-five point seven euros on the thirtieth of September, a market value of about one hundred and eighty-one billion euros.

Second, the thesis. The moat is integration plus cost. Upstream operating cost is below five dollars per barrel of oil equivalent, the proved reserve life is twelve point two years against seven point eight at Shell, and gearing fell to thirteen point one percent in the second quarter against a peer median of eighteen to twenty-two percent. The route to earnings per share runs through four channels. Upstream production is targeted to grow three percent a year to twenty thirty, from deepwater projects earning cash margins of twenty-five dollars a barrel. Liquefied natural gas volumes are targeted to grow fifty percent between twenty twenty-five and twenty thirty. Integrated power, after the five point one five billion euro E P H flexible generation deal closed on the twenty-ninth of April, moves toward free cash flow breakeven in twenty twenty-six or twenty twenty-seven. And a twelve and a half billion dollar savings plan, plus buybacks of one and a half billion dollars a quarter, widen margins and shrink the share count, partly offset by the ninety-five million new shares issued for that deal. The things to track are quarterly gas trading, the European refining margin, access through the Strait of Hormuz, Mozambique towards a first cargo in twenty twenty-nine, and the Venus decision in Namibia.

Third, the Target Setup. There is no Investment Analyst memo, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings, and this read is mine. The case matches none of the six crisply. The nearest is Demand-driven EPSU, where the demand is the company's own volume growth in deepwater oil, liquefied gas and power, rather than an end market pulling harder. It is not a cause-for-concern clearing. The shares sit under six percent below their fifty-two week high, and the European refining margin is near a cyclical high, not a trough.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. There is no Case Riskiness rating. The list-maker calls this the largest and simplest energy major in the pool. The business is simple to understand; the outcome is not. Middle East blockages disrupted fifteen percent of group production in the second quarter, easing to eight to ten percent in July, after the Strait of Hormuz was blocked again on the ninth of July. Production exposed to Hormuz rises from about a quarter to about a third of the total by twenty thirty, the highest among peers, precisely because the growth projects deliver. Add a June court ruling in Paris on customer emissions, security in Mozambique, and a combined chairman and chief executive role under Patrick Pouyanné.

Fifth, business quality. High, for an energy company. Return on average capital employed reached thirteen point nine percent for the twelve months to June, up from twelve point four, return on equity was fifteen point nine percent, and proved reserve replacement was one hundred and twenty percent last year. The weak spot is earnings quality. Adjusted net income exceeded statutory net income by nearly two and a half billion dollars in twenty twenty-five, the company rarely hedges, and the liquefied gas segment missed consensus by ten percent in the second quarter on gas trading.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The shares trade on eight point nine times earnings on FactSet's figure of the thirtieth of September, the fifty-third percentile on its measure. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of that date is D, a valuation reading only, with no Modal return behind it. The consensus median target in July was eighty point three two euros, about six percent above the price. Morgan Stanley moved to Equal-weight in September with a seventy-eight euro target. On my arithmetic, the ninety cent quarterly dividend yields about four point eight percent and buybacks add roughly three, so with modest volume growth a plausible return sits near twelve percent a year, well short of your twenty-five. Anything more needs commodity prices to stay high.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. On the plus side, upstream is the highest-margin stage of the energy chain, and liquefaction is a margin-earning bottleneck. Shrinking conventional discoveries reward a low-cost holder of long-life reserves. The company supplies round-the-clock power to data-centre customers, and the list-maker sees it as a yield-and-inflation beneficiary. On the minus side, seventy percent of its refining capacity is in Europe under carbon regulation, European power oversupply has pushed assumed margins down to thirty-two euros a megawatt-hour, and low-carbon spending fell forty percent between twenty twenty-three and twenty twenty-five. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading is C, on the environment only and provisional.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. The balance sheet improvement is a false friend, and the earnings are borrowed from disruption. The research's pre-mortem left the false-friend profile with no hard inconsistencies: the three point three billion dollar fall in net debt leaned on a one point two billion working capital release and on divestments. Refining margins of thirteen dollars fifty a barrel against four dollars seventy a year earlier, and diesel margins above one hundred dollars in late August, are disruption prices. If Hormuz reopens for good and oil falls below fifty dollars, the research models a thirty percent cut to buybacks and sharp downgrades. Estimates are already slipping. Redburn cut this year's earnings per share by four percent, below consensus, and the script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the twenty-ninth of September is C. And a French election that unsettles the French government bond market would hit the shares, however global the cash flows.

The one-line view. Neutral, as a working hypothesis. TotalEnergies has an excellent cost position, a strong balance sheet and a well-covered dividend, but its earnings per share depend on commodity prices lifted by disruption, so the plausible return looks nearer twelve percent a year than twenty-five, and the predictability you want is missing.
