The week in brief

The regime is risk-off beneath an index at its high, and this week Europe's own prices showed it.

  • Richard's European risk-appetite test fired for the first time. The STOXX Europe 600 closed 0.9% from its 52-week high while the share of stocks above the 100, 150 and 200-day averages sits at or below the tenth percentile of all near-high days since 2006. It is the strongest European price evidence yet for the "cracks emerging" line in Richard's view, and the direction of the regime is held with high conviction.
  • Defensives lead cyclicals on all five windows for the first time, so the second week of breadth recovery is read as defensive-led, not as a turn. Breadth met the Momentum row's 200-day leg (61%) while the three-month defensives line moved the other way (+6.6 points from +3.0); Momentum stays Underweight for a third test.
  • The causes were not read, so the 30-Oct-26 read of inflation, rates, yields and credit is held one week stale. The strategist and newsletter files for 30 October to 5 November did not reach the source pack, so conviction on the causes is lower than last week while conviction on the direction stays high. Undated headlines of the Fed and Bank of England cutting in 2026 run against the standing read and are the first thing to test when a dated source arrives.
  • Two rows move, both on the commissioned telecoms topic: C. Telecoms and C.1. Telecoms - MNOs go from Neutral to Underweight, with moderate conviction. The group's relative-strength uptrend is broken on every pre-set measure while 2027 estimates held, the Defence pattern of last week. No telecom stock is in the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection (the MI Pool), so the Lists do not change.
  • The tilt scorecard reads negative on all eight target tables for the first time. Eight weeks is noise and no row moves on it, but the tilts have not demonstrated value and the week ran against them for a second time, most plausibly through Consumer discretionary and Energy services. If the September tables are still negative at the first twelve-week reading in December, the question becomes the tilt structure, for Richard's monthly cross-check.
  • Three held rows to watch: Electrical equipment, grid and automation (relative strength F, the row most likely to move down next); Consumer discretionary (Double Underweight against four sectors at A or B on relative strength, the loudest disagreement in the table); and Momentum (third test).
  • No stock override this week. The four Top Down ratings read correctly for every MI Pool stock checked.

Special topics commissioned by Richard

Telecoms relative strength: pullback or structurally impaired?

Judgement: the telecoms relative-strength uptrend is broken, not pausing, and C. Telecoms and C.1. Telecoms - MNOs move from Neutral to Underweight, one notch, with moderate conviction. Richard's earlier view, a pullback in a longer uptrend, which he already judged less likely, fails on every pre-set measure in the Sector Memo (all 32 stocks, the median stock, breadth, and the group without its two satellite operators); only the absence of a lower low (October above the December 2025 low by a 3% margin) keeps the group short of a confirmed downtrend. Whether the group is structurally impaired is not decided and cannot be decided by 2027 estimates, which held. For a European long-only book the pattern matters more than that distinction: a leveraged defensive de-rating while estimates hold is the Defence pattern of last week, and the regime is selling it.

Why, ranked by evidence:

  1. A de-rating of a leveraged defensive with the discount rate above 5%, best supported. The median two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple fell 12% from end-May to end-September while 2027 estimates held, and the memo ties the group to the US ten-year. Against: no yield level was read this week, so the discount-rate leg is a week stale.
  2. A rotation inside defensives towards healthcare and staples and away from telecoms, well supported by the relative-strength table. Over the same two-month base Healthcare went to A from C and Consumer staples to C from D while Telecoms went to F from D: the market is choosing its defensives, and not the leveraged one. Against: relative strength describes, it does not explain.
  3. Structural impairment (satellites; AI agents, Barclays, 20-Oct-26), least supported now. The memo calls it long-term and untestable by 2027 estimates, and nothing in the pack tests it.
  4. Richard's hypothesis, tested: rejected in its original form and supported in his revision. The pullback-in-an-uptrend read fails on all 32 measures; his 2-Nov-26 revision ("that view seems less likely now") is what the memo finds.

The counter-case is a Neutral base rate and a yield leg that cuts both ways. Of 113 past group cases in today's position, 55% were back above the 200-day average within 26 weeks and 25% made a new relative high, with a median relative return of +2%: a Neutral base rate, not an Underweight one. A yield defensive rallies first if the ten-year falls under 4.5%, and the November results (Vodafone 10-Nov, Telefonica 12-Nov) may show stable churn and pricing. What would refute the view: Industry relative strength back above 50 by end-January (the memo's odds, one in four), a new relative high by mid-February (one in five), or the ten-year under 4.5%. For the counter to win, rates have to fall while estimates hold; this week neither was read.

What it means for how we invest:

  • Tilt letters: C. Telecoms and C.1. Telecoms - MNOs from Neutral to Underweight; D.3. Infrastructure - telecom towers held at Underweight, the row that shares the yield leg.
  • Live holdings: none in telecoms. Helios Towers (7.21%, the largest position) sits in Towers, already Underweight, and the ten-year trigger named here is the one written on its row.
  • MI Pool and Lists: no telecom stock is in the MI Pool (its sector relative-strength floor keeps the group out while the group's Composite is below 50), so the letters change nothing on the Lists this week; Hellenic Telecom, the case the memo was written beside, is neither in the MI Pool nor in the book.
  • Conflict with the regime: none. This is the regime (multiple compression with estimates intact, leverage and duration sold) reaching a defensive, as it reached Defence last week.

What could not be seen: this week's yield level; the full memo (only its short record reached the pack); the Industry and Sector Share Price Drivers Report's rating for telecoms, a quarter of the row's weight in Thematic Fit/Momentum; the November results; the final European Union merger guidelines; fund flows and positioning in the group.

Next actions: (a) the Investment Analyst records Vodafone's (10-Nov-26) and Telefonica's (12-Nov-26) churn and pricing against the memo's stable-churn test by the next weekly run; (b) the Assistant Portfolio Manager re-tests the two telecoms rows at the next weekly run against Industry relative strength at the 30 and 50 marks and the ten-year, and proposes as one line, not a decision, that the MI Pool's sector relative-strength floor be noted in the Lists note as the reason no telecom can reach the Lists while this question is open.
