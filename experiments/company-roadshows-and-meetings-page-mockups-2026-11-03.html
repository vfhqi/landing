My proposal for the "How To", which you also left to me. One short tab so the page explains itself in six months.

The loop

  • Observe. A free script reads the earnings calendar, your holdings, the cohorts and your mailbox every 30 minutes. No tokens
  • Orient. It sets each company's state, whether the window is open, whether a reply or a chaser is owed, and how warm the email should be
  • Decide. It derives the ask from your rules: market capitalisation, ownership, and what is realistically on offer. Nothing waits for you to approve a list
  • Act. Superhuman writes each draft from a short instruction. The drafts appear in the Company Roadshows and Meetings tab of your daily Executive Assistant page
  • You. Review and send. That is the only step that is yours
  • Then. Replies, slots and bookings run through your existing daily email run and calendar rules

Where each thing reaches you

  • This page: status only. It never asks you for anything
  • Your daily Executive Assistant page: every draft, question and choice, in its own tab
  • The requests page: a new box for this workstream
  • The Daily Digest: only when something has broken

What the states mean

  • Nothing due: results more than 4 weeks away
  • Window open: from 4 weeks before results to 3 weeks before, your ruling of 3 November. Every "window opens" date on this page is the results date less 4 weeks
  • Contact needed: no named investor relations contact yet, so the draft waits
  • Drafted: written and ready for your review
  • Sent: out. The chaser clock runs from this date, never from the date it was drafted
  • Chased 1, 2, 3 of 3: then it stops and shows as no reply
  • Reply in, booked, held: the good path
  • Window missed: results passed with no approach, so the ask changes from a roadshow place to a standalone call

The guideline this is sized against, in your words

"6-10 a week in 'earnings season' is acceptable/normal. And 1/4 to 1/3 that amount normally. These are v. rough guidelines, not rules." The page shows the week's count against that as information, never as a limit it enforces.
