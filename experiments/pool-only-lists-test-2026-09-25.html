3. How Fitness for Fighting behaves inside the Pool

Plain English first. Each candidate's score blends two halves: the bottom-up half from its own six headline ratings, and the Fitness for Fighting half from how well it fits your top-down view of the next twelve months. Last Friday the blend was 62.5% Fitness for Fighting. This month's view marks Energy, Banks and Insurance as overweights, which inside the Pool means oil, gas, refiners and banks score well on the top-down half even where little is known about them bottom-up (most have no memo).

What that did. At 62.5%, the top 20 by score includes Equinor, Orlen, Repsol, ING Bank Slaski, OMV, Var Energi, Anglo American, Subsea 7, HELLENiQ Energy, ArcelorMittal, MOL and SSAB. What the judgement call did about it. It leaned against the arithmetic on the Bench: 12 of the 20 Bench names are in the top 20 at the 25% weight, against 10 in the top 20 at 62.5%. It pulled Equinor down 34 places (score rank 5 to Shortlist 19) and OEM International up 42 places (score rank 60 to Bench 18) on its Early Stage 2 setup and a fresh memo. But the Shortlist stayed energy-heavy. The call's own notes say that energy names it listed, such as Neste (Shortlist) and Galp, Orlen, Repsol and HELLENiQ Energy (Bench), have no memo and three of their six ratings never read, so their rank rests largely on the top-down half.

Why this matters for later, not now. The Pool rests on a backtest. The Fitness for Fighting sector view rests on judgement with no backtest behind it yet. In this test they pull in different directions, and the 62.5% share decides much of the Shortlist. That is a question for APM List Compilation.

The three lists

The rating columns. Stage is the technical bucket: Proven Stage 2, Early Stage 2, or Stage 1 transitioning. P1 to P6 are the six headline ratings — technical momentum, market paradigm fit, fundamental change, fundamental foundations, sell-side estimate momentum, valuation. Fit is the ‘Per-stock Fitness-for-Fighting Rating’, 1 to 100: how well the stock suits the target shape of the book this week. Merit is the bottom-up score, the case on its own. Conf is how much of that case is actually visible, 0 to 1. Appr counts ratings read off a research digest rather than a memo. A dot means the rating does not exist, which is an absence, never a low grade. THIN marks a stock scoring well on fitness with no memo behind it: the number rests on the environment, not on anything read about the business.
