The week in brief

The regime is still risk-off beneath an index at its 52-week high, but the risk-off signal weakened at the margin this week for the first time since Richard's European test fired.

  • The price evidence moved against the bearish read without ending it. Breadth rose for a third week and cyclicals led defensives over the last week, so conviction on the regime's direction falls from high to moderate. No row is tightened this week.
  • The direction of rates is now "not established", no longer "rising". Two weeks of undated headlines say the Federal Reserve is easing, the opposite of the last dated read (30-Oct-26). The headlines move no row, but the rate-sensitive Underweights stop growing, and a dated source on the Federal Reserve is the first thing to look for.
  • Energy, commodities and metals mining moves from Overweight to Neutral at the industry level. The energy view now sits only in three sector rows, so energy names on the Lists are best judged sector by sector and not as a block.
  • Electrical equipment, grid and automation moves from Neutral to Underweight. The change touches one live holding, Arteche, and no stock on the Lists.
  • Consumer staples stays Overweight although its written condition for Double Overweight was met. The Assistant Portfolio Manager declined the condition because the defensive rotation narrowed this week; Richard may reasonably overrule that decision.
  • Consumer discretionary stays Double Underweight, now with its first European consumer evidence in six weeks behind it. German and euro-area confidence are low and edging lower.
  • Fragility (Double Underweight) and Momentum (Underweight) are the two rows most likely to move next. Each lifts one notch if 200-day breadth reaches 65% with the index within 1% of its high (63% today) and Momentum also lifts if the three-month defensive lead falls under +2 points (+6.3 today).
  • The causes of the regime went unread by any reasoned source for a second week, so conviction on the causes is low. The rates, credit and inflation read of 30-Oct-26 is held only because nothing reasoned replaced it.
  • The per-stock review made no override.

Geopolitics: the energy hedge is being unwound in the oil price while the reason for the hedge, Iran supply risk, remains.

  • The Middle East and Iran are the live tension in the dated items: Asian markets struggled on geopolitical tensions (wire report, 6 November), hopes of a Middle East de-escalation lifted bonds (LBBW, 6 November), and oil stayed below $100 as traders weighed Iran (Dow Jones Newswires, 5 November).
  • Ukraine and China were not read. The items on a truce between the United States and China carry 2025 links and are not this week's evidence.

The macro prices: oil is lower, and the direction of rates is no longer established

Rates: the Assistant Portfolio Manager now holds the direction of policy rates as not established, and for that reason tightens no row this week.

  • The shared headline framing is that the Federal Reserve is easing while the European Central Bank holds; the Bank of England is on hold in one outlet and cutting in 2026 in another (MarketScreener, Reuters, Irish Examiner, all undated). Macrohive asks whether the inflation print supports a December cut by the Federal Reserve.
  • The last dated, reasoned read (30-Oct-26) was a hawkish Federal Reserve hold with two dissents in favour of a hike. The headlines and the dated read cannot both be right, and no source reconciles them.
  • Two weeks of the same undated framing from several outlets is not evidence. It is enough to stop adding to rate-sensitive Underweights on a read that can no longer be dated.

Bond yields are high and were rising into the inflation data, which agrees with Richard's line and keeps the telecoms trigger (a United States ten-year yield under 4.5%) unmet. United States and eurozone yields rose on 9 November (Dow Jones Newswires) after a rally on 6 November (LBBW), and United States Treasury yields are "near 5%" (Investing Live, undated).

Oil was read for the first time in two weeks and is lower, which is the one commodity fact that moves a row.

  • Oil was below $100 on 5 November (Dow Jones Newswires), against Brent near $115 at the read of 30-Oct-26, with Iran supply risk the named skew.
  • With the Energy industry's relative strength at C, the lower oil reading accompanies the industry row's move to Neutral (see The optimal portfolio).
  • Copper is disputed: one headline reports a fresh record on supply-squeeze fears (MarketScreener, undated) and another reports "commodities on the back foot" on subdued risk appetite and a firmer dollar (Newsquawk, undated). Gold was not read.

Credit spreads were not read for a third week, so the Fragility row rests on European credit evidence of 23 October and now carries a staleness limit. No source gave a level or a view.

The tilts that matter this week

Energy, commodities and metals mining goes from Overweight to Neutral at the industry level, with moderate conviction.

  • Why: the row's condition was "to Neutral if Brent is read under $85 or the industry's relative strength falls to C", and relative strength is C. Oil moved the same way, on hopes of a Middle East de-escalation.
  • The industry letter was also lending weight to Metals and mining (relative strength F), Agriculture (D) and Energy services (D), which the market is selling.
  • Held: Downstream distribution at Double Overweight, Upstream and Energy services at Overweight, and Norway at Overweight. The condition on each of these rows (Brent under $85) is not met.
  • Counter-case: Iran supply risk is the one skew a source named, and Upstream and Downstream both read A on relative strength, so the hedge may be given up a week early. The industry row returns to Overweight if its relative strength returns to B with oil holding.
  • On the Lists: the MI Pool's 18 upstream and downstream names lose the industry's share of Thematic Fit/Momentum (30% of the tilt part) and keep the sector's. Five energy names are in the top 40 of the starting score, from eight a week ago, and the re-run will thin them further.

Electrical equipment, grid and automation goes from Neutral to Underweight, with moderate conviction.

  • Why: the condition was "to Underweight on a second F reading or a hyperscaler guide cut read in the sources". Relative strength read F last week and F this week, and no source made the positive case for AI capital spending.
  • Counter-case: the grid is the physical bottleneck of the largest capital-spending theme, and Arteche's Sell-side Momentum reads A. The demand read that held the row at Neutral (28 October) was never contradicted, only left unread. The row returns to Neutral if relative strength leaves F with a dated capital-spending read intact.
  • On the Lists: no stock in the Electrical equipment sector is in the MI Pool this week. The one live holding touched is Arteche.

Consumer staples stays Overweight: the Assistant Portfolio Manager declined its written condition for Double Overweight, and Richard may reasonably overrule that.

  • The condition ("to Double Overweight if relative strength reaches B with defensives still leading at three months") is met on its letter: relative strength is B and the three-month line is +6.3 points.
  • Why it was declined: the condition was written to confirm a strengthening rotation. This week the defensive lead narrowed on all five windows, breadth broadened with the index at its high, and the rate read that makes staples the destination is two weeks stale with the headlines running against it. Relative strength reaching B describes a move already made.
  • Counter-case: last week the Assistant Portfolio Manager refused to lift Consumer discretionary on the tape alone and called that a moved goalpost; declining a met condition is the same fault in the other direction.
  • What would change it: the row goes to Double Overweight if the industry holds B next week with defensives leading again at one week, four weeks and three months.
  • On the Lists: nothing changes. Staples names keep an A or B on Thematic Fit/Momentum through their sector rows.

Fragility stays Double Underweight and Momentum stays Underweight, each now with a price condition that would lift it.

  • Fragility rests on European credit evidence that no source has re-read for three weeks, while breadth has broadened for three weeks.
  • Momentum's condition is half met for a third week: 200-day breadth has held above 55%, but the three-month defensives line has not fallen under +2 points.
  • The conditions that lift each row are in the table under What would change the view.
  • Counter-case to holding: for the MI Pool stocks without case ratings, Fragility is size alone, so the Fragility row is a large-cap screen held on stale evidence while smaller stocks recover.
