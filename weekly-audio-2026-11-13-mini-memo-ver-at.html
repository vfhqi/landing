APM Mini-Memo. VERBUND. The thirteenth of November 2026.

VERBUND is new this week at rank three on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The one-line view. VERBUND is an exceptional hydropower fleet priced low in its own valuation range after the driest year in a century, and the story depends on Austria's rivers returning to normal flow.

First, what the company does.

VERBUND is Austria's dominant electricity producer. VERBUND also owns the national high-voltage grid. The heart of the company is a fleet of one hundred and thirty-six hydropower plants on Austrian and German rivers. That fleet earned about three quarters of operating profit before depreciation in 2025, roughly two billion euros out of two point seven billion. The grid earned about four hundred and forty million euros. Wind, solar and retail supply earned the rest. The Republic of Austria owns fifty-one percent, and less than a fifth of the shares trade freely.

Second, the thesis.

The story is a return to normal water. River flow ran at sixty-three percent of its long-run average in the year to late August 2026. That was the lowest level in a hundred years of records. First-half operating profit before depreciation fell by a quarter. Droughts have historically reverted to the average. VERBUND's profit follows the water quickly, because the plants burn no fuel.

Volume reaches earnings per share first. Each one percent of hydropower output moves the group result by nearly ten million euros. On my own arithmetic, a shortfall of thirty-seven percent is worth roughly three hundred and fifty million euros. Compare that with a guided 2026 group result of one billion to one billion one hundred and fifty million euros.

Price comes second. VERBUND sold most of its 2026 hydropower output forward at eighty-seven euros per megawatt-hour. The 2027 output sold so far fetched about ninety-six to ninety-seven euros. One more help arrives in December 2026, when the Limberg Three pumped-storage plant returns in full. That plant restores forty to sixty million euros a year.

Four drivers tell you how the story is going. The first is the monthly river-flow reading. The second is the monthly cost of buying power to honour forward sales. The third is the price of 2027 forward sales. The fourth is the Austrian budget's treatment of the profit levy. Third-quarter results are not in our files. Our research runs from July to September 2026.

Third, the Target Setup.

No Investment Analyst memo exists for VERBUND. There are no Bottom-up or Stock-driven ratings, and the Target Setup read is my own. The Target Setup is CfC in quality cyclical at trough-on-trough, leading to reversion to normal. The drought is the cause for concern. The hydropower fleet is the quality. Drought-year earnings sit on a multiple near the bottom of its ten-year range.

The earnings trough is the unproven half of that read. Consensus has earnings falling about two percent a year. The reason is price. Forward-sale prices dropped from about one hundred and seventeen euros per megawatt-hour to eighty-six euros in a year. An Investment Analyst memo should establish whether 2026 is the low point for earnings.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

The case is simple. The case turns on one variable, and that variable is weather rather than management execution. Full-year guidance assumes normal river flow for the rest of 2026. VERBUND beat its initial guidance every year from 2013 to 2024, before the drought broke the run in 2025.

The main risk is an uneven pay-off. A water shortfall has no floor. The gain from high power prices has a ceiling. Austria's levy takes ninety-five percent of generation revenue above ninety euros per megawatt-hour, and the levy runs until March 2030. Investment deductions lift that ceiling to between one hundred and five and one hundred and ten euros. Forward sales also turn a water shortfall into a cash cost. VERBUND spent nearly two hundred million euros buying power in the first half of 2026. Dry soil absorbs rain, so river flow recovers later than rainfall.

Two things need to go right. River flow must hold near normal through the winter. The levy thresholds must stay where they are. The warning signs are power purchases above fifty million euros a month, and any cut to the ninety euro cap. Deutsche Bank's dry-weather case puts earnings about thirty percent below consensus.

Fifth, business quality.

Quality is high at the asset level. Hydropower costs VERBUND about twenty euros per megawatt-hour in full, and five euros at the margin. That cost produced a hydropower margin of seventy-five percent in 2025, more than three times the peer average. Water concessions run for ninety years, so no competitor can build a rival fleet.

The grid adds slow, regulated growth. The grid's asset base is projected to grow about fourteen percent a year, from three billion euros to nine billion by 2033. Debt was modest at the end of 2025, at one times operating profit before depreciation.

Capital allocation is the weaker point. VERBUND wrote off nearly half a billion euros on Spanish renewables in 2022 and 2023. More Spanish solar is being built without price contracts.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

The valuation is low, and the return rests on the multiple. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of the thirteenth of November is A. That rating is a valuation reading only. The shares trade on seventeen times earnings, at a price of about seventy-four euros. The weekly routine's plausible return averages nineteen percent a year, which is a B against your bar. The eighteen-month figure is twenty-five percent a year, and the three-year figure is thirteen percent.

That return assumes the multiple rises from seventeen to twenty-three times while consensus earnings fall. Rising earnings would therefore be the upgrade to the case.

Two checks belong in a memo. Morgan Stanley's 2026 earnings estimate would put the shares nearer twenty-four times, so the earnings base needs reconciling. And in September most brokers were underweight, with price targets near fifty-five euros. The share price has run ahead of the analysts.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

On the positive side, VERBUND owns the scarce parts of the chain. Generation earns about eighty percent of group profit. Flexible storage plants earned about two hundred and seventy million euros balancing the grid in 2025. The regulator raised the allowed return on new grid assets for 2026. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of the thirteenth of November is A, with the shares two percent below their twelve-month high. The weekly Sell-side Momentum and Thematic Fit/Momentum ratings are both B. The thematic rating rests on the sector environment alone. The book holds no utility, and VERBUND is large enough to fill that gap.

On the negative side, the government sits in the chain. Our Sector Digest says to pick the regulator and the government before the power price. The same digest judged the drought a smaller theme, because normal rain lifts earnings once. Batteries are the slower threat, because more batteries narrow the price spreads that storage plants earn. The weekly Lists judgement also recorded a doubt. Three months of rotation into utilities may already have paid for the asset.

So, in short. VERBUND is interesting because an exceptional asset is priced low. One variable drives the case, and that variable is river flow. The plausible return needs earnings to rise, not only the multiple. The signals to track each month are river flow, power purchases and the levy cap. A memo is the next step, to confirm the earnings trough and the earnings base.

The one-line view. VERBUND is an exceptional hydropower fleet priced low in its own valuation range after the driest year in a century, and the story depends on Austria's rivers returning to normal flow.
