APM Mini-Memo. Indra. The twenty-third of October 2026.

Indra sits at rank fourteen on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

My view is neutral, and the conviction is a working hypothesis only. Indra has the best-evidenced demand tailwind in its peer set and an unusually complete corporate change, but the case rests on whether a company with a poor cash history can turn a doubled backlog into cash, at a multiple already at the ninety-seventh percentile of its own history.

First, what the company does.

On fiscal twenty twenty-five sales of five thousand four hundred and fifty-seven million euros, up thirteen per cent, the Minsait information technology services arm was still the largest piece at fifty-seven per cent. Defence was twenty-six per cent, Air Traffic Management ten per cent and Mobility seven per cent. It is an information technology services company whose defence and space arms are growing fast enough to redefine the whole. It is prime contractor on seventeen of Spain's thirty-one Special Modernisation Programmes, and over ninety per cent of the world's daily flights use air traffic solutions the group designed, software replaced about once a decade.

Second, the thesis.

Demand is the reliable part. Spain's Special Modernisation Programmes total thirty-four billion euros running to twenty thirty-seven, and Spanish equipment procurement is projected to double to twenty-one billion euros by twenty thirty. Indra's backlog doubled to twenty and a half billion euros in the first half of this year, and the defence backlog alone rose a hundred and fifty-nine per cent to eleven point seven billion. The mechanism to earnings per share is operating leverage on that volume. In the second quarter revenue rose forty-three and a half per cent and operating profit seventy-three per cent, and Morgan Stanley's operating profit estimate for this year, at eight hundred and four million euros, sits well above consensus of seven hundred and six million. The drivers to track are the defence margin against the seventeen per cent first-half reading, underlying free cash flow excluding prepayments, Minsait's book-to-bill against one times, and inventories against the June level.

Third, the Target Setup.

The Target Setup is Demand-driven EPSU combined with Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. The Investment Analyst's memo of the seventeenth of August graded both the big demand change-driven upgrade and the strong corporate change-driven upgrade at A. All nine corporate change deltas fired inside twelve months, from raised guidance and net cash to disposals, step-ups and a new chief executive, Josep Maria Recasens, from the seventeenth of June. The same memo set Setups' Fit and Crispness at B, mapped from the memo and not re-judged. It was held off A because the false friend guardrail grades D: reported first-half free cash flow of one thousand four hundred and eighty-seven million euros against an underlying fifteen million is the signature of growth without quality.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

This is a complex and risky case. The Investment Analyst's memo of the seventeenth of August rated Case Riskiness D, on a scale where A is simple and low-risk, and the simplicity guardrails also at D: eleven live change-from-current items against a guardrail of two, and six ranked drivers whose joint success the source itself puts at twenty-four per cent. Overrun provisions once reached thirteen per cent of sales against a one per cent peer average, free cash flow averaged one point seven per cent of sales over six years, and the company has had four presidents and four chief executives in five years.

Fifth, business quality.

Mixed, with one genuinely high-quality piece. Foundations' Robustness was rated B by the Investment Analyst on the seventeenth of August, mapped and not re-judged. Air traffic management is the most durable asset: ten-year replacement cycles, contracts of ten to fifteen years, low double-digit growth and a first-half divisional margin of twelve point six per cent. Minsait, fifty-seven per cent of sales, is contracting, with a divisional book-to-bill of zero point nine one times.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

This is the weakest leg. At fifty-nine euros ninety-two on the twenty-third of October, the price-to-earnings ratio of sixteen times sits at the ninety-seventh percentile of its own history. The script's TSR and Valuation reading of the twenty-third of October is F on the valuation pin. The plausible return is zero point seven per cent a year over eighteen months and nine point seven per cent a year over three years, averaging five point two per cent, as the multiple moves from sixteen times to a Phase-based fair twelve point two times while consensus earnings grow nineteen per cent a year. The bull answer is relative: roughly fifteen times this year's earnings is a forty-eight per cent discount to European defence peers at thirty-one times, which the research reads as three stacked discounts, for hybrid mix, governance and cash conversion, each closing on a different trigger.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

The script's Thematic Fit and Momentum reading of the twenty-third of October is B, environment only. Sell-side Momentum reads A as at the twenty-second and Technical Momentum B as at the twenty-third. The Investment Analyst's memo rated value-chain dynamics B on the political and regulatory leg: contracts restricted to cleared domestic integrators, escalation clauses in military procurement, and the active Iran conflict and oil thematic naming European defence contractors as beneficiaries. Against that, a specialised-labour constraint on the supply side, generative artificial intelligence pressing outsourcing prices faster than digital growth can offset, and the France-Germany halt of FCAS trimming long-term optionality.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case.

The Risks report's competing-hypotheses matrix names the false friend profile the winner, with zero hard inconsistencies against six for the bull profile. The backlog is being misread as guaranteed growth while physical capacity at Gijón, León and As Pontes is the binding constraint. Inventories rose by more than six hundred million euros in a year to one thousand nine hundred and twenty-six million, and defence margins fell to seventeen per cent from eighteen point nine. Underlying first-half free cash flow of fifteen million euros is zero point two per cent of revenue, against a defence peer median of four to six per cent during production ramps. If the company does not disclose production run-rates, the decisive question cannot be answered from outside. The third-quarter print is the evidence that matters: two consecutive quarters of underlying cash conversion with Minsait's book-to-bill back above one would turn the view bullish, and inventories rising past the June level with defence margins below fifteen per cent would turn it bearish.

The one-line view.

Neutral, working hypothesis: Indra has the best-evidenced demand tailwind in its peer set and an unusually complete corporate change, but the case rests on whether a company with a poor cash history can turn a doubled backlog into cash, at a multiple already at the ninety-seventh percentile of its own history.
