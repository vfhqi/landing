**European HVDC Cable Cluster: Order Quality, Execution Risk, and Competitive Durability (2024-Present)**

## Key Takeaways (from AS report)

- **Order books provide unprecedented visibility:** Prysmian holds **EUR 16.8bn in Transmission backlog** (EUR 4.0bn HVDC-specific), Nexans **EUR 7.9bn**, NKT **EUR 10.4bn**. All three effectively sold out through 2028.

- **Customer concentration heavily skewed to low-risk TSOs:** **90% of Prysmian's**, **85% of Nexans'**, and **>85% of NKT's** order books consist of sovereign or quasi-sovereign Transmission System Operators. Insulates revenue from speculative developer risk.

- **NKT exhibits elevated execution risk:** Karlskrona expansion experienced severe cost overrun on the EUR 1.0bn original budget program (extending into 2027); also constructing new NKT Eleonora cable-laying vessel.

- **AI-related demand for HVDC = small share but growing.** Prysmian + Relativity Networks partnership for hollow-core optical fiber for hyperscalers; Nexans 4Q25 PWR-Connect organic growth +10.9% partly driven by datacenter projects (medium/low voltage, NOT HVDC). HVDC remains overwhelmingly TSO/grid + offshore wind.

## Section 1 — Order Book Quality and Tenor

### Prysmian (PRY.MI)
- **Transmission backlog: EUR 16.8bn (Q1 2026)**, of which **EUR 4.0bn HVDC-specific.**
- Submarine Power backlog: EUR 12.6bn — heavily weighted toward strategic interconnectors (NeuConnect Germany–UK, Tyrrhenian Link Italy, Eastern Green Link EGL1/EGL2/EGL4 Scotland-England).
- **Customer concentration:** 90% TSO; 86% of Transmission backlog EMEA. Backlog avoids speculative financial developers; secured by down payments and firm notices to proceed.
- Major project awards: **Amprion Bundle (Feb 2024)** record-breaking EUR 5.0bn (BalWin1, BalWin2, DC34); **Eastern Green Link 4 (Feb 2026)** EUR 2.3bn HVDC subsea.
- Q1 2026 results: organic growth, margin expansion, strong free cash flow.

### Nexans (NEX.PA)
- **Total backlog: EUR 7.9bn.** **>90% of EUR 7.4bn end-2024 backlog driven by subsea interconnections + offshore wind.**
- Customer concentration: 85% TSO.
- Marquee project: **Great Sea Interconnector** EUR 1.4bn (Greece-Cyprus-Israel).
- Q4 2025: PWR-Connect organic growth accelerated to **+10.9%**, driven by datacenter & infrastructure (medium/low voltage, NOT HVDC).
- Strategic acquisition: Electro Cables (Canada) for automated manufacturing capacity.
- Backlog visibility: 4.5 years (former VP).

### NKT (NKT.CO)
- **High-voltage order backlog: EUR 10.4bn at end-Q3 2025**, plus **EUR 3.5bn in capacity reservation commitments awaiting final call-off.**
- Backlog phasing: ~30% of end-2024 backlog executes 2025-2026; 70% executes 2027 onwards.
- Margin profile: **Operational EBITDA margin 16.4% Q3 2025 vs 14.2% Q3 2024.**
- Customer concentration: >85% TSO; only 4% backlog exposure to volatile speculative developers (deliberately limited to insulate manufacturing schedules).
- Capacity expansion: Karlskrona facility (third extrusion tower) + NKT Eleonora vessel, both operational 2027. Post-2028 capex normalizes to ~4% of standard metal revenue.
- Financing: Green Hybrid Bond + €400m RCF refinanced as Sustainability-Linked Loan.
- ROCE temporarily diluted to 24% end-2025 as capital base expanded ahead of revenue.

## Section 2 — Demand Mix Decomposition

**Pillar 1 — Subsea interconnections.** Dominant. Prysmian's NeuConnect, Tyrrhenian, EGL1/2/4. Nexans Great Sea Interconnector (GR-CY-IL).

**Pillar 2 — Offshore wind export cables.** Substantial. Prysmian Amprion BalWin1/2, 50Hertz Germany framework, Ijmuiden Ver.

**Pillar 3 — AI / datacenter HVDC.** **SMALL SHARE BUT GROWING.**
- **Prysmian:** Strategic partnership + equity investment with **Relativity Networks** for next-gen hollow-core optical fiber (lower latency, signal distortion for hyperscalers). Management notes ongoing negotiations for long-term commercial agreements to capture future "inside" datacenter growth.
- **Nexans:** PWR-Connect Q4 25 +10.9% from infrastructure/datacenter projects — explicitly **medium/low voltage, not HVDC PWR-Transmission segment**.
- **NKT:** No specific AI/datacenter HVDC disclosure surfaced.

**Pillar 4 — Onshore transmission.** BalWin / German grid expansion.

**AI as a share of new awards:** Growing for cable cos overall but largely concentrated in the cable manufacturers' lower-voltage and accessories segments rather than HVDC transmission backlog. **HVDC backlog continues to be utility/grid + offshore wind anchored.**

## Section 3 — Capacity Expansion Economics

### Prysmian
- **~EUR 600m capex programme** for installation fleet expansion.
- Existing fleet anchored by Leonardo da Vinci + recently commissioned Monna Lisa.
- Two next-generation vessels under construction including **Alessandro Volta** (Monna Lisa class evolution; 185m; three carousels).
- Total capacity expansion programme through 2027 = full $2bn cumulative (Q4 prompt context).

### Nexans
- Halden + Charleston plants loaded **>90% utilization through 2028** — almost no spare capacity for incremental demand acceleration (Guosheng Securities, 10 Jan).
- Strategic acquisition Electro Cables (Canada).

### NKT
- **EUR 1.0bn Karlskrona + Eleonora vessel programme** (2023–2026/2027, with cost overrun reported).
- New Karlskrona production assets + Eleonora vessel both operational **2027**.
- Post-2028 capex normalizes to ~4% of standard metal revenue.
- Currently digesting only ~15% of incoming orders annually — capacity expansion visibly lagging order growth (Guosheng Securities).

**Sell-side return expectations:** Strongly de-risked (rather than aspirational) given visible TSO contracts, asymmetric liquidated-damages structures, and >5-year backlog visibility.

## Section 4 — Execution Risk Evidence

**Industry-wide context:** Penalty structure for execution failure has grown asymmetric. Liquidated damages calculated as agreed % of total contract value enforce strict delivery deadlines and quality standards. Even minor supply chain interruptions or manufacturing deviations can cascade into severe margin deterioration and reputational damage.

**NKT specific risk:**
- Karlskrona expansion + Eleonora vessel programme original budget EUR 1.0bn — **severe cost overrun reported.**
- Aggressive capacity expansion accompanied by quantifiable execution challenges 2024-2025.
- Risk insulated somewhat by deliberate 4% cap on speculative-developer exposure.

**Prysmian specific risk:**
- Execution risk tied to percentage-of-completion accounting on EUR 17bn backlog; cost-estimation errors trigger immediate margin downward revisions.
- Former Prysmian CFO: "the major players now hold backlogs stretching five to seven years, the sheer volume and complexity of the operations mean that a single e[xecution event can have material impact]."

**Nexans:** Halden + Charleston >90% utilized through 2028 — execution capacity tight; little buffer for delays.

## Section 5 — Vessel Availability and Installation Bottleneck

- **Prysmian operates the industry's most extensive captive installation fleet.** Constructing two next-generation vessels including Alessandro Volta (185m, three carousels).
- **NKT philosophy:** "Will not allow installation capacity to become the limiting factor for revenue generation." Strictly aligns marine capabilities to terrestrial factory output. Eleonora vessel operational 2027.
- **Vessel scarcity is becoming the binding constraint** rather than cable manufacturing per se. Captive fleet ownership = competitive moat for Prysmian and (post-2027) NKT.

## Section 6 — Competitive Landscape Evolution

**Tight oligopoly:** Prysmian + Nexans + NKT collectively control **~89% of top-tier HVDC market share.**

**Asian new entrants:**
- South Korean (truncated names) and Asian cable manufacturers aggressively expanding geographic footprint into European and North American core markets.
- Capacity bottlenecks in West are catalysing structural evolution.
- Heavy capital requirements for VCV (Vertical Continuous Vulcanization) towers and complex deep-water installation vessels continue to favor established European leaders.

**Technology shifts:**
- Industry definitively transitioned from **320kV to 525kV** XLPE extruded cables as new baseline.
- 525kV enables single cable pair to transmit **up to 2.5 GW** over vast distances.
- **Prysmian's P-Laser 525kV** = proprietary recyclable polypropylene-based insulation, qualifies as additional moat.
- SF6-free GIS evolution mentioned (gas-insulated switchgear) as further technology transition.

**Pricing pressure from new entrants:** Limited at HVDC tier-one level; Asian competitors more credible at lower-voltage/accessories.

## Section 8 — Capital Cycle Indicators

- **Sold-out capacity through 2028 is the primary signal of cycle maturity.**
- NKT capacity expansion visibly lagging demand — late-cycle signal.
- Prysmian and NKT have extended project lead times to ~7 years.
- BUT: TSO-anchored backlogs + multi-year frameworks + high replacement cost barriers (VCV towers, vessels) = supply discipline reinforced rather than overbuild.
- Asian new entrants are testing the cycle but the technology + capital + vessel barriers remain high.

**Net:** Cycle is mature but supply discipline reinforced by capital intensity + vessel scarcity + TSO demand frameworks. Different from a classic late-cycle commodity build.
