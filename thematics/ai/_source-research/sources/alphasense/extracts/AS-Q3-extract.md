## Section 3 — European Supply-Chain Order-Book Transmission Lag

**Legrand:** Steady visibility through near-term deliveries scheduled within 3–6 months. Datacenter book-to-bill remained firmly above 1.0 through end of 2024.

**Siemens Energy:** Substantial structural lags between order booking and sales fruition for power generation and grid equipment. After unprecedented order entry driven by US data center demand in fiscal 2024 and 2025, **order volumes expected to STABILISE rather than continue growing at the 12-18 month historical peak**.

**HVDC cable manufacturers (Nexans, NKT, Prysmian):** Operate on ultra-long-cycle transmission schedules due to severe global capacity constraints. Functionally **sold out for years**. NKT: only 29-30% (truncated — likely "available capacity in near-term"). 2-3 year framework agreements with key utility customers (Nexans Grid).

**Schneider Electric DCN:** 19% of total group sales (per Redburn Atlantic 4 Mar 25). 7% of that 19% is distributed IT and network businesses driven by broader enterprise (not pure hyperscaler).

## Section 4 — Order Book Composition (hyperscaler-direct vs other)

**Legrand:** Datacenters = **26% of group sales in 2025**, up from 20% in 2024. Customer mix:

**Schneider Electric:** Data Center & Networks (DCN) = 19% of total group sales. 7% of that comes from distributed IT/networks (broader enterprise).

**Cable manufacturers (Prysmian/Nexans/NKT):** Multi-year framework agreements with utility customers; HVDC backlog is utility-anchored not hyperscaler-direct, providing structural insulation.

Memo v1's pre-mortem timing assumes a 6-9 month transmission to drawdown — this timing depends on a moderation signal that has not yet emerged. HVDC cables effectively sold-out for years with utility (not hyperscaler) framework agreements. The transmission window from a hyperscaler cut to European order-book impact is materially insulated for cable manufacturers.

5. **Counter-cyclical backfill is real and large.** $300bn → $600bn IEA grid investment by 2030 is a structural tailwind that compounds for years even if hyperscaler capex moderates. Memo v2 should reference this $600bn IEA number explicitly as the base-case backstop.

6. **Material AS evidentiary gaps** vs prompt brief:
   - No quantified moderation scenarios from named US sell-side
   - No quantified cancellation history
   - No quantified margin sensitivity models
   These should be flagged in memo v2 as known unknowns; the bear case is harder to anchor in sell-side fundamentals than memo v1 implied.
