# AS Query 3 — Hyperscaler capex retrenchment risk + European supply chain transmission

**Mode:** Deep Research
**Date submitted:** 2 May 2026
**Target tickers:** ENR.DE (Siemens Energy), SU.PA (Schneider), LR.PA (Legrand), PRY.MI (Prysmian), NEX.PA (Nexans), NKT.CO (NKT), ABBN.SW (ABB)
**Memo conclusion to stress-test:** Picks-and-shovels names face multiple-compression risk that exceeds earnings risk over 12-24m as capital cycle does its job. A 20% hyperscaler capex cut would produce 30-40% drawdowns on most-stretched names within 6-9 months. Most probable single failure path in pre-mortem.

## Prompt

Provide a deep research report on (a) the risk of hyperscaler AI capex retrenchment from current $527bn 2026 consensus run rate, and (b) the speed and severity at which such retrenchment would transmit through to European industrial supply-chain order books and margins. Period of focus: late 2025 to present.

Synthesise across earnings transcripts, sell-side, and expert sources to address:

1. **Hyperscaler capex commentary detail.** What have Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Oracle CFOs / IR said in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 calls about: (a) 2026 and 2027 capex visibility, (b) AI ROI evidence from deployed workloads, (c) the dependency of capex on revenue ramp from AI products, (d) any commentary about prioritisation, efficiency, or moderation? Direct quotes preferred. Flag any commentary that suggests a moderation trigger.

2. **Sell-side capex moderation scenarios.** Which sell-side analysts (US tech analysts at GS, Morgan Stanley, JPM, BofA, Bernstein, Wells Fargo, etc.) are explicitly modelling a moderation scenario? What triggers do they identify? What is the consensus probability? What is the magnitude (10%, 20%, 30% cut)?

3. **European supply-chain order-book transmission lag.** Historical evidence on how quickly European industrial order books respond to upstream demand changes. Specifically:
   - Siemens Energy gas turbine and grid equipment — what is the typical lag between hyperscaler order signal change and Siemens Energy book-to-bill change?
   - Schneider Electric Data Center & Networks — same question
   - Legrand cooling and rack-level equipment — same question
   - Prysmian / Nexans / NKT HVDC cables — given the long-cycle nature, what is the lag and what cancellation risk exists on existing order books?
   Use 2022-2024 cycle data as base rate where available.

4. **Order book composition.** For Siemens Energy, Schneider DC&N, Legrand cooling, and the cable manufacturers: what proportion of the current order book is hyperscaler-direct vs telecom-customer vs utility-customer vs other? What proportion is binding vs framework-agreement?

5. **Margin sensitivity analysis.** Any sell-side modelled sensitivity of Siemens Energy, Schneider, Legrand, Prysmian margins to a hyperscaler capex moderation. Quantified preferred.

6. **Cancellation history.** Historical examples of hyperscaler order cancellations or deferrals from European industrial suppliers. What was the magnitude, what were the contractual terms, what was the equity-market reaction?

7. **Counter-cyclical demand drivers.** What proportion of European supply-chain demand is non-AI (energy transition, electrification, grid reinforcement, military capex, industrial decarbonisation)? Could non-AI demand backfill an AI capex cut?

Bias toward Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 sources. Quantitative evidence preferred. Flag where sell-side disagrees on capex sustainability.
