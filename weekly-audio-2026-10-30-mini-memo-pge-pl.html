APM Mini-Memo. PGE. The thirtieth of October 2026.

PGE is rank eight on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, promoted this week from the bottom of the Shortlist. The one-line view. PGE is a growing regulated grid wrapped inside a state-controlled coal company. The case depends on power prices and the regulator carrying the coal years until offshore wind pays.

First, what the company does. PGE is Poland's largest producer of electricity and heat. Four businesses sit inside one listed company. PGE mines its own lignite at Belchatow and Turow. PGE runs a nineteen gigawatt fleet of coal, gas and renewable plants. PGE owns the regulated distribution network serving nearly six million customers. And PGE supplies nearly six million retail accounts. The group holds about a third of Polish generation and nearly a third of distribution. The Polish State Treasury owns sixty-one percent of the shares, so the state decides strategy. The profit mix is already more grid than coal. The distribution network earned forty-one percent of recurring operating profit before depreciation in 2025. District heating earned twenty-seven percent and renewables eleven percent in early 2026. Conventional coal generation earned only three hundred and fifty-seven million zloty. The market value is about seven billion euros at a share price of sixteen zloty thirty on the thirtieth of October.

Second, the thesis. The story is a re-rating from coal holder to grid owner, with a power-price tailwind. Investors in Poland, the Czech Republic, Greece and Romania have begun valuing the state utilities as grid owners. The reason is that regulators there allow high or rising returns on state-funded grids. PGE's distribution business earns up to thirteen percent on its regulated asset base, against a baseline of seven and a half percent, because the regulator pays a premium for reinvestment. That asset base is planned to grow above nine percent a year to 2030, on thirty-seven billion zloty of grid spending. Regulated activities are forecast to reach sixty-two percent of group profit by 2030, from thirty-eight percent in 2020. The near-term earnings are stronger than the headlines. Recurring operating profit before depreciation rose twenty-one percent in the second quarter of 2026, to nearly four billion zloty. Heat and gas-fired plants set records on favourable fuel spreads, and generation rose eighteen percent. Our Sector note of the ninth of October argued that a European gas price shock is a power price shock for every unhedged coal megawatt-hour. That could turn the Polish generators from state-controlled coal into the cheapest generation in Europe. The weekly Lists judgement promoted PGE on the first European power-price reading in a month. Earnings per share rise through three channels. First, the grid's asset base compounds at a premium return. Second, Baltica Two, a one and a half gigawatt offshore wind farm, carries a twenty-five year indexed contract for difference. All one hundred and eleven foundations were installed on schedule in August 2026, and first power is due by the end of 2027. Third, six hundred million zloty a year of capacity payments on the coal reserve run through 2028. Those payments keep the legacy fleet from losing money while the wind farm is built. The research's bull mechanism puts adjusted earnings per share at two zloty nineteen for 2026, with a fifty-six percent joint probability. More likely than not, not safe. The drivers to track are the next household tariff decision, the distribution allowed return, Baltica Two's first power, and the new board's 2035 strategy update.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up, Stock-driven ratings, and this read is my own. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. The change is the conversion of a coal company into a grid and offshore wind company, with the coal separation the big event still without a date. A Demand-driven EPSU leg from higher European power prices combines with it. The match is loose. Consensus earnings fall nearly five percent a year over the horizon the weekly routine uses, so the step-up is in the plan and not yet in the forecasts. The clearing setups do not fit, because the multiple sits at the fifty-ninth percentile of its own ten-year range, not in the bottom half.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The case is not simple, and the main risk is sequencing under state control. The transition is funded by a regulated business that earns its premium by spending. The cash to service that spending comes from coal plants whose economics are deteriorating. The 2025 carbon bill was nearly sixteen billion zloty. Every fourteen zloty per tonne rise in carbon costs two hundred and twenty million zloty of profit. Capital spending roughly doubles to twenty-one billion zloty a year. The state has shown it can rewrite the plan. The Ministry abandoned the coal carve-out in July 2025, leaving a twelve billion zloty impairment, and the chief executive was replaced in January 2026 after less than two years. The weekly Lists judgement's own doubt is that this week's promotion rests on one broker's upgrade, one deal and a move in forward power prices without a figure. What needs to go right. Power prices stay high enough for coal to cover carbon. The regulator keeps the distribution return. Baltica Two connects on time. And net debt stays below three and a half times operating profit before depreciation, against Fitch's downgrade trigger of three point seven times, after the cut from triple B plus to triple B in 2025. The early warnings are the next household tariff decision, the distribution allowed return against the eight point three percent reference, net debt against three and a half times, and the receivables cycle extending beyond forty-two days. Five of the six ranked drivers are set by the regulator or the state.

Fifth, business quality. Good grid, poor earnings quality. The research's confidence in business quality is medium. The distribution moat is a legally designated territory with a cost-of-capital tariff formula, and retail customers rarely leave their default supplier. Against that, recurring operating profit before depreciation of nearly thirteen billion zloty in 2025 became a reported net loss of three point four billion zloty, on seven and a half billion zloty of coal write-downs. Household tariffs do not cover costs, with a potential loss of five hundred and sixty million zloty. No dividend was paid for 2019 to 2024, and a restart needs recurring net profit, two years of positive free cash flow and an investment-grade rating. The balance sheet is the best in its cohort, with net debt of four billion zloty and fourteen billion zloty of cash. That balance sheet is what makes the spending plan possible.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The plausible return is a C, at about fifteen percent a year on average. The weekly routine of the twenty-ninth of October reads nineteen percent a year over eighteen months and ten percent a year over three years. That assumes the multiple moves from under seven times to nearly nine times, its fair multiple for a broadening market, while consensus earnings fall nearly five percent a year, dividends included. The shares trade at under seven times earnings two years out, the fifty-ninth percentile of their own range. The eastern state utility cohort sits at over eight times, the ninety-fifth percentile of its range, after rising forty-three percent in twelve months. The shares have run ahead of published targets. The price of sixteen zloty thirty is above every published target in our files, which span eight to fourteen zloty. PGE was up fifty percent relative over thirty days by the ninth of October. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of the thirtieth of October is A, with the shares seven percent below their fifty-two week high. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of the twenty-eighth of October is A. A B return needs earnings to hold rather than fall, which is what higher power prices and the Baltica contract could deliver.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros. Regulators across Central and South-East Europe allow high or rising grid returns, and the state funds the grids. Electrification lifts network volumes, up four percent. A gas price shock lifts power prices for unhedged coal. Capacity payments are locked in to 2028. And the Polish state assets minister expects higher dividends from state companies. The weekly Thematic Fit, Momentum rating of the thirtieth of October is B, from the environment only. The cons. The cohort at the ninety-fifth percentile of its range needs the coal separation as its next catalyst, and no terms have been agreed since July 2025. Poland taxes 2026 fuel revenues more than twenty percent above average at sixty percent. Rooftop solar of thirteen gigawatts and batteries eat into merchant prices. Carbon sits at seventy-five euros a tonne, with fourteen percent a year growth projected. Bond yields move the whole Industry. And the Pool marks PGE as leaving, in the first of two weeks, so the tactical window may be short.

The one-line view. PGE is a growing regulated grid wrapped inside a state-controlled coal company. The case depends on power prices and the regulator carrying the coal years until offshore wind pays.
