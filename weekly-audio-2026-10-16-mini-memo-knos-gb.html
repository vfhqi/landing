APM Mini-Memo. Kainos. The sixteenth of October 2026.

Kainos sits at rank three on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The view is bullish on the business and neutral on the shares at one thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence, with medium conviction. A founder-led services company is turning itself into a software-weighted one while United Kingdom public-sector demand recovers, but the price has already paid for the revenue recovery and now depends on a margin recovery. The first test is the half-year results on the ninth of November.

First, what the company does. Kainos is a Belfast-headquartered digital services and Workday software company. Digital Services, fifty-six percent of revenue, builds digital systems mostly for the United Kingdom government and health service. Workday Services, a quarter of revenue, implements Workday's human resources and finance software. Workday Products, nineteen percent, sells Kainos's own software inside the Workday platform at a seventy-seven point eight percent gross margin. Revenue grew seventeen point four percent to four hundred and thirty-one million pounds last year, adjusted pre-tax profit was sixty-seven million pounds at a fifteen point six percent margin, and the company holds eighty-nine million pounds of net cash with no debt.

Second, the thesis. The Investment Analyst's memo of the second of October calls this a self-help case riding a demand recovery. The founder Brendan Mooney, chief executive again since December twenty twenty-four, re-set the company as a service around the software business, just as public-sector digital procurement came out of a two-year, election-induced trough. The change has reached every layer: bookings up thirty-two percent, backlog up eighteen percent, recurring revenue up twenty-three percent to eighty-nine million pounds, and consensus earnings for this year up fifteen percent over twelve months with no broker cut. The mechanism to earnings per share has three parts. Contracted backlog converts to revenue. The two hundred and fifty-nine contractors, up from sixty-nine are replaced by permanent staff, restoring the services margin from the second half of this financial year. And Workday Products scales toward one hundred million pounds of recurring revenue by the end of calendar twenty twenty-six. On the memo's path adjusted earnings per share rise from forty-one point one pence to fifty-three and a half pence this year and seventy pence in the twenty twenty-nine financial year, about nineteen percent a year compound.

Third, the Target Setup. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU: a chief executive change plus a guidance increase. The Investment Analyst rated Setups' Fit and Crispness B on the second of October, because the pattern fits crisply on inputs, revenue and estimates, and loosely on the margin, which has not yet moved. Demand-driven EPSU is the reading the counter-case prefers and the memo calls a partial fit.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The Investment Analyst rated Case Riskiness C on the second of October: simple, but not low-risk at the price. Simple because there are three drivers and one swing factor, the services gross margin. Not low-risk because the price carries what the company does not. The memo measured a nineteen to twenty-five percent downside on the multiple alone to levels the stock traded at within two years. At one thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence, above the memo's do-not-chase line of one thousand two hundred and fifty pence, the point is sharper.

Fifth, business quality. A good operator with real but bounded advantages. Foundations' Robustness is B from the Investment Analyst on the second of October: framework incumbency, integration switching costs, product intellectual property inside Workday, eighty-six percent of revenue from existing customers and ninety-nine percent cash conversion. Against that, a fifteen point six percent adjusted pre-tax margin below the framework's twenty percent threshold for solid, and a fully unhedged currency book with about a quarter of revenue in dollars. Workday is at once the moat and the largest dependency, and every channel term is the vendor's to change.

Sixth, returns and valuation. At one thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence on the sixteenth of October the shares trade at nineteen point two times consensus earnings two years out. That is the twenty-sixth percentile of the stock's own ten-year range, but the memo puts it at the ninety-second percentile of the last two years. The ratings store's TSR and Valuation is D as at the sixteenth of October, re-based to today's price: thirteen and a half percent a year over eighteen months and eleven point one percent over three years, which sits at your D anchor. The memo at one thousand two hundred and eleven pence had sixteen point nine and twelve point eight percent, and said the case clears the twenty percent bar only below about one thousand one hundred and sixty pence.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. On the positive side, demand is contracted and policy-backed to twenty thirty: a one hundred and fifty million pound DEFRA award, and NHS England and DVSA programmes. The script's Thematic Fit and Momentum reading of the sixteenth of October is B, where the memo had it pinned at D for Kainos and all sixteen peers; the script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the fifteenth is B against the memo's C; and Technical Momentum is B. On the negative side, displaced global integrators bid five to ten percent below others on public frameworks and took Kainos's share of the Digital Outcomes and Specialists framework from ten percent to four point six. Active United Kingdom Workday partners rose from sixty to one hundred and fourteen. And the planned disbandment of NHS England threatens twenty-five to thirty percent of next year's healthcare business. Kainos is the margin-prioritiser in a market that is not.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. The false friend: bookings and revenue surge while profit never follows. Last year is the evidence: revenue up seventeen percent, adjusted pre-tax profit up two percent. Contractor dependence is structural because public contracts must be staffed in weeks while a permanent hire takes five months; integrators and a doubled partner base set price; and artificial intelligence hands part of the delivery saving back to clients. On that path earnings per share still compound at about ten percent a year, so the case becomes mediocre at this price rather than wrong about the company, and a multiple at its five-year median while most peers sit near decade lows has nineteen to twenty-five percent to fall. The ninth of November decides.

The one-line view. Bullish on the business, neutral on the shares at one thousand two hundred and seventy-two pence, medium conviction: a founder-led services company is turning into a software-weighted one as public-sector demand recovers, but the price has paid for the revenue recovery and now depends on a margin recovery first tested on the ninth of November.
