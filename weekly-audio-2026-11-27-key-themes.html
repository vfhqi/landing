The strongest counter-case, and my reply.

The counter-case is that this reading buys the top of a crowded trade. The defensive rotation is four months old, Goldman calls it mature, Deutsche Bank's positioning reading is at the sixty-fourth percentile, and Morgan Stanley's fade window is a month away. The rate-sensitive Underweight rows, towers and real assets, led the week's rebound, and the ten-year yield is a third of a percentage point from the trigger that lifts them. A book half invested in a correction has already done its defensive work, and a Bench that is thirty percent healthcare would have it do that work twice. If the rotation ends, Healthcare returns to Neutral, the six healthcare names on the Bench lose their top-down support at once, and the gap report's Overweight rows become last quarter's trade.

My reply has three parts. First, the top of the Bench stands on bottom-up evidence that does not depend on the row: Convatec's Setups' Fit A and Case Riskiness A, Sonova's Foundations' Robustness A, Demant's memo with its dated tests. At Neutral they would lose a notch of thematic grade and keep their places. Second, the Bench already holds the cyclical cases with real evidence, Beijer Ref in first place, Azelis, WPP and Reply, which are the names that rise if the rotation ends, so the Bench is hedged by construction rather than by hope. Third, the book being half invested is the reason to engage, not to wait: the downward bias on gross exposure is spent, the next risk is being under-invested if the rebound broadens, and the question the Lists answer is what to add, not whether. What to track is precise: the rotation ends if the STOXX Europe 600 comes within one percent of its high with sixty-five percent or more of stocks above their two-hundred-day average, or if the three-month defensives lead falls under two points.
