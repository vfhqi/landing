Values and Behaviours

ETCs (Evergreen Target Conditions)

Three perpetual goals that set direction. They never "complete" — they define what success looks like on a rolling basis.

  1. Investing ETC: Have a portfolio and squad that makes outlier returns (5-10% outperformance per annum) on a rolling next-two-year basis by continually, fluidly and successfully adapting to any unfolding circumstances.

  2. Self/Performance ETC: Consistently execute the process at an elite level. Mark Douglas style. Conservative analysis, aggressive execution. Simple + completed beats complicated + incomplete.

  3. Business/Viewforth ETC: Build and sustain a viable solo investment operation with appropriate infrastructure, tools, and professional standards.

Winning Behaviours

What Drives Alpha

  • Breadth (rate tonnes of stocks) creates context for depth
  • Speed of OODA loop — observe, orient, decide, act faster than consensus
  • Predictability obsession — only own what you can track and forecast
  • Paranoid monitoring of live positions
  • Decisive exits — 30-day shot clock, default reduce to 0%
  • Meeting management teams — first-hand operator quality evidence

What Destroys Alpha

  • Holding deteriorating positions too long (endowment bias, normalcy bias, cockroach theory)
  • Solving deteriorations analytically instead of cutting
  • Over-complication — solving for "perfect" not "alpha test"
  • Creativity/growth drive pulling away from execution
  • Demand exceeding capacity — trying to do too much
  • Lazy extrapolation of cyclical highs

Standards

Investment Quality Standards

  • Every dimension must rate A/B or top quartile — anything less is "fail"
  • "This is not a business where second-best gets rewarded"
  • D or C rated predictability = structural risk that rarely changes. Only viable strategy: avoidance.
  • Painful Prudence: Only own stocks where conviction is 9-10/10. Rest of capital better deployed elsewhere.

Execution Standards

  • IAJA on every judgement — no analysis without a conclusion
  • 2 downstream actions minimum from every judgement
  • No open loops — every thread resolved or explicitly parked
  • Back-briefs before and after execution
  • Session handoffs every day
  • 30-day shot clock on deterioration: Default action is exit (0%). Re-argue from zero if risk is genuinely disproved.
  • Stop starting, start finishing: Creativity loop once per month. Remaining days devoted to disciplined execution of existing playbook.

Watson Standards

  • Show full reasoning chain
  • Always have a directional view — never end with "it depends"
  • Flag confidence level and key assumptions
  • Update memory files automatically
  • Log corrections immediately
  • Parallel [C] + [AS] execution on all research

"With Winning in Mind" — Detailed Practices

Core principle: What you picture vividly and focus on intently becomes reality. The nervous system and self-image cannot distinguish imagined from actual experience — rehearsal builds confidence equivalently to live performance.

Mental Imagery & Visualisation

  • Focus only on what you wish to happen. Neural pathways are carved by vividly imagined success. Avoid picturing failure; the nervous system treats it as real.
  • Rehearse mentally before every key action. Pre-event: see yourself calm, decisive, executing well. During action: trust the rehearsal. Post-event: reinforce wins.
  • Self-image cannot distinguish imagined from actual. This is the power and the peril. Rehearse relentlessly; catastrophising mentally is equally real to the nervous system.

Language & Self-Talk

  • Positive language only. Ban: "better," "worse," "mistake," "improve," "fix," "problem" (these imply deficiency and weakness).
  • Replace with: "done differently," "adjusted approach," "discovery," "version 2.0" (growth-focused, not deficit-focused).
  • No negative self-talk. Ever. No complaining. No judging others. All language shapes the nervous system toward or away from winners' mindset.
  • No internal narrative of failure. "I never three-putted" (Jack Nicklaus). Don't record bad performances mentally. They ossify into identity.
  • Tone check: "Would I be proud of this self-talk on a Jumbotron?" If no, don't say it.

Three Arenas of Self-Image Growth

  1. Pre-event anticipation: Vivid mental rehearsal. See yourself calm, executing, winning. Creates neural pathways.
  2. Action phase: Confidence comes from doing, not thinking. Execute the rehearsed plan. Small wins build momentum.
  3. Reinforcement phase: Celebrate wins — big and small. "That's like me" after every positive event. This anchors winner identity in behaviour.

Critique Protocol

  • No critique mid-action. Only in review sessions (Friday afternoons, post-market, calm context).
  • During action: Trust the rehearsal. Adapt if needed, but don't second-guess.
  • In review: Assess dispassionately. Capture what went well (reinforce). Note adjustments for next time (learning, not blame).

Success Journal

  • Record what went well, every day. Capture: decisions made well, communication that landed, process executed cleanly, markets read correctly.
  • Review weekly. Anchor wins in memory. Reinforce the pattern: "This is what I do."
  • Do not record bad performances. Let them go. They have no place in your identity narrative.

Implementation for Richard

  • Mental rehearsal 5-10 minutes before key portfolio actions (entering, exiting, calling managers)
  • Post-call/post-trade: "That went well" + 30-second record of what worked (tone, logic, timing)
  • Friday recap: Celebrate week's wins. Adjust next week's approach — never critique self
  • Ban "I made a mistake" — instead "I adjusted approach on X, learned Y, will do Z next time"

Limiting Beliefs to Manage

  • Catastrophising about permanence of negative developments (bad returns = no job = erroneous)
  • Gold medallist mindset — drop it (ref. Jonny Wilkinson, others who felt unworthy)
  • "Unbung" (Kahneman): what is blocking me from doing what I know I need to do?

Peter Kaufman Framework — Five Deep Things All Humans Want

Core principle: People desperately want to be deeply valued. This applies to team, to callers/connections, to yourself.

Richard notes he sometimes "leaks negativity" and loses this edge. When you radiate positivity and make people feel valued, they're more open, collaborative, and generous with time/insight.

The Five Dimensions of Valuation

  1. Pay deep attention to the person. Put the phone down. Eyes up. Mental energy 100% on them, not on the next call or the email in your inbox.
  2. Listen intently, actively. Not waiting for your turn to talk. Not half-listening. Listening to understand their world, their constraints, their aspirations.
  3. Respect the person — make it crystal clear. In tone, in language, in follow-through. "I value your view. I want to understand your thinking."
  4. Make them feel they really, really matter. Not performative. Genuine. "Your insight here is exactly what I needed. Thank you."
  5. Make them feel loved. Not in a romantic sense. In the "I genuinely want good things for you" sense. This is the hardest and most powerful.
  6. Allow no distraction. Phone silent. Meeting notes optional. All mental cycles on them.

Implementation for Richard

  • Pre-call: Set intention. "What does this person want to know I understand about them?"
  • During call: Ask 3-4 clarifying questions before stating your view. Let them speak 60%+.
  • Post-call: One-liner to them saying what you learned/valued. Keep the door open.

Positivity as Competitive Edge

Richard notes he's "leaking negativity" in some contexts and needs to "radiate positivity" as a conscious operating mode.

The Mechanism

  • Negativity is contagious and draining. It signals doubt, fear, and weakness.
  • Positivity is infectious. It signals conviction, energy, and forward momentum. People want to follow it.
  • In team/partner contexts, your emotional tone sets the room. Negative tone = people contract. Positive tone = people expand and contribute.
  • "Do you want to be world class?" — Inspire with "moving towards" energy, not "told off" feeling.

The Balance

  • This is NOT Pollyannaism. You still identify problems, manage risks, cut positions.
  • Tone shift: "We're seeing this risk; here's how we adapt" (positive action) vs. "This is broken; we're in trouble" (dread and complaint).
  • Language shift: "Here's what we'll do" (agency) vs. "Here's what's wrong" (blame).
  • Energy shift: Radiate "we've got this" not "we're drowning."

The Test

  • When discussing challenges (market downturn, position going wrong, team issue), do you leave people energised or depleted?
  • Are you the radiator or the drain in the room?
  • Do your team/partners want to work with you more, or do they avoid you?

Implementation for Richard

  • Morning check-in: "What's my baseline energy today? Where am I leaking negativity?"
  • Decision calls: Lead with "Here's the opportunity/action" not "Here's the risk."
  • Team/partner interactions: 2 positives (genuine) for every 1 critical feedback. Keep culture radiator, not drain.
