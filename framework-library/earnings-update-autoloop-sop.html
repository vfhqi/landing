The Earnings Update Autoloop SOP

When a company in scope reports results, the system reads the results, decides whether our investment case moved, and rebuilds the memo when it did, without you having to start anything. This page says what runs, when, what it spends, what needs you, and how you would know if it stopped.

The one-paragraph version. Every morning the earnings calendar is refreshed from FactSet. When a company in scope reports, an Earnings Review is ordered from the research production line. Once the review exists, and only if we already have an investment case memo for that stock, the Investment Analyst writes your Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case: what these results mean for our case, published to the Reports & Memos Repository within minutes whatever the verdict (added 25-Sep-26). For live holdings, Ideas Bench, Ideas Shortlist and interest level A, the Earnings Review is run again four working days later and the Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case is re-done on it, replacing the day-one one on the Repository (added 28-Oct-26). A rule called the gate then decides whether the full memo is worth rebuilding; for those stocks it decides on the re-done one, after the sell-side has responded. Live holdings are always rebuilt; other stocks only when the results clearly improve the case. A rebuild becomes a request card for the memo production line, which writes the new memo. Separately, research reports older than three months are refreshed every quarter. Everything is reported on the All-Team Briefing page, and anything you need to know reaches you in the Daily Digest email.

2. The rules that decide what happens

The stock isThe memo is rebuilt when
A live holdingAlways, after every results event, whatever the verdict
Top of the Ideas lists (Bench, Shortlist, interest level A)The case moved positively, by a semi-material or material amount
The wider Ideas list (Longlist, interest level B)The case moved positively by a material amount
Anything elseNever
  • Negative results never trigger a rebuild for a stock we do not own: a candidate that got worse is simply not a candidate. The Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case is still written and still reported.
  • Which one decides (added 28-Oct-26): for live holdings, Ideas Bench, Ideas Shortlist and interest level A, the re-done Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case, written on the repeat review, decides; the day-one one is reported but orders nothing. For the Ideas Longlist and interest level B there is no repeat, so the one written a working day after the results decides.
  • These routines never write a first memo. They only rebuild one that exists. (First memos for stocks on the Ideas lists come from the Ideas commissioner, a separate route.)
  • Spend limits: at most 10 automatic memos a UK week, counted by the memo production line; live-holding rebuilds sit outside the 10. Research: 20 orders a day, 30 on results days. Nothing is ever ordered twice.

3. What it costs

  • Free: the calendar refresh, the walk, the packs, the gate, the card writer and the briefing all run as scripts on your PC with no language model.
  • Paid: each Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case is one Fable run (two per results event for the same-day group from 28-Oct-26). Each rebuilt memo is roughly 645,000 Fable tokens, which is why the gate exists. Fifteen live holdings reporting two to four times a year means roughly 30 to 60 rebuilds a year.
  • Hidden cost: every Claude scheduled run uses a little of a fixed session disk that is never emptied. That is why only one step here runs in Claude.

4. How you would know it is working, or has stopped

  • The Daily Digest email tells you:
    • when an Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case was written (it says when it is the re-done one), and what the gate decided;
    • when a results-day review was missed;
    • when a ticker on your lists cannot be matched;
    • when any of these jobs fails.
    A known duplicate record is never put to you as a question.
  • Every job writes an "I ran" file, including on quiet days. A free watchdog checks them every thirty minutes and puts a stale or failed job in the Daily Digest. The task that writes the Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case is allowed 72 hours, because it does not run at weekends.
  • The All-Team Briefing page (locked with your passphrase) shows every order, every piece, every held or missed item and every job's health. It hides anything that looks like a password or access token.
  • A Stand-Up you start in a chat window reads the latest briefing first.

5. What needs you

  • Nothing routine. Results, reviews, the Initial Summary Implications to Investment Thesis/Case, the gate and rebuild requests all run without you.
  • Once per login: each copy of the writing task must be tied to your computer in its settings. A task only reaches your PC while its own login is signed in. While you are on one login, the platform switches off the other logins' copies at their run times; a small routine switches them back on (being tested on the Ricky login first, from 22-Sep-26).
  • Occasionally: answer a question the Daily Digest puts to you.
  • Your call only: any change to the rules in section 2 or the spend limits.
