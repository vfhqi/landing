E8 — Fit with acceptable case setups

Lifted from IC#3 Required Case SETUPS. This is the full six-setup taxonomy plus the two negative guardrails (unacceptable and false-friend setups).

RA1 [from IC#3 TC1] — Mis-modelled, high-quality EPS upgrader?

WHAT THIS SETUP IS. The first of the six acceptable case setups. It has two conditions that must BOTH hold, and the common failure is grading on one of them alone.

  1. MIS-MODELLED. The market's forward earnings model is wrong, and wrong in a knowable, nameable way. Not "the market is too pessimistic" as a mood, but a specific line the consensus model gets wrong: a mix shift it has not built in, a cost programme it has not credited, an under-promising management whose guidance it takes at face value, a segment it aggregates away. Name the line and say why consensus has it wrong.
  2. HIGH-QUALITY. The business clears Richard's quality bar on its own terms, stated verbatim in AI Prompts/investment-philosophy.md and memory/context/investment-strategy.md as three dimensions: right direction (big ambition, great strategy with growth as the priority, dynamic strategy across environments); right resources (great chief executive measured via action, hiring as top priority, well-resourced and well-invested business, holistic congruent culture, conservative guidance, clear communication); right record, action and behaviour (high return on newly invested capital, acts rather than talks, outlier standards, shareholders first, dynamic buybacks, prudent governance).

WHAT THIS SETUP IS NOT. (Written because the measured D/F rate of 52.9% suggests over-firing on the same class of confusion that broke RA8.) - A cheap stock is not a mis-modelled one. A low multiple is the market's PRICE, not its MODEL. Mis-modelling is about the earnings forecast being wrong, not the rating being low. - A well-covered stock is not automatically excluded. Heavy analyst coverage lowers the prior on mis-modelling and is worth stating, but coverage count is not itself the test — several memos graded D on analyst count alone, which is evidence, not a verdict. - Negative near-term revisions do not settle it. The setup is about where earnings GO, not where the last revision went. A negative revision cycle in a business that is structurally under-modelled is the setup, not its refutation. - A high-quality business that is correctly modelled is a C, not a D. It simply is not this setup. Reserve D and F for a case that fails the QUALITY bar, since that is the condition Richard will not compromise on.

WHERE A NON-FITTING FINDING MUST GO INSTEAD. Do not suppress it; route it and say so here.

Finding Owning home
Demand-driven upgrade rather than a modelling error Element E8 RA2 — Big demand change-driven earnings upgrade
Corporate-action-driven upgrade (guidance, buyback, disposal, new chief executive) Element E8 RA3 — Strong corporate change driven upgrade
Cheap multiple attached to deteriorating fundamentals Element E1 RA2 — Robust base?
Quality doubts about accounting, cash conversion or the earnings base Element E1 RA2 — Robust base?
Transmission mechanism cannot be traced at all Element E8 RA8 — False friend guardrail

CORE QUESTIONS. Both are mandatory; CQ1 is the mis-modelling test, CQ2 the quality gate.

  • CQ1: Is this a high-quality business whose forward earnings the market is demonstrably mis-modelling? Name the specific line consensus has wrong and the evidence that it is wrong. "Consensus looks low" without a named mechanism is not a pass.
  • CQ2: Is consensus demonstrably too low, and is the business high-quality on Richard's three dimensions? State the direction of recent revisions, and grade quality against right direction / right resources / right record explicitly. Both halves must hold: a mis-modelled low-quality business fails this Required Attribute and belongs elsewhere.

GRADE ANCHORS. A — both conditions plainly hold, the mis-modelled line is named and evidenced, quality is unambiguous. B — both hold, one with less evidence. C — one condition holds cleanly and the other is partial or unproven; also the correct grade for a high-quality business that is simply modelled correctly. D — the quality bar fails, or neither condition is evidenced. F — the premise is contradicted (consensus is demonstrably too HIGH, or quality fails on multiple dimensions).

RA8 [from IC#3 TC7] — Fit with false friend case setups? [NEGATIVE GUARDRAIL]

WHAT A FALSE FRIEND IS. Richard's definition, verbatim from memory/context/investment-strategy.md Pillar 2: a setup that looks good but has structural flaws, whose defining characteristic is zero clarity of the transmission mechanism from company actions to earnings per share. His test: "if Richard cannot clearly explain how strategy translates into financial outputs — and predict what tracking data will look like quarter by quarter — it's a false friend." Named historical examples: HBX (no clarity of tracking inputs to earnings), Adevinta (no "soul"), S4 (egotism in communication), Nexi (deep value but defensive strategy only). Plus "Pret sandwich risk" — nothing individually offensive, the collection fatal.

WHAT A FALSE FRIEND IS NOT. This is the load-bearing correction and it is where the guardrail has been over-firing. A weak business is not a false friend. A deteriorating business is not a false friend. A cheap multiple attached to falling margins is not a false friend. None of these is a false friend if the path from company action to earnings is perfectly traceable — they are simply a bad case, and other Elements own them. A false friend is defined by OPACITY, not by badness.

WHERE A NON-OPACITY FINDING MUST GO INSTEAD. Do not suppress the finding; route it to the Element that owns it, and say in RA8 that you have done so. At Triaging the in-scope destinations are:

Finding Owning home
Accounting or earnings-quality concern (capitalisation, adjusted-versus-reported gap, one-offs, off-balance-sheet items) Element E1 RA2 — Robust base?
Cash conversion, working-capital build, free-cash-flow divergence from earnings Element E1 RA2 — Robust base?
Cheap multiple plus deteriorating fundamentals Element E1 RA2 — Robust base? (and E3 valuation at ESA/Deep Dive)
Margin or volume decay driven by competitive or value-chain forces Element E11 — Value-chain dynamics
Too many concurrent moving parts Element E7 — Required case simplicity guardrails

THE RISKS REPORT IS AN INPUT, NEVER THE VERDICT. Where a Risks family report's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses names "false friend" as its winning hypothesis, that is evidence to be tested, not a finding to be reported. Three rules, all mandatory: 1. State what that report's false-friend finding actually rests on. If it rests on accounting, cash conversion or valuation rather than transmission opacity, say so, route it per the table above, and do NOT let it drive this grade. 2. Reach your own view on the opacity test independently, and say explicitly whether you agree with the report or not. 3. Never describe the Risks report as independent corroboration of your own finding. Its three-profile matrix ("no" / "false friend" / "yes") is Richard's own framework, so it is a second reading of the same idea, not a second witness. (Open verification item: whether the vendor would have run a standard Heuer competing-hypotheses analysis regardless has not been confirmed — but even if so, rule 1 still binds.)

CORE QUESTIONS. All four are mandatory. CQ1 and CQ2 are the test; CQ3 and CQ4 are the guards.

  • CQ1: Can the transmission mechanism be traced, link by link, with each link named? Use Richard's own chain, stated in memory/context/investing-system.md and in the Wisdom Library's False Friends entry: chief executive and strategy → operational execution → qualitative change → key fundamental milestones → financial statement outputs → earnings per share → share price. Walk it explicitly. If ANY link cannot be named, say which one and why — that is the finding. (This is also Richard's earnings-predictability checklist items 1 and 3.)
  • CQ2: Can the quarter-by-quarter tracking data that would confirm or refute this case be named in advance, and is it actually obtainable? Name the specific series. "The company reports segment revenue" is an answer; "results will show it" is not. (Checklist items 5 and 7.)
  • CQ3: Pret sandwich check — does the case rest on a collection of individually tolerable weaknesses that are fatal in combination? List them and assess the combination, not the items.
  • CQ4: If a source report names a false friend, what does its finding actually rest on? Apply the three Risks-report rules above and route anything that is not opacity.

GRADE ANCHORS. Grades remain relative percentiles against a named comparison set per the Viewforth scale (A = top 10%, B = 75–89%, C = 50–74%, D = 25–49%, F = bottom 25%). Name the comparison set and state the base rate. These anchors describe what each band typically looks like on the opacity test; they do not override the percentile system.

  • A / B — transmission unusually clear and fully instrumented. Every step nameable, tracking data specific and obtainable. This band is currently used on 4.5% of memos, which is itself evidence of skew — do not avoid it when the evidence supports it.
  • C — guardrail tested and not tripped. Transmission traceable end to end; tracking data nameable, if imperfect.
  • D — ONE leg fails. Either one step in the transmission cannot be named, OR the tracking data cannot be obtained. Not both. The case remains monitorable with a named, specific gap.
  • F — this IS a false friend. The transmission cannot be traced at all, or BOTH legs fail. Richard's four named historical examples all sit here.

MEASURED BASE RATE, for calibration against the corpus (17-Aug-26, 157 memos): A 0.0%, B 4.5%, C 23.6%, D 47.1%, F 24.2%. D is running at roughly double its expected share. If your grade is D, check that it is not simply the default.
