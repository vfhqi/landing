Part 1 — The Funnel (sections 1–4)

1. The 6-stage funnel

The investment research process moves an idea through six discrete stages. Each stage has its own primary purpose, its own target condition, and its own scope of analytical work. Cases that fail a stage's target condition are parked. Cases that clear a stage graduate to the next.

The fundamental shift from the old approach: stages are sequential filters, not deepening passes. In the old approach (pre-8-May-26), every stage covered the entire case but at greater depth — Richard called this "onion peeling." In the new approach, each stage focuses on its own specific elements and target condition, with cross-stage execution providing the cumulative coverage.

# Stage Roman Primary purpose Target condition (verbatim from xlsx)
1 Generation / screening I Qualify a case Require sufficient technical momentum
2 Filtering out II Filter out cases with obvious headwinds Plus … Avoid avoidable losses by side-stepping large and/or worsening 'exogenous' headwinds/risks
3 Triaging III Lightly triage a case's attractiveness (first A&J pass) Plus … Require large amounts of positive "change forces" (because stock markets react to change (2nd derivative) even more than the [vector])
4 ESA IV Broadly test a case's attractiveness (second A&J pass) Plus … (A) Remembering that "change forces" must manifest in the financials improving, too; not just the qualitative narrative. As measured by guidance beats and positive sell-side consensus earnings upgrade momentum (and ratings/price targets) drive algorithmic buying/selling, which then drives trend-following strategies. (B) That a sufficiently "bankable" foundation ("business quality") is needed for the stock market to extrapolate improving current momentum into the mid/long-term and drive large multiple expansion
5 Deep dive V Robustly probe a case's riskiness (third A&J pass) Plus … Avoid avoidable case failure and horrible return-on-time by being disciplined about (A) shunning mediocrity — in management quality, business quality, case predictability, case key driver trackability — and (B) 'weirder, worser, further (down), for longer' and 'cockroaches' type patterns; only backing simple, crisp, top decile/top quartile "A"/"B" investment cases
6 Live VI Mitigate / avoid / reduce risk of capital loss Use the defined "case components" and the monitoring of leading tracking indicators to assess monthly/more regularly if fundamentals investment case is tracking to plan or invalidated and to be exited

Key principle for stages I–V: target conditions are qualitative alignment language, not rated outcomes. Watson does not rate stage TCs. The APM rates dimensions inside each stage's scope, and the ratings determine whether to proceed (Q2 in alignment Q&A).

Stage VI (Live) is structurally different. It's not a research stage — it's the operating system for held positions. See section 12 for the full Live operating system.

Part 2 — The A&J Memo Architecture (sections 5–10)

5. The 6 master ratings

The master ratings are the 6 highest-level ratings for a case. They are the same subjects as the old P1–P6 pillars — only the name has changed (Richard, Round 1: "Only a change in nomenclature. The 6 'pillars' are now called 'master ratings'. They are the same subjects as before").

The 6 master ratings (these names are preserved verbatim from the existing strategy work):

# Master rating What it summarises
MR1 Technical momentum Needed for profitable risk/rewards
MR2 Sell-side momentum Needed for Stage 2 technical-momentum stocks to "keep going"
MR3 Thematic fit When long the right thematics, stock picking is easy; when not, analysing stocks is pointless (Richard's 10-year learning)
MR4 Pillar I — Case change elements Is there enough exciting change in the inputs and outputs to get investors excited and drive the SP up? Fundamental partner to technical momentum analysis
MR5 Pillar II — Case building blocks The investment change case + its bankability foundations
MR6 Pillar III — Investment case components The in-flight playbook + monitoring plan (Stage VI Live operational layer)

Master ratings = loosely-derived qualitative summaries (Q11, Watson recommendation, Richard confirmed Round 4). The APM uses element ratings as primary inputs but retains discretion to weight and override at the master rating level. They are informed by but not programmatically derived from the underlying element ratings. This avoids: - The rigidity of auto roll-ups (one bad element drags a master rating down arbitrarily) - The disconnect of fully independent ratings (which would make element ratings ornamental)

Master ratings are explicitly rated by APM at every stage from Triage onwards (D2). All Pillars + Groups + TCs + RAs + CQs are rated by APM when in scope. The rating model is hierarchical with explicit ratings at every layer.

6. Pillar I — Case change elements

Pillar I groups the 3 elements that capture whether the case has enough exciting change to drive the share price. The "change" thesis: stock markets react to change (the second derivative) more than to level (the first derivative). A case must have change forces to compound.

Pillar I contains:

  • E1 Required "input change forces" (internal/external)?
  • E2 Required case "outputs"? (financials must improve, not just qualitative narrative)
  • E3 Sufficient TSR / valuation upside?

These three are tightly linked: input change forces (E1) drive operational/strategic change → which manifests in case outputs (E2 — guidance beats, sell-side upgrade momentum) → which the market rewards via valuation upside (E3 — multiple expansion, TSR).

7. Pillar II — Case building blocks (5 groups)

Pillar II groups the 14 elements that capture the bankability foundations of the case — what makes the change forces extrapolable into the mid/long-term. Without bankable foundations, change forces fizzle out at near-term momentum without driving multiple expansion.

8. Pillar III — Investment case components

Pillar III is structurally different from Pillars I + II. It is the operational distillation of the case — the in-flight checklist (see plane analogy in section 9). Pillar III is built once Pillars I + II are sufficiently understood (post-DD), then Pillar III becomes the operating layer for Stage VI Live.

Pillar III breaks into 3 groups:

Group 1 — Position management playbook

Element scope: TBD — to be specified in Stream C (APM A&J SOP rewrite) when authoring the playbook structure for Pillar III. Concept: a short, simple, scannable playbook for managing the position in flight — entry rules, sizing rules, trim/add triggers, exit triggers. Avoids cognitive overload.

Group 2 — Monitoring plan (of leading tracking indicators)

Element scope: TBD — links directly to RR23 (the weekly Monitoring Plan RR; see section 12). Each key driver has a best leading tracking indicator (LTI). The monitoring plan is the schedule + structure for tracking those LTIs weekly.

Group 3 — Key drivers (Upstream + Downstream)

The case is decomposed into upstream (input) and downstream (output) drivers, each with specifications.

Upstream drivers (the inputs that drive the case): - #23 Key case assumptions / hygiene factors [foundations] — HW tag, 2 to 6 [tbd] - #24 Primary case drivers [input change forces, internal or external] — DW tag, 1 to 3 [tbd] - #25 Secondary case drivers [input change forces, internal or external] — NW tag, 2 to 4 [tbd] - #26 Key risks/fragilities [from any/all elements of the investment case] — QW tag, 3 to 5 - #27 Key positive optionality [input change forces, internal or external] — HW tag, 0 to 3 [tbd]

Downstream drivers (the outputs the inputs produce): - #28 Key financial metrics [financial outputs] — QW tag, 1 to 3 - #29 Key FSOs [financial outputs] — QW tag, 1 to 3 - #30 Key risks/fragilities [from any/all] — QW tag, 3 to 5 - #31 Peers/sector developments — DW tag, 1 to 3 - #32 Key technical developments — DW tag, 1 to 3

Per-component spec (xlsx columns I–O for each component): for every key driver, the framework requires: - I What specifically is required (general definition) - J The best associated leading tracking indicator (LTI) to monitor - K The number of key drivers to define and monitor (e.g. "1 to 3 [tbd]") - L What specifically we expect in our modal case - M What specifically is in guidance (explicit/inferred) - N What specifically does the sell side expect - O What specific threshold would be "invalidation"

This per-component spec is the architecture of the in-flight checklist. Once filled in for a stock, it becomes the live monitoring contract — Watson knows what to watch, what to expect, and when to flag invalidation.

Tag legend (column A in xlsx): - HW — hygiene/wider (background or wider context indicators) - DW — driver/wider (drivers requiring wider context) - NW — next-step/wider (secondary/derivative) - QW — quantifiable/wider (financial outputs / specific metrics)

9. The plane analogy

Richard's Round 2 explanation, paraphrased and developed:

The CASE is about CHANGE (Pillar I) on a foundation base (building blocks, Pillar II). These are the case ATTRIBUTES.

Case COMPONENTS (Pillar III) is a synthesis of all that qualitative judgement — distilled down into a position management playbook.

Plane analogy: - ATTRIBUTES (Pillars I + II) = a plane educational teaching manual - Pillar I = how it can fly (what it does) - Pillar II = what enables it to fly (building blocks — engine, etc.) - COMPONENTS (Pillar III) = very simple, short in-flight checklists / playbooks — for in-flight flying. Simple. Easy. Avoid cognitive overload. Crunches the 1000-page teaching manual into a handful of metrics/rules.

"Think of it as 'you need to research, analyse and judge Pillar I and II to be able to define Pillar III (components), then you can sort of discard all that learning you did' (just like a pilot probably never re-reads his teaching manuals once he's learnt how to fly the plane, he just focuses on the in-flight checklists again and again)."

Why this analogy matters operationally: 1. Pillar III is the high-leverage Stage VI artefact. Once a position goes live, the APM mostly works from the in-flight checklist (Pillar III), not the teaching manual (Pillars I+II). This drives cognitive efficiency. 2. Pillars I+II become reference-only post-DD. They get touched at major case re-evaluations (re-rating events, earnings surprises, thesis-challenging news), not at routine monitoring. 3. The Monitoring Plan RR (RR23) operates entirely off Pillar III. Weekly monitoring doesn't need to re-engage the teaching manual. 4. Position-management decisions are made from Pillar III. Trim/add/exit triggers live in the playbook, not in the attributes.

This is a load-bearing concept for the framework's higher-intent (efficiency / throughput). Without Pillar III, every monitoring action would require re-engaging the full case understanding — cognitively expensive. With Pillar III, monitoring is fast and repeatable.

10. The A&J memo writing SOP — per-row authoring matrix (load-bearing)

THIS IS THE LOAD-BEARING SECTION. Block B of the xlsx (rows 45–83) plus the Pillar III block (rows 85–125) IS the SOP for writing the A&J memo. The APM produces the memo by walking the rows of this matrix top-to-bottom AT EACH STAGE, and writing/updating the content for each row according to what the cell at the (row × stage column) intersection says.

This replaces the old SOP that said "C.II → A → D → C.I → B → E → F" within-section authoring order. The new SOP is stage-row-walking: at each stage, walk the matrix top-to-bottom and produce per the cell instructions.

10.1 — The A/B/C/D/E/F document spine survives

The A&J memo's document spine is preserved (D3): - A Financials - B Investment case — Executive summary - C Investment case "Attributes" (Pillars I + II) - D Investment case "Components" / Mechanics (Pillar III) - E Actions - F Appendices

What changes: the content within Sections C and D reorganises around Pillar I/II/III: - Section C = Pillar I + Pillar II - C.I — Master ratings table (the 6 master ratings + ratings cascade down to elements) - C.II.1 — Pillar I content (3 elements: E1, E2, E3) - C.II.2 — Pillar II content (5 groups: case momentum, case simplicity, core philosophy, case riskiness, case optionality) - Section D = Pillar III - D.I — Position management playbook - D.II — Monitoring plan (with leading tracking indicators) - D.III — Key drivers (Upstream + Downstream, per the per-component tabular spec in 8b)

Reading reference: HTRO V20 mockup — https://vfhqi.github.io/ratings/memo-style-sheet3-htro.html?v=20 is Richard's mental reference for the "Investment Case Attributes" memo writing visual.

10.2 — How to read the per-row matrix

  • Each row = a topic the APM addresses (executive summary, master ratings, a Pillar, a Group within a Pillar, an individual Element, or a Pillar III component-driver)
  • Each column = a stage of the funnel (I Gen → II Filter → III Triage → IV ESA → V DD → VI Live)
  • Each cell = the instruction for what the APM does to that topic at that stage

10.3 — Cell-instruction vocabulary

Cell value Instruction
(blank) Topic is n.a. (not applicable / not addressed) at this stage
"First pass" First time the APM writes this topic; produce the initial pass
"Second pass" The APM re-engages the topic with the new stage's research; updates with deeper writing
"Third pass" Third re-engagement, adding more depth
"Fourth pass" / "Fifth pass" Continue pattern (used for exec summary which gets re-written 5 times)
"First pass — light" First pass at a deliberately light depth (used for groups/pillars at early stages)
"Second pass — solid" Solid depth
"Third pass — robust" Robust / mature depth
"[no change]" The APM does not re-touch this topic at this stage (carry forward unchanged)
"Rated" A rating is produced (only used in Master ratings column where applicable)
"No" Explicitly do nothing at this stage

12. Stage VI Live — the operating system for held positions

This is the third major piece of new content (alongside the 6-stage funnel and the element-renumbering). It's a structured operational system, not just a research stage.

The 5-step Live operating system (per D5)

  1. APM determines the case components (Pillar III). The position management playbook, the monitoring plan structure, and the key drivers (Up + Down) are all defined by APM during ESA + DD work.

  2. Research determines the best leading tracking indicators (LTIs) for each driver. For each upstream and downstream driver, an LTI is identified (the per-component spec column J in Pillar III). LTIs are researched once per case, then become stable.

  3. Monitoring Plan RR (RR23) runs weekly. The weekly research report: - Searches for news on the stock - Searches for keywords related to drivers and the case - Pulls current values/movements of the LTIs - Outputs a structured weekly log (per-driver tracking status: positive / neutral / negative / approaching invalidation) - Logged to the Reports and Memo Repository

  4. Monthly Review Meeting — Richard + APM (Watson). Cadence: monthly, fixed. - Inputs: ~4 weekly monitoring reports per held stock from the prior month - Process: walk through each held stock — any drivers tracking negatively? Approaching invalidation thresholds? Confirmed exits? New positive optionality? - Output: per-stock decision (hold / trim / exit / increase monitoring frequency) - Logged to the Reports and Memo Repository as a monthly review note

  5. Exit triggers — the 10 INVALIDATION ACHs (see section 24) are the formal exit-trigger framework. When triggered, APM escalates to Richard with sell/trim recommendation.

Cadence summary

  • Weekly: RR23 monitoring run (research-side)
  • Monthly: review meeting (decision-side)
  • Event-triggered: earnings, news, sector shocks may force off-cycle review

Vocabulary for Live stage

  • Note = monitoring note (the output of the monthly review meeting + any off-cycle review)
  • RR23 = the weekly Monitoring Plan report (research-side input to the note)
  • Driver = an upstream or downstream key driver of the case (Pillar III, Group 3)
  • Leading tracking indicator (LTI) = the proxy/early signal for a driver, monitored weekly
  • Invalidation threshold = the specific level at which a driver is judged "broken" (per-component spec, column O)

19. The mediocrity-shunning discipline (Stage V)

From the Stage V (DD) target condition (S24 in V2 xlsx):

"Avoid avoidable case failure and horrible return-on-time by being disciplined about (A) shunning mediocrity — in management quality, business quality, case predictability, case key driver trackability — and (B) 'weirder, worser, further (down), for longer' and 'cockroaches' type patterns; only backing simple, crisp, top decile / top quartile "A"/"B" investment cases."

Three load-bearing principles here:

A. Mediocrity-shunning

Cases scoring C/D in management quality, business quality, case predictability, or driver trackability are parked, not progressed. The discipline is to stop investing in mediocre cases at Stage V — not to grind on improving mediocre judgments through more research.

B. Pattern recognition: "weirder, worser, further (down), for longer"

A pattern where a problematic case keeps surfacing new issues over time — each issue worse than the last, the timeline keeps extending, the share price keeps falling. The pattern signals: park immediately. This is cockroach behaviour (one cockroach in the kitchen → many).

C. Top decile / top quartile A/B only

The bar for progressing through Stage V is high. Only top-decile/top-quartile cases (A or B grade) clear DD. C/D/F grades park.

Operating implication: Stage V is where the framework most aggressively kills cases. The funnel is a filter; Stage V is the finest mesh.

20. The two-engine doctrine (Stages III + IV)

From the Stage IV (ESA) target condition (S15 in V2 xlsx):

"(A) Remembering that 'change forces' must manifest in the financials improving, too; not just the qualitative narrative. As measured by guidance beats and positive sell-side consensus earnings upgrade momentum (and ratings/price targets) drive algorithmic buying/selling, which then drives trend-following strategies. And (B), that a sufficiently 'bankable' foundation ('business quality') is needed for the stock market to extrapolate improving current momentum into the mid/long-term and drive large multiple expansion."

The doctrine: investment cases need two engines working together: - Engine 1 — Change momentum: drives near-term performance via earnings beats, sell-side upgrade momentum, algorithmic buying, trend-following - Engine 2 — Bankability: drives mid/long-term performance via market extrapolation of improving momentum into multi-year compounding, supporting multiple expansion

Both required. A case with strong change but weak bankability fizzles after near-term momentum exhausts. A case with strong bankability but no change has nothing to fuel multiple expansion. Both engines have to be present and reinforcing.

This doctrine is the central thesis test the new framework runs at every stage. The exec summary judgement (section 11) explicitly evaluates this: are both engines present?

Per Q24 (Round 1): "This has been the core before, but perhaps not clearly expressed." The new framework makes the doctrine explicit — it was always implicit but is now load-bearing.

21. The "broadly closed but ebb-and-flow" principle for the 32 elements (Q6)

The element framework is broadly closed — Watson treats E1–E32 as the universe of analytical questions. But:

  • The set will ebb and flow over time as Richard learns and refines
  • Watson is constrained from tinkering: changes require explicit "high value-add" justification
  • New elements added or existing ones removed must be deliberate decisions, not casual revisions

22. The "if it bubbles up" cognitive economy principle (D1)

This is a meta-principle about how the APM allocates attention.

Default: focus APM attention on key topics. Don't force time onto low-value re-rating.

Override: if work in progress surfaces a reason to update a low-value element rating, fine — update it. Don't suppress.

Why this matters: the framework's whole point is efficiency — moving cases through the funnel rapidly. Cognitive overhead is the enemy. The "if it bubbles up" principle preserves judgement quality (override allowed) while protecting throughput (default = don't force).

This connects to the plane analogy (section 9): once Pillar III is defined, the APM mostly works from the in-flight checklist. Re-engaging the teaching manual is expensive and rare.
