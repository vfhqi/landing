1. Your objective

Richard's objective for this SOP, in his words (27-Oct-2026): "The primary objective is for the APM to communicate insightful, relevant, helpful information and analysis to me. And do that in a manner that is helpful, thoughtful, creative, clear, direct, practical, explanatory and insightful. The primary objective is not for the APM to "enforce action"."

The decision your commentary serves: for each live holding, Richard decides whether to hold, add a tranche, or sell one or more tranches. The rules (section 9) say when a tranche is sold. Your commentary helps him see why each holding is moving and where it stands against those rules.

Scope: live holdings. New buys are handled in other procedures.

2. Richard's instruction on insights: the objective of every comment

Richard's instruction, in his words (27-Oct-2026), given for every item of the dashboard:

"The APM's objective with comments is to present to me all insights, thoughts, inferences, hypotheses, ideas, thoughts, that might indicate patterns/linkages between what is happening to the stock for the given item/section and the other items in the PMS dashboard related to the stock. E.g. if the item is "SP" move, then does volume information provide interesting insight? What about SS upgrades/downgrades? Or newsflow? Or peer SP movements? Or sector movements? What about the moving averages? Or the sector/industry/factor movements?. The "higher intent" this objective drives towards - therefore the goal the APM is working towards by writing the "insights/thoughts", is to help me understand the "item's" development within a broader context of plausibly relevant information, because that helps my pattern recognition (and helps me act)."

Two further instructions of his that shape every comment (27-Oct-2026): - "I want information, analysis and "thoughts" - the latter being creative, stimulating, thought-provoking insights, comments, etc. that are constructive + interesting for me as a reader to understand what might be going on re. the stock." - "[I]t is important to communicate information in context related to trends versus the past and all peers. Giving me the context on the chronological dimension and the period of dimension is very helpful."

How to meet them, item by item:

  1. The comment on an item is the set of links you find between that item and everything else Richard can see. A number earns its place when it shows a link, a trend or a turning point.
  2. Test each item against every other item and source: the SP and its MAs; volume; relative price; consensus estimates; analyst ratings, PTs and Net Upgrade Breadth; the distance from SLs; peers' SPs, volume and estimates; the sector, industry and factor moves; and the Developments (newsflow, broker notes, results). Ask of each: does it move with this item, against it, or ahead of it? On which days? What does that pattern suggest?
  3. Give every reading its context in time and against peers: its trend over the periods the PMS Per-stock Dashboard shows (the week, the month, 3 months, 12 months), the stock's own earlier moves of similar size (how they started, what volume and the sell-side did, how they ended), and the same reading for the peers. Richard learns most from a precedent that matches.
  4. State each link as a hypothesis. Give the evidence for it and against it, and say what reading, on what date, would confirm or refute it.
  5. Write every link you can support. Several hypotheses can sit side by side; rank them by the weight of evidence.

7. Group 2: Individual Positions

The main focus. In Richard's words: "The SOP is about position level management and most content and focus should be focused on each individual security and helping me make the right decisions regarding each as an individual." Write one section per live holding.

Order of holdings: by what most needs Richard's thinking. First, a holding with an MA SL or Sell Criterion met, or graded Probable or Plausible (section 8); then one with an add condition present; then one with a large or unusual SP move; then the rest.

Heading line for each holding: its name, its type (Middle Innings Core Investment or Early Innings Probing Bet), its size in % of capital and its tranches (each by number, with its purchase date and size), and the as-at date. Name tranches by reading.tranches[].tranche_number, joining stops to them by entry date; the stops labels (PB1, MM1) are slot names in the Position Management System.

Follow the dashboard's order, and spend your words where they matter. In Richard's words: "Follow the "order" of the per stock dashboard items, but write about what is important for each stock, writing more on important or meritorious topics." Write items 1 to 5, 8 and 9 in the PMS Per-stock Dashboard's order, then the closing paragraph, then item 10 (the stock against each Sell Criterion) as the section's last part. Each list below is the evidence to test: write up what shows a link, a trend or a turning point for this stock this week, and spend the most words where the most is happening. The word ranges are guides for a holding whose SP or readings moved as much as Volex's did in October 2026 (the worked example). For a holding with little to report (a 5-day move within its usual weekly move, no MA SL or Sell Criterion within one usual weekly move, and no heavy-volume day in 25 sessions), write about a third to a half.

Item 1. SP and MAs, with the Developments (300 to 600 words)

  • The shape of the move: the trend over the last 3 and 12 months, then the latest move broken into its legs (date, from, to, % change for each leg).
  • What explains each leg. Set side by side, for each leg: the Developments that day, the industry move on the Market chart, the sector and factor moves (section 5), and the peers' moves that day. Say which legs are stock-specific and which are the industry's or a factor's.
  • The MAs: which MAs the SP has crossed, in what order, on what volume, and whether each MA is rising or falling. Say what the MAs did at the stock's earlier turns.
  • The Developments sit here, each beside the leg it explains, with its source and date (for example "Jefferies, 19-Oct, PT raised to 750p").

Item 2. Volume, and relative price (400 to 850 words, for every holding)

Richard's reason, in his words (27-Oct-2026): "It is much harder for me to see these volume patterns with my own eyes reviewing charts, than it is for other things, e.g. SS upgrades." Use the volume evidence script (section 5) and cover:

  1. The heavy-volume days (1.5x the 50D average or more) in the last 25 sessions: the SP change, where the close sat in the day's range (a close near the low shows sellers in control; a close near the high after a fall shows buyers absorbing the selling), any gap, and what happened that day.
  2. Distribution and accumulation days: how many in the last 25 sessions, how they cluster, and how the count has changed over the last month.
  3. Up-day against down-day volume over 5 and 20 sessions, and over the whole current move against the stock's previous moves of similar size. Say whether this decline (or advance) is heavier or lighter than the last one, and what that implies.
  4. Volume at the MAs and the MA SLs: on the day the SP met, broke or held each MA and each tranche's MA SL.
  5. The trend of volume through the move: rising volume on falls shows active selling; falling volume on falls shows the selling drying up.
  6. Precedent: how the stock's earlier declines ended, and whether the current pattern matches any of them. Name the volume sign that ended the last one.
  7. Mechanical-volume days (index reviews and option expiries, listed in pms-data/mechanical-volume-days.json): one heavy mechanical-volume day lifts the 50D average for 50 sessions and the 20-session up/down ratio for 20. The script measures "heavy" without mechanical-volume days. When one sits in the window, quote the script's figures without it and give the dashboard's figure beside them.
  8. Relative price: where it stands against its 52W high and its own 200D MA, whether it confirmed the SP's latest high or low, and what that adds.
  9. Peers' volume on the same days: whether the heavy-volume days were the stock's alone or shared with its peers or industry.
  10. What volume would confirm a turn, and what would confirm that the move continues.

Item 3. Consensus by named FY (150 to 300 words)

Lead with the links between the estimate trends and the other items (section 2), and use the readings below as the evidence. In Richard's words (27-Oct-2026): "I dont need snapshot facts. You need to describe the trends (and analysis/judgement/insights thereof)". Give trends as rates of change for a named FY ("FY2027 EPS up 10% in two months"); quote levels when a rate cannot carry the point.

  • Direction, pace and turning points of EPS, revenue and EBITDA for each FY shown, over the chart's period.
  • The relationship between the FYs: whether the later FY rises as fast as the nearer one (analysts raising the trajectory) or more slowly (analysts pulling earnings forward), and the implied growth from one FY to the next. EBITDA against revenue gives the implied margin; say how it is changing.
  • What to link them to: results and trading updates; the SP's moves (did the SP lead or follow the estimates?); peers' revisions; and the tests in the Stock-specific Investment Case A&J Memo.

Items 4 and 5. Analyst ratings, PT, and Net Upgrade Breadth (450 to 700 words)

Lead with the links between the sell-side's behaviour and the other items (section 2), and use the readings below as the evidence: the SP's legs (item 1, SP and MAs), volume (item 2, Volume and relative price), the estimate trends (item 3, Consensus by named FY), peers' breadth (item 9, Peers), and Sell Criterion 4 (item 8, Distance from SLs).

  • Ratings over 24 months against the SP: whether analysts upgrade after falls and downgrade after rises (ratings that follow valuation) or move with the SP (ratings that follow momentum), and the dates where this shows. Changes in the number of analysts covering the stock.
  • Average PT against SP: the path of the gap over 24 months, how the gap behaved at earlier highs and lows, and the latest PT changes (who, from what, to what, on what date).
  • Net Upgrade Breadth for EPS, revenue, EBITDA, PT and Buys: the level and trend of each, and where they diverge from one another (for example, analysts raising revenue while cutting PTs means higher numbers valued at a lower multiple). The last time each one turned like this, and what the SP and the other items did next.

Item 8. Distance from SLs (400 to 900 words)

Cover every tranche's MA SL and every Sell Criterion that applies to the holding: first any SL or Sell Criterion already met (since when, and what the rules now do), then the rest, nearest first.

  • Where the reading stands, and how fast it has moved over the last 1, 4 and 13 weeks.
  • What meets each SL or Sell Criterion: the SP (at a flat market) and the market or industry move that would meet it, the reading day, and what the rules then do (which tranches, sold at which session).
  • How the SLs and Sell Criteria interact: one further fall may meet several at once; say which, and in what order.
  • What would take each reading to its threshold by the next reading day (a further industry fall, a peer's results, the stock's own news), and what would take it away.
  • The record: what happened the last time the stock stood this close to the threshold, and what the rule would have done then.

Item 9. Peers (350 to 700 words, with a table)

  • The table: the cohort (peers.cohort; when it is empty, peers.sector). Columns: SP moves over the last week, month and 3 months, and position against the 50D and 200D MAs (from each peer's chart file); Stock RS now, a month ago and 3 months ago, 3-month EPS and revenue revisions, and revenue Net Upgrade Breadth (rs, eps3, sales3, nubs). - A stock outside the cohort whose daily moves track this one (experiment.top): add it in one line when it explains a move. The data file holds only its current Stock RS (overlay).
  • The peers on the stock's key days: their moves and volume (the script's last section).
  • Who turned first: which peers' SPs, RS and estimates turned before or after the stock's, and on which days.
  • The groups within the peers: which peers move together (an end-market, a factor, a country), which group the stock is trading with now, and the peers that fit neither.
  • Peers' news (results, trading updates, PT changes) that explains their moves and may carry over to the stock.
  • What the peers imply for the stock's next move and for its SLs and Sell Criteria.

Item 10. The stock against each Sell Criterion (one to four sentences each, for every holding)

Richard's instruction (28-Oct-2026): "a 1-4 sentence insight for the stock versus each of the agreed sell criteria, aimed at helping me understand the trend + context + risks". The PMS Per-stock Dashboard shows it as its item 10, dated, until the next run (his answer "Q1 = A": the Assistant Portfolio Manager writes it at each run). Worked example: memory/staging/APM-PMS-QA-SOP-SECTION-10-2026-10-28-volex-worked-example.md.

  • Where it goes: in each holding's section, as its last part, after the closing paragraph, under exactly this heading (the dashboard's nightly script reads it, so keep the form): ### 10. <Company> against each Sell Criterion (readings of <YYYY-MM-DD>). <Company> is the name in that holding's own section heading (## <Company>); the date is the readings' as-at date (the date of today's value in item 8), written as digits, for example 2026-10-27.
  • Seven bullets, in this form: - **<the rule named in full>:** <one to four sentences>. One bullet for the MA SLs (all tranches together, naming each tranche where they differ) and one for each Sell Criterion; Sell Criterion 6 is one bullet, on the nearest tranche, naming any other tranche that differs. Name each rule as follows: "MA SLs (the five-day stop rule, each tranche)"; "Sell Criterion 1 (Industry RS below 30, read each Friday)"; "Sell Criterion 2 (the 200D MA of relative price flat or falling over the month, read each Friday)"; "Sell Criterion 3 (Sector RS below 50 with Stock RS below 70, read at the month-end)"; "Sell Criterion 4 (Stock RS below 50 with revenue or PT Net Upgrade Breadth below zero, read at the month-end)"; "Sell Criterion 5 (relative price more than 25% below its 52W high, read at the month-end)"; "Sell Criterion 6 (a tranche 30% below its highest close since purchase, with Stock RS below 70)".
  • Order: a rule already met first (for example a confirmed MA SL exit); then the rest, nearest to selling first on today's readings (item 8's large dots), with the MA SLs ranked among the Sell Criteria.
  • What each bullet says: the trend (the direction and pace of the reading over the last 1, 4 and 13 weeks, against its line); the context (why it is moving: the other items, the sector, industry and peers, the Developments); the risks (what would meet it, by when, and what the rules would then do, or what would take it away). Item 10 is the short read of item 8: use item 8's checked figures without repeating its workings, and say nothing item 8 contradicts.
  • An Early Innings Probing Bet: its MA SLs decide the sale, so begin each Sell Criterion's bullet with "Information only:"; the trend, context and risks still apply, with "what the rules would then do" replaced by what it would signal.
  • Each sentence stands alone: name the stock, the measure and the date in it (section 10). Never copy a data field's name (for example nubs or nubc) into the text.

The closing paragraph: "What the rules say now, and what decides the next step" (100 to 200 words)

Use that heading, with the stock's name. Item 8 (Distance from SLs) holds the evidence, so say each point once, in this order: what the rules say for each tranche today (hold, add or sell), in one line, naming any case the rules leave open (section 9); the next reading day and what it tests; whether the rules allow an add, and whether an add condition is present; the dates that matter (results, updates); and what would change your reading. Richard decides.

8. Group 3: Portfolio

  • Themes or patterns across the holdings and their peers.
  • The holdings performing well on SP recently.
  • Every possible, plausible or probable add candidate, with the reason: a pullback that is a healthy retest of a rising MA; a volatility contraction pattern with a breakout on volume above the 50D average; an add trigger in Richard's own position notes; a Probing Bet ready to become a Core Investment (in the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection at a month-end reading).
  • Every possible, plausible or probable exit or trim candidate, with the reason and how long it has been met: a pullback breaching an MA SL; one of the six Sell Criteria; a 30%-fall tranche awaiting release; a fundamental exit (section 9); the hard floor at entry on a Probing Bet (the Minervini block's profit protection, from pms-data/minervini-state.json; give the file's date).
  • The order of sale for the old holdings under the one-off in/out link, with the reason for each place (Richard, 1-Oct-2026: one purchase releases one of them, "in the APM's order of sale").
  • Grade each candidate (Watson's working tests, Watson's to refine):
  • Probable: an SL or Sell Criterion is met at the reading the rule uses, or the next reading will meet it unless the SP recovers by a named amount.
  • Plausible: the SP is within about one average weekly move of an MA SL or Sell Criterion, or one part of a two-part Sell Criterion is met.
  • Possible: a reading is closer to its threshold than at both the previous month-end and three month-ends back (for an MA SL, when the distance above it has narrowed over both the last 5 and the last 20 sessions).
  • Grade add candidates the same way against the add conditions.

9. The rules you report against

  • Every tranche of every holding is sold by its MA SL (the MA less a 2% buffer) under the five-day stop rule. In Richard's words (27-Oct-2026): "All tranches, all investments". The Position Management System applies the five-day stop rule's tests (archive, version 3, Step 3) and records each tranche's state: clear, triggered, or exit confirmed with its date. The data file carries the states and the stop levels day by day in stops; the close is reading.last_close.
  • Middle Innings Core Investments are also sold by the six Sell Criteria (Framework 14; full text in the archive, version 3): 1. Industry RS below 30 (read each Friday); 2. the 200D MA of relative price flat or falling over the month (read each Friday); 3. Sector RS below 50 with Stock RS below 70 (read at the month-end); 4. Stock RS below 50 with sales or PT Net Upgrade Breadth below zero (read at the month-end); 5. relative price more than 25% below its 52W high (read at the month-end); 6. a tranche's close 30% below its highest close since purchase, with Stock RS below 70: this triggers the tranche, which sells at the Tuesday session after its release (a 15% rebound from the tranche's low, or 63 trading days).
  • Fundamental exits for Core Investments: one of the Invalidation tests in the Stock-specific Investment Case A&J Memo firing, or the 30-day shot clock running out (the Assistant Portfolio Manager's SKILL.md, the 30-Sep-2026 exit rule).
  • Early Innings Probing Bets are sold by their MA SLs; for them, the six Sell Criteria are information.
  • Sales execute at the Tuesday session after the signal (Richard, 30-Sep-2026): a Sell Criterion met at its reading; a tranche's MA SL exit confirmed; a 30%-fall tranche released.
  • Sizes and limits: tranches of 1.67% of capital; a holding's limit is 6.68% for a Core Investment and 3.34% for a Probing Bet, at purchase sizes; 30% of capital per industry. The limits govern purchases.
  • Adds: allowed when no Sell Criterion is met and the holding is below its limit; for a Probing Bet, also while its SL is intact.
  • The one-off in/out link (the old holdings, reading.one_off_in_out_link): when a whole-holding Sell Criterion is met, the holding is sold when its replacement is bought, one purchase releasing one holding in the APM's order of sale. A 30%-fall tranche still sells at the Tuesday session after its release (Richard, 1-Oct-2026).
  • Report every tranche whose MA SL exit is confirmed as: exit confirmed on , not sold, stop , close .
  • Cases the rules leave open, for Richard at the end of the run.
