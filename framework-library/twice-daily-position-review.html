Purpose

Close the loop between what Richard's trading rules say his positions should be and what they actually are. Two conversations a day, in chat, anchored on the Position Management System.

The higher intent, in Richard's words: get position sizes in line each day with the trading rules for adding capital and stop-losses for tranches of capital, and have the Assistant Portfolio Manager ensure it happens — tell him what to do, check he has done it, and chase him about it.

Drift is the enemy. Positions diverge from the rules through inaction, not decision: a stop that should have moved up, a tranche that should have been added on a pullback, a size that no longer matches intent. Drift is invisible day to day and expensive over months.

He asked for this to apply the vast majority of the time, not just when convenient. It applies to every line of every run, including headers, freshness reporting and open-items banners, not only the stock bullets.

Rule B — Every claim must be checkable. No unsupported assertions.

Richard's ruling, 04-Aug-2026: "The analysis needs to be more rigorous - these errors shouldn't happen."

The trigger case, and the lesson is subtler than it first looks. The 04-Aug run said Hexatronic's "pattern of lows has been getting steadily worse". Richard challenged it: the stock had just made a higher low. Checking the actual price series settles it, and both were right about different things:

  • Measured as swing lows (a low that is the lowest within five sessions either side), the last five were 42.18, 42.02, 39.85, 35.10 and 32.87 — five consecutive lower lows. The claim was defensible on that measure
  • Measured on the most recent daily lows, 30 July was 35.48 and 3 August was 36.93 — a higher low, exactly as Richard said
  • The two disagree because a swing low cannot be confirmed until five sessions have passed after it. The measure is structurally blind to the most recent week

So the defect was not sloppiness. It was an unstated measure and an unstated window, presented in the present perfect tense as though it described the current state. A rule saying "be more rigorous" would not have caught it. These rules do:

  1. Name the measure. "Its swing lows are falling" and "its daily lows are falling" are different claims. Say which one you are making
  2. Name the window, and the as-of date. "Over the last five swing lows, the most recent of which was 14 July"
  3. Say whether the most recent data confirms or contradicts the trend. If a measure lags, say by how much and what has happened since. A lagging measure stated in the present tense is a defect, not a shortcut
  4. Quote the numbers. A trend claim without its underlying series is unverifiable and therefore not allowed. Richard cannot challenge what he cannot see
  5. Where two measures disagree, report the disagreement. It is a finding, not a problem to be tidied away. The disagreement above is more informative than either measure alone
  6. Never assert a direction from a single observation. One session is not a trend
  7. If the data does not support a claim, do not make it. "I cannot tell from this data" is a valid and useful answer

The standing rule underneath all seven: verify before asserting. Check the actual series before characterising it. This is the canonical rulebook's own standard and it binds here without exception.

Rule B8, and it is the one most likely to bite silently: MISSING DATA IS NOT GOOD NEWS

A test that cannot run has not passed. Every health check in this SOP is phrased as "does this fail", so a measure with no data answers "no" and the holding reads as healthy. It is not healthy; it is unmeasured, and those are opposite findings.

Measured on the live book, 04-Aug-2026: Bally's Intralot returns nothing for nine of eleven measures — no relative strength, no cohort rank, no volume tests, no lows counts, no candle quality. It therefore silently passes every quality gate in this document while sitting 1.11% from its stop, which is the second-tightest position in the book.

So, on every run:

  1. Before judging a holding, check which measures actually returned a value. A holding with a thin data set gets that stated in its opening line, not buried
  2. Never write a positive or neutral verdict off an absence. "No distribution days" is a finding; "no distribution-day data" is a gap, and the two must never be phrased alike
  3. Say explicitly what cannot be judged and what that means for the position. For Bally's: the stop distance is known and trustworthy, and almost nothing else is
  4. If a holding that previously carried data stops carrying it, escalate. One name falling back is a known gap; a second is a build problem

Rule B8b, the MIRROR of B8: an absent number is sometimes the STRONGEST reading

B8 says missing data must never read as healthy. The inverse also happens, and it bit on 11-Aug-2026: healthy data read as MISSING.

utr_updown_ratio_5d is up-day volume divided by down-day volume. When there were no down-volume days at all, the denominator is zero and the producer returned a bare null. That null is not a computation gap — it is the strongest bullish reading the field can give. On 11-Aug it fired on three live holdings at once: Hilton Food after four straight up days, Theon up 9%, and Xvivo up 15.5%. All three were reported to Richard as a probable defect. Universe-wide it was null on 63 of 982 names, 6.4%.

The mirror case is just as misleading in the other direction: a clean-looking 0.0 means no UP days, which is maximally bearish, not neutral. D'Ieteren carried 0.0 on 11-Aug after five down sessions.

Since 12-Aug-2026 the reason travels with the number. utr_updown_ratio_5d_note and utr_updown_ratio_note state which case applies, and the alignment engine fills in the definition if a build has not yet written them.

So: before calling any absent number a defect, ask what the producer does when the measure is undefined. An undefined ratio and a failed computation look identical in the output and are opposite findings. Read the note field; if there is none, say the reason is unknown rather than assuming either way.

On lows specifically, quote BOTH counts. The engine ships both for this reason.

lower_lows_count and higher_lows_count both exist. Quoting one without the other is exactly the failure above. On Hexatronic the engine reads 5 lower lows and 1 higher low — the whole argument in two numbers, and reporting only the first is how the wrong impression was created.

Say both, then say which is more recent. "Five successively lower swing lows, but the most recent swing low is higher than the one before it, and the daily low on 3 August was above 30 July's" is a complete and honest statement. Anything less is a partial one.

Where a mechanical check is possible, run it rather than eyeballing the chart

The price series is on disk. A claim about lows, highs, gaps or streaks can be computed in seconds and should be, rather than inferred from a moving-average reading. Two guards learned on 04-Aug:

  • Test the claim over more than one window. The Hexatronic swing-low result was re-run over 100, 140 and 250 sessions and held identically in all three. A finding that only appears in one window is a finding about the window
  • Prefer a second, independent source for the same fact. The hand computation above was checked against the engine's own counts and agreed exactly. Two sources agreeing is worth far more than one source stated confidently

Session digests — added 14-Aug-2026, Richard's ruling

The problem this solves. Every run does real analytical work — freshness reads, reconciliations, judgement calls like "Arteche is still dicey" — and until now none of it survived past the session that produced it. A fresh run either re-derived the same reasoning from scratch or missed it entirely. Richard's instruction, 14-Aug-2026: write a digest every session, read the last ten days of them automatically, optimised for Watson to read cheaply, not for Richard to read at all.

This is a different artefact from the end-of-session Notion page below. This digest is Watson-facing and dense — token-efficient shorthand, not prose. The Notion page is Richard-facing and readable. They serve different readers and are written separately; do not try to merge them.

Where it lives: memory/skills/apm-pms-alignment/digests/YYYY-MM-DD-HHMM-{morning|evening}.md — one file per run, per Richard's answer ("Q1 = Yes") to running this per-session rather than per-calendar-day. A day with both a morning and an evening run produces two files.

What it contains — dense, not prose, but judgement is the point, not just data. Richard's explicit instruction ("Q3 = Key is the judgements/insights, too") means this is NOT simply a copy of the numbers already sitting in holdings-coverage.json or evening.json — the engine outputs are already on disk and cheap to re-read; duplicating them into the digest would waste the exercise. What the digest captures instead:

  1. The verdicts a re-read of raw data wouldn't hand you back. "Arteche still dicey, today's move tilts it negative" is a judgement, not a fact — a fresh run reading tonight's price alone wouldn't reconstruct that framing without also reading Richard's own notes.
  2. Anything flagged as unresolved, watched, or provisional, and why — so a future run doesn't silently drop a thread Richard would expect carried.
  3. Any data-quality finding, one line each, with a pointer to the fuller write-up rather than the full explanation repeated.
  4. One line of raw context per name that had anything happen — ticker, the number, nothing more. This is the part closest to "data," kept minimal on purpose.

Reading on start — every run, before Step 1. Read every digest file dated within the last 10 calendar days, oldest first. Track the cost, per Richard's instruction ("Q2 = I don't know, keep it lean and let's track") — there is no fixed token budget yet; instead, note the combined byte size of the last-10-days digest set at the top of tonight's own digest, so the trend is visible over time and a future session can decide whether a cap or a trim is warranted, mirroring how this file's own 138KB/140KB size-hook was arrived at.

Step 0 — Read the coverage gate BEFORE anything else

python3 <COWORK>/scripts/check_holdings_coverage.py

It also runs on the nightly build and leaves its verdict in pms-data/holdings-coverage.json, so reading that file is equivalent when you want last night's state.

What it answers: is every stock Richard actually holds present, and current, in every dataset this SOP reads — price, chart bars, peer group, Stage 2 history, cohort, cache — and is any slot holding capital with no ticker attached.

Why it exists, and why it is Step 0 rather than a footnote. Three incidents in five days had one cause: every dataset here is built for the UNIVERSE, or hand-built ONCE, and nothing ever asked whether the stocks Richard OWNS were covered by all of them.

  • 07-Aug, Flutter's dead series masked a fired stop
  • 11-Aug, Flutter again: 11-day-stale price, no peer group, no Stage 2 history, no cohort
  • 11-Aug, D'Ieteren entered the book at 17:56 and had no peer group by 18:00

Each was found by a human noticing an odd line partway down a long report, after the analysis had been written on top of it. A coverage failure invalidates the commentary built on it, so it has to be known before the commentary is written, not after.

How to report it:

  • GREEN — say so in one line and move on. Do not pad it.
  • AMBER — a warning, usually a venue still open at build time or a pending entry. State it where it bears on the stock, not as a headline.
  • RED — lead the note with it, above the open-items banner. Name the holding, the dataset and what it costs the reader. A missing peer group means that holding's peer table cannot run at all; say that plainly rather than letting the section come back empty.
  • An orphan slot is the highest severity finding in the whole SOP. Capital with no instrument means a position exists in the book that no price, no stop and no verdict can be computed for. Report it first, before the portfolio judgement.
  • Exit 2 means the guard could not run. That is not a pass. Treat an unreadable guard as RED and say the coverage question is unanswered this run.

Two signals the gate carries that no other check in this document covers:

pipeline_freeze — answers "is the whole price file behind the WORLD?", where every other freshness test only asks "is this holding behind the others?". Both are needed. A build that stops running leaves every holding perfectly consistent with every other holding, so the per-stock checks all pass. Measured 12-Aug-2026: before this existed, a price file frozen for a full year still reported "every holding covered and current". A FAIL here invalidates every price statement in the note — say that before quoting a single number. An AMBER of one weekday is normal before the evening build.

slot_rebinds — a PMS slot that changed identity while keeping its tranche. Slots are reusable and re-pointing one is legitimate, but it leaves no trace: on 11-Aug slot 15 went from Flutter to D'Ieteren keeping the same PB1 size and stop, so pms-state.json now holds no record that Flutter was ever in that slot, no exit date and no exit price, and the original 30-June entry date was overwritten. Every other guard is blind to it because the resulting state is perfectly valid. When a rebind is reported, say so plainly rather than presenting the new stock as though it had always been there, and note that the prior holding's exit is not recorded. Only the daily auto-backup preserves the old binding, one snapshot per day, so two re-points between backups lose the middle one.

Do not repair anything from inside this SOP. The gate reports; fixing peer groups, cohorts or price identity is Systems Architect work and goes to the plumbing list at the end of the note.

Step 1 — Check the data is fresh enough to trust

Run the engine and read its header before writing a word of commentary:

python3 <COWORK>/scripts/apm_pms_alignment.py --evening

Resolve <COWORK> first: it is the mount directory containing Files/ and memory/. It varies per session.

Three freshness gates. State the result of each in the output, do not skip them silently.

  1. price_data.generated — the nightly master-dashboard build, ~18:00-18:10 UK. If it is not from today (or the last trading day), say so plainly and label every price as of its actual date. Never present stale prices as current.
  2. state_saved_at — when the Position Management System last saved. If it is more than a few days old, Richard may have traded without saving, and every size in the report is suspect. Say so.
  3. price_date per stock — individual stocks can lag the build. Report any that differ from the majority.

The fourth freshness question, and it outranks the other three: is a STOP VERDICT wrong?

A stale price is not merely imprecise. It can give the wrong answer to the only question that matters: is this position stopped out.

Measured on 04-Aug-2026: 14 of 16 holdings had a more recent closing price in the chart series than the price their stop distances were computed from, because continental exchanges lag the nightly build. On that day Volex was reported IN BREACH at 0.24% below its stop on a 31 July price, and was 0.73% CLEAR on its 3 August close. It was carried on the open-items register as urgent. That is a wrong verdict on a live position, not a rounding difference.

The engine now reports this. Read stop_verdict_conflicts on every run and act on it:

  1. If the list is non-empty, lead with it — above the open-items banner, above everything. It means a stop verdict in this very report is contradicted by a more recent price
  2. State both, plainly, and do not pick a winner. "On the 31 July close this tranche is 0.24% below its stop. On Monday 3 August's close, which the price build has not yet picked up, it is 0.73% above it. I cannot tell you which is current; the position may not be stopped at all"
  3. Never silently prefer either price. The older one remains the basis for the numbers, by a deliberate 03-Aug-2026 ruling against creating a third price path. The conflict is reported, never resolved by Watson
  4. Any breach in conflict is provisional. Do not chase Richard to sell a position whose breach may not exist. Say it is unresolved and why
  5. Where a holding has a fresher bar but no verdict flips, say so once at portfolio level — "13 holdings carry prices two to five days old, so anything within a few percent of a stop is provisional" — rather than repeating it on every stock

This is a reporting rule, not a data fix. The underlying cause was root-caused and fixed by another session on 04-Aug-2026, and tonight's build should begin closing the gap. Until a run shows no conflicts, keep making the check.

Step 2 (FIRST RUN OF THE UK DAY only) — Fetch and reconcile the UBS valuation

Richard's ruling, 12-Aug-2026, superseding the original "morning only" gate. This step is no longer tied to the morning/evening label. It runs on whichever alignment run is FIRST for the UK calendar day — morning or evening, whichever happens to come first — and is SKIPPED on any second or third run the same day. Check this before anything else in Step 2, before even checking whether a UBS email exists:

python3 <COWORK>/scripts/ubs_fetch_ledger.py --should-fetch

It prints FETCH: ... or SKIP: ... with a plain-English reason and exits 0 or 1 to match. If it says SKIP, do not attempt the fetch at all. State one line in the output confirming the valuation was already pulled earlier today, who last fetched it (the ledger's last_fetched_at_uk) and which trading day it covered, then move straight to Step 3 — this mirrors exactly how Step 9 reports its own once-a-day guard, for the same reason: silence would read as "nothing to report" rather than "already done."

If it says FETCH, run the full sub-step below, then mark it done as the very last action:

The market-relative trap. Read this before quoting any market figure.

Two different fields exist and they measure different things. Confusing them puts a false statement in front of Richard. Verified against the source code on 04-Aug-2026:

Field What it actually measures How to describe it
rs_composite Relative strength against the STOXX Europe 600 index, over 3, 6, 9 and 12 months weighted 0.4 / 0.2 / 0.2 / 0.2 This is the true market-relative figure. "Against the STOXX Europe 600 index"
rs_excess_market Relative strength against the equal-weighted average of the roughly 978-stock universe — not an index "Against the average stock in the universe". Never call this market-relative or index-relative

Why the distinction is real and not pedantic. The index term inside rs_composite is subtracted identically from every stock, so it cancels out completely when the universe mean is taken. What survives in rs_excess_market is purely stock-versus-universe-average. The dashboard's own column header reads "vs Mkt", which is wrong. Do not inherit that error.

Both are legitimate and they answer different questions. Use rs_composite for "how is this doing against the market", and rs_excess_market for "how is this doing against a typical stock in my universe". Name whichever you use, in full, every time.
