§0.5. QUALITY OF RATINGS — How IA and APM Produce Defensible Ratings [Phase G v2, 12-May-26]

Why this section exists at the top of APM SKILL.md: Ratings are the operational currency of Richard's investment system. Every IA memo produces ~283 ratings (CQ + RA + Element + Group + Pillar + Master + Case-level). APM consumes those ratings for portfolio integration. Richard makes capital decisions on case-level ratings. A bad rating chain = bad investments. This section synthesises the rating-quality discipline that prior sections only referenced in fragments.

§0.5.3 — How APM stress-tests IA's ratings

IA produces ratings. APM receives them in the memo + GNG CHECKS handoff. APM's quality role is to stress-test before integrating into portfolio context. Four standing tests, applied in order:

1. Concept B test — go to the most-different viewpoint. For each pillar-level rating IA committed, APM asks: "What's the strongest argument that this rating should be one grade lower?" If APM can construct that argument easily, the rating is over-stated. If APM cannot construct it after 5 minutes of stewing, the rating is robust.

2. Cohort consistency test. If the stock is part of an active cohort (e.g., Cables theme PRY/NKT/NEX/HTRO), APM compares the IA rating against cohort peers' ratings at the same stage: - Are the relative rankings within cohort consistent with what APM would expect? - If HTRO rated B+ and NKT rated C+, is the differential structural (different business quality, different paradigm fit) or arbitrary? - If arbitrary, the cohort hot wash (Phase 4.6) is the place to resolve

3. Archetype consistency test. APM checks coaching/stock-archetypes.md for the closest archetype match (Liquidity Trap / Cockroach / Consolidation Play / Cyclical / Demand-Driven EPSU / Corporate Change EPSU / etc.). For that archetype, what's the typical Pillar I/II/III rating profile? If IA's rating profile diverges materially from archetype, APM probes: - Has IA correctly identified the archetype? - If yes, what makes this case different from archetype norm? - If no, IA needs to re-archetype and re-rate

4. Pret Sandwich test (mediocrity-accumulation check). Per Richard's discipline: no single rating may be fatal, but the collection of C/D ratings can be. APM scans the C.I.1 ratings table: - Count of C ratings: if >40% of in-scope ratings are C, the case is in mediocrity territory regardless of any individual B/A - Count of D ratings: even 2-3 D ratings flag the Cockroach Rule — assume 2-3 more in dark spaces - If both: APM recommends Park at Step 4 even if IA recommended Progress

These 4 tests are APM's stress-test discipline. APM doesn't author the memo; APM CHALLENGES the memo. Quality of A&J + ratings improves when both roles do their job — IA authors with rigour, APM challenges with rigour.

§0.5.4 — What Richard looks for at Step 4

At Step 4 of STAGE PROGRESSION SOP (the weekly review meeting), Richard reviews IA memo + APM stress-test + GNG CHECKS. He makes the case-level decision. The disciplines he applies:

1. Hell Yeah or No filter (per richard-investing-approach.md) At ESA: if honest summary is "interesting but not Hell Yeah," → Park signal. Most ESA exits should be Parks; only outlier confluence cases progress. A B+ rating without Hell-Yeah energy = Park, not Progress.

2. Track-record-lens parallel (per APM SKILL.md NON-NEGOTIABLE) Richard reads for the historical parallel. "Your Fasadgruppen experience (peak 11.47%, held 34 months, lost 70 bps) shows what happens when liquidity traps aren't exited early." If IA didn't cite a parallel, Richard does it himself and adjusts accordingly.

3. Painful Prudence 9-10/10 conviction bar (per investment-strategy.md) Only own stocks at 9/10 to 10/10 conviction. Opportunity cost of 7/10 capital is too high. Ratings A/B = potentially Hell-Yeah territory; rating C = Painful Prudence flag (capital better deployed elsewhere); ratings D/F = kill. Richard recommends accordingly.

4. Big Change Case + Bankability test (at ESA) (per V3 framework + 09-May reframe) Do we have both Big Change Case (Pillar I — change momentum) AND Bankability (Pillar II — foundations, esp. BB#2)? Are they reinforcing? If either is missing, the case fizzles after near-term momentum or has nothing to fuel multiple expansion. Both required.

5. Calibration log signal If Richard's decision diverges from IA's rating, APM logs to memory/apm/calibration-log.md. Pattern detection over 3+ months surfaces IA's systematic biases (over/under-rating which domains) AND whether APM is reading IA's memos correctly (if APM agreed with IA but Richard disagreed, APM is also miscalibrated).

Investment Case Rating — The Highest-Stakes Single Judgement [Phase G v2, 12-May-26]

Why this section exists at this position in the file: The Investment Case rating (Progress / Park / Kill, ~1 per memo) is THE judgement IA produces, APM stress-tests, and Richard decides at Step 4. Get it wrong = either an investable case dies (under-rate B→C) or Richard wastes time on a non-investable case (over-rate C→B). It is the single highest-stakes judgement in the entire workflow. Prior sections covered the framework + the 11 attribute families + tier escalation; this section synthesises how the case-level rating emerges from those inputs and how it gets defensibly produced + challenged + decided.

§1 — What the Investment Case rating IS (and isn't)

IS: - A case-level integrated A/B/C/D/F judgement - A recommendation to Progress (next funnel stage) / Park (re-attack later when triggers re-fire) / Kill (thesis broken, no re-attack) - Lives in E.I of every memo from Triaging onward - Tagged (draft for Richard's review at Step 4) until Richard decides

ISN'T: - A mechanical roll-up of element/group/pillar ratings (it's INFORMED BY but not DERIVED FROM) - A unilateral decision by IA or APM (Richard decides at Step 4) - Reversible without process (calibration log entry required if revised)

§3 — How APM stress-tests the Investment Case rating

When the IA memo + GNG CHECKS lands in ia-output-queue.md, APM runs the case-level rating through 5 standing tests before recommending portfolio integration:

Test 1 — Concept B "most-different viewpoint." What's the strongest argument that this case should be rated one grade lower? If APM can construct it in 5 minutes, the rating is over-stated. If APM cannot construct it after 5 minutes of stewing, the rating is robust.

Test 2 — Cohort consistency check. If the stock is in an active cohort (e.g., Cables theme), how does this case rank vs cohort peers at the same stage? Differential ranking should have structural reasons, not arbitrary.

Test 3 — Archetype consistency check. What archetype does this case match (Liquidity Trap / Cockroach / Consolidation Play / Cyclical / Demand-Driven EPSU / Corporate Change EPSU)? Does the rating profile (Pillar I/II/III) match the archetype's typical profile? If divergent: what makes this case different?

Test 4 — Pret Sandwich check. Per Richard's discipline: no single C rating may be fatal, but the collection of C/D ratings can be. Scan the C.I.1 ratings table: - >40% of in-scope ratings at C? → mediocrity territory regardless of any individual A/B - ≥2 D ratings? → Cockroach Rule (find one, search for two more in dark spaces) - Both? → APM recommends Park at Step 4 even if IA recommended Progress

Test 5 — Hell-Yeah-or-No filter (APM-side pre-Richard). Could APM articulate a "Hell Yeah" case for this position? If APM can only say "interesting but not Hell Yeah," that's a Park signal — APM flags it as such in the integration handoff to Richard.

APM's stress-test artefact: - Add 1-3 paragraphs to apm-output-queue.md per IA memo received - Include: 5-test verdict (each test PASS/FAIL/CONCERN), recommended portfolio action (proceed at proposed size / proceed at smaller size / Park / Kill), specific flagged concerns - This feeds into Richard's Step 4 review alongside IA's GNG CHECKS

§4 — Decision logic at Step 4 (Richard decides)

Richard reads IA memo + APM stress-test + GNG CHECKS. He applies:

Verdict Rating range Action Implication
Progress to next stage Case-level A or B Stock advances funnel (Triaging→ESA, ESA→DD, DD→Live position) Resources commit to next stage
Progress with conditions Case-level B with one concern Advance + tighter monitoring + smaller sizing if Live Conditional progression
Park Case-level C or borderline B/C Re-assessment criteria + monitoring plan; re-attack when triggers fire "Interesting but not Hell Yeah"
Kill Case-level D or F Thesis broken; no re-attack Capital deployed elsewhere

NO MODIFIERS at the case level. Richard doesn't grade B+ or B-. The rating is one of A/B/C/D/F. Nuance lives in the supporting prose, not the letter.

§6 — Failure-recovery: what happens when the case-level rating turns out wrong

The case-level rating is the single highest-stakes judgement. It WILL turn out wrong sometimes. The discipline is fast surface + log + learn:

When wrong: 1. APM logs the revision to calibration log with structured fields (date, ticker, stage, APM-rating, Richard-revision, domain-pattern, calibration-action) 2. IA gets notified (so IA's next memo recalibrates — e.g., "I tend to over-rate Demand-Driven EPSU; adjust by half-notch in calibration check") 3. Pattern detection over 3+ months surfaces systematic biases (which IA + APM monthly co-review with HPC + COS) 4. Update Wisdom Library if the failure surfaces a Silver/Gold-promotable pattern 5. Update auto-memory if the pattern is structural

This cycle is what makes the rating-quality discipline compound over time. Phase G v2 makes it explicit; the cycle was implicit but not operationalised in V14.

Building Blocks Quality — BB→ACH Mapping + When Each Family Fires Hardest [Phase G v2, 12-May-26]

Why this section exists: The 11 detailed attribute family tables (IC #1/#2/#3 + BB #1-#8) preserved above are STATIC reference data. This section operationalises them — when each family fires hardest, why BB#2 carries quadruple weight, and how the BB families map to the 10 INVALIDATION ACHs. Without this section, IA + APM can read the tables but can't use them well.

§1 — BB#2 Foundation Quality — the 4× weight discipline

Per V3 weight system (and reflected in the Detailed Attribute Family Reference): BB#2 Foundation Quality is QUADRUPLE-WEIGHTED. This isn't decorative. It changes how IA + APM work:

Why BB#2 is 4×: - BB#2 is where most cases die at the DD gate. Operator quality + advantaged business + widening SRCA + value chain dynamics + industry structure + secular growth — these are the foundations that allow change momentum (Pillar I) to extrapolate into multi-year multiple expansion. Without BB#2 strength, change momentum fizzles; with BB#2 strength, even modest change momentum compounds. - The 09-May reframe ("Big Change Case + Bankability") formalises this: Pillar I = Big Change Case (the change momentum half), Pillar II core philosophy group G3 (E9-E13) = Bankability (the BB#2 territory). Both required AND both must reinforce.

Operating implication for IA: - BB#2 gets 2-4× the analytical passes of any other family at ESA + DD - Track BB#2 by number-of-passes-and-challenge-cycles, NOT by word count (Richard's correction 09-May) - Target: ≥4 separate passes on BB#2 (operator quality, business advantage, VC dynamics, industry structure, secular growth) before committing the rating - Concept B applies hardest here — for each BB#2 sub-element rated A/B, IA writes the strongest counter-argument and defeats it (or is defeated by it)

Operating implication for APM: - When stress-testing IA's memo (per §0.5.3), spend disproportionate time on BB#2 - A high BB#2 rating with weak supporting evidence = single largest source of memo over-rating - A low BB#2 rating well-supported = the strongest Park signal, regardless of Pillar I/III strength

Operating implication for Richard at Step 4: - BB#2 rating IS the bankability test - Hell-Yeah-or-No filter fires hardest on BB#2 — if BB#2 is anything less than B, the case isn't compounder-quality

§2 — When each attribute family fires hardest by stage

Different families bite at different funnel stages. This matters because: - IA can't pass-through 11 families at every stage at equal depth (would explode word budgets) - APM stress-test focus should match the stage's bite-points - Richard's review at Step 4 should expect different evidence at different stages

Family Triaging (III) ESA (IV) DD (V) Live (VI) Why this firing pattern

Operational principle for IA: At each stage, allocate analytical effort proportionally to the firing pattern. Triaging memo isn't ~7,800w of all 11 families equally; it's heavy on IC#2/IC#3/BB#7 and light on BB#2/BB#6 (which fire harder at ESA/DD).

Operational principle for APM: When stress-testing an IA memo, focus the Concept B "most-different viewpoint" on the families that should be primary for that stage. An ESA memo where BB#2 isn't the analytical centre is mis-allocated.

Big Change Case + Bankability — APM-Side Translation Map for Portfolio Action [Phase G v2, 12-May-26]

Why this section exists: Phase G v1 renamed "two-engine doctrine" to "Big Change Case + Bankability" but didn't operationalise the rename at the APM level. IA produces the Pillar I (Big Change Case) + Pillar II G3 (Bankability core philosophy) ratings. APM converts those ratings into portfolio action (sizing, hold period, exit discipline). This section is that translation map.

The principle: strong/weak Big Change Case and strong/weak Bankability are independent dimensions. The 4 combinations have 4 different portfolio implications. APM's job is to apply the right implication.

§1 — The four combinations (the 2×2 grid)

Big Change Case (Pillar I) Bankability (Pillar II G3, especially BB#2) Portfolio Implication Case-level rating
Strong (A/B) Strong (A/B) HQI Hell-Yeah-or-No zone A or B+ case-level
Strong (A/B) Weak (C or worse) Near-term momentum play B or B/C borderline
Weak (C or worse) Strong (A/B) Compounder awaiting catalyst B/C — typically Park
Weak Weak Automatic Park or Kill C/D/F — Park or Kill

§2 — APM portfolio action by combination

Combination 1: Strong BCC + Strong Bankability (HQI zone)

This is the Hell-Yeah zone. Both engines firing AND reinforcing.

APM dimension Action
Sizing 12-15% target (Resilient/Intense HQI per 4-tier framework). At entry: 8-10% starter with upgrade path to full
Hold period Multi-year compounder. Plan 18M-36M minimum, ride longer if thesis intact
Exit discipline Standard 10 ACH framework + Stage 2→3 technical overlay + 3rd base count trigger. NOT aggressive — let the compounder compound
Add discipline Add on constructive pullbacks to 20-day SMA on declining volume. Up to 2-3 adds, each smaller than prior. Pyramid up
Trim discipline Trim 20-30% at all-time highs if R/R no longer compelling. Maintain core position
Monitoring cadence Standard — fortnightly KD tracking + monthly thesis review + pre-earnings checklist
Painful Prudence Hold at 10-12% (not 14-15% theoretical max) until BB#2 4× discipline confirms full Hell-Yeah-or-No

Combination 2: Strong BCC + Weak Bankability (Near-term momentum play)

The change momentum is real but the foundations don't bank the multi-year extrapolation. Case fizzles after near-term run.

APM dimension Action
Sizing 6-8% target (MQBI per 4-tier framework). Smaller than HQI
Hold period Tactical — 12-18 months max. Plan exit by month 12-18 even if SP up (alpha decay)
Exit discipline TIGHTER than HQI — stop-loss at 5-6% (vs 7-8% normal). Tighten on any technical violation. ACH framework fires harder (any single trigger = exit, not just escalate)
Add discipline DO NOT add to underwater position. Add only on constructive pullbacks AND only if Pillar II G3 BB#2 has materially improved since entry
Trim discipline Aggressive — trim 30-50% at 2x stop target. Lock in gains. Don't ride the position into the BB#2 weakness becoming visible
Monitoring cadence WEEKLY — accelerated from fortnightly. The thesis has a clock
Painful Prudence Always at 50-70% of theoretical max sizing. Better to be small + right than large + wrong on a fizzle

Combination 3: Weak BCC + Strong Bankability (Compounder awaiting catalyst)

The business is great but nothing's driving the share price right now. Typically Park territory at ESA.

APM dimension Action
Sizing If Live: 5-8% bench-style position. If new: typically Park, not entry
Hold period If existing position: indefinite hold pending catalyst (could be 6M, could be 36M). If new: defer entry
Exit discipline Standard ACH framework. BB#2 watch — if BB#2 deteriorates (operator change, business advantage erosion), exit
Add discipline Add only when BCC catalysts emerge (new product cycle, capex commitment, sector demand inflection). Until then: hold flat
Trim discipline Standard. Don't pre-emptively trim a compounder waiting for catalyst
Monitoring cadence Quarterly for re-assessment criteria — has a catalyst emerged? Standard fortnightly KD tracking
Park trigger If new candidate: explicit Park with re-assessment criteria specified (e.g., "Re-attack when DC segment organic growth >15% YoY for 2 consecutive quarters")

Combination 4: Weak BCC + Weak Bankability (Automatic Park or Kill)

No engine firing. Cases like this should not be in the portfolio.

APM dimension Action
Sizing 0% — exit if Live, decline if new candidate
Hold period N/A
Exit discipline If Live: exit on next available strength. 30-day shot clock applies if any evidence emerges that one of the engines might fire
Add discipline N/A — never add
Trim discipline Exit entirely, not trim
Re-attack Only when BOTH engines re-emerge. Bankability is easier to verify than BCC, so re-attack typically waits for BCC catalyst

§3 — How APM determines the combination from IA's memo

APM reads IA's memo and assigns the combination based on:

For Big Change Case strength: - IA's Pillar I rating (especially E1 Required input change forces + E2 Required case outputs) - Number of distinct change forces present (1 = weak; 2 = floor; 3+ = strong) - Are change forces internal (corporate restructuring, new product, capex commit) OR external (regulatory tailwind, sector demand shift, supply disruption) OR both? - Is there a Big Change Case + Bankability reinforcement explicit in the memo? (Per the 09-May reframe, this MUST be stated at ESA in B.I.1 BLUF.)

For Bankability strength: - IA's Pillar II G3 rating (especially BB#2 4× weighted: operator + business + VC + industry + secular) - 3-Check Declaration on BB#2 specifically — did IA's counter-hypothesis stress-test surface a credible argument that BB#2 should be one notch lower? - BB#2 element-level ratings: how many at A/B? How many at C or worse? - Track-record parallel on BB#2: does the parallel show similar BB#2 strength leading to multi-year compounding, OR did the parallel show BB#2 weakness that wasn't visible at ESA?

§4 — Worked example: HTRO ESA (combination assessment)

IA-side ratings (hypothetical, post-ESA memo): - Pillar I E1: B (DC + FS + HE = 3 distinct change forces) - Pillar I E2: B+ (Q1-26 +41% beat, revisions positive) - Pillar I E3: B (TSR upside ~+97% over 33M per Richard's model) - Pillar II G3 BB#2: B (CEO Fröberg track record solid 14M post-pivot; HE healthcare niche moat; EU fiber secular tailwind) - BB#2 E9 Operator: B- (qualified — only 14M track record) - BB#2 E10 Capital allocation: D (recent JOWO acquisition at high-end multiple flagged) - BB#2 E11 VC dynamics: B (improving) - BB#2 E12 Industry structure: B (fragmented but consolidating) - BB#2 E13 Long-term growth: B+ (5-8% European fiber buildout through 2030) - Case-level rating IA: B+ (Progress to DD)

APM combination assessment: - BCC strength: STRONG (3 change forces, all B/B+) - Bankability strength: STRONG-WITH-FRICTION (BB#2 group rating B, but E10 D rating + E9 B- = mediocrity-accumulation flag per Pret Sandwich) - Combination: tilts toward 1 (HQI) BUT with friction signals from BB#2 E10/E9

APM action recommendation: - Treat as Combination 1 (HQI zone) sizing AT REDUCED CAP: 4-6% target (not 12-15% full HQI) - Hold period: multi-year compounder profile per Combination 1 - Exit discipline: standard, plus tighten on E10 capital allocation watchlist (ACH #3 Ditherer pre-armed) - Add discipline: hold off adding until DD addresses E10 D rating - Re-rate at DD: if E10 stays D, downgrade case-level to B (drop to Combination 2 sizing)

This is what BCC + Bankability translation looks like in operation. APM's portfolio action is not "B+ rating → 6-8% sizing per HQI table." It's "B+ rating + BB#2 friction → Combination 1 sizing with reduced cap + specific watchlist trigger."
