0. KEY QUESTIONS FRAMEWORK (Foundational SOP — Jan 2023)

Source: January 10, 2023 journal entry (15,090 characters). Central to Richard's process rebuild after 2022 catastrophe.

The Core Principle

Direct quote (Jan 10, 2023): "Combining a lot of research process insights (over the last few weeks), I feel strongly we need to have 'key questions' front-and-centre in all we do."

Why this matters: The Marlowe project (2022) revealed that analysis without clear framing leads to biased conclusions. Key Questions provides the frame.

The Process Loop (Not Linear)

  1. Establish the key questions — Define 4-6 critical questions that determine thesis validity
  2. Make them public — Share KQs with team, integrate into all documents/discussions
  3. Conduct work in congruence with KQs — Every research task answers one or more KQs
  4. Adjust as you learn — If work reveals KQs are wrong, amend them. If work goes tangential, ask: "Is work wrong or are KQs wrong?"

Direct quote: "This approach maximises directed opportunism... It's a loop, not a linear process. You constantly adjust the key questions and work as you learn more."

Integration with Memo Structure

Memo cover sheet standard (from Evolution meeting with JL): All research memos must include: 1. Key Questions — Front and centre 2. Key Drivers — Business model levers 3. Ratings — Quality, resilience, optionality 4. Potential Returns — Bull/base/bear case with inputs

"There is huge benefit to having a cover sheet for all memos... Because it provides our 'frame.'"

Key Distinction: KQs vs. Analysis

  • KQs drive work directionality — What information would change the thesis?
  • Analysis provides scaffolding — Building conviction or demolishing it
  • Judgment calls KQs — Once work is done, KQs force a conclusion

Not: "Here's all the information; what should I do?" But: "These are my key questions; I need answers to these 4 specific things."

Watson Coaching Prompts — Key Questions

  1. On research briefs: What are your 4-6 key questions for this case? Can you state them in one sentence each?

  2. On work prioritization: Are you spending time on KQs or tangential details? If tangential, should you adjust KQs or stop the work?

  3. On team alignment: Have you shared KQs with team? Do they understand which questions matter most?

  4. On conclusion-forcing: Once work is done, do your KQs force a definite conclusion (8/10 conviction) or leave you in "could be X or Y" purgatory?

1. POSITION SIZING

Non-Negotiable Rules

Max position size: 12-15% of AUM. Three top names should represent 40-50% of portfolio (15-20% each). If position gets above 15%, something is wrong with conviction or the business changed.

The 80% rule (empirical, critical). Looking at share price charts, positions fall 10-15% on noise then recover. Richard's observation: "If we top up (and definitely don't cut a super-sized position), we'd be better off. Don't cut super-sized positions into drawdowns." Corollary: If position reaches max size, don't increase. Stay at 80% of max instead to preserve dry powder for adding into volatility.

Ultra High Potential but Fragile (UHPYHQI) rule: 0-5% maximum. Never add capital when thesis is still in "skunkworks." Accept volatility; ignore the position while de-risking. Once it moves to Resilient & Intense HQI, can upsize.

Toe-hold positions: almost always avoid. Particularly dangerous for UHPYHQIs and Young HQIs. Only possibly useful in genuinely Resilient and Intense HQIs you're late to finding (second third of lifecycle). Toe-holds create commitment bias before thesis is proven.

Conviction-Based Sizing Framework (6 Dimensions)

Position size should reflect conviction across these six:

  1. Resilience — Quality of business, no permanent impairment risk
  2. Inflection/Optionality — Clear catalyst, market mispricing, range of outcomes
  3. Potential returns — Targets 100-120%+ on 3Y probabilistic view
  4. Conviction in assessment — Information widely gathered, bear case explored, own eyes on work, deep expertise, recent analysis
  5. Market sentiment — Fragility of sentiment, trend, margin-of-safety vs. expectations
  6. Near-term beats — Clear, high-conviction forecasts of near-term surprises

Conviction checklist (before sizing up): - Have you done lots of primary research (10+ quality sources minimum)? - Is this in your circle of deep expertise (years following sector/company, previously invested)? - Have you done your own work (model, memo, re-read all notes quarterly)? - Have you explored the bear case recently (two-hat brainstorms, pre-mortems)? - What's your monitoring process quality on this stock?

Critical insight: "Sizing is a superpower. What separates average investors from superinvestors is their ability to make big bets when they think they're right. Sizing is the control rod for emotional bandwidth."

Sizing and Volatility (The Oversizing Trap)

Richard's known weakness: "I also make a consistent over-sizing error where I oversize stocks with volatile share prices and that volatility shakes me out of my conviction because I have made the position too large."

Correction rule: Assign simple expected volatility bands to each stock. All stocks can fall 30% on earnings/rotation. Size inversely to expected volatility: - High-volatility positions: max 8-10% (not 12-15%) - Normal volatility: 10-12% - Low volatility/quality compounders: can go to 15%

Psychology note: "People hate feeling stupid. That's why they don't add a lot. When you size large in a position, the opposite happens—when it goes down, you feel negative. This dynamic is why many investors under-add and why oversizing creates emotional fragility."

Four Sizing Approaches for Different Market Conditions

Framework 1: Strategic Sizing (Normal Conditions) - Maps HQI dimensions (Resilience, Inflection, Returns, Conviction) + market sentiment - Right for sizing in normal markets - Emphasizes: resilience, quality, mid-20% growth rates - Approach: 8-10 core position philosophy; top 3 at 40-50% of capital

Framework 2: Tactical Overlay (Temporary Market Dislocations) - For capitalizing on temporary market mispricings and technical overshoots - De-risks core robust HQIs selectively (trim 2-3% to create dry powder) - Identifies "trading slugs" (4-5% tranches recycled for high IRR additions into panic bottoms) - Approach: Keep core intact; rotate 10-15% of portfolio tactically - Timing: Huge market dislocations (March 2020 pattern) — emphasize N2Y return-to-mean potential over resilience

Framework 3: Risk-Off Environment Overlay (Macro Retrenchment) - Tweak standard sizing framework toward resilience and domestic footprint - Reduce illiquid/speculative positions; add to defensive ballast - Maintain concentration in highest-quality/lowest-volatility subset - Approach: Maintain position count; reduce average position size to 8% (down from 10%) - Example (March 2026): Iran conflict context — prioritize recurring non-cyclical revenues, domestic EU footprint, banks/insurance, cable, healthcare

Framework 4: Skunkworks Positions (Pre-Research Accumulation) - Don't add positions while thesis is still forming - Accept volatility; don't try to time; don't trade - Cap at 0-5% (UHPYHQI sizing rule) - Approach: Build conviction through monitoring; upgrade sizing once thesis crystallises and Resilient & Intense HQI score hits 8+/12

Dynamic Adjustment Principle: The six-dimension framework is a map, not terrain. Use all four approaches; divergences between them are where it's interesting. The right approach depends on: environment assessment (Radar Process output), portfolio construction stage, and conviction confidence.

Risk Categories Framework — Structured Risk Taxonomy

External — Fundamental (Market & Environmental Factors Beyond Company Control)

Macroeconomic: - Recession/growth slowdown, inflation cycles, interest rate movements, FX movements, credit cycle turns

Societal/Geopolitical: - War, pandemics, demographics, gig economy shifts, labor market disruption, vulnerable population protection policies

Political: - Trade wars, regulatory leanings, voter optics, regulatory favours/penalties, political transitions

Regulatory: - Industry-specific regulation changes, anti-trust action, environmental/ESG mandates, preferential treatment, licensing changes

Competitive & Industry: - New entrants, substitute products, existing competitor aggression, industry consolidation/fragmentation, structural capacity changes

Disruptive Innovation: - Technological disruption, business model disruption, supply chain circumvention, customer behaviour shifts

Ecosystem Dynamics: - Supplier concentration risk, customer concentration risk, regulatory dependency, partnership fragility, co-investor stability

Internal — Fundamental (Company-Specific Factors Within Management Control)

Execution Risk: - Management capability, strategy clarity, operational agility, capital allocation discipline, incentive misalignment

People Risk: - Key person dependency, organizational depth, culture fragility, turnover risk

Strategic Risk: - Strategy/plan complexity (if >3 Excel tabs to understand, too complex), pace of change, degree of operational transformation required

Balance Sheet Risk: - High operating leverage (thin margins), high financial leverage (debt serviceability), high NWC leverage (negative cash conversion), external funding dependency (M&A engines)

External — Sentiment (Market Perception & Pricing Factors)

Valuation Risk: - Trading at high multiples (sentiment leverage), technical stretched positions, valuation fragility

Sector/Market Dynamics: - Sector in bear market (risk of sympathy selling), market index moves dragging stocks down, liquidity risk (<$2m-4m ADV), momentum/retail positioning

Narrative Fragility: - Short seller attacks, "story stock" risk (narrative matters more than fundamentals), consensus estimate fragility, meet/beat guidance risk, analyst coverage disruption

Risk Assessment & Aggregation

Severity scoring combines: - Range and number of identified risks - Complexity (breadth of risk types; interconnectedness) - Forecastability (F) — can we see these coming? - Impact (I) — how much damage? - Resource intensity (R) — how much attention required?

Decision gate: Positions with >4 material risks (F×I×R) in red zones require either: - Smaller position sizing (reduce to 50% of conviction sizing) - Higher conviction requirement (move to conviction + 2 standard deviations) - Explicit decision to accept risk as thesis-beta (documented pre-mortem)

Trading Slugs & Accumulation (The 4-5% Recycling Strategy)

Ideal situation: One stock at a time, gracefully selling off to create superb top-up window. 4-5% trading slugs, two to three at a time.

The math example: If 10% position falls 30% to 7%, buy 4% at 70-80 to take it back to 11%. If underlying IRR is 25%, the new slug IRR is 40%.

When to deploy: Before technical inflection turns, when contractual catalysts aren't yet priced.

Liquidity Discipline

Liquidity is almost universally bad. Materially hampers position management. An illiquid name should be sized at 50% discount to liquid comparables.

Keep core buffer liquid. Must have funding source. Opportunity cost of being in all illiquid small-caps means can't readjust if opportunity comes up (Lecher/Vinall insight).

Liquidity floor: >$2m ADV minimum (May 21, 2022 discovery). This lesson came harshly during 2022 drawdown. XVIVO and FASADGRUPPEN trap: illiquid positions force holding through stress, preventing nimbleness.

The specific case (May 21, 2022): "We've snookered ourselves with liquidity in Fasadgruppen and XVIVO." XVIVO position was only 3% but represented 10%+ of company; couldn't scale up or exit efficiently. FASADGRUPPEN similarly trapped.

Position sizing rule linked to liquidity: Can't hold >10% of a company (creates illiquidity). If you can only own 3%, do you really have conviction in the thesis, or are you just locked in? Force yourself to confront this.

Portfolio impact: Illiquid portfolio can't trade opportunistically. "My irritation is not having a portfolio suited for current conditions. I value nimbleness and dynamism and I've not done it" (May 21, 2022).

Sell into strength on surprise moves (May 21, 2022): If surprised by price move up, market will likely soften next day on lower volume. Execute sizing framework immediately; don't wait for "perfect moment." Liquidity dries fast.

Watson Coaching Prompts — Sizing Decisions

  1. Pre-entry: What's the volatility profile of this stock? Are you sizing it inverse to volatility or are you oversizing the volatile ones? (Check for known oversizing trap.)

  2. When considering adding: Does this position still meet conviction across all six dimensions? Or are you adding because you like the company + share price fell? (Different decisions.)

  3. When position hits 12%+: What would make you add to 15%? What would trigger you to hold at 10%? Have you pre-committed to the trigger or are you deciding in real-time? (Real-time sizing is emotional.)

  4. On illiquid positions: Are you sizing this at 50% of what you'd size an equivalent liquid company? Are you counting the liquidity cost in your IRR target?

  5. When position gets volatile: Are you staying at this size because conviction is still high, or because you've anchored to the position? Ask: "If I were selling this today at 70% of entry price, would I buy it back immediately at current price?" If yes, conviction is real. If no, size is wrong.

2. ENTRY DECISIONS

The Four Investment Setups (Richard's Repeatable Patterns)

Richard has identified these four consistent setups that work for him:

1. Demand Explosion (EPT)

Revenue or earnings per share pulse from sudden demand increase. Market hasn't yet realized it will persist. - Example: COVID-driven infrastructure / datacentre demand surge (AI theme) - Entry signal: Clear, near-term visibility of demand (contractual, announced, evident in data) - Exit signal: When demand pulse looks like it will normalize or competing supply comes online

2. Transformative Corporate Change (EPSU)

Management, strategic pivot, M&A, operational restructuring creating earnings-per-share uplift. - Example: Hilton cost-out initiatives, Rightmove platform transition - Entry signal: New management team with proven track record, or clear value-add case - Risk: Takes longer than expected; must have quality operator

3. Oversold Quality (Predictable, High-Conviction Rebounds)

Quality business selling off on temporary, non-fundamental concerns. Market sentiment fragile. But 18-month EPS clearly visible with margin of safety. - Examples: Entain (March 2020), Protector (March 2020), oversold quality after macro shocks - Entry signal: Business is fundamentally good; share price is just low due to short-term noise - Key discipline: Only works if you've done the work ex ante so you're not reactive ex post

4. Trough on Trough (Cyclical Rebound + Quality Growth)

Cyclical demand bottom coinciding with quality growth business in sweet spot. Compounding ahead. - Entry signal: Demand indicators bottoming (hard data, not sentiment) - Key risk: Timing the trough is hard; requires patience and discipline

Entry Checklist (ESA Stage — Early-Stage Assessment)

Before committing meaningful capital, verify ALL of these:

1. Quality Test (Non-negotiable gate) - Is it a solid or high-quality business? (Minimum 7.5/10 LT/DT rating) - If not, reject immediately. "Any investment case involving a mediocre business (less than 8/10 LT/DT) is a hypothesis trade with very tight red-line criteria."

2. Mispricing Identification - Market has clear cause(s) for concern sending multiple into bottom 50th percentile of fair range - Can you articulate exactly why the market is worried? (Not vague; specific)

3. Return Target - Gets to 20-30% IRR through: EPS growth 10-15% p.a. over 3-4 years + cash returns 0-5% p.a. + trading multiple re-rating adding 10-20% p.a. over 2-3 years - 18-month target holding period (unless lots of positive optionality)

4. Edge / Right to Play - Genuine conviction that market's cause for concern will reverse following specific catalysts - Is this in your circle of excellence? (Years following sector/company, primary source knowledge)

5. Clear Catalysts - Development/catalyst clear, allowing precise Bayesian updating on key triggers - Avoid vague fading away of concerns; prefer clear developments (reduces risk of getting judgment wrong)

6. No Speculating on Bottoms - Don't try to time the exact bottom. Identify where you are in the S-curve and enter accordingly. - Enter with bottom quartile/50th valuation range. Don't enter on stretched multiples.

7. Thesis Mapping (Per Timeframe) - Map investment case for IT (immediate term), NT (near-term), MT (mid-term), LT/DT (long-term) - Financials and outputs per timeframe - Assess degree to which will positively surprise market - What's the base case, bear case, bull case for each timeframe?

8. Red-Line Invalidation Criteria (Per Timeframe) - Defined red-line criteria tightly for fundamental developments that would invalidate thesis - NOT lazy on IT (map expectations precisely, set ratings accordingly) - Avoid boiling frog

9. Risk Mapping - Mapped all plausible/probable risks (real and perceivable) per timeframe - Investigated thoroughly in DD - Identified impact, forecastability, resource intensity of each risk

10. Two-Hat Bear Case Exploration - How would skeptic/bear view recent developments? - Run bear brainstorms - Are there "tip of iceberg" risks you're not seeing?

Entry Mistakes to Avoid (Lessons from Track Record)

Mistake 1: Overstaying Complexity From XVIVO: "Too much detail, no simple ACH invalidation guide fails. Got lost in trees, missed forest. Learning all that stuff was trees not wood. Not hell yeah = don't invest." - Lesson: If you can't state the case simply, don't invest. If case is more than 3-4 clear drivers, it's probably too complex for your circle.

Mistake 2: Mediocre Quality + Weak Inflection From Attendo, Intertrust, Fagron, Britvic: "All too weak as inflection intensity is mediocre; all materially underperformed the fund in the subsequent 6-12 months by 10 to 30%." - Lesson: Don't let quality be a 7/10 and inflection be 6/10 and hope it works out. It doesn't. One or both must be strong.

Mistake 3: Falling for Management Reassurance From Instalco/Corbion: "Companies often say 'no drama' to start with, then they start to point out the issues. So be sceptical." - Lesson: When inflation/structural headwinds emerge, management downplays initially. Listen for the second message, not the first.

Mistake 4: Oversizing Hypotheses (Sdiptech Pattern) "Sdiptech was speculating in mid-term earnings power transformation of a mediocre business. Must have clearly defined ex ante invalidation criteria and tight stop-loss(es)." - Lesson: Hypothesis trades on mediocre businesses are OK, but they must have tight red lines. Assume 50% failure rate and size accordingly.

Watson Coaching Prompts — Entry Decisions

  1. Before initiating position: Can you state the investment case in one sentence? If not, go do more work before entering.

  2. On conviction checklist: Which of the 10 boxes are you unsure about? Don't enter if more than 1-2 are uncertain. You need 8/10 minimum.

  3. On return targets: Are you getting to 20-30% IRR? Or are you hoping for it? Show the math: growth rate + multiple re-rating + cash return = total.

  4. On risk mapping: What would you see first if this thesis was wrong? Define that signal right now (not later). If you don't know, go back to research.

  5. On catalysts: Is your catalyst "conviction in the market eventually realizing quality" or "specific event X happens in 6 months"? The second is much better than the first.

3. EXIT DECISIONS

Exit Philosophy (The Core Rules)

Rule 1: Short-term concern = wait. Fundamental, mid-term concern = exit. If it's a short-term shock (earnings miss, temporary sector weakness), hold and prepare to add. If it's fundamental and nagging for 3-6+ months, exit decisively.

Rule 2: Three-thirds lifecycle framework — exit when moving into third third. - First third: Waiting, researching (Ultra High Potential but Fragile) - Second third: Riding the wave (guidance upgrades, beat-and-raise, sentiment improving) ← ALPHA DECAY RISK GROWS HERE - Third third: Optionality slowing, multiple near top of range → EXIT and recycle

Greggs case study: "Trading multiple was in top quartile. No immediate/near-term thesis re. high-conviction forecast beats ahead. Therefore, share price would be fragile when fundamental momentum rolls over." Edward called it perfectly. Lesson: Be objective about position sizing in second third.

Rule 3: "Tip of iceberg" vigilance. When you spot any negative brewing, be SUPER paranoid there may be more to come. That's often the pattern. From Ambu case: "People hate cutting once and cutting hard; they do the opposite (hope it improves). Anytime negatives brewing, we need to be extremely paranoid."

Rule 4: ACH threshold breached = exit decisively; don't wait. From BFF: "Endowment bias, commitment bias held too long despite mounting evidence. ACH threshold breached = EXIT; don't wait until getting closer. Don't let fallen SP stop right action."

Exit Framework — Decision Tree

Trigger Type Signal Action Notes
Thesis invalidation (red-line hit) Fundamental development that invalidates thesis Exit decisively (SF sell: one go, one day) Existential/strategic risk. No debate.
Fundamental, mid-term concern (nagging 3-6M+) Recurring negatives in earnings, execution, or conditions Propose exit (write memo, run checks) If in purgatory for months, fine to exit.
Moving into third third Inflection slowing, multiple rising, sentiment fragility Plan exit (no fixed timeline yet) Reduce or monitor, prepare to exit if optionality doesn't recover.
Narrow optionality played out Forecast that was "narrow" and now played out Trim 30-40% (take profits) Ask: is range of outcomes narrowing (good) or was this just one-off? If one-off, trim. Rarely evolves into broad optionality.

Deterioration in technical indicators Sentiment support gone, trading multiple compressed, momentum negative Monitor closely; if deterioration continues, exit Deterioration in TIs makes sentiment far more fragile. Cockroaches never alone.

Exit SOP (Formal Process)

  1. Run all normal checks. Rigorous analysis re-validation.
  2. Hold investment team discussion. Critically challenge thinking/judgement/decision. Is this emotional or rational?
  3. Write "Proposal to Exit" memo. Rigor, judgment, decision documented.
  4. Allocate to monitoring list. Gold/Silver/longer list. Keep tabs without holding capital.
  5. Write specific tracking indicator that would make you reconsider (tilt positive to avoid scepticism bias).
  6. Document investing lessons learned. What will you do differently next time?

Special Exit Patterns

The Mediocre Quality + Weak Inflection Rule: If business is 6-7/10 LT/DT and inflection is 5-6/10, exit. This combination underperforms by 10-30% over subsequent 6-12 months. (Attendo, Intertrust, Fagron, Britvic pattern.)

The "Boiling Frog" Defense: If thesis tracking below expectations, ask: "Invalidation or just update ratings?" If in doubt, tilt to invalidation. "Sticking in things too long has been my major problem." Strongly default to exiting and moving on.

Binary Exit vs. Downsize (Nuanced Positioning)

Richard's pattern: Don't like pure binary exit. Prefer "15% = full-sized, 3% = monitoring" (TCI tail approach).

When to downsize: Near-term issues, execution risk, temporary concerns that might resolve When to exit: Existential/strategic/resilience issues that are fundamental to the thesis

Use analyst portfolio/monitoring list to keep tabs on exited positions. Sometimes they re-enter if risks clear.

§3a. Technical Exit Validation — Minervini Overlay

The Core Principle

A stock breaking its 50-day SMA on heavy volume shortly after a catalyst event is the market telling you something your fundamental analysis hasn't yet captured. Technical signals lead fundamental deterioration — sometimes by weeks, sometimes by a full earnings cycle. The sell discipline (not stock selection) is the primary mechanism of long-term outperformance.

Minervini's retroactive cap study: capping every loss at 10% shifted compounded return from −12.05% to +79.89%. Same stocks, same entries — only the exit discipline changed. A 90+ percentage point swing from exits alone.

Non-Negotiable Technical Rules

  1. Stop-loss maximum: 10%. Never exceed. If the structurally correct stop requires >10%, pass on the trade entirely.
  2. Stops only move up. Never widened. Never moved down "to give it more room." No exceptions.
  3. Three simultaneous violations = exit immediately, regardless of stop distance.
  4. Stage 3 distribution confirmed (50D crosses below 150D) = reduce to bench or exit. Do not wait for Stage 4 to be fully confirmed.
  5. Never average down. Only add to winning positions on constructive pullbacks to 20-day SMA. "Losers average losers."

The 8-Violation Checklist — Run Daily for First 10 Days Post-Entry

# Violation Severity
1 Close below 20-day SMA post-breakout Warning
2 Close below 50-day SMA on above-average volume Major
3 3+ consecutive lower lows on increasing volume Major
4 Low volume on breakout, high volume on reversal Major
5 More down days than up days in first 2 weeks Warning
6 More bad closes than good closes Warning
7 Full retracement of meaningful gain Major
8 Close below pivot price on volume Critical

1 violation = noted. 2 = serious concern, tighten. 3+ simultaneously = exit immediately.

Stage 2→3 Transition — Early Warning Signals

Watch for the MA collapse sequence (in order):

  1. Price whipsaws around 50-day SMA (floor → centreline) → tighten to 20-day trail
  2. 50-day SMA flattens and curves down → reduce 25–50%
  3. 50-day crosses below 150-day (bullish stack breaks) → exit or bench position
  4. 200-day SMA flattens → too late, should be out

Volume distribution signals (concurrent): - Down days on above-average volume; up days on below-average volume - Heaviest volume day is a down day - Average volume rising while price flat (supply building) - Rallies on declining volume (no institutional sponsorship) - Volume climax on apparently good news (institutions selling into retail excitement)

Price behaviour signals: - Expanding daily ranges, increasing gap-downs - Failure to hold new highs (upthrusts / false breakouts) - Lower highs within consolidation (rounding top) - Bad closes dominating

Signal Count → Response Framework

Signal count Across categories Response
1–2 1 category Normal vigilance. Monitor daily.
3–5 Any Tighten to 20-day trail. Reduce 25–50%. Stop adding.
6+ 3+ categories Exit into next available strength.

Critical distinction: - Technical signals + fundamental deterioration = highest urgency, exit immediately - Technical signals alone (strong fundamentals) = tighten and monitor, escalate shot clock to 2 weeks - Fundamental deterioration alone (no technical signals) = standard 30-day shot clock

Base Counting as Risk Calibration — Non-Negotiable Rule

Base count Risk level Hard rules
1st–2nd Lowest Full position, standard stops
3rd Inflection Reduce 25–50%, tighten to 20-day, stop adding
4th–5th Highest Max 50% of normal size, 3–5% stops, quick exit mindset

Former leaders (4th–5th base) have 50% probability of declining 80% from peak and 80% probability of declining 50%. The fundamental thesis often still looks intact at the top. Base count is an objective risk calibration independent of the fundamental view.

Trailing Stop Hierarchy (All SMAs — simple, not exponential)

  • 20-day SMA → short-term trail for fast movers; first warning level
  • 50-day SMA → primary sell trigger at +20%+ gain; decisive close below on above-average volume = exit
  • 150/200-day SMA → structural boundaries only; these should never be tested on a position managed correctly

Progressive exposure rule: Trade largest when trading best. Start new cycles small (5–10%). Winning trades → increase size. Losing trades → scale down aggressively, move toward cash. Never fight the signal.

4. ADDING TO POSITIONS

When to Add (The Discipline)

Add before technical inflection turns. Don't wait until everyone sees the inflection; add beforehand.

Add when contractual catalysts not yet priced. BFF lesson: "The market sometimes overreacts and sometimes underreacts to events. Sometimes the market prices them in well in advance, and sometimes it waits for the event to literally happen, even though it has been contractually agreed many months earlier."

Add on dislocations in core robust HQIs. If quality is proven and sentiment is temporarily panicky, add 5-10% on drawdowns (assuming position isn't already max-sized).

Add before earnings beats / upgrade cycles. "Try and be disciplined about adding before the turn in technical momentum" (Edward's XVIVO reminder).

Adding Mistakes (What Not to Do)

Mistake 1: Adding to UHPYHQIs while thesis in skunkworks. Richard's rule: "Don't add capital when thesis still in skunkworks. Accept volatility and ignore the position while in that state."

Mistake 2: Adding without conviction across six dimensions. If conviction fades on any dimension, don't add. This is different from "stock went down so it's cheaper" (which might not be a good reason to add).

Mistake 3: Adding after you've already sized aggressively. If already at 12-14% and position fell 20%, you don't have dry powder to add meaningfully anyway. This is why staying at 80% of max is smart.

The Trading Slug Strategy (4-5% Recycling)

When position recovers from drawdown, don't hold it all at peak. Trim 4-5% to recycle capital or take profits. Allows for dynamic management without binary exit.

Watson Coaching Prompts — Adding Decisions

  1. Before adding: Does conviction across six dimensions still hold? Or are you adding because price fell? (Check the motive.)

  2. On ammunition: How much dry powder do you have in terms of position capacity? Can you actually add meaningfully, or are you already at 90% of max?

  3. On timing: Is this technical dislocation or fundamental deterioration? If fundamental, be cautious about adding. If technical (share price fell on noise), this is optimal adding time.

  4. On size: If adding, what will the position be post-add? Still below 15%? Good. If it'll be 16%+, wait until you trim something first.

5. TRIMMING / REDUCING vs. EXITING

Downsize When: Near-Term Concerns

Use case: Company hit a real but temporary issue (supply chain, earning miss, sector rotation). Fundamentals still intact. But you're nervous.

Action: Reduce from 12% to 8% to manage risk while you figure out if this is temporary or fundamental.

Monitor: Watch for inflection recovery. If inflection returns to normal, can re-add back to 12%. If deterioration continues, exit completely.

Exit When: Existential / Strategic / Resilience Issues

Use case: Business model is broken, management changed materially for worse, permanent structural decline emerging, capital allocation deteriorating.

Action: Exit fully, decisively. No partial positions.

Note: Once you've exited, move to monitoring list. Don't keep a tiny position for FOMO (fear of missing out). Either own it or don't.

Top-Slicing Winners (The Nuanced Approach)

Richard's learning: "There's beguiling but incorrect naivety about not top-slicing big winners. The nuance is when it is right to do it."

Conditions for riding a winner without trimming: - Company is a snowball/flywheel — business AND investment case getting stronger with time, not just share price rising - Range of outcomes is narrowing (improving conviction), not expanding - More positive optionality is emerging (new catalysts, new markets, new management initiatives) - You have margin of safety (cone of uncertainty still wide even at higher price)

Conditions for trimming: - Share price went up but business/case fundamentally didn't improve - Range of outcomes expanded (new uncertainties emerged) - Multiple is now extended relative to quality and growth - No new positive optionality emerged; just riding old inflection

The test: "Do I see better risk/reward at current price than entry price?" If no, trim 20-30%.

Key insight: Panicky reactions disguise themselves as mature risk management. The urge to "derisque" when analyst is nervous is often the chimp brain, not the executive function.

  2. On winners: Has the business gotten stronger, or just the share price? Are you still seeing new positive optionality, or are you coasting on old thesis?

7. DRAWDOWN MANAGEMENT

Historical Context

Richard's worst drawdown: -54.75% in 2022 (a catastrophic year). He recovered to +12% the following year.

Key learning from recovery: Conviction in process, not panic selling, and disciplined rebalancing enabled recovery.

Expectation-Setting (Accept Drawdowns as Default)

Empirical pattern from studying 20%+ multi-decade CAGR stocks: - 15% drawdown multiple times per year (normal) - 20%+ drawdown most years (regular) - 50% drawdown every decade (expect it)

Target condition: Accept 10-40% of portfolio will be in slight to medium drawdown at any time. It's the default state.

Reframe: "Happiness = reality - expectations." If you expect 20% drawdowns and get them, you're not disappointed.

The Key Question (Test of Real Conviction)

This cannot be seared into eyes enough:

"If market completely crapped out and shares down 30%, 40%, 50%, am I doing a 'protector' or buying more, even if I feel sick? Am I willing to say, 'One-year investment frame from here, given radar risks and stock-specific risks, looks safe enough that I will add big chunk—5-10%—on share price weakness'?"

If answer is "I'd be a protector," you don't have real conviction. Don't size up now.

If answer is "I'd add 5-10%," you likely have conviction. Size accordingly.

Preparing for Drawdowns (Fire Avoidance SOP)

Before positions even exist:

  1. Rehearse really bad futures. Role-play bad scenarios (recession, rate shock, scandal, industry disruption). Ask: "Would I add on this outcome?"

  2. Identify 6-12 month risks. Look ahead and identify any likely relevant events/developments that could cause share price to fall 25% on semi-fundamental news such that you wouldn't want to add on 6-month view.

  3. Identify all risks that could make SP fall 25% such that you don't think it retraces. These are your red lines.

  4. If you can see the risk ex ante, you're less likely to panic-sell ex post.

Conviction During Drawdowns (OODA Loop Principle)

Framework: Knowledge → Clarity → Position capital → Mentally be ready to pivot. - Observe/know: What's happening? Is it macro, sector, or stock-specific? - Orient/understand: What could happen based on this development? Is this a red-line trigger or noise? - Decide/Act: Position capital proactively (add, hold, reduce, exit) - Adapt/pivot: Mentally be ready to adjust if new information emerges

Key reminder: "If right on resilience, optionality, inflection, market sentiment, share price will rebound on 3-6 month timeframe. Trust the process. Average intra-year peak-to-trough swings are 40% for all stocks."

Emotional State in Drawdowns

Target condition: Hope for / welcome the counter-punch. Not annoyance (which is fragility).

Richard's framing: "Want short-term storm-in-teacup 30% decline to add 50%."

Work on mindset. Flip it. When stock falls 30% on noise: - Excitement (opportunity to add at better prices) - Confidence (if conviction is real, this is perfect) - Calm (process is working; volatility is expected)

NOT: - Anxiety (position is blowing up) - Regret (should have been smaller) - Doubt (was I wrong to enter?)

Selective Burning / Trading Slugs Strategy

"Ideal situation is stock selling off in controlled fashion. Symphonic and harmonious. One stock at a time, gracefully selling off to create superb top-up window."

Mechanics: 4-5% trading slugs, two to three at a time. - If 10% position falls 30% to 7%, buy 4% at 70-80 to take it to 11%. - If underlying IRR is 25%, new slug IRR is 40%. - Keeps position managed; prevents all-or-nothing dynamics

Market Oversold Opportunities (Entain / Protector Lessons)

"When there is a market-level sell-off, do not exit stocks that were previously worrying you because of 'mediocre quality'. They were oversold. The stock will recover as it is low in its range almost purely due to technical pressure, not fundamental concerns; baby and bathwater."

Key lesson: "I can make a lot of money buying oversold companies. In fact, this is a big P&L opportunity. Be opportunistic."

BUT only if you've done the work ex ante. You can't opportunistically buy at lows if you don't have conviction beforehand. Pre-analysis is critical.

Watson Coaching Prompts — Drawdown Management

  1. When market is down 10%+: Are you welcoming the opportunity or feeling anxious? Which positions would you be buying more of at current prices?

  2. On conviction test: Pick one position. Imagine it falls 40% tomorrow on sector shock. Would you add or protect? Honest answer?

  3. On red-line tracking: As positions fall, are you checking whether red-line invalidation criteria have been triggered? Or are you in denial?

  4. On fire avoidance: Did you rehearse this scenario before entering? Do you know your response, or are you deciding in real-time?

  5. On oversold opportunities: Are there stocks that have fallen but fundamentals haven't changed? What's your plan to add on the dislocation?

8. PSYCHOLOGY & EMOTIONAL DISCIPLINE

The Chimp Brain Pattern (Steve Peters / Peters Model)

Richard's pattern: Chimp brain panic disguises itself as mature risk management.

Instalco example: "It feels 'mature' and 'calm' to take risk off in that scenario, but that's the chimp brain tricking you."

How to recognize Chimp mode: - Urge to de-risk is sudden and strong (not calm) - Feeling anxious or threatened (not clear) - Making decisions fast without re-analysis (not deliberate) - Justifying the decision with selective facts (confirmation bias)

How to move to Observer mode: 1. Recognize: "I'm in Chimp mode right now." (Non-judgment, just observation) 2. Pause and breathe. 10 deep breaths shifts nervous system. 3. Move to neutral observation. "What are the facts? What can I control? What's my process?" 4. Then act from Observer mode. Decision quality is radically better.

Conviction is an Emotion (Denise Shull Insight)

"You can't take emotion out of conviction." Conviction is not a rational checklist; it's a felt sense.

Implication: You need both analysis (the intellectual conviction checklist) AND emotional comfort (you feel good about the thesis).

If you have analysis but don't feel it, conviction is weak. You'll panic-sell when volatility hits.

If you feel it but can't articulate the analysis, conviction is also weak (you don't have enough scaffolding to hold through draws).

Both required.

Team Dynamics & Independent Judgment

Richard's pattern: Strong tendency to dismiss others' analysis, preferring his own judgment.

His quote: "I am the decision maker. ALWAYS form my own judgment on a stock. Force myself to get to a conclusion. Never abrogate responsibilities to the analyst for judgement. They're an advisory resource. I'm the Decision-maker."

But he also learned: "I let Pedro's lack of attention to detail and his emotional flappiness influence me. I need to trust my own assessment and judgement far, far more highly than his, and preserve my independent emotional energy."

The skill: Trust your own judgment on the investment case. Don't let team nervousness or emotional contagion override your analysis.

But also: Integrate team analysis on details (supply chains, management quality, competitive dynamics). They're experts on those things.

Signal Escalation Failures (Kainos / Keywords Lesson)

Richard's team saw the AI slowdown and market demand slowdown, but "didn't shout loudly enough" and Richard "didn't default to capital reduction/risk-off."

Root cause: Analysis detected, but escalation failed. Richard was "drowning in unnecessary detail/info/analysis" rather than acting on clear signals.

Lesson: Create explicit escalation protocol. Team flags: "This doesn't pass 'hell yeah or no' test." Richard's response: Default to capital reduction.

Commitment vs. Motivation (4Cs Framework)

Steve Ward distinction: Commitment is different from motivation.

Motivation fluctuates (you feel energetic some days, flat others).

Commitment is a structural decision: "I am committed to executing my process consistently, regardless of emotional state."

For Richard: Set pre-commitments to rules (max size, exit criteria, adding discipline) BEFORE emotional state matters. Then execute those pre-commitments, not your current feelings.

Solving for Desired Outcome Bias

Richard's self-awareness: "Can feel that if you like stock, will give slightly higher marks throughout. Circularity and non-objectivity."

Mitigation: Use multiple frameworks to cross-check. Where frameworks diverge, dig deeper. Trust gut over granular when you have a system.

"Divergences between frameworks are where it's interesting. Divergences between gut and granular buildup. Generally trust gut, not granular, when you have a system."

Meaning: Once you have analysis frameworks in place, if your gut says "this doesn't feel right" but the checklist says "looks good," that divergence is interesting. Investigate why gut and head are misaligned. Often the gut is seeing pattern-recognition risk the granular analysis missed.

  1. On emotional urges: Before acting on an urge to trim/add/exit, pause and ask: "Is this Chimp or Observer mode?" Describe how you're feeling.

  2. On team influence: When team member raises concern, do you want to (a) dismiss and override, or (b) integrate and reconsider? How did you decide?

9. RESILIENCE-OPTIONALITY FRAMEWORK (Aug 2023 — Quality Assessment)

Source: August 15-17, 2023 journal entries. Introduced after Josh Tarasoff essay integration and deep reflection on what makes companies exceptional.

The 12-Dimension Framework for Investment Quality

Richard developed this framework to systematically assess whether a company has the characteristics needed for long-term outperformance:

1. Positive Optionality — Multiple sources, multiple time frames, not just lumpy big ones. Visualized as "planes stacked about an airport" (various catalysts, different timeframes).

2. Negative Optionality — Accept it in 0-6 and 6-18, plan accordingly. Want to see it be small as % of fundamental business value (FBV).

3. Balancing Feedback Loops — When negative events occur, there should be offsetting mechanisms (e.g., share price falls triggers share buyback, or customer switches spark cost-cutting).

4. Resilience (to negative optionality) — Limited negative optionality on mid- to long-term; absence of reinforcing feedback loops that compound negative events.

5. Grabbing of Positive Optionality — Habitual behavior of company seizing opportunities to widen SRCA (Structural Relative Competitive Advantage); proactive not reactive.

6. Leverage (all forms) — Financial, operating, multiple, NWC, liquidity. Magnifies up/down cones; narrows management degrees of freedom. More leverage = less resilience.

7. Positive Gravitational Pull Upwards — "Seemingly inevitable" pull towards SRCA widening. Time widens the moat, not shrinks it.

8. Non-Ergodicity Risk — Path is critical to outcome. Avoid cases where negative event at point A eliminates or reduces probability of positive optionality from point B onwards.

9. Opacity/Clarity/Predictability — How confident are you in predicting all this? Lower opacity = more edge.

10. Soft & Hard SRCA — Degree of both determines extent of resilience and ability to grab positive optionality. (Hard = structural/patent-like; Soft = culture/mission-driven.)

11. Skew and Cone of Outcomes — Want positive skew; want cone to improve over time with upside widening and downside narrowing.

12. MDF Thesis (Materiality-of-Difference Framework) — Thesis is variant and material (not marginal).

Target Condition for Ownership

Direct quote (Aug 17, 2023): "Only own companies that have: 1. High resilience-optionality 2. Manifesto congruency (mission-driven culture) 3. Ratings in the quality framework"

"Lots of positive optionality along multiple sources and time frames; Little negative optionality; Resilience to negative optionality; Grabbing of positive optionality; Minimal leverage; Positive gravitational pull upwards; Clear path; Positive skew that improves over time"

Sizing Based on Cone Width

Direct quote (Aug 17): "Size positions based on the probable share price downside volatility... accepting a cone of up and down scenarios means being sized probably c. 50-70% of what I (RB) would want [based on conviction alone]."

Implication: Two positions with equal conviction might be sized differently based on the width of potential outcomes. Wide cone = smaller position.

What's Priced In

Critical insight (Aug 17): "Very little optionality or resilience in the price. The visualization is that the what's-in-the-price line is below the base case line and should be materially above and widening with each future period."

Question: If market is only pricing base case (no optionality premium), is it worth owning? Only if there's genuine positive surprise probability.

Integration into Capital Allocation

(August 17, 2023): Position size should reflect: - 18-4+ timeframe conviction first (this is the fulcrum) - Cone width (wide cone = discount size) - Resilience score (fragile = smaller) - Optionality depth (lumpy optionality = smaller vs. distributed)

Watson Coaching Prompts — Resilience-Optionality

  1. On positive optionality: Can you name 4-5 sources of optionality for this stock? Or is it all lumped into one "if growth accelerates"?

  2. On resilience: If this company faced a material negative event tomorrow, what's your confidence it could recover? What feedback loops prevent recovery?

  3. On cone width: What's your upside scenario? Your downside scenario? The width of that cone tells you what % of AUM to own.

  4. On leverage: How many forms of leverage (financial, operating, multiple, etc.) is this company taking? More leverage = less resilience = smaller position.

  5. On gravitational pull: Is there "seemingly inevitable" SRCA widening over time, or is maintenance of current moat all you can count on?

22. BFF FRAMEWORK: QUALITY THAT DOESN'T LOOK LIKE QUALITY

Source: May 2023 (specifically May 14, 2023, 15,121 chars). Core framework that became central to Richard's portfolio construction philosophy for 2023+.

The Core Insight (May 14, 2023)

Direct quote: "Mapping out the potential investment universe and then high cadence parsing through them all is the key. I then unearth a handful of totally mispriced gems. BFF style companies. These are quality that do not look like quality. And invest hard."

What "Quality That Doesn't Look Like Quality" Means

On paper, BFF looks mediocre: - Not a high-growth business (solid 10-12% organic growth) - Not a household name (small-cap financial services) - Not a visibly obvious competitive advantage (no network effects, patents, or scale moats)

In reality, BFF is exceptional because: - Customer-centric culture (every decision optimizes for customer value, not revenue) - Mission-driven (financial inclusion for working-class people) - Mission attracts talent naturally (people want to work there) - Talent quality → better execution → better customer outcomes → customer loyalty → pricing power - Structural advantage emerges FROM culture, not despite it

Why Markets Misprice BFF-Type Companies

  1. Excel models miss soft SRCA — Models capture revenue/margin/growth. They miss "culture attracts talent" → "talent drives execution"
  2. Sell-side misses it — Analysts follow hard factors (comps, growth rates, multiples). Miss culture signal.
  3. Institutional money ignores it — Can't easily model mission-driven culture. Prefer obvious growth stories.
  4. Narrative is boring — "Steady-hand operator with mission-driven culture" doesn't excite markets. But it compounds for decades.

Characteristics of BFF-Type Companies (from 2023 Framework)

Hard factors: - Clear structural advantages (even if subtle) - Durable competitive position - Resilient cash generation - Low capital intensity often

Soft factors: - Mission-driven culture (objective pursued for its own sake) - Exceptional customer relationships - Natural talent attraction - Continuous improvement mindset - Decision-making aligned with mission, not just profit

Valuation: - Usually trading at modest multiple (8-14x FCF, not 20x+) - Underappreciated because growth is steady, not explosive - Rich opportunity for 15-20% IRR over 3-5Y

Where to Find BFF-Type Companies

"High cadence parsing through [investment universe]" — rapid screening to find candidates, then deep analysis on handful that show BFF characteristics.

Not a passive strategy. Requires: 1. Comprehensive universe knowledge 2. Pattern recognition for soft SRCA signals 3. Deep diligence on team/culture 4. Conviction to buy businesses that "don't look quality"

Richard's 2023 Portfolio Tilt

Explicit shift toward BFF-type companies in 2023, away from: - High-growth "scare stories" (needs extreme precision on forecasts) - Hyped platforms/networks (paying premium for obvious advantages) - Transformations (too much binary risk)

Watson Coaching Prompts — BFF Framework

  1. On candidate screening: When scanning your universe, what signals do you look for to identify BFF-type candidates (vs. obvious growth or value)?

  2. On soft SRCA assessment: Can you articulate the mission/culture of this company without reference to its financials? If you can't, it's not BFF-type.

  3. On mispricingness: Why is this company trading at 10x FCF instead of 15x? Is it temporary mispricedness or structural lack of market appreciation for soft SRCA?

  4. On three-year case: For each BFF-type company, can you build 15-20% IRR case with modest growth assumptions? If not, it's expensive relative to opportunity.

23. DAILY EXECUTION SCORING & MENTAL ROUTINES (May 2023 Onwards)

Source: May 14, 2023 (integrated from "With Winning in Mind" by Lanny Bassham, foundational sports psychology).

The Paradigm Shift: Execution > Outcomes

Direct quote (May 14, 2023): "I don't think about winning and outcomes in the middle of the investing day. I focus on executing my mental routines and my investing fundamentals."

Key principle: Obsess over the process (execution quality), not the score (returns).

Daily Execution Scoring System

Richard adopted Bassham's sports psychology framework:

The scoring: "I rate my score daily. Just like the alternative golf scoring system of rating your execution each shot out of 10, not the number of shots I take."

Questions for daily self-rating (after each day/decision): - Did I follow my decision-making process? (IAJA loop, red-line checking, conviction assessment) - Did I stay in Observer mode (calm, clear) or drift into Chimp mode (reactive, emotional)? - Did I execute my mental routines (breathing, clarity check, confidence affirmation)? - Did I prioritize 18-4+ thinking, or get dragged into 0-6 noise? - If I trimmed/exited, was it from conviction or emotion? - Did I communicate clearly with team on what matters?

Not scored: Returns that day. Only execution quality.

Why this works: Over weeks/months, high execution quality (9/10 days) naturally produces good returns. Returns become lagging indicator of execution, not leading target.

Mental Routine Architecture (Pre-Decision)

Before major allocation/sizing decision:

  1. Breathing routine (2 minutes, Wim Hof style or simple 4-count in, 6-count out)
  2. Clarity check — "Am I in Observer mode (calm, clear) or Chimp mode (emotional)?"
  3. Conviction assessment — "Do I have 8/10+ conviction on 18-4+ case?"
  4. Framework review — Check sizing framework, red-line criteria, conviction checklist
  5. Confidence affirmation — "I have world-class investment mind. I have done my work. I trust my judgment."

Time required: 5-10 minutes. Built into daily routine at same time (e.g., 1000h market review).

The Cookie Jar (Positive Reinforcement)

From Steve Ward coaching: maintain running log of: - Execution wins (days scored 9-10/10) - Great decisions (correct conviction, perfect timing) - Contrarian calls that worked out - Team wins and breakthroughs

Reviewed: Weekly, to build internal reference point independent of recent returns.

Accountability & Metrics

The system makes accountability simple:

Monthly review question: "What % of days did I execute at 8/10 or higher?" (Target: 70%+)

If <70%, it's not markets; it's process/execution.

25. APPENDIX A: MINERVINI FRAMEWORK — QUANTIFIED POSITION MANAGEMENT (from Notion JOURNAL, 06-08 Apr 2026)

Source: https://www.notion.so/33c35e90-9b0b-8087-81c9-ee55bb4853c9

4-Slug Capital Deployment Model

  1. Max Acceptable Loss Per Slug: 50bps of AUM - If stop is 20% from pivot, slug size = ~0.25% of AUM - Determines position size mechanically — not conviction alone

  2. Max Loss Per Investment: 2.0% of AUM - Up to 4 slugs per stock (5%, 4%, 3%, 2% of AUM) - With graduated stop-losses (50D, 100D, 150D MA) - Realistic max loss might be 2.5% if blow-through to 200D

  3. Graduated Stop-Losses: - Slug 1: 50-day MA stop - Slug 2: 100-day MA stop - Slug 3: 150-day MA stop - 200-day MA breach = FULL EXIT of all slugs (inviolable)

  4. Pyramiding In: - Add capital only after successful test of pullback on declining volume - Stock rebounds/tests specific MA for that slug - Each new slug gets its own wider MA stop

  5. Pullback Counting (Trend Exhaustion Signal): - 1-2 pullbacks = Green (low risk) - 3rd pullback = Amber - 4-5+ pullbacks = Red (high trend exhaustion risk)

  6. De-Risking: - Tighten stops (100D → 50D) if concerned - OR sell the slug with widest stop to reduce total risk

Probing Bet Slug (Pre-Minervini): One speculative entry allowed without full 8-point screen, but must have tight trailing 20D or 50D stop. Max loss 50bps. If rebound occurs, add Minervini slugs to compound.

Van Tharp: Position Sizing as Primary Determinant

Source: https://www.notion.so/33735e90-9b0b-819eafc8e5e803abc58b

"Position sizing accounts for 91.5% of variability in returns, not stock selection."

  • Expected return per position = expectancy × average risk per position
  • If expectancy is +1.265R and average risk is 0.7%, expected return per cycle = 0.89%
  • The constraint is not "pick winners." The constraint is "size them correctly."

This supports the 50bps max loss rule and 4-slug structure. Size determined by: (1) Max portfolio risk per trade (1.5% AUM), (2) Stop-loss distance from entry, (3) Dollar risk constraint.
