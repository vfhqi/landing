Investment Strategy

Core Belief

"Doing investing" is not the top priority. Executing the system in an appropriately innovative and dynamic manner to prioritise profiting in the environment — delivering on the investing ETC — that is the priority.

Target: 25%+ IRR with high predictability. Every dimension must rate A/B or top quartile — anything less is "fail."

Method: Conservative analysis, aggressive execution. Simple + completed beats complicated + incomplete.

Painful Prudence / 9-10/10 Bar (Oct 2023)

Investment entry standard: only own stocks where Richard is genuinely 9/10 to 10/10 convicted. This is not arrogance — it's market realism. The opportunity cost of capital is high. If a stock is only 7/10, the capital is better deployed. "Painful prudence" = saying no to many ideas that look reasonable but aren't exceptional.

The discipline: before entry, run the ACH (Analysis of Competing Hypotheses) test ruthlessly. Can you disprove "no" and "false friend"? Only when both are disproven with high confidence is it a "yes" worth acting on.

BFF Framework: Quality That Doesn't Look Like Quality (May 2023)

"Best Friend Forever" stocks are paradoxically less obvious. They are not the obvious "quality" that everyone owns. They are: - Solid, under-the-radar businesses with pricing power - Trading at reasonable multiples because market is indifferent or slightly negative - Clear mid-term catalysts that will reprove quality - Minimal visibility competition; not crowded trades - Management with proven track record of delivery without fanfare

The edge: owning quality when it looks ordinary. The crowd owns obvious quality (expensive). The opportunity is owning future-obvious quality while it still looks boring.

Examples: Diploma (supply chain positioning without glitz), Fevertree (category builder in spirits, quietly commanding premium), Ryanair (hated cyclical but best-in-class operator).

1% Companies Concept (Hard + Soft S.R.C.A.)

Businesses that are in the top 1% of their sectors on combined metrics: - Hard S.R.C.A. (Sustainable Relative Competitive Advantage): Pricing power, switching costs, network effects, moats - Soft S.R.C.A.: Culture, leadership quality, hiring standards, execution track record, governance

The insight: a company with only hard advantages is fragile (moats can erode). A company with both hard + soft is structurally resilient. 1% companies have both in abundance. They are rare and command premium valuations — but deserve them.

High Quality Inflections (HQI) Framework

Richard's approach centres on High Quality Inflections (HQI) — the intersection of business quality and inflection/transformation. A HQI candidate exhibits:

Quality Dimensions: - Substantial mid-to-long-term growth (3Y+) - Strong secular tailwinds (5%+ minimum, preferably 8%+ for 5Y+) - Evidence of continual "Greater Than Average" (GTA) emergence — the company consistently finds ways to expand revenues and margins beyond simple top-line growth - Excellent execution cultures and leadership alignment - Defensive/recurring/forecastable revenues with demonstrated pricing power - Absence of existential risks over 5-10 year horizon

Stock Categories: - "Gravitational pull" stocks (VHQ): Top tier — network effects, economies of scale, consolidating sectors. Highest conviction, highest sizing. - UHPYHQI (Ultra High Potential but Young/Fragile HQIs): Exceptional long-term thesis but young/unproven. Require special care and higher conviction thresholds. Size accordingly at 0-5%. - "Quality compounders with spice": Good underlying businesses hitting temporary air pockets, presenting entry opportunities. Repricing play. - MQBI (Medium Quality Big Inflection at value): Legitimate category but limited long-term upside. Size modestly (6-8%).

Quality Signals vs Deterioration: - Tennis balls: Solid operating momentum, will rebound to trend. Safe to hold through noise. - Eggs: Negative momentum, structural deterioration. High risk — exit first, re-attack only after troughing evidence.

Mission-Driven Culture as Competitive Advantage: Companies with mission-aligned cultures (employees genuinely motivated by the mission, not just paycheck) have lower turnover, higher productivity, and better execution. This is soft S.R.C.A. Visible through: employee engagement data, interview feedback, management communication patterns, pricing power (employees stay motivated through pricing pressure without resentment).

Goldilocks Growth 20-30% (Confirmed Jan 2022): Sweet spot for growth rate. 10% growth is too slow (inflection unlikely, defensive positioning may deteriorate). 50-70% growth is cognitively hard to forecast, prone to stumbles and multiple compression. 20-30% growth is "10x easier to forecast" and allows for reasonable valuation expansion as confidence increases. This is the target growth profile for new positions.

Portfolio Constraints — No Treadmills, Seek Flywheels: Treadmill = business that requires constant effort to maintain position (commodity inputs, cyclical demand, thin margins, capital intensity). Flywheel = business that gets stronger as it scales (network effects, pricing power, margin expansion from scale, customer lock-in). Portfolio should be biased toward flywheels. When holding a treadmill (temporary opportunity), size it small and set clear exit criteria.

The Four Pillars

Pillar 1: What Makes a Great Investment

Richard works "right to left" — start with the financial output (predictable 18M-3Y EPS), then work backwards to understand what investment case delivers it, then what inputs and transmission mechanism deliver the investment case.

The singular obsession is predictable 18M-3Y EPS.

The best ideas are confluences of at least two, ideally three, of four simple patterns: 1. Animal CEO — the Slootman archetype: relentless, commercial, ambitious 2. Massive corporate change plan — big restructuring, strategic pivot, or transformation 3. Huge demand explosion at sector level — structural theme or demand pulse 4. Very large share price decline onto bottom quartile multiples

Richard explicitly inverts: he does not solve for "yes." He disproves why it's a "no" or a "false friend." If it survives disproval, it might be an investment. This is the ACH (Analysis of Competing Hypotheses) approach — profiles of "no," "false friend," and "yes" along one axis, dimensions of evidence along the other.

Revenue Optionality Framework

"Make to explode this one on the wall in the office."

Revenue optionality is a critical quality dimension. Richard seeks companies with multiple sources of growth optionality: - M&A expansion optionality — ability to consolidate fragmented markets or adjacent spaces - Geographic expansion optionality — capacity to move into new countries/regions (especially emerging markets for European businesses) - Product expansion optionality — adjacent products/services leveraging existing customer relationships and infrastructure - Pricing optionality — ability to raise prices via value creation or contracting structures

Key criteria for optionality assessment: - Multiple sources of growth optionality (not dependent on one large binary project) - Record of continual GTA (Greater Than Average) emergence — company demonstrates repeated ability to find incremental growth - "Goldilocks" growth pattern — not too lumpy. Consistent, sustainable delivery of GTA above organic baseline

Companies that can layer multiple optionality sources are substantially de-risked and command premium valuations. This sits at the heart of HQI framework.

Quality Assessment — Three-Layer Reductionist Framework

A. Normalised Growth (Revenue Optionality Framework): - Recurring, defensive, resilient revenue streams (mission-critical to customers) - Diverse customer base (avoid concentration; limit top-5 customers to <25-30% of revenue) - Secular growth tailwinds (5%+ headwind minimum for thesis resilience) - Company aggressively "grabs" revenue and improves quality (not just chasing size) - Multiple sources of growth optionality (M&A, geographic, product, pricing) - Record of continual GTA emergence — quantifiable upside surprise history

B. Strategy / Hard SRCA (Sustainable Relative Competitive Advantage) / Barriers to Entry / Sector Structure: - Win-win-win strategies: Customer-centric (solving real pain), ecosystem symbiosis (partners benefit), massive relative surplus customer utility (company captures minority of value created) - Moat dimensions (hard competitive advantage sources): - Pricing power (demonstrated in market, not theoretical) - Customer loyalty and switching costs (high friction to leave) - Network effects (value increases with scale; re-Twitch, payment networks, platforms) - Vertical integration as BtE (control of critical inputs/distribution) - Negative working capital cycles (customers pay before supplier pays — float benefit) - Information asymmetries (proprietary data, analytics, algorithms) - Lock-in (multi-year contracts, high exit costs, deep technical integration) - Brand, trust, reputation (premium pricing justified by reliability) - Unique resource access (mineral rights, spectrum, skilled labour pools) - Regulatory protection (licenses, certifications, patent moats) - Deep process integration with customers (switching costs spike over time)

C. People / Culture / Governance / Execution (Soft SRCA): - Founder-involved or owner-operator leadership (ideally with meaningful board ownership) - Top quartile management team — assess in person. Look for: - Commercial nous and customer obsession - Hiring standards (best people attracting best people) - Strategic clarity and adaptability - Track record of delivery - Right vision, culture, values, winning behaviours - Alignment of interests — delineate compensation (follow the money: is CEO compensated for growth, margins, ROIC, or share price?) - Governance and self-imposed standards (Audit Committee, Remco independence, disclosure quality)

Pillar 2: Setup Profiles

Three acceptable profiles: - Predictable CfC: Bankable earnings + change catalyst + reasonable multiple - EPT (Earnings Power Transformation): Earnings power transformation from demand surge - EPSU (Earnings Power Step-Up): Earnings power step-up from internal change/push

Six sub-setups detail how these manifest in practice — from big corporate change plays to oversold rebounding quality compounders to demand pulse transformations.

Unacceptable setups (avoid): - Silver medallists (second-best operators/businesses) - Weak management - Large call options with no edge - Event-driven names where you'd exit on a negative node - Generic "sorta quality maybe" pitches - Companies with <100 employees or <$5m daily liquidity

False friends (most dangerous): - Setups that look good but have structural flaws - Defining characteristic: zero clarity of the transmission mechanism from company actions to EPS - If Richard cannot clearly explain how strategy translates into financial outputs — and predict what tracking data will look like quarter by quarter — it's a false friend - Historical examples: HBX (no clarity of tracking inputs to EPS), Adevinta (no "soul"), S4 (egotism in communication), Nexi (deep value but defensive strategy only) - "Pret sandwich risk" — nothing individually inoffensive but the collection is fatal

Pillar 3: The Predictability Obsession

8-step EPS predictability checklist: 1. Can I clearly bridge strategy to financial outputs? 2. Is guidance conservative vs. modal case? 3. Do I understand the transmission mechanism? 4. Will IR give me useful intra-earnings operating momentum? 5. Can I track quantitative data on key drivers? 6. Can I Bayesian update my forecasts as data comes in? 7. Do I have high-frequency/fortnightly tracking data on leading indicators? 8. Can I predict quarterly consensus trajectory and edge opportunities?

Key insight: More information does not equal better judgements. What matters is having the right information channels (trackable, quantitative, cross-referenceable) and the right mental framework to process them rapidly.

Pillar 4: Operator Quality

Three dimensions of a great operator: - Right direction: Big ambition, great strategy with growth as priority, dynamic strategy for range of environments - Right resources: Great CEO measured via action, hiring as top priority, well-resourced/well-invested business, holistic congruent culture, conservative guidance, clear communication - Right record/action/behaviour: High RONIC allocation, acts not just talks, outlier standards, shareholders first, dynamic buybacks, prudent governance

"I love Slootman operators with big ambition with holistic/congruent cultures. I detest Meh-ness."

The lower the quality of the business/sector/value chain, the more essential the great operator. A "C" rated operator needs "B" or better on everything else.

Conviction Assessment — Eight Elements

Conviction is not purely analytical. It is an emotion grounded in deep evidence integration.

Eight elements of conviction: 1. Gut feel — Intuition AFTER analysis, not before. Pattern recognition from years of study. Trust it if backed by elements 2-8. 2. Conviction in framework inputs — Believability of quality ratings. Have you stress-tested the quality thesis? Are the moats real? Is the operator actually top quartile? 3. Gathering information widely and recently — Minimum ≥5 primary research checks in last 3 months: management meetings, customer checks, supplier checks, competitor intel, trade/regulatory monitoring. 4. Circle of deep expertise — Years following company/industry. Have you invested in this space before? Deep familiarity with sector dynamics, competitive landscape, cyclical patterns? 5. Eyes-on work — Done own financial model? Written own memo? Re-read all broker memos, call transcripts, and investor letters? Full day of refamiliarisation in last month? 6. Recent bear case exploration — "Two Hat" brainstorm with analyst or peer. Have you stress-tested the downside? What would invalidate the thesis? 7. Pre-mortems and mid-mortems — Actively tested downside scenarios. "The company delivers zero growth for 18 months. What happens?" Model fragility impact. 8. Position monitoring quality — Strong link to monitoring discipline. Are you tracking fortnightly? Rating deterioration? Testing fragility assumptions monthly?

Key insight: "Conviction is an emotion. You can't take emotion out of conviction." (Denise Shull, The Intuitive Investor). The goal is to anchor that emotion in deep, multi-faceted evidence.

Guiding Principles (10 Principles from Journals)

  1. Discretion is the better part of valour — Act ex ante on probabilistic deterioration in 12-18M forward EPS
  2. Track and flag — Define all drivers, track fortnightly, rate deterioration RED, review monthly
  3. Exit early and re-attack once concern cleared — Visualise quick cut and re-attack lower
  4. Form a judgement, then invert — Run lessons bible + ACH. Embrace inversion
  5. Worser, odder, longer, further — Things can get worse than expected, in stranger ways, for longer
  6. Never try to solve deteriorations analytically — Wait until troughing pattern
  7. Meet tonnes of management — First-hand evidence of operator quality
  8. Rate tonnes of stocks — Breadth creates context for depth
  9. Focus purely on making money — Being analytically right ex ante is irrelevant (Mark Douglas)
  10. Do the mundane consistently — Foundation of high performance. What would Flow State Richard do?

Fragilities Assessment Checklist

"Forces × Fragilities = Movement" — a company's resilience stems from its defences against macro shocks, political/regulatory risks, industry disruption, competitive threats, management/execution risks, and exogenous shocks.

Assess fragility across every dimension:

Revenue Fragility: - Revenue stability across range of market conditions (recession, inflation, demand destruction) - Customer concentration (top 5 customers >30% of revenue = high fragility) - Contract duration/renewal risk (short-term contracts = high risk in downturns) - Pricing power/pass-through in inflation - Discretionary vs contractual (government contracts = lower fragility)

Margin Fragility: - Fixed cost operating leverage (N2-3Q and N1-2Y: will margins compress rapidly in slowdown?) - Cost stability vs uncertainty (inflation pass-through, commodity exposure, labour cost inflation) - Working capital efficiency (can the company absorb NWC expansion in downturn?)

Financial Fragility: - P&L leverage (interest expense; debt service coverage) - Net working capital fragility (cash conversion cycle; inventory buildup risk) - Free cash flow fragility (committed capex, pension obligations, earn-outs) - Share price leverage (net debt quantum; covenant ratios)

Market Structure Fragility: - Short interest levels (susceptibility to forced short covering, narrative risk) - Liquidity (ADV, bid-ask spread; $10m+ ADV minimum) - Shareholder base (loyal/long-term vs speculative/momentum-driven) - Industry/market sentiment (is the stock/industry cyclically momentum-driven?)

On Deterioration and Exits

Two-mode approach: pre-ownership = assess and seek quality/predictability. Once live = total dispassionate assessment with paranoid tilt.

Frame exits as "reallocating capital," not "accepting a loss."

Disprove-or-exit framework: Any emergence of deterioration triggers a 30-day shot clock, default is reduce to 0%, position can be re-argued from zero immediately if risk is genuinely disproved.

Fundamental exit triggers: - Erosion in a driver that would reduce 18M EPS by 5%+ or trading multiple by 20%+ - Value chain deterioration - Insufficient operator quality

Technical exit overlay (Minervini-derived): The following technical signals, when accumulating, indicate the market is ahead of the fundamental analysis. These do not replace the fundamental triggers — they supplement and often lead them. A stock breaking its 50-day SMA on heavy volume shortly after a catalyst event is the market telling you something your fundamental analysis hasn't captured yet.

Technical signals that escalate the shot clock from 30 days to immediate/2 weeks:

  • Stage 3 confirmed (50-day crosses below 150-day): Bullish stack broken. Reduce to bench or exit unless fundamental thesis is definitively intact and re-tested.
  • Three or more simultaneous violations: (1) Close below 20-day post-breakout; (2) Close below 50-day on above-average volume; (3) 3+ consecutive lower lows on increasing volume; (4) Low volume on advance, high volume on reversal; (5) More down days than up days; (6) More bad closes than good closes; (7) Full retracement of meaningful gain; (8) Close below pivot on volume.
  • Volume distribution pattern: Down days on above-average volume, up days on below-average volume — confirmed over 2+ weeks.

Practical rule: Technical alone (with strong fundamentals) = tighten and monitor, escalate shot clock. Fundamental alone (no technical signals) = standard 30-day shot clock. Technical + fundamental simultaneously = exit decisively.

Full technical exit checklist: See memory/coaching/risk-management-lessons.md §3a and memory/skills/assistant-portfolio-manager/annexes/annex-01-position-management.md §Technical Exit Overlay.

Internal Changes / Push Checklist

Maps every dimension of potential positive internal change — the company's own actions to improve financial performance. These are high-priority triggers for workflow prioritisation.

Leadership / Governance: - Leadership changes (new CEO with track record, strong CFO hire, board refresh) - Ambition/target increases (revisited 3Y plan, margin target expansion, acquisition pipeline opening) - Strategic priority shifts (exiting low-return businesses, pivoting to higher-margin product mix, geographic expansion acceleration)

Growth Building Blocks: - New product launches (with credible distribution/pricing power) - Market expansion (new geographies, new customer segments, new distribution channels) - M&A integration (accretive acquisitions coming on-stream; synergy realisations) - Pricing actions (demonstrated ability to raise prices above inflation)

Financial Improvements: - Revenue growth acceleration (from internal initiatives, not just market tailwinds) - Margin expansion (operating leverage, mix improvement, cost restructuring realisations) - Leverage reduction (debt paydown, improving debt metrics) - Cash conversion improvement (NWC optimisation, capex efficiency)

Organisational / Operational: - Restructuring/rightsizing (one-time costs now behind, margin/EPS accretion ahead) - Sales force expansion or quality upgrade - M&A/divestitures (exiting low-return assets; consolidating platform) - Changes in investment intensity (transitioning from heavy capex phase to maintenance capex)

Capital Allocation / Messaging: - Improved stock market messaging/IR discipline - Capital return increases (buyback acceleration, special dividends) - Covenant relaxation (indicating improved confidence)

Most Attractive Scenario: Company emerging from large investment phase — multi-year margin/EPS depression from doing the right thing (new platform investment, market entry, product R&D), then revenue growth accelerates, margins expand as one-off costs fall away and operating leverage materialises. This is the "Q-turn" setup: Quality company turning from investment phase to harvest phase.

"Any change on any of these fronts — the more the better — is a high, high priority for workflow prioritisation."

Portfolio Architecture (from 1Q26 OKRs)

Fixed structure: 16 stocks - Split: 8 risk-on, 8 risk-off (overlay adjustable) - Target condition: prevent large drawdowns through diversification

Five diversification dimensions: 1. Industry/business model (limit +15% vs Eurostoxx) 2. End-market geography (countries, not regions; limit +15% vs Eurostoxx) 3. Listing geography (countries, not regions; limit +10% vs Eurostoxx) 4. Demand profile (consumer/corporate/government; contracted/defensive/deferrable/discretionary; cyclical/non-cyclical) 5. Setup/profile diversification (6 setups + lifecycle stages)

Position sizing framework: - Resilient/Intense HQI: 15% - Core: 10% (top up back to 10% on sell-off to 6-8%, top-slice back to 10% on re-rate) - Resilient/Low Optionality: ~8% - Ultra High Potential but Fragile/Low Conviction: 0-5%

Advanced sizing dynamics: - 80% of max sizing approach — Avoids being caught in pullbacks that precede new highs. If theoretical max is 12%, hold at ~10% until case fully proved/framework crystallised. - Stock price noise tolerance — Stock price can fall 10-15% on temporary momentum loss or noise before rounding to new highs; top-ups valuable, cuts risky. Distinguish noise from structural deterioration. - Theoretical size is the most important point — BFF (Best Friend Forever stock) could merit 12% even if framework says 8%; sizing is Bayesian, not formulaic. Use "theoretical size" as anchor, then apply judgment on entry/conviction. - Dynamic Bayesian updating — Stay 3 steps ahead, not 1. Update mental model intra-quarter as data arrives. Rebalance sizing as conviction/framework confidence change.

Base Counting Overlay for Position Sizing (Minervini — added 15-Apr-26): The base count of a stock provides an objective, technical sizing calibration that is independent of the fundamental view. Former leaders have a 50% probability of declining 80% from peak and 80% probability of declining 50% — often while the fundamental thesis still looks intact. Base counting prevents oversized positions in late-stage advances.

Base count Risk level Sizing implication
1st–2nd base Lowest Full intended position. Full aggression.
3rd base Inflection — reduce Cut 25–50% of position. Tighten stops. Stop adding. Flag to APM.
4th–5th base Highest Tactical position only (50%+ cut from normal size). Very tight stops (3–5%). Quick exit mindset.

A "base" is a consolidation period following an advance, ideally forming a VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern — contracting price ranges with declining volume). The count resets only after a stock undergoes a full Stage 4 decline and re-advances from a new Stage 1 base.

APM responsibility: Track base count for all live positions. Proactively flag when a stock reaches its 3rd base. - Avoid going too big too soon in unformed theses — UHPYHQIs and early-stage ideas (0-5% sizing). Wait for framework crystallisation, multiple data points, thesis de-risking before going to Core (10%). - Don't fully exit stocks you have concerns about — Use bench as extension of portfolio. Hold 3-5% to "hear the company out," re-attack from bench if concern disproved. - If worried about share price over-extension — Size at 2/3rds of theoretical (8% → 5%). Maintains exposure, caps downside, leaves room for re-entry. - Sell "meh" when quality × inflection is mediocre — If you can't articulate clear inflection + quality framework, mirage upside is high. Reallocate to higher-conviction ideas.

Themes and S-Curves

Informed by studying ARK's Cathy Wood framework: disruption occurs through "stacking of S-curves" (sustained innovation is a series of overlapping S-curves with increasing adoption rates) and "co-evolution of disruption technologies" (adjacent technologies simultaneously enable each other — e.g. GPUs enabling AI, AI enabling multiomic sequencing enabling drug discovery).

For European investor seeking scarcity premium: - Find listed companies exposed to themes that command scarcity premium (early-stage large-cap gains on structural growth) - Focus on "deliverability doesn't matter for first 12 months" — first part of S-curve is about changing expectations without need for proof - Build positions in companies well-positioned on the S-curve's inflection point

Critical caveat: "Most demand pulses totally reverse to trend. Assume 100% cyclical/temporary unless proven structural." Only hold for structural tailwind themes, not cyclical demand pulses.
