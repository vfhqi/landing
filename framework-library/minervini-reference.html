The bottom line

Minervini's rules are built for trades that last three to four weeks; Viewforth holds for 12 to 24 months. So this document keeps his architecture (define the stop before buying, move it up and never widen it, count the bases, read the warning signs after a breakout, protect a 20% profit) and sets the widths itself, labelled ours.

Every rule below shows two fields that are never blended: HIS RULE, with a grade saying how well it is sourced, and OURS, saying what Viewforth actually does with it. Where our live tools disagree with his checked rule, the disagreement is listed in the crosswalk for Richard's ruling; nothing in a live tool has been changed by this document.

Of the 48 rules, 37 are on the Assistant Portfolio Manager's daily core card. The rest are reference: attributions that correct our own files, and facts that frame the translation.

The Trend Template: is the stock in a confirmed uptrend?

4 rules, 4 on the core card. Eight tests, all of which must hold, that he uses to say a stock is in a Stage 2 advance and worth looking at. Our master dashboard, our Stage 2 qualification SOP and our MM 8-Point Notion tags each carry a version of it, and the versions disagree with each other and with him on two numbers; those are crosswalk items 1 to 3.

MM-TT-01 · The Trend Template, eight criteria

grade: minervini-secondarysources S-001, S-005core cardT2
HIS RULE (his teaching, as reported): A stock qualifies as a Stage 2 candidate when all eight hold: (1) price above BOTH the 150-day and 200-day moving averages; (2) the 150-day above the 200-day; (3) the 200-day trending up for at least one month, preferably four to five months or more; (4) the 50-day above both the 150-day and 200-day; (5) price above the 50-day; (6) price at least 30% (2013 book) or 25% (2017 book) above its 52-week low; (7) price within 25% of its 52-week high; (8) relative strength ranking (Investor's Business Daily scale) of at least 70, preferably 80s or 90s.
OURS (his rule adopted; live divergences held for the crosswalk (D-MMW-33)): Keep all eight as his. The reports read do not say whether the averages are simple or exponential (S-024); every Viewforth implementation uses simple averages, and that choice is ours. The live Stage 2 gates use four of them and no 52-week-low test; the Notion tag SOP uses the split variant with no relative-strength test; the dashboard's MM99 test 7 uses 20%. All three are crosswalk findings, unchanged.
Why it matters to a 12 to 24 month holder: This is the gate that says a stock is in an advance worth timing. For a long holder it matters less as a buy trigger than as a reason not to fight the tape: a fundamentally attractive stock that fails the template is early, and early costs time and patience.
Where it bites in our system: Three live tools carry a version of it: the master dashboard's MM99 screen (its test 7 uses 20% above the 52-week low, matching neither book: crosswalk item 1), the MM 8-Point Notion tag SOP (the split variant, 30% low test, no relative-strength test: item 2), and the Stage 2 qualification SOP's four gates (no 52-week-low test and no rising-200-day test: item 3). The daily program's T2 moment quotes it when a Stage 2 anchor forms or breaks.
Note: The count of eight is not the invariant; the content is. Third-party variants split criterion 1 in two and drop the low test to keep the count at eight; that split is the corruption in our MM 8-Point tags (S-001).

Stage analysis: the four stages, and where our clock starts

3 rules, 3 on the core card. The four-stage picture of a stock's life (neglect, advance, top, decline) is Stan Weinstein's, adopted by Minervini. He gives no mechanical rule for when a Stage 2 advance starts or ends, so the base counter has its own, labelled ours. Two meanings of 'Stage 2' therefore coexist in our system on purpose, and the daily program always says which one it is quoting.

MM-ST-01 · Four stages, from Weinstein, adopted by Minervini

grade: other-practitionersources S-006, S-023core card
HIS RULE (another practitioner's, not his): Stage 1 neglect (consolidation; price wavers around its 200-day), Stage 2 advancing (accumulation; price above a rising 200-day), Stage 3 topping (distribution; the 200-day flattens, loses upside momentum, then turns down), Stage 4 declining (capitulation; the 200-day in an obvious downtrend). Named in both books as summarised (S-023); the framework is Stan Weinstein's.
OURS (adopted; chat quantifications labelled viewforth and not in the card): Adopted as is. The quantified stage tests in the chat record (slope within ±1%, one-third-of-sessions rules, eight-week volume tests) are the chat's and are not carried as his.
Why it matters to a 12 to 24 month holder: The stage picture is the reason the whole timing layer exists: buy only in Stage 2, and recognise Stage 3 (distribution, wider and looser swings) before Stage 4 arrives. For a long holder it is the frame for 'is this pullback a base or the start of a top'.
Where it bites in our system: The chat record's quantified stage tests (slope within a percent, one-third-of-sessions rules) are not his and are not carried. The daily program's Stage 2 anchor (MM-ST-03) is the mechanical version of 'Stage 2' this project uses; the Stage 2 qualification SOP's gates are the other.
Lineage: Weinstein (Secrets for Profiting in Bull and Bear Markets), adopted by Minervini

MM-ST-02 · Never buy below a declining 200-day; Stage 1 is watchlist only

grade: minervini-secondarysources S-021, S-015, S-010core cardT2
HIS RULE (his teaching, as reported): He does not buy stocks below a declining 200-day average and does not buy Stage 1; the 'power play' (a very fast advance on a catalyst) is the one situation he enters with a dearth of fundamentals.
OURS (adopted): Adopted as is for the timing layer. Fundamental selection decides what to own (D-MMW-3).
Why it matters to a 12 to 24 month holder: His hardest line: no buying below a declining 200-day average, and nothing in Stage 1 beyond a watchlist. It protects a fundamental investor from the classic error of buying a cheap stock that is still going down.
Where it bites in our system: The master dashboard's MM99 screen tests that the 200-day has been rising for at least a month, but its Stage 2 rating's four gates test only price against the 150- and 200-day, the 150-day against the 200-day, and distance from the 52-week high (criteria 1, 2 and 7 of MM-TT-01); they never ask whether the 200-day is rising (criterion 3) (verified in generate_master_data.py, 11-Sep-2026; crosswalk item 3). The daily program's T2 moment reports when a holding's anchor breaks, which is the moment this rule says the timing case has ended even if the fundamental case has not.

Bases and base count: how far along the advance is

7 rules, 7 on the core card. A base is a pause in an advance; counting them tells you how mature the advance is. MM-BC-01 · Constructive base depth 10–35%; avoid bases correcting more than 60%

grade: minervini-secondarysources S-010core cardT1
HIS RULE (his teaching, as reported): Most constructive bases correct 10 to 35%; avoid those correcting more than 60%. A correction of up to a year can justify a deeper decline.
OURS (his lines adopted as the classes; Viewforth caution at 30% adopted): The counter's classes are exactly these lines: constructive 10–35%, wide-and-loose 35–60%, correction 60%+. No Viewforth number inside them (D-MMW-37). A Viewforth caution flag fires on a base in progress 30% or deeper (`vf_caution_depth` 0.30, ruled D-MMW-47), inside his constructive range; his class is unchanged by it.
Why it matters to a 12 to 24 month holder: Depth is his first quality test of a base: a healthy pause corrects 10 to 35%; deeper than 60% is a stock to avoid. Between the two is 'wide and loose', a base that can still work but fails more often. Richard added a caution flag at 30%, inside his healthy range, because a 12 to 24 month holder should notice a base that deep before it becomes a problem.
Where it bites in our system: The counter's three classes use his lines exactly. The daily program's state line shows the class and the depth for every holding and adds CAUTION at 30% or deeper (on 10-Sep-2026 that flagged HTRO-SE and VLX-GB). T1 fires when the class changes.

MM-BC-02 · Minimum base duration three weeks

grade: minervini-secondarysources S-010core cardT1
HIS RULE (his teaching, as reported): A base needs a minimum of three weeks. (Five weeks is O'Neil's flat-base minimum, not Minervini's.)
OURS (adopted): Counter `min_duration` 15 trading days, peak to breakout.
Why it matters to a 12 to 24 month holder: A pause shorter than three weeks is a pullback, not a base, so it does not count towards maturity. The five-week figure in our chat notes was O'Neil's flat-base minimum, not his.
Where it bites in our system: The counter's minimum duration is 15 trading days, peak to breakout, which is his three weeks. The master dashboard's own base counters use a different construction and are not Minervini counts (MM-BC-07).

OURS (principle adopted; the annex's numbers not adopted): The counter flags `late_stage` at base 4 or later. Why it matters to a 12 to 24 month holder: His maturity read: the first and second bases are the best entries; by the fourth or fifth the trend is obvious to everyone and failures multiply; the decline usually comes after three to five bases. Where it bites in our system: The daily program flags 'late stage' from base 4 and quotes this rule at T1.
