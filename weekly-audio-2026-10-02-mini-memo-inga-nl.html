APM Mini-Memo. ING Groep. The second of October 2026.

ING Groep is new this week at rank six on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

My view is neutral, a working hypothesis. ING Groep has the most predictable earnings engine a bank can offer, hedged deposit income rolling onto higher swap rates and buybacks shrinking the share count, but at thirty point six eight five euros the script's plausible return of eleven point seven percent a year is a D, and Stage three is already Possible. A good business arriving late in its move.

First, what the company does. ING Groep is a large Dutch bank with a market value of about ninety-two billion euros. Most of its income is the gap between what it pays on deposits and what it earns on loans and on the swaps that hedge those deposits, with fees as the second leg. It runs retail banking in the Netherlands, Belgium, Germany, Spain and elsewhere in Europe, with fifteen point four million mobile primary customers at the end of 2025, and a wholesale bank, and is shifting the capital mix to forty-five percent Wholesale and fifty-five percent Retail by 2027.

Second, the thesis. The change-forces digest of the twenty-ninth of July ranks the replicating portfolio first. As the hedging swaps on deposits roll off at about one point seven percent and are replaced at over two and a half percent, management guides one point four billion euros of extra income in 2027, with replicating income of twelve point three billion euros that year on the March 2026 forward curves. Roughly fifty-five percent of the portfolio has one to fifteen years still to run, so this income arrives on a timetable set by contracts already written. Net fee income is targeted above five billion euros by 2027, from four point six billion in 2025, retail fees up thirteen percent in the first quarter of 2026. A cut of about twelve hundred and fifty operations roles in 2026 supports around three hundred and fifty million euros of savings, with 2027 costs guided near thirteen billion euros. And analysts estimate eight billion euros of capital returns over 2026 to 2028, about a third of the market value, so earnings per share grow faster than earnings. The 2027 target is a return on tangible equity above fifteen percent. Track the swap curve, fee income against five billion euros, cost guidance, the common equity tier one ratio against thirteen percent, and the Dutch mortgage floor expiring on the thirtieth of November 2026.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so this read is my own. The Target Setup is Delivering, Serial Acquirer, Mechanical EPS Upgrader, the mechanical element being contracted swap rolls and a shrinking share count, not acquisitions. A secondary Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU sits underneath, in the shift from Wholesale to Retail, the cost programme and the refreshed board, but it is the smaller part. None of the three cause-for-concern setups applies: the shares sit about five percent below their fifty-two week high. The script's Technical Momentum reading of today is A, relative strength eighty-seven, Pulling Back within a medium-term and long-term Uptrend, Stage two Probable.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. There is no Case Riskiness rating because no memo exists. The case is simple to state and moderately risky to hold. The replicating income is contracted, but the digest's bear mechanism is sharp rate cuts compressing unhedged short-term positions, and Morgan Stanley's two percent neutral rate would materially compress the one point four billion euros guided for 2027. The chief financial officer, chief risk officer and chief human resources officer all changed at once, and the common equity tier one ratio slipped ten basis points to thirteen percent in the first quarter, twenty below consensus. The credit tripwire, as the list-maker notes, is the one every bank on the list shares.

Fifth, business quality. Good for a bank, with the usual caveats. First-quarter 2026 profit before tax of two point two six billion euros beat by six percent on higher customer balances. Management sets low targets and upgrades them, retail straight-through processing reached eighty-two percent from seventy-one percent in 2023, and Spain earns a twenty percent retail return on equity. Against that, deposit markets in Germany and the Benelux are fragmented and competitive, digital entrants pay top-of-market savings rates, and this is still a leveraged, rate-sensitive balance sheet.

Sixth, returns and valuation. This is the weak link. FactSet today shows a price to earnings ratio of nine point four times on forward earnings per share of about three point two five euros, at the seventy-eighth percentile of its own history. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of today is D, on the valuation reading only. Its plausible-return arithmetic of yesterday gives nine point three percent a year over eighteen months and fourteen percent over three years, eleven point seven percent on average with dividends, assuming consensus earnings growth of fifteen and a half percent a year against a multiple drifting from nine point four times towards seven point nine times, the D anchor of about twelve percent a year. The bull version is that the multiple holds and the share count falls faster than modelled; the bear version is that the seventy-eighth percentile already prices the swap roll.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of today is A, but on the environment layer only, so it says the banking environment is favourable, not that ING Groep is specially placed in it. On the pro side: capital relief as the Dutch mortgage floor expires, securitisations worth three point four billion euros of relief in late 2025, and investment customers up eight percent to five point two million. On the con side: a rates-and-regulation thesis shared with every European bank, Sell-side Momentum already at A on the script's reading of yesterday, and wage inflation of two point six percent in 2026 working against the cost plan.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. A prolonged Eurozone recession compresses credit demand, raises defaults and forces deposit competition, neutralising the cost and replicating benefits at once. Rate cuts take the neutral rate to two percent and the 2027 uplift shrinks. Meanwhile the shares trade at the seventy-eighth percentile of their own multiple, Stage three is Possible, three executives are new, and the plausible return is eleven point seven percent a year before any of that goes wrong. You would be buying a consensus bank late, for a D return.

The one-line view. Neutral, as a working hypothesis. ING Groep has the most predictable earnings engine a bank can offer, but at today's price the script's plausible return of eleven point seven percent a year is a D and Stage three is already Possible: a good business arriving late in its move.
