Stock notes: the settled stocks only

Every stock in this section is settled: its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back, 30 October to 26 November. Each note is numbered by the stock's row in the table near the end of this note. For every one of the 15 stocks, 6 of the 9 Stage 2 tests were not computed this week: three of the four gates (the price above the 200-day moving average, the price above the 150-day moving average, and the price within 25% of its 52-week high) and all three relative-strength tests. Not computed is a gap in our data, not a fail. The fourth gate, the 150-day moving average above the 200-day, passes for all 15. What differs between the stocks is the rung reached and how long it has held, and the two moving-average tests: the 50-day moving average above the 150-day and above the 200-day.

In general a turn is confirmed when the rung holds: our backtest finds that a rise to Probable has been worth something only once it has held for 20 trading days, and a fresh turn very little. A turn is undone when the rating falls back below Plausible, or when the 150-day moving average drops back below the 200-day. For a stock reached on the screen's last day, the next few builds will show whether the rung holds at all.

The signpost at the start of each bullet, from [+++] to [---], shows whether that fact strengthens or weakens the stock's technical picture; [=] means it does neither.

How to read this note

  • Stage 2. Richard's second stage of a stock's technical life: the sustained advance that follows a base. The rating is the master dashboard's own read on how firmly a stock qualifies. The ladder runs None, Possible, Plausible, Probable
  • The two kinds of trigger. Either the rating is Plausible or Probable now and was lower on the anchor day, or the 150-day moving average crossed from below the 200-day moving average to above it inside the look-back and is still above it now. A move up to Possible alone does not count (Richard's ruling of 10 August 2026). A stock is in this week's note when its first or latest trigger fell inside the week, 20 November to 26 November
  • The look-back and the anchor day. The screen compares each stock's rating on the anchor day, 29 October, with its rating on each of the dashboard's last 20 weekday builds, 30 October to 26 November. This note calls that span the look-back
  • Settled and unsettled; round trips. A stock is settled when its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back. A stock is unsettled when it did, one or more times; each time is a round trip, so an unsettled stock's current reading may be a flicker on a threshold. Only settled stocks get a written note
  • The Stage 2 set. The stocks whose trigger inside the look-back still stands: 152 now. A stock leaves the set when its trigger no longer stands, or when the trigger is older than the look-back although the stock may still be Plausible or Probable
  • Reported here before. The stock appeared in this note in an earlier week, from this project's record of what each week reported
  • The nine Stage 2 tests. The four gates: the price above the 200-day moving average; the price above the 150-day moving average; the 150-day above the 200-day; and the price within 25% of its 52-week high. The two moving-average tests: the 50-day moving average above the 150-day and above the 200-day. The three relative-strength tests: the stock's industry at 70 or above in relative strength; its sector at 70 or above; and the stock itself at 70 or above against its industry. Relative strength here is the composite measure the master dashboard uses. A test the data marks as not computed is a gap in our data, not a fail
  • Our tilt. The industry's or sector's tilt as set on 13 November 2026, on the ladder Overweight (B), Neutral (C), Underweight (D), Double Underweight (F)
  • The signposts. Each bullet in a stock note begins with a marker from [+++] to [---] showing whether its fact strengthens or weakens the stock's technical picture; [=] means it does neither
  • What a turn has been worth in our own backtest. Our backtest (project "APM - Data Analytics - Backtesting", the answer of 2 October 2026 and its addendum of 3 October 2026, which wins where they differ; triggers from January 2002 to 22 September 2026, price returns, the Stage 2 definition of 1 October 2026) finds that a stock whose rating rose to Probable and stayed Probable for 20 trading days then beat the average stock of the same day by 1.4 percentage points over three months and 2.2 over six. A rise to Probable on its first day beat it by only 0.5 points over three months, and only 16% of rises to Probable stay Probable for 20 trading days; longer holds did better (40 days: 1.8 and 3.3 points). The gain comes more from fewer large losses than from more winners: 4.9% of the 20-day triggers ended three months down more than 20%, against 7.1% of all stocks. It is mainly a small-company effect: for companies worth EUR 5 to 50 billion it was 0.5 points over three months and 0.9 over six against stocks of the same size, only borderline reliable. A day at Plausible beat the average stock by only 0.2 points over three months, and the moving-average cross on its own was not tested. So a turn is a prompt to look, not a forecast
  • The group-move bar. A sector qualifies as a group move when at least 3 of its stocks triggered in the week, and a cohort when at least 2 of its stocks did, AND those triggers are at least a quarter (25%) of all that sector's or cohort's stocks in 11-universe-lookup.md (1364 stocks this week). Every trigger counts, settled or not. Where a cohort's triggered stocks are exactly a sector's triggered stocks, the two are one group, reported once under the cohort. Richard's rule of 28 October 2026: 3 for a sector and 2 for a cohort, because cohorts are smaller
