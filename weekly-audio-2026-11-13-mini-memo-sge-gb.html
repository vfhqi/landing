APM Mini-Memo. Sage. The thirteenth of November 2026.

Sage is new this week at rank one on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The one-line view. Sage is a highly predictable software compounder on a bottom-of-range multiple, and the story depends on margins rising as artificial intelligence pricing spreads.

First, what the company does.

Sage sells accounting and business software, by subscription, to about three million small and medium-sized businesses. Annual recurring revenue is about two point seven billion pounds, growing eleven per cent. Ninety-seven per cent of revenue recurs. Sage reaches its customers through forty thousand accountancy practices and thirty thousand resellers. That channel makes the software hard to dislodge.

North America is the growth engine. North America grew fourteen per cent, led by the cloud finance product Sage Intacct at twenty-six per cent. The UK and Ireland grew ten per cent, and Europe seven.

Second, the thesis.

The story is a steady compounder whose earnings grow faster than its sales. Sales grow about ten per cent a year. Customers renew at a hundred and two per cent of last year's value. Price adds more than five per cent, linked to the Copilot assistant. Earnings per share then grow faster than sales, through a rising margin and through buybacks. The margin was just under twenty-four per cent in the first half, up almost a point on the year.

Here is what is improving now. First-half growth beat consensus, and management raised guidance, from a team with a habit of caution. Sage has also hired new product, strategy and growth chiefs to scale software powered by artificial intelligence. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twentieth of July rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength B. The drivers are credible, and ten per cent growth trails the seventeen per cent or more of premium software peers.

You have three drivers to track. Recurring revenue growth should hold above ten per cent, with a warning below nine. North American growth should stay above twelve per cent. And the margin should move from twenty-four per cent towards the peer median of about twenty-five. The lever for the margin is pricing. Sage's artificial intelligence tiers in the UK carried price lifts of twenty-five to thirty per cent, and the task is to repeat those lifts abroad.

Third, the Target Setup.

The Target Setup is CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twentieth of July rated Setups' Fit/Crispness B. That memo's primary fit was a concern clearing in a high-quality compounder. Matching that wording to your named setup is my own mapping. The cause for concern is that artificial intelligence and stronger rivals squeeze a mid-market vendor. The multiple is about fifteen times earnings, in the bottom one per cent of its ten-year history.

The clearing has begun. The share price was about eight hundred and forty-five pence in mid-July. On the thirteenth of November the share price was eleven hundred and eighty pence, two per cent below the year's high. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of the thirteenth of November is B, where the July memo had D. Our chart screen rates a new uptrend as probable. Demand-driven EPSU is a poor fit, because growth is steady rather than inflecting.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

The case is moderately risky. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twentieth of July rated Case Riskiness C, on a scale where A is simple and low-risk. The core is simple, one recurring revenue engine. Three regions, several product lines and two or three drivers that must all work make the case less simple.

The main risk is a squeezed middle that shows up as a stalled margin. Sage sits between fast small-business platforms, such as Intuit and Xero, and the large suites of SAP and Oracle. Suppose that squeeze holds the margin near twenty-three per cent, which is the bear case at Deutsche Bank. Earnings per share would then grow only as fast as sales, and the low multiple would be deserved. Debt is at the top of Sage's own range, at two times operating profit before depreciation. In the bear case, that debt would limit buybacks.

Three things need to go right. The margin must keep rising each half. The UK price lifts must repeat in other countries. And Sage Intacct must gain pace in Europe. Track the renewal rate as well. A renewal rate moving from a hundred and two towards a hundred and three says customers accept the pricing. A renewal rate below a hundred and one is the warning.

Fifth, business quality.

Sage is high quality on cash and predictability, and upper-middle on growth and margin. Cash conversion was a hundred and sixteen per cent, and has been above a hundred for seven years running. That record is in the top five per cent of European software peers. Recurring revenue and cautious guidance suit your test of predictable earnings per share. The margin sits a point and a half below the peer median.

The quality grade is provisional. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twentieth of July rated Foundations' Robustness C. That memo drew on one research report, on the forces of change. The report on the business foundations had not been written.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

The return reads A, and the A is unproven. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of the thirteenth of November is A, set on valuation alone. The weekly routine's plausible return is about fifty per cent a year over eighteen months, and thirty per cent a year over three years. Your bar is twenty-five. Consensus earnings growth is about twelve per cent a year, which is a D-grade return by itself. So the A comes from the multiple rising from about fifteen times to about twenty-two times. Twenty-two times is the routine's fair multiple, taken from the ten-year history.

The multiple needs checking first. Our files show the same fifteen times earnings in July and today. The share price has risen forty per cent between those two dates. Earnings growing twelve per cent a year do not explain that gap, so one of the two readings is stale. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of the twelfth of November is C, where the July memo had B. Analysts' estimates have not yet followed the chart.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

The pro is that Sage holds the accounting record. Our sector note of the thirteenth of October sorts software by how the vendor charges. That note places Sage among the toll collectors of the mid-market, whose billable activity grows as artificial intelligence automates work. The sector is recovering one company at a time. The median enterprise software share fell about fifteen per cent over twelve months, and rose about eighteen per cent over the latest three. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of the thirteenth of November is B, read from the environment only.

The con is that the window has no dated end. The advantage lasts until artificial intelligence agents bypass the application layer, and nobody dates that. Europe, growing seven per cent, is the weak region.

The next step in our files is a re-read of the July memo against Sage's full-year results. The July memo expected those results in November, and they are not yet in our files.

The one-line view. Sage is a highly predictable software compounder on a bottom-of-range multiple, and the story depends on margins rising as artificial intelligence pricing spreads.
