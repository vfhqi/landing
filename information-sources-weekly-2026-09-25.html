Goldman Sachs (Sarah Dong, 24 Sep) reports developed-market input prices reaccelerating in September, with US services prices at their highest since November 2022 (Jan Hatzius, 23 Sep). Goldman Sachs (Mei, 24 Sep) counters that much of the move is nominal bias, with overheating confined to a few AI-linked manufacturing industries.

Disagreement: Dong and Hatzius read pressure building; Mei reads a healthy economy.

Why it matters: this is the channel by which higher-for-longer rates reach European discount rates.

2. Economic health: resilient growth, a labour-supply question

Barclays (Macro Outlook, 24 Sep) says growth is proving more resilient than expected despite tightening from the Federal Reserve (Fed) and other central banks: the US expands solidly, Europe has surprised on the upside and China's slowdown is manageable. Barclays (Jonathan Millar, 24 Sep) cautions that microdata show a sharper labour-supply fall than the aggregates, concentrated among non-citizens. Goldman Sachs (Mei, 24 Sep) reads the labour market as stabilised and in balance.

Disagreement: Millar's shrinking labour supply against Mei's balanced labour market; both imply firm wage pressure.

Why it matters: resilient US and European growth underpin Barclays's equity view; a labour-supply squeeze is why hikes could persist.

TrendStable to firmer

Disagreement: none on direction; Fiotakis says past French risk events were buying opportunities, but complacency is not justified now.

Why it matters: sovereign credibility sets the European discount rate and the French spread.

1. AI capital spending: the revenue it must earn, and the sceptics

Goldman Sachs (Hammond, 24 Sep) sets the bar: hyperscalers need about $300bn of annual AI revenue to break even on 2026-27 capex, and users would need to spend roughly $1tn a year on AI applications, against $1.5tn of global software spending today. He stays constructive because cloud revenue is running $70bn above the pre-AI trend, backlogs exceed $1.5tn, and some semiconductor earnings "would have to decline substantially" just to trade at historical multiples. Barclays (Macro Outlook, 24 Sep) treats the AI investment cycle as one of three supports that let markets absorb higher rates. Burry (24 Sep) reads the same spending as a capital cycle at mid-point, with $3tn of commitments at the big five and depreciation understated. Outset Global (24 Sep) adds that SoftBank became the world's largest corporate junk borrower to fund OpenAI pledges, at its highest ever dollar cost.

Disagreement: Goldman Sachs and Barclays see returns emerging and capex growth slowing gracefully; Burry sees financing strain and write-downs.

Why it matters: European exposure is through suppliers, and even Hammond's bull case has capex growth slowing in 2027.
