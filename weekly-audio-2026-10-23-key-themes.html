Key Themes of the Middle Innings Lists in the Week of the twenty-third of October 2026.

This is Watson's reading of the key themes in this week's Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection and in the Ideas Bench, Ideas Shortlist and Ideas Longlist drawn from it, including what is absent, seen from portfolio construction and Portfolio Fitness for Fighting. It runs about fifteen minutes: seven themes tested against the mix tables, the strongest counter-case and my reply, a recap and two next actions.

The headline.

The Pool and the lists are a mirror of the regime, not of the book, and the two are nearly opposite. The book is small, cheap by the store's valuation reading, Swedish and British, in staples and industrials, with weak momentum. The Pool is energy-heavy, expensive, larger, Norwegian and Polish as well as British, and built on strength. That is what Portfolio Fitness for Fighting asked for, because the book's gaps are where the Pool bunches. But a Pool drawn from the regime's winners two months into their leadership carries the regime's risk, and the memos say so in one voice.

Theme one. Energy is the Pool's centre of gravity and the book's largest absence, and the two facts explain each other. High conviction. Energy, commodities and metals mining is six percent of the universe, seventy-seven stocks. It is twenty-four percent of the Pool, thirty-two percent of all eighty list places, and thirty-five percent of the Ideas Bench, seven of twenty. The live holdings have none. That is a four-fold overweight from universe to Pool and a six-fold one from universe to Bench, from the Downstream distribution row at Double Overweight, Upstream and Services at Overweight, and the week's leadership with Brent at one hundred and eleven dollars. The data also shows the split inside it. Sixteen energy memos are on file and not one argues for capital; thirteen of the eighteen upstream Pool names read F on TSR/Valuation. The conviction on the Bench is in services and downstream, Neste, SBM Offshore, Tenaris and Odfjell Drilling, where earnings rest on contracts, backlog and refining capture. The twelve producers and integrateds on the Shortlist and Longlist are the cyclical-peak case dressed as a cash-return case. The construction question is yours: whether one sector may hold a third of the Bench and the Shortlist. Amendment six proposes a cap at a share you set. I would propose it apply to the upstream producers alone, because the Energy rows at A and B will not thin themselves, while services and downstream are where the Investment Analyst still finds a leg. So, theme one: the Pool is a quarter energy and the Bench a third, against a book with none, and only the contracted half of that is a case.

Theme two. The Pool is expensive by the store's own valuation reading, and the lists inherit it, while the book is cheap. High conviction on the fact, medium on what it means. In the universe thirty percent of stocks read A on the valuation reading and seventeen percent read F. In the Pool the picture inverts: thirty-eight percent read F, nineteen percent D, seven percent A, so fifty-seven percent of the Pool sits in the two worst bands against twenty-nine percent of the universe. The list places soften it, twenty-five percent F and twenty-four percent D, and the Bench further, fifteen percent F and thirty-five percent D, because the judgement moves pulled valuation toward the middle. The live holdings are the opposite: forty-four percent A, twelve percent F. This says the Pool is built from what has already been bought: construction on relative strength of eighty or more selects stocks the market has re-rated, and the valuation reading records the bill. Nine stocks carry a majority of A and B grades and all nine memos say wait on price. The compounders' entry levels, Diploma near five thousand five hundred pence, Clariant at nine francs thirty-five, ASML at one thousand two hundred euros, Kainos below one thousand one hundred and sixty pence, are the queue. So, theme two: the lists are drawn from the expensive end of the universe because strength and price travel together, and the memos' entry levels are how that gets resolved.

Theme three. The lists are a large-cap set and the book is a small-cap book, and Fragility at Double Underweight says the lists have the right of it. Medium conviction. Median market value in the universe is about three point three billion euros; in the Pool four point three billion; across the list places five point five billion; on the Ideas Bench ten point eight billion, with four of nineteen measured names above fifty billion and one under two billion. The live holdings have a median of about one point five billion euros, ten of sixteen under two billion and none above fifty: a seven-fold difference in median size between the Bench and the book. The gap report's second gap is over-exposure to fragility, and amendment two proposes a size floor for new positions, still unappliable because size is blank in the holdings file. Anything that comes off this Bench into the book moves the book's size up, and the regime, with global quality gaining ground and the levered end of credit the epicentre, favours that. The counter is that a size floor turns the lists into a large-cap screen for the two-thirds of the Pool without case ratings. This is your rule to set. I would propose the floor apply at the point of a Core Investment, not at the point of listing, so the Early Innings Probing Bets keep their range. So, theme three: the Bench is seven times the size of the book by median, and Fragility says that is a feature, not a fault.

Theme four. What the Pool cannot supply: the book's other missing Overweight rows and a third of the universe's sectors. High conviction. The gap report says the book holds no energy, no insurance, no pharma and no tobacco, four rows at Overweight. The Pool can close the first gap and none of the others: there is no insurance stock in the Pool, no tobacco stock, and one pharma or biotech name, Genmab, on the Shortlist with nothing on file. Healthcare is eight percent of the universe and two of eighty list places. Five of the universe's sixteen sectors are absent from the Pool entirely: Real assets, seven percent of the universe, Defence, Telecoms, Infrastructure and Media. Consumer discretionary is twelve percent of the universe, six percent of the Pool and zero list places. Energy, technology, industrials and financials make up sixty-nine percent of the Pool and eighty-one percent of the list places. The target letters are at work, thirty-one percent of the Pool in target-A rows against thirteen percent of the universe, but the Bench's largest bucket is Neutral rows at forty-five percent, where the information technology services, bank and industrial distribution names live. The Pool is a strength filter before it is a fitness filter, so rows where the book's gaps are and the tape is only just turning, insurance and pharma, cannot reach the lists at all. That is right for a Middle Innings list and wrong for closing gap one in full; it needs a separate route. So, theme four: the Pool can close the energy gap and cannot touch insurance, pharma or tobacco, and five of sixteen sectors are not represented at all.

Theme five. Country: the lists would move the book away from Sweden and toward Norway, Poland and the Netherlands, mostly for the right reasons. Medium conviction. The United Kingdom is nineteen percent of the universe, twenty-one percent of list places and thirty-five percent of the Bench, seven names; it is also thirty-one percent of the book. Sweden is twelve percent of the universe and twenty-five percent of the book, four holdings and fifteen and a half points, the gap report's third gap; the list places are four percent Swedish and the Bench has one Swedish name, Bufab, marked leaving. Norway is four percent of the universe and eleven percent of list places, nine names, almost all energy and shipping, with the country row at B. Poland is three percent of the universe and eight percent of list places, six names, five of them banks, none on the Bench by judgement. The Netherlands is four percent of the universe and fifteen percent of the Bench: ING Groep, ABN AMRO and SBM Offshore. France is eleven percent of the universe and none of the Bench, with the country row at F on sovereign stress. The construction note is the Polish cluster: one Polish bank is a view, five is a country row the lists did not choose, and I propose they be sized as one position if any is ever bought. That rule is yours. So, theme five: the lists dilute Sweden, avoid France, and bunch in Norway and Poland on energy and banks, and the Polish bunch should count as one.

Theme six. The lists know least where they rank highest, and the fix is memos, not scoring. High conviction. Sixty of the one hundred and forty-nine candidates have a memo and eighty-nine do not; forty-five of the eighty list places are no-memo names, where all four case ratings are averages. Of the top forty by starting score, eighteen are thin, scoring well on the environment half with nothing known about the business. The Bench is better: fifteen of twenty have memos. But across the lists, memo-backed quality names, Diploma, Bufab, Renishaw, DiscoverIE, Indus, are parked at five to ten percent a year on their own arithmetic while no-memo names, Harbour Energy, Ithaca Energy, OMV Petrom, Hafnia, TORM, Maersk, rank above them on averages for the case and A grades for the environment. The market says the environment is the case; the memos say that when the environment is the case, the price already knows. The judgement moves pushed against the pattern: Tenaris and Kuehne and Nagel up because the research reads as a memo without a return, Genmab down because nothing is on file. So, theme six: two-thirds of the Pool is unmemoed and the no-memo names rank above the memo-backed compounders on averages, which is a knowledge gap, not a signal.

Theme seven. Strength is the Pool's foundation and the regime has just turned against strength. Medium conviction. The Pool is built on stock relative strength of eighty or more, and the Momentum row moved from Neutral to Underweight this week on its written condition, European defensives beating cyclicals by two percentage points at three months: a tilt against the Pool's own construction, held at one notch for that reason. The lists now lean less on the strongest charts and more on names the tape has de-rated, which is why Kesko reached the Bench and SMA Solar left it. The week's broken charts, ASML, Diploma, SMA Solar, Subsea Seven, Glencore, Anglo American and ArcelorMittal, all from A or B to D on Technical Momentum, are the Pool's deepest or most crowded cases breaking together, in the artificial intelligence chain and the capital-spending beneficiaries, while second-order sensing and measurement held. The book sits on the other side: Technical Momentum D on ten of sixteen holdings, the fourth and newest gap. That is two-sided: the book is on the favoured side of a mean-reversion tilt and will read fitter next week for a target reason, not a portfolio one, and it is also where the Sell Criteria fire first. For the lists, the Semiconductors row at Underweight against relative strength A is the loudest disagreement, and a quarter will resolve it. So, theme seven: the Pool selects on strength, the tilt now punishes strength, and the book's weakness has become a tactical virtue it did not earn.

The strongest counter-case to this whole reading, and my reply.

The counter is that the lists are the regime's winners two months in, and that is exactly when regimes reverse. Every upstream memo reads the return as paid for or negative; the bond market rallied on firm data for the first time this cycle; Goldman Sachs upgraded bonds from headwind to hedge; positioning is at its lowest since April. If the ten-year breaks under four and a half percent with the December hike priced out, the rate-sensitive Underweights move a notch, the Momentum Underweight looks premature, and the week's broken charts, ASML first, are where relief lands. On that reading the lists point the book toward large, expensive energy at the top of a cycle and away from the compounders that would re-rate first.

My reply is in three parts. First, the lean the lists actually recommend is not the producers: the Bench's conviction is in contracted services and downstream, and the producers sit on the lower lists with their memos saying wait. Second, the compounders are not missing, they are parked with named entry levels, and this week's breaks brought ASML and Diploma to those levels. Third, the counter is a condition, not a fact: the policy rate is still rising, the ten-year is near five point one percent, and one week's hedge is not a peak. I hold the reading at medium conviction, and the counter-case is what I would watch first.

The recap. Energy is a quarter of the Pool and a third of the Bench against a book with none, and only the contracted half is a case. The Pool is expensive because it is built on strength, and the memos' entry levels are the queue. The Bench is seven times the book's median size and Fragility says that is right. The Pool cannot touch insurance, pharma or tobacco and leaves out five of sixteen sectors. The lists dilute Sweden and bunch in Norway and Poland. Two-thirds of the Pool is unmemoed. Strength is the Pool's foundation and the tilt now punishes it.

Two next actions. For you: three construction rules are yours to set and I have proposed a position on each, a sector cap on the Bench and Shortlist applied to upstream producers, a size floor applied at the point of a Core Investment, and the Polish banks sized as one position. For Watson: build the missing route for the Overweight rows the Pool cannot reach, insurance and pharma, as a short screen of the universe outside the Pool, and commission the memos on Tenaris, Kuehne and Nagel and Shell.

That is the end of the summary.
