APM Mini-Memo. Orion. The thirtieth of October 2026.

Orion sits at rank four on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. The one-line view. Orion's earnings are rising mechanically on Nubeqa royalties, and the return from today's price depends on its own pipeline proving a second leg, with the first read-out now due a year early.

First, what the company does. Orion is a Finnish pharmaceutical company that has become, in effect, a global oncology royalty business. Its prostate cancer drug Nubeqa is sold by Bayer, and Orion earns a royalty that rises in tiers with Bayer's sales. About four fifths of operating profit now comes from that one stream, and Bayer is forty-three percent of sales. Around the royalty sit older businesses in generics, consumer health, branded products, animal health and drug ingredients, with about one point one billion euros of revenue between them. In 2025 the group made nearly one point nine billion euros of revenue at a thirty-three percent operating margin. Earnings per share were three euros fifty-six, up from one euro fifty-four two years earlier.

Second, the thesis. The story is a mechanical earnings step-up that the sell-side has under-modelled eight times in a row. Bayer guides Nubeqa sales up about fifty percent in 2026. The royalty tiers reset each calendar year and rise with cumulative sales, so the top tier is reached earlier every year, and Orion's average royalty rate climbs from about twenty-five percent towards its thirty percent ceiling. The top tier was reached in the second quarter of this year. Royalties rose seventy-seven percent in the first quarter. Management has beaten every initial guidance range for two years and has raised this year's guidance twice. The transmission to earnings per share is one division. The Investment Analyst's path has earnings per share rising about fifteen percent a year, from three euros fifty-six in 2025 to five euros forty in 2028, about six percent above consensus. Growth then slows to six or seven percent a year as a United States price cut lands in 2029. The second leg is the pipeline, and its clock has moved forward. The memo gave the company's own oncology pipeline no value, and its trigger for progressing the case was an event that changes the pipeline odds. The weekly Lists judgement of the thirtieth of October records that the prostate cancer read-out is arriving a year early. The drivers to track are the weekly United States prescription data for Nubeqa, Nubeqa's United States net price once a generic enters its class in August 2027, and that read-out.

Third, the Target Setup. The Target Setup is Delivering, Serial Acquirer, Mechanical EPS Upgrader, on its mechanical upgrader leg. The Investment Analyst's memo of the seventeenth of September rated Setups' Fit at B. The case matches crisply on the record of upgrades and on quality. The match is only fair on the mis-modelled line, because the within-year tier step-up is now three quarters spent, and what remains of the consensus gap is a judgement about class pricing rather than a model error.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The case is crisp and not robust, and the Investment Analyst rated Case Riskiness C on the seventeenth of September. Simplicity is high because the case turns on one drug's volume and price, tracked weekly, with a one-step transmission to earnings per share and a conservative guider. The risk is concentration with dates on it. One customer is forty-three percent of sales. A generic rival enters the class in August 2027, with payers already reported to be resisting Nubeqa's premium. A United States price cut of about a quarter follows from 2029. United States exclusivity ends in 2033, and the rest of the business, earning perhaps eighty million euros a year before tax, cannot cushion that. What needs to go right is that Nubeqa prescriptions keep growing through the generic entry, and that the pipeline proves a second product before 2033. The pipeline has failed three times outside oncology in two years, so the read-out carries real weight. What to track is the weekly prescription series and the read-out itself.

Fifth, business quality. Quality is top-decile on the numbers and borrowed on the moat. The operating margin of thirty-three percent and a return on invested capital of thirty-seven percent, on one broker's definition, sit in the sector's top decile. The Investment Analyst rated Foundations' Robustness B on the seventeenth of September. Nubeqa's advantage is tolerability rather than survival, and clinicians confirm it in their own words. But Bayer sells the drug, sets its price, and negotiates with United States payers without Orion in the room. Capital allocation is the weak point, with about two billion euros of firepower unused. Orion's articles cap any shareholder at five percent of the votes, so no bid premium underpins the shares.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The return grade is F, and the grade is dated. At one hundred euros on the thirtieth of October, the shares trade at about nineteen times earnings, the thirty-fourth percentile of their own ten-year range. When the memo was written in September, at about eighty-two euros, the shares traded at under sixteen times, at the eighth percentile. The memo's path gave nine to eleven percent a year over eighteen months from eighty-three euros. Re-based to one hundred euros, the same path returns about minus three percent a year over eighteen months and about nil over three years, which is why the Investment Analyst's TSR slash Valuation rating is F. The market has already paid most of the re-rating that the memo gave one-in-five odds. A return towards your twenty percent bar now needs one of two things. Either the earnings path is too low, which eight consecutive beats make plausible, or the early read-out adds a pipeline leg that the path gives nothing for. The Portfolio Fitness for Fighting call has asked the Investment Analyst to re-base the path. Until then, any return above the modal one is not in our files.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros. Bayer is a strong, aligned partner growing the drug about fifty percent a year towards peak sales above four point seven billion euros. The class is a three-brand oligopoly. The Sector Digest of the twenty-second of October names Orion among the purest plays on specialist launches lifting mid-cap margins, a story it rates big and early. The weekly Thematic Fit rating is B, the weekly Technical Momentum rating is B with the shares at a fifty-two week high in a proven uptrend, and the weekly Sell-side Momentum rating is B. The cons. Payers and regulators are moving against branded prostate cancer drugs on a timetable. The patent expiries of 2027 to 2032 are a story the Sector Digest rates big but no longer early. The Sector Digest also warns that once the market values a story, the share price needs it delivered. Orion has risen thirty-eight percent over twelve months against fifteen percent for its mid-cap specialty peers, so the market now values this one.

The one-line view. Orion's earnings are rising mechanically on Nubeqa royalties, and the return from today's price depends on its own pipeline proving a second leg, with the first read-out now due a year early.
