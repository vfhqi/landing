APM Mini-Memo. Kuehne + Nagel International. The twenty-third of October 2026.

Kuehne + Nagel sits at rank seven on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The view is bullish as a working hypothesis. The world's largest freight forwarder saw operating profit fall to a conversion of only fourteen point one per cent of gross profit in 2025 and is rebuilding it through cost and artificial intelligence savings, so every point of conversion recovered falls to earnings per share without needing volume. The catch is that freight yields it does not control can take it all back, and at twenty-two point eight times earnings the shares already price much of the recovery.

First, what the company does. Kuehne + Nagel buys transport capacity from shipping lines, airlines and road hauliers and sells it on to four hundred thousand customers. It owns neither ships nor aircraft. It is the largest forwarder in the world by both sea and air volume, moving four point three one million twenty-foot container equivalent units in 2025. Operating profit in 2025 was one point two four billion Swiss francs. Klaus-Michael Kuehne holds fifty-five point four per cent of the votes, which rules out activists and takeovers alike.

Second, the thesis. The mechanism is conversion, not volume. Procurement scale, an owned transport management system and customer integration give gross margins of thirty-two to thirty-eight per cent against a sector average of twenty, so gross profit is defended. What collapsed was the share of gross profit reaching operating profit, from thirty-four per cent in 2022 to fourteen point one in 2025. Roadmap 2026 takes two hundred million Swiss francs of annualised cost out, with more than one hundred and twenty million of gross savings due this year. The artificial intelligence roadmap targets a further one hundred to one hundred and fifty million of operating profit by the end of 2027. Those two pots are worth roughly a quarter more operating profit if retained, with no help from volume. The second quarter of 2026 showed it working. Conversion rose one hundred and thirty basis points to sixteen point nine per cent, operating profit of three hundred and eighty-one million beat consensus by eight per cent, and full-year guidance rose to between one point three five and one point five five billion. Track quarterly conversion against that sixteen point nine, and gross profit per container against operating cost per container, because savings competed away show up as the first falling faster than the second.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up, Stock-driven ratings, and this read is mine alone. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. The step-up is manufactured inside the company, by the cost programme, the flattened structure and the artificial intelligence roadmap, not by demand. The setup of a cause for concern in a quality cyclical at trough on trough, leading to reversion to normal, tempts, because conversion is plainly at a trough, but it fails on the second trough: the multiple sits at the seventy-ninth percentile of its ten-year history.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. No Case Riskiness rating exists. The case is simple to state and hard to isolate. One variable, conversion, carries it, but conversion is the net of two forces of similar size pulling opposite ways: self-help of three hundred to three hundred and fifty million Swiss francs against a yield normalisation the change forces digest sizes at a fifteen per cent contraction in operating profit, with high plausibility. Management concedes some early artificial intelligence gains will be competed away, and second quarter cash conversion was thirty to thirty-five per cent against a norm of fifty-four. My impression, not a rating, is mid-table riskiness.

Fifth, business quality. High, with a cyclical weak spot. Return on capital employed was fifty per cent in 2025. Free cash flow was nine hundred and seventeen million Swiss francs, up forty-eight per cent, and no customer exceeds ten per cent of revenue. The weak spots are volume and mix. Sea volumes fell in the second quarter while DSV grew, and the 2030 conversion target of thirty-five per cent concedes ground to DSV's fifty-five.

Sixth, returns and valuation. Below your bar on consensus. At two hundred and twenty-seven Swiss francs on the twenty-third of October the shares trade on twenty-two point eight times current earnings, the seventy-ninth percentile of their ten-year history. The script's TSR and Valuation reading of the twenty-third of October is D on the valuation pin. Its plausible return of the twenty-second of October is nine point nine per cent a year, built on consensus earnings growth of six point four per cent a year and a flat multiple, below even the D anchor of about twelve per cent. The gap between the savings pot and six point four per cent earnings growth is the market's verdict that yields eat most of it, so the case needs conversion to beat consensus.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The script's Thematic Fit and Momentum reading of the twenty-third of October is B, environment only. Sell-side Momentum of the twenty-second is B. Technical Momentum of the twenty-third is A. On the plus side, DSV's purchase of DB Schenker for fourteen point three billion euros leaves three large competitors and, in the chief executive's words, has structurally raised pricing power. The technology vertical and leadership in pharmaceuticals lift mix. On the minus side, the container orderbook is forty-two and a half per cent of the active fleet, with 2027 supply growth of seven point nine per cent against demand growth of three point two, and a Red Sea or Suez normalisation would reset sea yields abruptly.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. Conversion fell to fourteen point one per cent for reasons nobody in our files has explained, and the second quarter beat was cost-led while gross profit was merely in line. If yields normalise quickly, which that orderbook makes likely, the savings pot is negated, and the cost cutting behind the beat has a record of impairing the sales force and losing volume to DSV. Paying twenty-two point eight times earnings for a consensus plausible return of nine point nine per cent is paying a growth multiple for a cyclical whose next move in yields is down. Morgan Stanley's bear multiple of twelve times and Jefferies' downside of one hundred and forty-three sit roughly a third below the price.

The one-line view. Bullish as a working hypothesis: the world's largest freight forwarder is rebuilding a collapsed conversion rate through cost and artificial intelligence savings, so every point recovered falls to earnings per share without needing volume; the catch is that freight yields it does not control can take it all back, and at twenty-two point eight times earnings the shares already price much of the recovery.
