## Core Philosophy

OKRs (Objectives and Key Results) are a goal-setting framework popularised by John Doerr in *Measure What Matters* (2018), based on a system he learned from Andy Grove at Intel in the 1970s. The framework's power lies in its simplicity: an Objective is a qualitative description of what you want to achieve (inspiring, directional, time-bound); Key Results are 2-4 quantitative measures that tell you whether you have achieved it.

Doerr's formulation: "I will [Objective] as measured by [Key Results]."

The framework forces two disciplines that most goal systems lack: first, every objective must have measurable evidence of progress (not just activity, but outcomes); second, the number of objectives must be ruthlessly limited (3-5 per cycle) to maintain focus. OKRs are not a comprehensive to-do list. They are a declaration of what matters most.

For a solo investor, OKRs solve a specific problem: the gap between knowing what you should be doing and actually doing it consistently. Richard's ETCs (Evergreen Target Conditions) provide the long-term direction; OKRs provide the medium-term measurement layer that connects daily process goals to those enduring aims.

## Key Frameworks & Concepts

### The Anatomy of Good OKRs

**Objectives:**
- Qualitative, not quantitative. The objective answers "what" and "why," not "how much."
- Inspiring but concrete. "Build a world-class research process" is too vague. "Operate a disciplined pipeline where every stock gets stage-appropriate attention" is specific enough to guide action.
- Time-bound. Typically set monthly or quarterly.
- Limited to 3-5 per cycle. Fewer is better. Doerr: "If everything is important, nothing is."

**Key Results:**
- Quantitative and measurable. Must have a number. "Improve research quality" is not a KR. "Complete ESA for 3 pipeline stocks" is.
- Outcome-focused, not activity-focused. "Read 10 annual reports" is an activity (input). "Advance 3 stocks from IG to Triage with documented thesis" is an outcome (output).
- 2-4 per objective. More than 4 means the objective is too broad.
- Stretch but achievable. Doerr recommends a 70% expected completion rate for aspirational OKRs — if you're hitting 100% every time, you're sandbagging.

### Input Goals vs Output Goals — The Critical Distinction

This is the single most important concept for Richard's system. Most people confuse activity (inputs) with progress (outputs/outcomes).

**Input goals** measure effort: hours spent researching, number of reports read, calls made, checklists completed. They are necessary but not sufficient. You can complete every input goal and still make no progress if the inputs are not connected to meaningful outputs.

**Output goals** measure results: stocks that progressed through a pipeline stage, theses that were validated or killed, positions that were sized according to conviction framework, monitoring triggers that were actually acted upon.

**Outcome goals** measure impact: portfolio returns, quality of decision-making over time, reduction in unforced errors, improvement in thesis accuracy.

The hierarchy for Richard:
- **Inputs** (process goals — Selk's domain): "Complete 5 triaging checklists this month."
- **Outputs** (key results): "Advance 3 stocks from IG/Triage to ESA with documented thesis and key questions."
- **Outcomes** (product goals / ETCs): "Portfolio is positioned in highest-conviction ideas with appropriate sizing."

Watson's Chief of Staff role operates primarily at the input-output boundary: ensuring that the right inputs (process goals) are producing the intended outputs (pipeline movement, decision quality), and flagging when they are not.

### Committed vs Aspirational OKRs

Doerr distinguishes two types:

**Committed OKRs:** These must be achieved. Expected score: 1.0 (100%). Failure to hit a committed OKR requires a post-mortem. For Richard: "Every active position has an up-to-date monitoring note by month-end" is a committed OKR — it should happen, and if it does not, something is wrong.

**Aspirational OKRs (stretch goals):** These represent the best possible outcome. Expected score: 0.6-0.7 (60-70%). Consistently hitting 100% means the stretch is not ambitious enough. For Richard: "Advance 5 pipeline stocks by at least one stage this quarter" might be aspirational — it depends on the quality of the pipeline and external events.

The distinction prevents two failure modes: setting goals so easy they drive no improvement (all committed, no stretch), or setting goals so aggressive they demoralise (all aspirational, constant perceived failure).

### OKR Cadence — Monthly Set, Weekly Check, Daily Nudge

**Monthly Setting (first Monday of the month, or aligned with Richard's planning cycle):**
- Watson proposes 2-3 objectives with 2-3 key results each, based on pipeline state, ETC progress, and current condition data from the kata
- Richard approves, adjusts, or redirects
- OKRs are posted to Notion (visible, not buried)
- Process goals for the month are derived from the OKRs (the Selk layer)

**Weekly Check (Friday watson-weekly-review):**
- Score each KR on current progress: where are we on a 0.0-1.0 scale?
- Are we on track, at risk, or off track? Traffic light: green / amber / red
- If off track: what is the obstacle? (Link to kata obstacle log)
- If on track: what is the next action to maintain momentum?
- Brief written update logged in Notion or COWORK files

**Daily Nudge (embedded in morning check-in):**
- Watson references the most relevant OKR in the morning check-in: "Today's process goal [X] contributes to KR [Y] under Objective [Z]. Current progress: [score]."
- This is not a lengthy review. One sentence. Keeps the OKR visible and connected to daily work.

### Scoring OKRs

Three approaches, in increasing sophistication:

**1. Binary (done/not done):** Simple but loses nuance. Useful for committed OKRs with clear finish lines.

**2. Percentage completion:** "Complete ESA for 3 pipeline stocks" — if 2 are done, score is 0.67. Most practical for Richard's context.

**3. Google-style 0.0-1.0 scale:** Average the percentage completion of all KRs under an objective. Committed OKRs target 1.0; aspirational OKRs target 0.7. Overall OKR health = average of all objective scores.

Recommended for Richard: percentage completion for individual KRs, averaged to an objective score. Monthly. Simple, honest, no gaming.

### Target Conditions vs OKRs

Richard uses the term "Target Conditions" (TCs) from his kata/improvement system. TCs and OKRs are complementary, not competing:

- **TCs describe a process state:** "By end of March, every pipeline stock has an up-to-date Notion page with stage, next action, and trigger."
- **OKRs describe a desired outcome and measure progress toward it:** "Objective: Operate a well-maintained pipeline. KR: 100% of active stocks have current Notion pages (monthly audit)."

The TC is "how the process should work." The OKR is "what we are trying to achieve and how we measure it." The TC often becomes the mechanism by which a KR is achieved.

Watson's job: maintain both. Use TCs for process improvement (kata). Use OKRs for priority-setting and progress measurement. They reinforce each other.

## Application to Richard's Context

### OKR Structure for a Solo Concentrated Investor

A solo investor does not need the alignment and cascading features of team OKRs. What Richard needs from OKRs:

1. **Focus:** With ~15 pipeline stocks and 3 ETCs, there is always more to do than time allows. OKRs force the question: "What are the 2-3 things that matter most this month?"

2. **Measurement:** Richard's pipeline can stagnate without visible consequence — stocks sit in triage for weeks, DD phases drag on, monitoring slips. OKRs make stagnation visible by measuring throughput and completion.

3. **Connection between daily work and long-term aims:** Without OKRs, daily process goals can become disconnected from strategic priorities. OKRs provide the bridge: "I am doing this triaging checklist today because it serves KR #2 under my pipeline throughput objective."

### Example Monthly OKRs (Illustrative)

**Objective 1: Operate a disciplined, high-throughput pipeline**
- KR1: Advance 2 pipeline stocks by at least one stage (IG→Triage, Triage→ESA, etc.)
- KR2: Complete triaging checklists for DKSH and FLTR
- KR3: Post all outstanding AS reports to Notion with 30%+ highlighting (MTU, FEVR)

**Objective 2: Maintain investment discipline during risk-off environment**
- KR1: Update monitoring notes for all 5 active positions by month-end
- KR2: Complete Iran sector impact analysis for all held positions
- KR3: Review and confirm position sizing is consistent with conviction levels

**Objective 3: Build robust Watson Chief of Staff capability**
- KR1: Morning check-in delivered 4/5 weekdays for 4 consecutive weeks
- KR2: Done well / get better logged every session handoff
- KR3: First kata cycle completed (target condition set, 3+ experiments run)

### Common OKR Anti-Patterns to Avoid

1. **Too many OKRs.** More than 3 objectives or 4 KRs per objective dilutes focus. If it cannot fit on a single screen, it is too many. For a solo investor: 2-3 objectives, 2-3 KRs each, monthly cycle.

2. **Measuring activity, not outcomes.** "Read 5 annual reports" is an input. "Complete ESA for 2 stocks with documented thesis" is an outcome. Always ask: "If I do this activity but nothing changes, was the OKR a success?" If yes, it is measuring the wrong thing.

3. **Vague objectives.** "Improve research quality" is not actionable. "Operate a pipeline where every stock has a documented thesis and clear next action" is specific and verifiable.

4. **Not ambitious enough.** If every OKR scores 1.0 every month, the goals are too comfortable. At least one objective should be aspirational (target: 0.7 completion). Stretch prevents complacency.

5. **Disconnected from daily work.** OKRs that are set monthly and forgotten until review are decoration. The daily nudge (morning check-in) and weekly check (Friday review) keep them alive.

6. **Confusing OKRs with KPIs.** KPIs monitor ongoing health (portfolio return, pipeline count, process compliance rate). OKRs drive change (move 3 stocks forward, build a new capability, fix a recurring friction). Watson tracks both but does not conflate them.

7. **Linking to emotional self-evaluation.** OKR scores are data, not judgements. A 0.5 on an aspirational OKR is useful information, not evidence of failure. Watson presents scores neutrally, with RSF framing: "We scored 0.5 on KR2. What is one thing we do differently next month?"

## Chief of Staff Protocols

### How Watson Uses OKRs in the Chief of Staff Role

**Monthly OKR Setting (first Monday of the month):**
1. Watson reviews: ETC progress, pipeline state, current condition (kata data), last month's OKR scores, corrections.md for patterns
2. Watson drafts 2-3 proposed objectives with KRs, each connected to an ETC or pipeline priority
3. Presents to Richard for approval/adjustment
4. Derives weekly process goals from the KRs (Selk layer)
5. Posts finalised OKRs to Notion

**Weekly OKR Check (Friday, embedded in watson-weekly-review):**
1. Score each KR: percentage complete, traffic light (green/amber/red)
2. For amber/red KRs: identify the obstacle (link to kata obstacle log)
3. For green KRs: confirm next action to maintain momentum
4. Overall objective scores: average of KR scores
5. One-paragraph narrative: what moved, what didn't, what changes next week

**Daily OKR Nudge (morning check-in):**
1. Reference the most relevant KR: "Today's [process goal] serves [KR] under [Objective]. Progress: [X/Y]."
2. One sentence. Not a review. Just connection.

**Monthly OKR Close-Out (last Friday of the month):**
1. Final scoring of all KRs and objectives
2. For each objective: what worked, what didn't, what carries forward
3. Aspirational OKRs scored 0.6-0.7: on track. Scored 1.0: not ambitious enough. Scored below 0.4: either too ambitious or structural obstacles unaddressed.
4. Carry-forward items become inputs to next month's OKR setting
5. Log in COWORK memory for longitudinal tracking

### OKR-Kata Integration

The OKR and kata systems reinforce each other:
- OKRs set the "what" (priorities and measures)
- Kata addresses the "how" (experiments to overcome obstacles blocking KR progress)
- When a KR is off track, the kata asks: "What obstacle is preventing progress? What experiment can we run?"
- When a kata target condition is reached, the OKR score should improve. If it does not, the target condition was addressing the wrong problem.

Watson maintains both systems and cross-references them in weekly reviews.

## Key Quotes / Principles

- "Ideas are easy. Execution is everything." — John Doerr
- "If everything is important, nothing is." — Doerr, on why OKRs must be limited
- "Objectives are the stuff of inspiration and far horizons. Key results are more earthbound and metric-driven. They typically include hard numbers." — Doerr
- "It almost doesn't matter what you know. It's what you can do consistently that matters." — Selk (applied to OKR execution)
- "OKRs are not a silver bullet. They cannot substitute for sound judgement, strong leadership, or a creative workplace culture. But if those fundamentals are in place, OKRs can guide you to the mountaintop." — Doerr
- "Measure what matters, not what's easy to measure." — A common OKR maxim
- "A committed OKR is like a train schedule: it must be met. An aspirational OKR is like a compass bearing: it guides the direction." — Derived from Doerr's distinction
- "The enemy of OKRs is not failure to hit targets. The enemy is failure to set the right targets." — OKR community principle
