# IR Two-Pager — Verbatim Prompt
<!-- [W] Created 22-Apr-26. This is the canonical prompt that governs a two-pager draft. Edit this file when the prompt itself is revised. SKILL.md references this file but does not duplicate it. -->

## Prompt

Write a one-page investment memo on {COMPANY} suitable for sharing with an institutional investor as a talking-point agenda. Assume the reader is a sophisticated financial PM or analyst who does not know the company.

## Objective of the Memo

The reader should come away with three takeaways, in this order of weight:

1. This is a high-quality business.
2. Whose investment case is positively inflecting.
3. And that is currently mispriced by the stock market, with the change in flight resolving that mispricing over the next 2 to 3 years.

## Structure (in this order, with word targets)

1. Summary of write-up — 150 to 250 words
2. Business quality: Strong SRCA — 150 to 250 words
3. Business quality: Favourable value chain dynamics and industry structure — 150 to 250 words
4. Business quality: Good operator — 150 to 250 words
5. Investment case setup — 150 to 250 words
6. Investment case inputs — 150 to 250 words
7. Investment case simplicity — 150 to 250 words
8. Investment case financial outputs — 150 to 250 words
9. Key risks — 150 to 250 words
10. Why the company is mispriced by the stock market — 150 to 200 words
11. Valuation and potential returns — 150 to 200 words

Do not label sections with internal Viewforth framework codes (BB#, IC#, SRCA as an acronym). Use the plain English headings above.

## General Principles

Apply in the vast majority of cases; not universally required.

### Audience and Voice

- Write for a sophisticated financial reader who does not know the company. No teaching voice. No patronising scaffolding. No euphemisms. No analogy explainers.
- Use the standard language of a high-quality, deep-research-oriented, long-term fundamental investor. No Viewforth jargon. No acronyms. No management buzzwords ("best-in-class", "strategically positioned", "leveraging synergies", "world-class"). No financial buzzwords.
- Direct, concise, unemotive, non-hyperbolic, mature, professional. Combined with the benefit-led phrasing below, the reader should feel excitement about the opportunity without the tone ever feeling salesy.

### Benefit-Led Writing

- Lead with the benefit for every fact: why is it interesting, positive, or relevant. The header/opening phrase of each bullet is the institutional takeaway; the fact justifies it.
- Every number is contextualised with a claim, analysis, or judgement, and every claim is supported by a number or concrete evidence. Claim plus proof paired, always.
- Use anecdotes to bring the case to life wherever possible. Named competitor failures, specific incidents, analyst quotes, timestamped decisions. Anecdotes beat abstract descriptors.

### Content Selection

- Avoid unimportant financial minutae. Stick to key financial statement items unless a specific metric is load-bearing for the investment case or the business.
- Avoid minor factoids that do not clearly imply one of: high-quality business, strong change-centric investment case, material upside, or mispricing where we have an edge the market does not.
- Focus on the 2 to 4 year horizon and on business quality, predictability, and durability. Short-term noise gets cut.

### Formatting

- Bullets 50 to 100 words (flexible when the idea requires it).
- The first sentence or phrase of each bullet is the core claim. The rest of the bullet is the support. Bold the opening claim.
- Each section's first bullet should carry the core claim for that section; subsequent bullets support it.
- Do not use em-dashes or en-dashes of any kind. Replace with commas, colons, semicolons, parentheses, full stops, or clause restructuring. No date ranges with dashes either. Use "to" or "through".
- Do not use any other common AI/LLM markers (repeated triads, hedging adverbs, "it is worth noting", ornamental rhetorical flourishes).
- Plain markdown. No horizontal rules. No italics on the summary. No decoration.

## Process

- Before drafting, load the investment case file from the local COWORK `Files/` database first (via `index-master.json` and `{TICKER}/index.json`), then check Notion second for incremental detail authored by Richard. Reconcile the two sources.
- Ask up to 5 clarifying questions before drafting if there is material ambiguity (positioning, horizon, facts to prioritise, audience nuance).
- Deliver in text form first. Save to .docx in COWORK only on Richard's confirmation.
- Link the Notion case file at the foot of the memo under a "Sources:" heading.

## Key Mnemonic

Lead with the benefit, not the fact. High quality first, positively inflecting second, mispricing third.
