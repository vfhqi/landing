## 1. Mark Douglas — Trading in the Zone

### Core Principles
- Every edge has a random distribution of wins and losses
- You cannot predict individual outcomes — only the aggregate probability
- Consistency comes from consistent process execution, not from being right on any single trade
- The market is neutral — it doesn't know or care about your position
- Pain comes from expectations, not from the market

### Application to Richard's System
- "Consistently execute my process. Mark Douglas style." — direct quote from ETCs
- Focus purely on making money, not on being analytically right (Principle #9)
- Accept that some good decisions will have bad outcomes — this is structural, not failure
- The 18-month ownership horizon is a probabilistic framework — each position is one instance in a distribution

## 2. Lanny Bassham — With Winning in Mind

### Core Principles
- Performance is a function of self-image
- Self-image operates below consciousness — it drives behaviour automatically
- To change behaviour, you must change self-image first
- Techniques: mental rehearsal, directive affirmations, building a "mental management" system
- Winners think about what they want to happen; losers think about what they don't want

### Application to Richard's System
- Target self-image: "I am a disciplined, systematic investor on the path to becoming great"
- Not: "I am great" (imposter syndrome) or "I'm not there yet" (self-sabotage)
- Progressive framing aligns with Bassham's "directive affirmation" approach
- Building identity around process execution: "I am a person who executes checklists"

## 3. Timothy Gallwey — The Inner Game

### Core Principles
- Performance = Potential - Interference
- "Self 1" (conscious, critical mind) interferes with "Self 2" (natural capability)
- The goal is to quiet Self 1 and let Self 2 operate
- Techniques: focus on process, non-judgemental awareness, observation without correction
- Trust the training

### Application to Richard's System
- Over-analysis and tinkering = Self 1 interference
- The creativity/growth drive that pulls away from execution = Self 1 wanting something more interesting
- Solution: structured creativity time (monthly) + execution the rest of the time
- Checklists serve as Self 2 autopilot — remove decision fatigue, let the trained response operate

## 4. James Clear — Atomic Habits

### Core Principles

- Identity-based habits > outcome-based habits
- "I am the type of person who..." is more powerful than "I want to achieve..."
- Four laws: make it obvious, make it attractive, make it easy, make it satisfying
- Environment design > willpower

### Application to Richard's System

- Environment: Watson as external structure (morning questions, handoffs, monitoring) — makes good behaviour easier

## 5. Steve Ward — The Mind of a Trader / High Performance Trading

- Commitment ≠ motivation. Motivation fluctuates; commitment is structural.
- "Change or Die" drivers: relationships, reframing, repetition
- Performance is cyclical — recovery is not optional
- Self-awareness is the foundation — you can't manage what you don't see

### Application to Richard's System
- Watson supports all 4Cs (see high-performance.md)
- Morning questions = commitment structure
- Corrections log = self-awareness tool
- Weekly review = pattern recognition
- Recovery protocol: recognise when pushed too hard, honour rest needs

### Key Ward Insights for Richard

- "Unbung" (Kahneman): What is blocking you? Usually: over-complication, creativity drive, demand > capacity.
- Three behaviour change drivers: social support, reframing, repetition until habitual.

## 6. Bob Rotella — Golf Is Not a Game of Perfect / How Champions Think

<!-- [W] Added 28-Mar-26 from web research + training knowledge -->

### Background

Dr. Bob Rotella was Director of Sports Psychology at the University of Virginia for 20 years. Worked with Hall of Fame golfers (Pat Bradley, Tom Kite, Nick Price, Rory McIlroy, Padraig Harrington, Keegan Bradley), Olympic athletes, a Cy Young winner, and top traders/business leaders including billionaires. His work translates directly to investing because golf and investing share the same core challenge: performing under uncertainty with imperfect information, where the mental game determines outcomes far more than technical skill.

### Core Philosophy

**"It's impossible to overestimate the importance of the mind."** Technical skill gets you to the table. Mental game determines who wins. Two performers of equal skill — the more confident one wins nearly every time. This maps directly to Richard's insight: "I genuinely know what I need to do but am not executing it."

### The Training vs Trusting Distinction (CRITICAL)

This is Rotella's most powerful framework. He separates two completely different mental modes:

**Training Mindset (Practice / Research / Analysis):**
- Hypersensitive awareness of what's wrong
- Analytical, mechanical, conscious mind engaged
- Break things down, fix components, build skill
- Appropriate for: research sessions, model-building, post-mortems, process design

**Trusting Mindset (Performance / Execution / Game Day):**
- Subconscious mind runs the show
- Trust the preparation, don't second-guess
- Instinctive, fluid, committed to each action
- Appropriate for: live capital decisions, IR calls, market reactions, position management

**The critical rule:** You MUST separate these. Thinking mechanically during performance produces worse results than trusting a flawed process. After doing the analytical work (Training), switch to Trusting when executing. Everyone has flaws in their process — thinking about and trying to fix mechanics during execution makes it worse, not better.

**For Richard:** The over-analysis and tinkering he does mid-execution is Training Mindset contaminating Trusting Mindset. Monthly creativity sessions = Training. The rest of the time = Trusting. When he's in the middle of a position decision, the analysis should already be done — the job is to commit and act, not re-open the analysis.

### Rotella's 10 Commandments

I. **Play to play great.** Don't play not to lose. Aim for excellence, not avoidance of mistakes.
II. **Love the challenge of the day, whatever it may be.** Embrace difficult conditions, volatility, uncertainty — they're the arena where edge manifests.
III. **Get out of results and into process.** When focused on outcomes (P&L), bad outcomes feel devastating. When focused on process (did I execute well?), bad outcomes feel manageable.
IV. **Nothing will bother or upset you.** Pre-commit to equanimity. Decide in advance that you will be in a great state of mind for every decision, regardless of what happens.
V. **Playing with a feeling that the outcome doesn't matter is almost always preferable to caring too much.** Paradox: caring less about the specific outcome produces better outcomes. Caring too much creates tension, overthinking, and fear.
VI. **Believe fully in yourself so you can play freely.** Confidence is not arrogance — it's freedom from self-doubt that allows full commitment to each action.
VII. **See where you want the ball to go before every shot.** Visualise the positive outcome. Champions think about what they want to happen; losers think about what they don't want to happen.
VIII. **Be decisive, committed, and clear.** No half-commitments. Once the decision is made, execute fully. Hesitation and hedging produce the worst outcomes.
IX. **Be your own best friend.** The internal voice should be encouraging, not critical. Self-criticism during performance destroys confidence.
X. **Love your wedge and your putter.** Love your tools, love the mundane fundamentals. The basics win championships.

### Key Principles

**1. Accept Imperfection**
If you love investing, you must love the fact that investing is inherently imperfect. Fighting imperfection — trying to make it a game of perfect analysis, perfect timing, perfect entry — means you don't actually like investing. The essence is reacting well to inevitable mistakes and misfortunes. Recovery from mistakes defines the champion, not absence of mistakes.

**2. The Free Throws Analogy**
If you're committed to never shooting an airball, you have "a perfect attitude for avoiding airballs, but a lousy attitude for making free throws." To be a great free-throw shooter, you have to accept an airball now and then. Applied to investing: if you're committed to never having a losing position, you'll never take the concentrated, high-conviction positions that produce exceptional returns. Accept losses as the price of the strategy.

**3. Smallest Possible Target**
Before every action, pick the smallest possible target. "Aiming down the middle of the fairway" (vague thesis, general optimism) is not good enough. Aim at the specific branch on the specific tree (specific driver, specific EPS number, specific timeline). Precision of target creates precision of execution.

**4. Confidence Is Simple**
Confident performers think about what they want to happen. Unconfident performers think about what they don't want to happen. That's it. That's the entire difference. When Richard catches himself thinking "what if this stock goes down" instead of "here's why this stock goes up," he's in unconfident mode.

**5. Staying in the Present**
Don't be seduced by the score until you run out of holes. In investing: don't be seduced by the YTD P&L or the drawdown. Get lost in the process of executing each decision well and accept the result. The worst thing you can do is get down when things don't go well — feeling sorry for yourself means you're not focused on the next decision.

**6. Optimism as Foundation**
"The correlation between optimism and success is imperfect, but there is an almost perfect correlation between negative thinking and failure." Optimism is a choice. Richard's natural optimism is a competitive advantage, not a weakness to be corrected.

**7. Enthusiasm as Catalyst**
"Enthusiasm acts like a catalyst that makes all the other attributes of a champion's mind work better." This aligns with Richard's "energise / be the dynamo" winning behaviour. Enthusiasm isn't soft — it's a performance multiplier.

**8. Pre-Shot Routine (Pre-Decision Routine)**
Rotella insists on a routine so ingrained that pressure cannot disrupt it. Keep it short, simple, repeatable. Don't step up to the ball (don't commit capital) until you've: (a) picked your target, (b) committed to the shot, (c) visualised the outcome. Applied: Richard's investment checklist IS his pre-shot routine. Execute it every time, mechanically, without variation.

**9. Competitive Rounds Teach What Practice Cannot**
"Competitive rounds teach you things about your game and your nerves that casual golf never will. The more often you put yourself in uncomfortable spots, the less uncomfortable they become." Applied: taking positions, making IR calls, living with drawdowns — these are competitive rounds. The discomfort is the training.

**10. Support Systems**
"You cannot succeed alone." Champions reward the people who help them by being loyal, enthusiastic, and helpful. Applied: Steve Ward, Julia, Dad, James, Watson — these are Richard's support system. Investing solo doesn't mean investing alone.

**11. Resilience and Dream Architecture**
Champions nurture a positive self-image, break ambitious dreams into realistic goals, learn to grow from setbacks. They pursue goals fully recognising they may not attain them, and find joy in the process of striving rather than in external rewards. The dream (£1.001bn AUM) is motivating; the process (daily checklist execution) is the actual work.

### Overlap with Other Frameworks

- **Bassham (With Winning in Mind):** Both emphasise self-image driving performance. Rotella's "be your own best friend" = Bassham's "that's like me." Both insist on positive self-talk and visualisation.
- **Gallwey (Inner Game):** Rotella's Training/Trusting distinction IS Gallwey's Self 1/Self 2 in different language. Training = Self 1 (conscious, critical). Trusting = Self 2 (subconscious, capable). Same insight, different framing.

## 7. Mark Minervini — Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard / Think & Trade Like a Champion / Mindset Secrets for Winning

<!-- [W] Added 03-Apr-26 from full coaching memo -->

### Core Principles
- **Trend Template (8-point checklist):** Mechanical filter for whether a stock is in a healthy uptrend. Price vs 50/150/200-day MAs, rising 200-day, RS ranking ≥70. If a held stock fails these criteria, the exit clock starts — no discretion.
- **Weinstein's 4 Stages:** Basing (1) → Advancing (2) → Distribution (3) → Declining (4). Only buy in Stage 2. Exit when Stage 3 confirmed. Stage 4 = immediate exit, no exceptions.
- **7-8% max loss rule:** Automatic exit if position declines 7-8% from entry. No debate about whether the thesis has changed. No waiting for next earnings. Out.
- **Progressive exposure:** 3% test position → add to 6-8% on price confirmation (holds above buy point 4+ weeks, higher high) → full size only on fundamental + technical validation. Limits downside on initial positions to portfolio rounding errors.
- **Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP):** Decreasing volatility and tightening ranges = institutional accumulation = buy signal. Increasing volatility and widening ranges = distribution = sell signal.
- **"Sell half at double the risk":** When gain equals 2× the distance to stop, take partial profits and move stop to breakeven.
- **Earnings acceleration:** Not just the level of growth, but whether growth is accelerating or decelerating. Deceleration is the Stage 3 warning in fundamental terms.

### Application to Richard's System
- **Bridges the Douglas gap:** Douglas gives the *philosophy* (probabilistic thinking, detachment from outcomes). Minervini gives the *engineering* (mechanical rules that enforce the philosophy when cognitive biases override intellectual understanding). They are a pair — Douglas is WHY, Minervini is HOW.
- **Directly addresses the #1 coaching target:** "Too often, I own for too long the stocks with negative price and fundamental momentum." The Trend Template + 30-day shot clock + immediate exit signals provide the mechanical enforcement layer.
- **RS Dashboard integration:** The dashboard Richard built is already the infrastructure for Minervini's system. Daily RS check → held position stage analysis → watchlist scanning → one decision. 10 minutes every morning.
- **Progressive exposure resolves the concentration tension:** Richard's 15% max positions mean each loss is painful. Starting at 3% and scaling up only on confirmation makes early losses into rounding errors — Douglas's "cost of doing business" made real.
- **Self-image bridge (via Bassham):** Every small loss cut quickly = evidence for the new self-image: "I am a disciplined position manager who protects capital ruthlessly." Every stop honoured reinforces the identity. Every override reinforces the old pattern.
- **Energy management (via Loehr):** The mechanical exit system is an energy management intervention — eliminates the most draining experience (sitting with a deteriorating position debating whether to act). Cutting at -7% frees bandwidth, eliminates tension, clears the priority stack.

See full protocol in `coaching/references/mark-minervini.md`, Section 5.3. Summary:

## 8. Annie Duke — Thinking in Bets / Quit / How to Decide

<!-- [W] Added 03-Apr-26 from full coaching memo -->

### Core Principles
- **Resulting:** Judging decision quality by outcome quality. The master bias for investors. Evaluate every decision on process alone.
- **Kill Criteria:** Pre-committed, observable conditions (states + dates) defined at the point of investment that specify exit conditions. Set when rational, honoured when biased.
- **Monkeys and Pedestals:** Solve the hardest thing first (the monkey / fulcrum driver). Don't build analytical infrastructure (pedestals) for unvalidated premises.
- **Sunk Cost / Endowment / Status Quo Bias Triad:** Three biases that compound to prevent exits. Sunk cost (analytical investment), endowment (overvaluing owned positions), status quo (preferring inaction). Together they multiply.
- **FOFR (Fear of Future Regret):** The fear that quitting and being wrong twice is worse than holding and being wrong once. Pre-commitment strips FOFR of its power.
- **Identity-Based Persistence:** When identity is tied to position outcomes, quitting feels like quitting who you are. Build identity around process execution instead.
- **Belief Calibration:** Express conviction as explicit probabilities. Track calibration over time. Update continuously with new information.
- **Decision Journals:** Record decisions, reasoning, and probabilities before outcomes are known. Prevents hindsight bias. Enables calibration.
- **Quitting Coach:** A structural role that advocates for exit when biases push toward holding. Watson APM role serves this function.

### Application to Richard's System
- Kill criteria enhance the 30-day shot clock: defined at entry (not at pain), objective states replace judgement calls, default is EXIT not reassess.
- Monkeys-first reframes IG process: test the fulcrum driver in 30 minutes before building the full case file.
- Probability calibration adds precision to conviction assessment: "Hell yeah" = 80%, "Meh" = 55%, "No" = below 50%.
- The inversion test ("Would I buy this today if I didn't own it?") strips endowment premium from the "Love" portfolio test.
- Duke + Minervini = cognitive pre-commitment + mechanical enforcement = complete exit discipline system.

### Overlap with Other Frameworks
- **Douglas:** Both demand process-over-outcome evaluation. Douglas provides philosophical acceptance; Duke provides operational tools (decision journals, calibration, kill criteria).
- **Bassham:** Duke's identity-based persistence diagnosis directly supports Bassham's self-image work. Target identity: "I am someone who sets kill criteria and honours them. That's like me."
- **Ward (4Cs):** Kill criteria serve all 4Cs — Commitment (to exit process), Concentration (reduces decision fatigue), Confidence (evidence of discipline), Control (pre-commitment is within your control).
- **Minervini:** Duke = cognitive architecture (WHY you don't exit). Minervini = mechanical enforcement (WHEN to exit). Together = most complete exit discipline framework. Neither works fully without the other.
- **Rotella:** Setting kill criteria = Training Mindset. Honouring them in the moment = Trusting Mindset. Rotella's separation applies directly to Duke's pre-commitment framework.
- **Van Tharp:** Duke provides the cognitive architecture (WHY you don't exit). Minervini provides the mechanical enforcement (WHEN to exit). Van Tharp provides the mathematical sizing (HOW MUCH to risk so the exit is structurally painless). All three together = cognitive + mechanical + mathematical = complete exit discipline system.

## 9. Van Tharp — Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom / Super Trader / The Definitive Guide to Position Sizing

<!-- [W] Added 03-Apr-26 from full coaching memo -->

### Background

Van K. Tharp, Ph.D. (1946–2022) was a trading performance coach and psychologist who spent 40+ years studying what makes traders/investors successful. Featured in Schwager's *Market Wizards* as "the trader's coach." Trained 5,000+ traders through the Van Tharp Institute and the four-year Super Trader programme (discontinued after his death in Feb 2022). Not primarily a trader himself — a researcher and coach, giving his work an empirical, process-design focus.

### Core Principles

- **"You don't trade the markets; you trade your beliefs about the markets."** Every decision is filtered through beliefs — about markets, risk, yourself. Many are unconscious and harmful. The Beliefs Audit identifies and replaces them.
- **The Six Components of a Trading System:** Setup, Entry, Stop/Protective Exit, Profit-Taking Exit, Position Sizing, Understanding Yourself. Most investors obsess over 1-4 and underinvest in 5-6. Tharp's hierarchy: Position Sizing and Understanding Yourself determine 90%+ of results.
- **R-Multiples:** R = initial risk per position. Every outcome expressed as a multiple of R. Strips emotional narrative from results. A -1R loss is structurally identical regardless of which stock produced it.
- **Expectancy:** Mean R-multiple across all trades. Expectancy = (Win% × AvgWinR) + (Loss% × AvgLossR). If positive, position sizing determines compounding speed. If negative, no sizing model saves you.
- **CPR Formula:** C (Capital at risk) / R (Risk per unit) = P (Position size). The universal position sizing equation. Makes emotional sizing impossible.
- **Percent Risk Model:** Risk a fixed % of equity per position. Position size = mathematical output, not a judgement call. Natural fit for concentrated fundamental approach.
- **Percent Volatility Model:** Size based on ATR. Useful as volatility normalisation overlay (comparing positions by volatility contribution, not nominal weight).
- **System Quality Number (SQN):** SQN = √N × (Mean R / StdDev R). Scores: <1.6 (Poor), 1.6-1.9 (Below avg), 2.0-2.4 (Average), 2.5-2.9 (Good), 3.0-5.0 (Excellent), 5.1+ (Superb). Single-number system quality metric.
- **Beliefs Audit:** List all beliefs about trading/sizing/exits. Test each: Is it useful? Accurate? Serving objectives? More than half of most traders' core beliefs are harmful.
- **Mind to Muscle (M2M):** Technique for installing beliefs at unconscious level — identify target belief, find evidence, physically embody through practice, test under stress.
- **"Trading is 100% psychology."** Not 80%. The system is a psychological construct. Beliefs, mental states, and self-awareness are the only controllable variables.
- **Position sizing accounts for 91.5% of variability in portfolio performance** (academic study Tharp cites on pension funds).

### Application to Richard's System

- **Primary gap filled:** The quantitative bridge between "I have a good process" and "I know, mathematically, how good my process is, and I size every position accordingly." R-multiples, expectancy, SQN, and CPR provide this bridge.
- **Progressive exposure formalised:** Test position (0.2% risk → ~3% size) → Confirmation (0.5% risk → ~7%) → Full (0.9% risk → ~11%) → Maximum (1.4% risk → 15% ceiling). Each step is a CPR calculation, not a conviction judgement.
- **Stop slippage quantified:** If 20% of losses become -4R instead of -1R, expectancy leaks ~0.26R per trade. Over 20 positions/year = ~5% of annual returns donated to the market. Eliminating stop slippage alone could raise CAGR from 8.3% to ~12%.
- **Beliefs Audit targets:** "I don't like binary fully exiting" (rewrite: stage-dependent, not attachment-dependent). "Trust my judgement on sizing" (rewrite: trust my system's mathematics). "I'm smart enough to override my rules" (the single most dangerous belief — replace with mechanical compliance).
- **SQN as meta-metric:** Track quarterly alongside 7-keystone scoring. Target SQN ≥ 2.5 within 12 months.
- **R-multiple log:** Every position tracked by Entry | Stop | 1R (%) | Current R-Multiple | Position Size (%). Objective self-image calibration tool.

### The 10-Point Van Tharp Implementation Plan

1. **R-Multiple Log** — set up for all held positions (30 min)
2. **Historical expectancy calculation** — last 12-24 months of completed trades
3. **CPR formula on next new position** — compare with "feel" sizing
4. **Percent Risk overlay on full portfolio** — flag oversized positions
5. **Beliefs Audit** — 10 most deeply held sizing/exit beliefs
6. **Quarterly SQN tracking** — target ≥ 2.5
7. **R-multiple language in all discussions** — "Greggs is at +3.6R" not "up 25%"
8. **Quarterly beliefs review** — did I honour the belief? Cost in R if not?
9. **Weekly "Van Tharp Check"** — "Worst R-multiple this week? Did it exceed -1R?"
10. **Position Sizing Pre-Mortem** — before every new position, "if this hits my stop, what's the R cost?"

### Overlap with Other Frameworks

- **Douglas:** Both demand process-over-outcome thinking. Douglas provides philosophical acceptance of uncertainty; Van Tharp provides mathematical infrastructure (expectancy, SQN, R-multiples) that makes the acceptance concrete and measurable.
- **Bassham:** R-multiple tracking provides the evidence base for self-image work. "I cut losses quickly" is a testable claim when you have an R-multiple log. Each -1R loss honoured = identity evidence.
- **Gallwey:** CPR formula reduces Self 1 interference at the sizing decision. The maths operates as Self 2 — trained, automatic, not subject to conscious override.
- **Clear:** R-multiple log = habit tracking for position management. Makes good behaviour visible. "I am someone who sizes by CPR" = identity-based habit.
- **Ward (4Cs):** CPR serves all 4Cs — Commitment (to mathematical sizing), Concentration (only three numbers matter per position), Confidence (measured positive expectancy), Control (position size is within your control).
- **Rotella:** Position sizing model designed in Training mode (analytical). Executed in Trusting mode (mechanical). Rotella's separation applies directly.
