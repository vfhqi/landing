## DEPTH AND COMPLETENESS — MANDATORY

Aim for comprehensive coverage. Every analytical sub-question named in SECTIONS TO COVER must be addressed substantively. The bulleted format constrains the *shape* of output, not its *depth*.

**Q10-specific:** present the bear case FAIRLY and in DETAIL — not dismissively. Tilt the entire memo toward bearish interpretation. The sceptical bullets per section invert the bear case (i.e., they're the bull-side rebuttal); the body is the bear case.

If in doubt, write more substantive bullets rather than longer ones. Fact-check all bear allegations: separate fact from opinion.

**The test:** would Richard learn something about the bear case from this memo that he couldn't get from the most pessimistic sell-side analyst's note? If the bullet just restates the lowest-PT analyst's view, it's filler. If it triangulates short-seller reports + accounting concerns + online commentary + counter-arguments to bull thesis to surface a non-obvious downside vector — that's analytical content.

### §6 — Counter-Arguments to Bull Case

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on the strongest bull-thesis-rebuttal — single most credible bear inversion of the case — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Bull thesis #1:", "Bear rebuttal #1:", "Bull thesis #2:", "Bear rebuttal #2:", "Evidence supporting rebuttal:", "Track record evidence:", "Competitive evidence:", "Macro evidence:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- For EACH major bull thesis point (drawn from prior research output Q1-Q9), articulate the strongest bear rebuttal.
- Structure as direct rebuttals: for each bull thesis point, provide the corresponding bear thesis with supporting evidence.
- Examples (illustrative):
  - Bull: "Management is fixing the company, margins should expand to 25%" → Bear: "Management has track record of disappointing. Margin expansion has stalled before. Competitors are price-cutting, suggesting margin pressure is structural."
  - Bull: "New product launch will drive growth to 15%" → Bear: "Customer adoption is slow. Competitive pressure is intense. Sales organisation is weak."

**Coverage:** 10-15 parent bullets + sub-bullets. One bull-thesis-point parent bullet, with bear-rebuttal sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet (BULL rebuttal):** "Across the bear rebuttals, where is the bear most likely wrong? Which rebuttal has the weakest evidence base?"

### §9 — Risk Assessment + Sceptical Synthesis (cross-cutting)

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Cross-cutting bear synthesis — what's the strongest bear case AND probability assessment — ≤30w.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**

**Coverage:** 12-15 parent bullets + sub-bullets. This is the dedicated cross-cutting bear synthesis. The per-section bull rebuttals above feed in here.
