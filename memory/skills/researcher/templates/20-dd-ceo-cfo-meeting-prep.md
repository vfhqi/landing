## MISSION

Prepare 15-25 questions for a meeting with {COMPANY} CEO and/or CFO. Each question is specific, testable, financial-centric where applicable (walk the P&L), and designed to REVEAL information not available in public filings. Output: bulleted memo with J-front verdict per theme, expected-answer + good-vs-bad-answer scaffolding per question, ⚡ outlier-detection flags for non-obvious questioning angles, peer/base-rate anchoring on questions that reference cohort norms. ≥3,000w per source ([C] + [AS]).

## CONTEXT — What the reader cares about

**Audience:** Concentrated long-only equity investor (5-15 positions), UK/Europe focus, $5-50bn market cap sweet spot. Holds 12-24 months. Singular focus: predictable 18M-3Y EPS trajectory with 25%+ IRR potential.

**Stage — DD:** Q20 sits in DD per D-RSR-32. Direct access to management is the load-bearing data-gathering opportunity. The questions in this memo are the reader's prep — they should be sharp enough that management's answers (and non-answers) are diagnostic.

**What downstream uses this output:** Reader's pre-meeting prep + post-meeting back-brief structure. APM cross-references Q20 questions with Q15-Q18 DD findings to identify which questions surface evidence on the highest-uncertainty pillars. Memo also surfaces on RESEARCH STAGES dashboard tab.

**Canonical taxonomy reference (per D-RSR-33):** the authoritative source is `memory/skills/assistant-portfolio-manager/analysis-judgement-SOP.md`. Hierarchy: **Pillar > Group (BB#) > Element / TC > Required Attribute (RA) > Core Question (CQ)**. Q20 questions cross-cut all six pillars but weight to the pillars the analyst has highest uncertainty on. RESEARCHER does NOT grade — APM grades.

**Mental models:** Walk the P&L (top-down financial probing), Diagnostic question (the answer discriminates between competing hypotheses), Power of incentives (per Concept A — what management is incentivised to disclose vs withhold), ACH (cross-reference with Q19 pre-mortem — questions should test the diagnostic items in the ACH matrix), Strong views weakly held (each question should have a stated prior — what answer the analyst expects, plus what answer would surprise).

## SECTIONS TO COVER

### §1 — Executive Summary (top-3 most-diagnostic questions)

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Top 3 most-diagnostic questions identified — questions whose answer materially discriminates between bull/bear/false-friend profiles — ≤30w.

**Coverage:** 5-8 parent bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What questions does the analyst NOT want to ask, and why? Concept B CRITICAL ACTION 1 — go to the most-different question."

---

### §2 — Theme 1: Revenue drivers + visibility

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on revenue-side uncertainty + which questions are diagnostic — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Backlog visibility:", "Customer concentration:", "Churn / retention:", "Pricing power:", "Volume vs price split:", "Share gain / loss:", "End-market segmentation:", "Forward order book:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Required questions: 3-5.** For each:
- **Question (verbatim, ≤30w).**
- **Expected answer (analyst's prior — what is the most likely answer? ≤30w).**
- **Good answer signature (≤25w).**
- **Bad answer signature (≤25w).**
- **Follow-up probe** if the answer is ambiguous (≤25w).
- **Diagnostic note:** which competing hypothesis (bull / bear / false-friend) does each plausible answer most strongly discriminate? Cross-ref Q19 ACH matrix.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read if management answers fluently — does fluent answer = comfort, or = rehearsed?"

Same scaffolding. Questions probing BB#2 TC1 RA2 + RA3 — culture, communication clarity, sandbagging discipline, animal-CEO archetype probes.

### §7 — Theme 6: Risk + downside (incl. pre-mortem cross-ref)

Same scaffolding. Cross-reference Q19 pre-mortem — questions that test the highest-plausibility failure modes.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Bear-case probe:", "Failure mode test:", "Hedge effectiveness:", "Inventory at risk:", "Customer churn vector:", "Regulatory exposure:", "Pricing fragility:", "False-friend probe:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "Which Q19 ACH-matrix diagnostic items are NOT covered by any question in this memo? Surface explicitly."

---

### §8 — Cross-cutting meta + non-obvious questions (LOAD-BEARING)

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on questions designed to surface what management WOULDN'T volunteer — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Non-obvious angle:", "WH Smith probe:", "Cross-roads question:", "Edge-detection probe:", "Incentive probe:", "Multiple-truths probe:", "Diagnostic crystalliser:", "Stewing trigger:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Apply Concept A (look at the edges — deliberately weird):** at least 2-3 questions in this section should be the *unconventional* questions. The WH Smith CEO archetype: ask the question the consensus interview wouldn't. Examples:
- "What is the most surprising piece of customer feedback you received in the last 6 months?"
- "What single metric, if it moved against you, would most worry you — and what's the current trend on that metric?"
- "If you had to cut 20% of capex tomorrow, which projects would you keep?"

**Apply Concept B CRITICAL ACTION 3 (multiple truths coexist):** at least one question should explicitly invite the "and yet" answer — i.e. acknowledge the bull case AND ask for the bear-case nuance simultaneously, so management can't dodge into either-or framing.

**Coverage:** 8-12 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "Which questions has the analyst NOT included because asking them would feel uncomfortable? Surface them. The obstacle is the way (per Marcus Aurelius / Concept B CRITICAL ACTION 1)."
