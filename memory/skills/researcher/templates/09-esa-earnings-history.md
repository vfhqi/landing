## CONTEXT — What the Reader Cares About

**Audience:** Richard Black, concentrated long-only equity investor (5-15 positions), UK/European focus, $5-50bn market cap. Holds 12-24 months. Singular focus: predictable 18M-3Y EPS trajectory with 25%+ IRR potential.

**ESA purpose:** (1) Interrogate the change thesis — is the change significant enough to drive required financial outputs? Is management dynamic enough to execute? Are the required outputs (EPS trajectory, returns) compelling? (2) Breadth coverage — ensure ALL aspects of the investment case are covered at light or medium depth. Setup title crystallises during ESA. ESA also establishes key risks, key questions, and key confusions for DD.

**Why this query matters:** The forward thesis rests on a specific EPS trajectory (typically 3Y CAGR, 25%+ IRR). Before betting on that trajectory, the reader must understand: Does the company HAVE A TRACK RECORD of delivering on this magnitude and type of growth? Are margins expanding sustainably or via one-off benefits? Does management sandbag (guide conservatively) or overpromise (guide aggressively)? Are there quality concerns (accounting adjustments, working capital tailwinds, asset disposals)? Q9 answers those questions by examining what the company has ACTUALLY delivered vs what it SAID it would deliver.

**Mental models:** Earnings quality framework, beat/miss analysis, management credibility, margin sustainability, working capital trends, cash conversion, inflection point analysis.

### §2 — Quality of Earnings (red flag / green flag assessment)

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on earnings quality — clean, mixed, or concerning — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "One-off impact:", "Adjustments trend:", "Recurring vs one-off:", "Non-GAAP gap:", "NWC tailwind:", "NWC headwind:", "Receivables turn:", "Payables turn:", "Cash conversion:", "OCF vs NI:", "D&A trend:", "Aggressive accounting:", "Conservative accounting:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- Recurring vs one-off items: quantify the impact of reported one-off items (asset sales, pension gains, restructuring charges, M&A costs) on reported vs adjusted earnings.
- Adjustments trend: are the company's adjustments (non-GAAP earnings) increasing or decreasing over time? Are there recurring items the company treats as one-off?
- Working capital trends: is NWC a tailwind or headwind? Are receivables/payables turning faster or slower?
- Cash conversion: operating cash flow vs net income. Is the company converting earnings into cash or deteriorating?
- Depreciation/amortisation trends: stable or increasing (suggesting more aggressive accounting or heavier CapEx depreciation)?

**Coverage:** 10-15 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on earnings quality? Through what mechanism might apparent margin growth be flattered by recurring items dressed up as one-off? What hidden quality deterioration might be lurking?"

**Format:** `⚡ RARE: signpost: finding statement. Peer / base-rate anchor. [Cross-ref: §X]`
