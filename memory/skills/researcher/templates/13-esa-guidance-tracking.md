**ESA purpose:** Interrogate the change thesis + breadth coverage. ESA establishes key risks, key questions, and key confusions for DD.

**Why this query matters:** The investment thesis typically rests on management's forward guidance: "Revenue will grow 15% over N3Y, margins will expand to 25%, EPS will CAGR at 20%." Q13 answers those questions through (1) historical guidance-vs-delivery comparison, (2) pattern identification (beat/miss behaviour), (3) credibility assessment, and (4) defining specific monitoring metrics.

## SECTIONS TO COVER

### §1 — Current Management Guidance

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on what management has currently promised — quantitative targets + caveats — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "FY current — revenue:", "FY current — EBITDA:", "FY current — EBIT:", "FY current — EPS:", "FY current — FCF:", "FY current — CapEx:", "Medium-term — N2Y:", "Medium-term — N3Y:", "Medium-term targets:", "Source — earnings call:", "Source — investor day:", "Source — written:", "Update timing:", "Caveat:", "Conditional assumption:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- Quantitative targets for current fiscal year and medium-term (2-3 years forward): revenue growth %, EBITDA / EBIT margin %, EPS growth %, FCF, CapEx, working capital, other KPIs.
- Where was guidance provided? (Earnings calls, investor presentations, written guidance, analyst day.)
- When was it provided? Most recent update? Any change?
- Caveats or conditions? (e.g., "assuming no further acquisitions", "organic growth before M&A", "excluding one-time items".)

**Coverage:** 8-12 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on the current guidance? Through what mechanism might apparent quantification mask aggressive embedded assumptions?"

### §2 — Historical Guidance Accuracy — Last 4 Reporting Periods (LOAD-BEARING)

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on management's track record of guidance delivery across L4 periods — ≤30w.

**MANDATORY: historical guidance-accuracy table** with the following columns for each of the last 4 reporting periods:

| Period | Metric (revenue / EBITDA / EBIT / EPS / FCF) | Guidance provided | Actual delivered | Variance ($) | Variance (%) | Beat/miss direction + magnitude |

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "L4P period:", "Beat magnitude:", "Miss magnitude:", "Beat/miss frequency:", "Average variance:", "Direction of surprise:", "Worst miss:", "Best beat:", "Volatility — high:", "Volatility — low:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- Build the L4-period table covering all major metrics (revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, EPS, FCF).
- Summarise: across all periods, what's the average beat/miss magnitude? % of periods with beats vs misses?
- Worst miss + best beat: identify the extremes and the context.

**Coverage:** 10-15 parent bullets + sub-bullets surrounding the table. Table is the centre.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on the historical accuracy? Through what mechanism might apparent beats be late-cycle / one-off / accounting-flattered?"

### §3 — Guidance Pattern (sandbagging vs over-promising)

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on management's guidance posture — sandbagger / over-promiser / honest tracker — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Sandbagger signal:", "Over-promiser signal:", "Honest tracker:", "Mixed pattern:", "Beat consistency:", "Miss consistency:", "Magnitude — beat:", "Magnitude — miss:", "Pattern shift:", "Improving accuracy:", "Deteriorating accuracy:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- Consistent sandbagging: does management consistently guide low and beat? If so, current guidance may be conservative and credible.
- Consistent over-promising: does management consistently guide high and miss? If so, current guidance may be aggressive and risky.
- Mixed: does accuracy vary (some beats, some misses, no clear pattern)? If so, assess variability and explanation.
- Trend: is the pattern improving or deteriorating over time?
- Specific examples with magnitudes from recent periods.

**Coverage:** 8-12 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on the guidance pattern? Through what mechanism might apparent sandbagging be running out of cushion? Or apparent improvement be cyclical not structural?"

---

### §4 — Trend in Guidance Accuracy

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on whether management's predictive accuracy is improving or deteriorating — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Recent accuracy:", "Historical accuracy:", "Accuracy improving:", "Accuracy deteriorating:", "Volatility driver — macro:", "Volatility driver — execution:", "Volatility driver — complexity:", "Metric — best accuracy:", "Metric — worst accuracy:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- Compare guidance accuracy in most recent period vs 2-3 years ago.
- What's driving any deterioration? (Market volatility, macro unpredictability, internal execution issues, business complexity.)
- Are there specific metrics (revenue vs margin vs EPS) where guidance accuracy is better or worse?

**Coverage:** 6-10 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on accuracy trend? Through what mechanism might recent accuracy improvement reverse?"

---

### §5 — Sell-Side Consensus vs Management Guidance

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on whether SS consensus is more bullish or more cautious than management — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Consensus FY:", "Consensus N2Y:", "Consensus N3Y:", "Mgmt-vs-SS gap:", "SS more bullish:", "SS more cautious:", "Aligned:", "Revision trend L6M:", "Revision trend L12M:", "Improving outlook:", "Deteriorating outlook:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- Current forward guidance from management (if provided).
- Current consensus estimates from sell-side (FactSet, Bloomberg).
- Comparison: is sell-side consensus above or below management guidance? By how much?
- Interpretation: SS above guidance = market more bullish. SS below = market more cautious.
- Estimate revisions trend: over past 6-12 months, have consensus estimates been revised UP (improving outlook) or DOWN (deteriorating)?

**Coverage:** 8-12 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on the consensus-vs-guidance gap? Through what mechanism might SS confidence be misplaced or lagging?"

---

### §6 — Key Assumptions Behind Guidance

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on the credibility of underlying assumptions + sensitivity vulnerability — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Revenue assumption — customer:", "Revenue assumption — pricing:", "Revenue assumption — M&A:", "Revenue assumption — market:", "Margin assumption — cost:", "Margin assumption — leverage:", "Margin assumption — pricing power:", "EPS assumption — tax:", "EPS assumption — share count:", "EPS assumption — capital structure:", "Macro assumption — GDP:", "Macro assumption — currency:", "Macro assumption — commodity:", "Sensitivity — 1pp revenue:", "Sensitivity — 50bps margin:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- For each major forward metric (revenue growth, margin expansion, EPS growth), identify the key assumptions:
  - Revenue: customer growth rate, price increases, M&A. Markets growing vs shrinking.
  - Margin: cost structure assumptions. (Input cost inflation, operating leverage, pricing power.)
  - EPS: implied tax rate, share count, capital structure.
  - Macro: GDP, currency, commodity, interest rates.
- Sensitivity analysis: if any assumption changes (e.g., 1% slower revenue growth, 50bps higher input costs), what happens to EPS?

**Coverage:** 12-18 parent bullets + sub-bullets. Mandatory: sensitivity table (assumption variable × ±change × EPS impact).

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on the assumption stack? Through what mechanism might 2-3 assumptions deteriorate simultaneously? Which assumption is most fragile?"

---

### §7 — Credibility Assessment

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on overall credibility of current guidance + margin of safety — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Credibility — verbal verdict:", "Red flag — recent miss:", "Red flag — aggressive assumption:", "Red flag — macro risk:", "Red flag — execution risk:", "Green flag — recent beat:", "Green flag — conservative assumption:", "Green flag — execution track record:", "Margin of safety — high:", "Margin of safety — low:", "Thesis sensitivity:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- Based on historical accuracy + pattern + assumption plausibility, rate guidance credibility verbally: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW. (Verbal verdict, NOT a grade.)
- Red flags: recent miss pattern, aggressive assumptions, macro risks, execution risks.
- Green flags: recent beat pattern, conservative assumptions, execution track record, macro tailwinds.
- Margin of safety: if guidance is off by 10% (either direction), is the investment still compelling? Or highly sensitive?

**Coverage:** 8-12 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on credibility? Through what mechanism might apparent credibility prove brittle? What single assumption failure would invalidate the credibility verdict?"

### §8 — Sell-Side Guidance Credibility Consensus

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Net signal on what SS analysts are saying about management's guidance credibility — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "SS confidence:", "SS skepticism:", "SS expecting beat:", "SS expecting miss:", "Consensus skeptic — metric:", "Bullish analyst:", "Cautious analyst:", "Estimate revision direction:", "Implied PT vs guidance:", "Specific concern theme:", "Specific enthusiasm theme:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- From recent sell-side equity research, are analysts expressing confidence in management guidance or skepticism?
- Are analysts expecting beats or misses? (Evidence from estimate revisions, analyst commentary, price targets implied by guidance.)
- Is there consensus skepticism about specific metrics? (e.g., "revenue outlook conservative but margin assumptions aggressive".)
- Per-named-broker positioning where breadth allows.

**Coverage:** 10-15 parent bullets + sub-bullets.

**End with sceptical bullet:** "What's the bearish read on SS positioning? Through what mechanism might analysts be lagging in their skepticism / over-confident?"

---

### §9 — Forward Tracking Metrics + Sceptical Synthesis (cross-cutting)

**Open with J-front verdict bullet:** Cross-cutting bear synthesis on guidance credibility + key forward tracking metrics — ≤30w.

**Canonical signpost vocabulary:** "Tracking metric — revenue:", "Tracking metric — margin:", "Tracking metric — cash conversion:", "Tracking metric — leading indicator:", "Tracking cadence:", "Early warning — miss probability:", "Reverse-engineered bear:", "Single largest credibility risk:", "Threshold for invalidation:". Invent where pattern warrants.

**Analytical sub-questions to address:**
- What specific metrics should be monitored to test whether forward guidance will be achieved?
- For each tracking metric, define: what to monitor, expected trajectory, warning thresholds, monitoring cadence.
- Examples (illustrative): customer churn rate (quarterly), input cost inflation (monthly), order book trajectory (quarterly).
- Reverse-engineer the bear case: what's the most credible negative read on guidance achievement?
- Single largest credibility risk: which assumption / metric / pattern most damages the case?

**Coverage:** 10-15 parent bullets + sub-bullets. Mandatory: tracking-metrics table (metric × expected trajectory × warning threshold × cadence).

**End:** No sceptical bullet (whole section is synthesis). Instead end with: "Confidence in forward guidance achievement: HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW — verbal verdict only, ≤30w, NOT a grade."
