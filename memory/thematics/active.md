## Purpose

Codifies the major macro/strategic themes that matter for capital allocation. Each thematic defines beneficiaries and at-risk profiles with detailed attribute lists and probable setups. The APM uses this file as an operational overlay during stock analysis, portfolio construction, and risk management. The RESEARCHER uses it as a reference when running thematic research (Query #23).

## How to Read This File

**For each thematic:**
1. **Definition** — 2-3 sentences describing the thematic
2. **Beneficiary Summary** — 1-2 sentences on who wins
3. **At-Risk Summary** — 1-2 sentences on who loses
4. **Beneficiary Attributes** — Detailed characteristics that make a stock a beneficiary
5. **Beneficiary Probable Setups** — What stock-level patterns to look for (thematic-specific; FCS setup mapping where natural)
6. **At-Risk Attributes** — Detailed characteristics that make a stock vulnerable
7. **At-Risk Probable Setups** — What stock-level patterns emerge for at-risk names

**A-F Rating Scale (consistent with FCS):**
- **A** — Strong beneficiary / strong alignment. Multiple attribute matches, clear transmission to EPS.
- **B** — Beneficiary / positive alignment. 2-3 attribute matches, plausible EPS uplift.
- **C** — Neutral / mixed. Some attributes match both sides, or thematic is immaterial to the stock.
- **D** — Mild headwind. 1-2 at-risk attributes match, limited EPS drag.
- **E** — At-risk. 3+ at-risk attributes, clear negative EPS transmission.
- **F** — Strong headwind. Core business model directly threatened by the thematic.

## Thematic 1: Bear Market / Top of Bull Market

**Research Status:** [C+AS synthesised — 16-Apr-26] | Notion: https://www.notion.so/34435e909b0b81e59aedfd3b6955d41d

### Definition
European equity markets showing late-cycle characteristics: elevated valuations in growth/momentum names, narrowing breadth, rising correlation, and increasing fragility to macro shocks. The question is not IF a correction comes but WHEN and HOW DEEP. Capital allocation must be defensive: favour companies with pricing power, recurring revenues, and low financial leverage that can compound through a downturn, while reducing exposure to stocks whose valuations depend on sustained multiple expansion or cyclical peak earnings.

### Beneficiary Summary
Companies with pricing power, recurring non-cyclical revenues, and fortress balance sheets. Infrastructure monopolies (utilities, telecom), essential healthcare, domestic-facing banks with deposit franchises, and insurance companies with strong underwriting discipline. They compress less on the downside and re-rate materially upward as risk premiums expand and capital rotates from growth to stability.

### At-Risk Summary
Companies dependent on sustained multiple expansion, cyclical earnings peak, or high financial leverage. Consumer discretionary, chemicals, luxury goods, and highly leveraged small-caps face structural repricing — valuations reset downward faster than earnings deteriorate, compounding losses over 12–24 months.

### Beneficiary Attributes

**Revenue/Demand:**
| Attribute | Transmission | Examples | Importance |
|-----------|-------------|----------|------------|
| Recurring, contracted revenues (subscription/SLA-based) | Earnings pre-sold, immune to demand shocks | Deutsche Telekom, Orange, Enel | **Critical** |
| Inelastic demand for essential services | Households cannot cut electricity/water/telecom to zero | E.ON, Engie, Roche, Nestlé | **Critical** |
| Domestic EU footprint with pricing power | Tariff barriers + regulatory protection; cost inflation passable | Tesco, UniCredit, European telcos | **Important** |
| Secular tailwinds (healthcare, digital infra) | Structural demand independent of macro cycle | Roche, Novartis, Sartorius, Swisscom | **Important** |

**Strategic/Competitive:**

### Beneficiary Probable Setups

**"Defensive Compounder at Undervaluation":** Quality company trading 15-20% below historical median P/E or 10-15% below peers. Earnings revisions stable or gently rising. Relative strength flat or slightly negative (being de-risked by market). This is the BFF hiding in plain sight. Entry targets: utilities 13-15x P/E, incumbent telecoms 6-8x EV/EBITDA, pharma 16-18x P/E.

**Catalysts:** Multiple expansion asymmetry as market reprices from late-cycle to risk-off (utilities 14x→17-18x, pharma 16x→18-19x). Dividend acceleration as growth stocks cut returns. Credit market stress driving flight-to-quality. Earnings recession in cyclicals creating self-reinforcing "safety bid."

**Risks even for beneficiaries:** Valuation trap if earnings are at peak (utilities energy pricing normalisation). Over-crowding once rotation is underway (utilities at 19-20x, seen in 2011). Regulatory risk (UK energy price caps). Liquidity/capex stress if 2008-style credit event.

**FCS setup mapping:** Maps to "Quality re-rating from undervaluation" and "Dividend income plus capital appreciation."

### At-Risk Probable Setups

**"Cyclical Peak Trap":** Earnings at peak or inflection (recent beats mask guidance for deceleration). P/E above historical median assuming continued growth. Relative strength rolling over after 6-12 month outperformance. Examples: BASF/Covestro at 10-20Y EBITDA highs (2021-23); LVMH at €800 (2021, now €650); peak-margin autos.

**Triggers:** Earnings guidance cuts (5-10% re-rating per miss). Margin compression >100bp signalling structural headwinds. Credit market stress / refinancing risk. Macro shocks resetting growth expectations. Analyst downgrades triggering momentum unwinds.

**Temporary vs. Structural:** Temporary = cyclical trough visible, forward guidance shows 12-18M recovery, leverage manageable, pricing power intact on recovery. Structural = earnings peak in rear-view, no recovery path, leverage >4x, declining market share, management in denial. European chemicals (BASF, Covestro) currently **temporary** — energy normalising, demand will recover. Luxury (LVMH, Kering) moving from temporary toward **structural** — six consecutive quarters negative EPS growth. Leveraged small-caps **structural** — operating leverage negative, refinancing 2026-28, covenant thin.

**FCS setup mapping:** Maps to "Earnings deterioration + multiple compression," "Leverage trap," "Structural disruption and competitive decline."

## Thematic 2: AI Disruption / Opportunities

**Research Status:** [C+AS synthesised — 16-Apr-26] | Notion: https://www.notion.so/34435e909b0b81719134d3d80a5997fc

### Definition
AI (large language models, autonomous agents, computer vision, robotics) is reshaping competitive dynamics across every sector. The question for capital allocation is twofold: (1) which companies are AI enablers — selling the tools, infrastructure, or data that make AI work — and will see durable demand uplift, and (2) which companies are AI-disrupted — facing margin compression, disintermediation, or obsolescence as AI automates their value proposition or empowers their competitors. European-listed companies are disproportionately in the "adopter" or "disrupted" camp rather than "enabler," making the at-risk analysis especially important.

### Beneficiary Summary
Companies that supply AI infrastructure (semiconductors, equipment, optical networking), embed AI into existing products to increase stickiness and pricing power (enterprise software, cloud platforms), or provide essential data/logistics for AI training. They capture a material share of $3T+ cumulative capex through 2030. EU beneficiaries concentrate in semi-cap, equipment, optical connectivity, and enterprise software at €5-50bn market cap.

### At-Risk Summary
Professional services firms, staffing agencies, commoditised SaaS, and any company whose value proposition depends on labour arbitrage or commodity software features. AI enables clients to bring sourcing in-house, bypass intermediaries, and automate 40%+ of labour-intensive processes. Margin compression already visible; structural disruption deepens through 2027-28.

### Beneficiary Attributes

**Revenue/Demand:**
| Attribute | Transmission | Examples | Importance |
|-----------|-------------|----------|------------|
| AI-specific revenue contribution (€500m-€3bn+/year by 2027) | Hyperscaler AI capex ($700bn 2026) creates explicit demand forecasts | Infineon (AI power €1.5bn FY26→€2.5bn FY27), STMicroelectronics (silicon photonics), Aixtron (datacom lasers) | **Critical** |
| Pricing power tied to AI infra criticality | AI capex non-discretionary and time-critical; customers can't delay or switch | ASML (EUV monopoly), VAT Group (vacuum components), ASM International | **Critical** |
| Long-term revenue visibility (3-5Y contracts) | Hyperscalers lock in capex 12-24M ahead; suppliers gain forward visibility | ASML (2Y+ backlog), Infineon (explicit AI guidance to 2027) | **Important** |
| Cross-sector design wins and TAM expansion | AI chips needed in industrial automation, autonomous vehicles, edge computing, healthcare | Infineon (automotive AI), STMicro (industrial vision) | **Important** |

**Cost/Margin:**
| Attribute | Transmission | Examples | Importance |
|-----------|-------------|----------|------------|
| Operating leverage in scaled manufacturing | Semi equipment enjoys 60-75%+ gross margins; SG&A absorbed across larger base → 200-400bp OPM expansion | ASML (45→55%+ OPM), Infineon (targeting 25%+ AI segment OPM) | **Important** |
| No labour cost inflation headwind | Cost structure is materials and engineering (fixed/amortised), not headcount-sensitive | ASML, Aixtron, VAT Group | **Important** |

**Strategic/Competitive:**
| Attribute | Transmission | Examples | Importance |
|-----------|-------------|----------|------------|
| Moat via scale and switching cost | Multi-year lock-in once hyperscaler standardises; switching operationally prohibitive | ASML (switching costs = $billions), Infineon (5Y+ embedded design wins), SAP (ERP switching costs) | **Critical** |
| Regulatory tailwind (EU Chips Act, RAID-E) | €43bn allocated to boost EU semiconductor manufacturing; reduced permitting friction | ASML, Infineon, STMicro benefit from subsidies | **Important** |
| Market share gains vs. non-AI-focused competitors | AI-native products or AI layers gain share from slower rivals | SAP (AI layers in ERP, NRR >120%), Siemens Healthineers (AI diagnostics) | **Important** |

### Beneficiary Probable Setups

**"AI Enabler Growth at Inflationary Multiples":** 20-32x PE (premium to European 12-15x), justified by 15%+ EPS CAGR and explicit AI revenue forecasts. Multi-quarter earnings beats driven by AI-specific revenue. Stock in 6-9M uptrend with pullbacks contained to 10-15%. AI revenue becoming 20%+ of total by FY2027.

**Catalysts:** Quarterly deliveries showing AI revenue >15% sequential growth. Hyperscaler capex extensions ("elevated through 2028"). Supply constraint relief (Infineon Dresden fab summer 2026, ASML capacity). New design wins.

**Risks:** Over-earning risk if AI capex slows (macro recession, policy shift). Valuation stretch at 28-32x pricing 5+ years of 15%+ CAGR. Crowding and institutional buying creating liquidity risk on downside. Geopolitical capex reallocation (US/China prioritise domestic).

**FCS setup mapping:** Maps to "Growth at Scale" and "Earnings Inflection."

### At-Risk Attributes

**Revenue/Demand:**
| Attribute | Transmission | Examples | Importance |
|-----------|-------------|----------|------------|
| AI disintermediation of core revenue stream | Staffing fees (15-25%) reducible 30-50% in 3Y as AI recruiting platforms enable in-house | Adecco (18-20% placement fees), Heidrick & Struggles (exec search 25-33% fee) | **Critical** |
| Dual squeeze: wage inflation + volume decline | Wage inflation for remaining staff persists; fee volumes decline simultaneously | Adecco (organic revenue -5% FY25, gross margin compression) | **Critical** |
| Commoditised SaaS facing free/cheap AI alternatives | Generic features (HR, finance, basic CRM) compete with AI agents and open-source; NRR collapsing | Deltek, Unit4; SaaS valuations compressed 40-50% (7.0x→5.1-5.5x EV/Rev) | **Critical** |
| Pricing power collapse | Once product reproducible by AI, suppliers can't raise prices or prevent churn | Adecco (placement rates declining 5-10%), generic SaaS (subscription increases rejected) | **Important** |

**Cost/Margin:**
| Attribute | Transmission | Examples | Importance |
|-----------|-------------|----------|------------|
| Labour cost inflation with declining productivity | Junior staff costs rise 8-12%, but project hours decline 20-30%; fixed costs can't scale down | Adecco (recruiters), consulting firms (junior consultants) | **Critical** |
| High fixed cost base inflexible to volume decline | Office rent, management layers, legacy IT = 30-45% of revenue; 15-25% revenue decline compresses EBITDA | Adecco (fixed costs ~35%), mid-market consulting | **Important** |
| Tech investment without revenue upside | Must invest 3-5% of revenue in AI tools to compete, but reduces cost/unit not revenue | Adecco (€200m Salesforce/r.Potential platform) | **Important** |

**Strategic/Competitive:**
| Attribute | Transmission | Examples | Importance |
|-----------|-------------|----------|------------|
| No defensible moat against AI replacement | Value from labour arbitrage and information asymmetry; AI eliminates both | Heidrick (exec search model), Adecco (placement matching) | **Critical** |
| Market share loss to AI-native competitors + in-house | New AI-native recruiting/consulting emerges; larger clients build internal AI teams | Adecco (losing to Eightfold, Phenom; large clients internalising) | **Critical** |
| Valuation re-rating downward | As disruption becomes visible in earnings, multiples compress from 8-10x→5-7x PE | Adecco, European SaaS | **Important** |

### At-Risk Probable Setups

**"Structural Revenue Decline with Sticky Costs":** 8-12x PE (below historical 12-15x) but still priced as if costs adjust proportionally (they won't). Flat-to-negative 12M returns with periodic rallies that sell off post-earnings. FY26 flat to -5%, FY27 declining 5-15%.

**Triggers:** Earnings misses on placement/consulting volumes (AI-driven decline 10-15% faster than expected). Guidance cuts flagging AI-driven margin compression. Client concentration risk (loss of major client). Dividend cuts or suspension as FCF erodes.

**Temporary vs. Structural:** Temporary = macro-sensitive consulting with strong relationships and diversified services; stable EBITDA 25%+; recovery by 2027 if macro improves. Structural = commodity placement model, no adjacent services, high fixed costs (Adecco template); margin compression 200-400bp/year; ROIC below WACC by FY27-28. Separation occurs Q2-Q4 2026 earnings: temporary shows volume -5-8% but stable margins; structural shows -10-15% and 200-300bp compression.

**FCS setup mapping:** Maps to "Value Trap" and "Structural Decline."

## Thematic 3: Iran War + Oil Price / Value Chain

**Research Status:** [C+AS synthesised — 16-Apr-26] | Notion: https://www.notion.so/34435e909b0b816a8f3dc37a03aee61c

### Definition
Active military conflict involving Iran (late March 2026 onwards) creating oil supply disruption risk, shipping route uncertainty (Strait of Hormuz), and second/third-order effects through energy costs, input price inflation, consumer confidence erosion, and geopolitical risk premia. Oil price is the primary transmission mechanism but not the only one — insurance costs, shipping rates, defence spending, and sanctions regimes all propagate through European corporate earnings. Duration and escalation are unknowable, making attribute-based screening (rather than point forecasts) the right approach.

### Beneficiary Summary
Oil/gas majors capture immediate upside from $100+ Brent through cash flow leverage. European defence contractors benefit from NATO spending acceleration (+5% GDP by 2035). Shipping rates and insurance surcharges create margin expansion for specialised operators. Cable/infrastructure plays (undersea cables, grid resilience) benefit from EU strategic autonomy capex. TotalEnergies, ENI, Shell, Rheinmetall, Leonardo, NKT/NEX/PRY emerge as structural winners.

### At-Risk Summary
Airlines face unhedged/thin-coverage fuel exposure (SAS 0% hedged, easyJet thins Q3-Q4). European chemicals structurally uncompetitive — naphtha-based production (75% EU capacity) absorbs oil shocks while US competitors run on cheaper ethane. Consumer discretionary bleeds from demand destruction and ECB tightening. Automotive suppliers dependent on margin recovery face directional headwinds.
