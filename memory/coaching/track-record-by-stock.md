### BFF — QUALITY THAT DOESN'T LOOK LIKE QUALITY (2015-2020, revisit 2025-26)

**FactSet Data:** +0.08 bps | Peak size: 19.69% | Held 71 months | Exit: 2020-09

**Entry Thesis (Gold Standard Example):**

Multiple independent drivers: (1) depobank closing (contractual), (2) 20% capital freed (calculatable), (3) CEO buying, (4) low valuation range. One telegraphed, one calculatable, one surprise.

> "What happened: In January, BFF had 1. the depobank closing, 2. the change in risk weighting (20% capital freed up, returnable) 3. the Chief Executive buying shares, and 4. the share price was very low in the range - in the 4s and 5s. One was well telegraphed, one was calculatable in advance, and one was a pleasant surprise."

**Process Lesson Applied (June 2021):**

> "The lesson is that I know that the market sometimes overreacts, and sometimes underreacts, to events. Sometimes the market prices them in well in advance, and sometimes the market wait for the event to literally happen, even though it has been contractually agreed many months earlier. Depobank was a clear example of this."

> "The improvement is to lean in and trust my analysis in these situations; when I see a clear development/catalyst contractually happening and it's not been priced in, upsize where the risk vs. reward is very compelling."

**2022 DRAWDOWN INSIGHTS (from journal):**
BFF held through Jan-2022 drawdown as a relatively modest position. Not as severely hit as XVIVO, KAINOS, S4, MEDARTIS (the concentration traps). Demonstrates: (1) lower beta than growth names, (2) quality moat held through stress period, (3) "quality that doesn't look like quality" framework was correct during drawdown.

**2023 RECOVERY & FRAMEWORK VALIDATION:**
BFF became the exemplar of Josh Tarasoff "self-driving portfolio" thesis. In July 2023 entries, Richard identifies BFF as textbook example of:
- High-quality business misunderstood by market
- Customer-centric model (soft S.R.C.A.)
- Structural advantages (hard S.R.C.A.)
- Holistic pan-system congruence between mission and value creation

> "How to define what makes Ryman or Fortnox or BFF exceptional? Amazing customer proposition combined with super execution in a structurally advantaged business combined with economies of scale" (July 2023)

**EXIT FAILURE LESSONS (Feb 2026 — CRITICAL):**

This stock became the crown jewel of exit discipline failures. Seven specific lessons documented:

**1. ENDOWMENT BIAS & COMMITMENT BIAS**
Position held past clear invalidation signals due to emotional attachment. "I chose this carefully" mentality overrode probability shifts. Richard's self-critique: ACH threshold breached = exit; don't wait or rationalize.

**2. ACH THRESHOLD BREACHED = EXIT (Don't Walk Towards Fire)**
BFF's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses shifted from "yes/growth" to "no/false friend." Instead of exiting, Richard did more analysis in attempt to resolve the conflict. The mechanism: analytical deep dives substituted for decision-making.

**3. DON'T LET FALLEN SHARE PRICE STOP RIGHT ACTION (FOFR)**
BFF fell 10-15% on sell-side downgrade. Fear of future regret (FOFR) paralyzed exit decision even as new information arrived. Richard's insight: Fallen price is sunk cost. Future decision should be independent of past price.

**4. COCKROACHES NEVER ALONE (Weirder, Worser, Further, Longer)**
First problem at BFF (IR quality): discovered late and felt "super weird" in Jan-26 call. Second problem: bad hiring ("no respect for great people"). Third problem: poor capital allocation history. Pattern: one problem implies more. Problems compound and extend beyond initial expectations.

> "Bad hiring, no respect for great people, bad ir. Lots here."

The lesson: Early red flags often precede larger failures. Trust the pattern. Once you see one cockroach, assume there are more in the dark spaces.

**5. TRUST GUT FEELING**
IR call in Jan-26 "felt diagnostic and was super weird." Richard's intuition flagged it as off. But position was held anyway. Lesson: When gut says something is wrong, probability has shifted. Act on it immediately.

**6. BAD HIRING, NO RESPECT FOR GREAT PEOPLE, BAD IR = RED FLAGS**
These three factors together are invalidation signals. Not in isolation, but in combination they suggest deeper cultural/execution risk. Management quality red flags (hiring, respect for talent, IR clarity) are high-signal.

**7. THE COCKROACH LESSON IN PRACTICE**
Once you see one cockroach, assume there are more in the dark spaces. BFF: IR weirdness → management risk → capital allocation risk → execution risk. Don't wait for all to emerge. Exit on first clear signal.

**Psychology Notes:**
- Held too long despite mounting evidence (endowment bias)
- Failed to exit when ACH threshold breached
- Complex reasoning used to rationalize a position that should have been exited
- Gut signal (Jan-26 IR call felt "weird") correctly identified problem, but was overridden by analysis

**Process Lessons Applied:**
"Cockroaches are never alone." "Worser, weirder, further, longer." Multiple independent deteriorations → exit immediately, don't wait for full thesis breakdown. ACH threshold = automatic exit trigger.

This alone should trigger exit. Management quality is non-negotiable. If CFO is weak, the entire system becomes risky.

**4. "NOT HELL YEH" CLASSIFICATION**
Didn't pass conviction filter but held anyway. When conviction is ambiguous, the position should be exited, not researched deeper. The rule: if it's not "hell yeah," it's a no.

**5. ERRORS IN ONE ORGAN = FIRST COCKROACH**
Kidney US errors seemed isolated but signaled execution risk more broadly. Same cockroach pattern as BFF: small visible problem implies hidden problems.

**6. COMPLEX CASES WITH MULTIPLE MOVING PARTS = HIGH EXECUTION RISK**
Three+ different product lines, three+ different geographies, three+ different regulatory regimes, CEO quality unknown. Each multiplies uncertainty.

- Simplicity > complexity. Cases must be explainable in one page.

- Board member → CEO transitions are amber flags

**FactSet Data:** +0.02 bps (winner) | Peak size: 5.92% | Held 19 months | Exit: 2021-02

**Return:** +6.6% | **Opportunity Cost:** Missed +25% YTD and +15% in Nov/Dec 2020 | **Total Regret:** ~+40% opportunity cost

Richard wanted to own Greggs. Pedro (team member) was "freaked out" by pre-mortem exercise. Pedro was unwilling to take aleatory risk (scared because forecasting was hard).

**The Critical Lesson:**

> "I am the decision maker. ALWAYS form my own judgment on a stock. Force myself to get to a conclusion. Never abrogate responsibilities to the analyst for judgement. They're an advisory resource. I'm the Decision-maker. Buck Stops Here. I've paid for this lesson in hard P&L."

**What Happened:**

Richard deferred to analyst concern. Analyst risk aversion ≠ PM's problem. Portfolio managers exist to make judgments under uncertainty. If PM cedes all judgment calls to team, PM is not doing the job.

**The Margin of Safety Application:**

The reason PM can take risk that analyst is unwilling to take: PM has margin-of-safety framework. PM doesn't require narrow forecasts. PM can survive if worst-case scenario plays out.

**The P&L Reality:**

Stock up 25% YTD (Richard missed it) and 15% more in Nov/Dec 2020 (Richard missed it). Total regret: ~40%. Hard lesson in portfolio management hierarchy.

**Psychology Notes:**
- Deferred to authority (analyst) on a stock judgment
- Overweighted team nervousness vs. own conviction
- Didn't push back on analyst risk aversion
- Later said: "This is not the way to run a portfolio"

**Process Lessons Applied:**
- Form own conviction (don't abrogate to analysts)
- Analysts are resources, not judges
- Portfolio manager's risk tolerance > analyst's risk tolerance
- Trust own margin-of-safety framework

### OCADO — HUMILITY ON CAUSATION (2020-2025)

**FactSet Data:** +0.06 bps (winner) | Peak size: 14.71% | Held 9 months | Exit: 2020-10

**Return:** 100%+ peak (from entry) | **Current:** Declined from peak | **Status:** Long-term hold

**The Setup:**

Position built during COVID. Large move up occurred. Position was held into subsequent years as case evolved.

**Critical Retrospective:**

> "Maybe we were partly lucky last year and did loads of work into Ocado but ultimately it went up 100% / 70% from pre-COVID levels purely due to the COVID pull forward, and there wasn't much more science than that."

**Key Insight:**

Just because thesis worked doesn't mean analysis was correct. Humility about causation required. Exogenous moves (COVID pull-forward) can validate thesis when actually driven by external factors, not internal thesis strength.

**The Logic Error:**

1. Thesis: Ocado is strong compounder, margin expansion, recurring revenue
2. Stock moved: +100% from entry
3. Conclusion: Thesis was correct
4. **Reality: COVID pulled forward 3-year demand in 3 months. Stock would have moved regardless of our analysis.**

This is selection bias. Just because outcome was positive doesn't mean the decision-making was sound.

**Margin of Safety Framework Applied:**

Ocado case used two-tier margin-of-safety approach:
- **Tier 1 (Quantitative):** DCF with huge error bands (admits aleatory risk)
- **Tier 2 (Qualitative):** Cracking business + cracking people + staircase of positive inflection

But even good MoS can be invalidated by massive exogenous shocks (or validated by them, purely by luck).

**Psychology Notes:**
- Hindsight bias: assumed analysis was sound because outcome was positive
- Lack of counterfactual thinking: what would have happened if COVID hadn't occur?
- Confirmation bias: COVID move "proved" the thesis, when actually it was orthogonal to thesis

**Process Lessons Applied:**
- Distinguish luck from skill
- Just because thesis worked doesn't mean analysis was correct
- Exogenous moves require humility about causation
- Portfolio luck is real and indistinguishable from skill in the short term

## COACHING FRAMEWORKS DISTILLED

### The Cockroach Pattern (BFF, XVIVO, Baltic Classifieds)
Once you see one problem, assume more in dark spaces. Problems are: Weirder, Worser, Further, Longer. Exit on first signal, don't wait for full thesis breakdown.

### Execution Not Potential (XVIVO, MEDARTIS)
Judge companies on what they DO, not what they COULD do. Legacy moat ≠ reinvestment moat.

### ACH Threshold = Automatic Exit (BFF)
When Analysis of Competing Hypotheses tips from "yes/growth" to "no/false friend," exit immediately. Don't do more analysis.

### Complexity = High Execution Risk (XVIVO, MEDARTIS, KAINOS)
If you can't explain in one page + three exit triggers, it's too complex. Avoid.

### Hiring Bottleneck = Growth Governor (KAINOS, INSTALCO)
Companies that can't hire enough → can't grow. No levers to pull. Growth is capped by labour market.

### Treadmill vs Flywheel vs Fragile (2023 Framework)
- **Flywheel:** Success breeds more success (network effects, increasing returns)
- **Treadmill:** Constant effort needed; success doesn't compound (services, custom implementation)
- **Fragile:** Dependent on single person/product; vulnerable to disruption

### Quality That Doesn't Look Like Quality (BFF, 2023 Josh Tarasoff thesis)
1% of companies have both hard S.R.C.A. (structural) + soft S.R.C.A. (mission/culture).
These are mispriced because quality is hard to model in Excel.

### The 18-4+ Discipline (2023 onwards)
Ask: "How does this information change the 18-4+ case?" If it doesn't, it's 0-6 noise. Avoid firefighting.

### Resilience-Optionality Framework (2023)
12 dimensions: positive optionality, negative optionality, balancing feedback loops, resilience, grabbing behavior, leverage, gravitational pull, non-ergodicity risk, opacity, hard/soft S.R.C.A., skew, MDF thesis.
