## Core Philosophy

Professor Steve Peters is a consultant psychiatrist with 30+ years' clinical experience who developed the Chimp Paradox model — a distillation of neuroscience into an accessible, actionable framework for managing emotions and improving decision quality under pressure. His approach rejects "controlling" emotions in favour of recognition, management, and integration.

The paradox: your inner Chimp (emotional brain) is simultaneously your greatest asset — providing intuition, courage, and rapid decision-making — and your greatest liability — generating anxiety, impulsivity, and hijacking rational thought. Management, not elimination, is the goal.

Peters has applied the model with elite athletes (British Cycling, Liverpool FC, Team GB) producing Olympic gold medals, World Championships, and sustained high-performance cultures.

## Key Frameworks & Concepts

### The Three Brains Model

**The Chimp (Limbic System / Emotional Brain)**
- Basis for thinking: feelings, impressions, intuition
- Processes information 5x faster and approximately 5x more powerfully than the Human
- Operating mode: reactive, catastrophic, survival-focused
- Timescale: immediate (milliseconds to seconds)
- Decision style: fight-or-flight, tribal, hierarchical
- At its best: provides intuition, courage, quick decisions, social connection
- At its worst: anxiety, impulsivity, overreaction, self-sabotage

**The Human (Frontal Lobe / Rational Brain)**
- Basis for thinking: facts, logic, evidence, reasoning
- Slower than the Chimp; processes sequentially and deliberately
- Operating mode: analytical, perspective-taking, solution-focused
- Timescale: reflective (seconds to minutes); plans ahead
- Decision style: principled, systemic, values-aligned
- Core strength: can observe itself, recognise patterns, learn, and modify behaviour

**The Computer (Parietal Lobe / Belief System)**
- Function: automatic storage and retrieval — beliefs, habits, learned responses, memories
- Consulted by both Chimp and Human before action
- Speed: automatic, often unconscious
- Programmability: can be deliberately reprogrammed through repetition and new experience
- Contains both helpful patterns (Autopilots) and destructive patterns (Gremlins/Goblins)

### The Hijacking Mechanism

1. External signal (criticism, loss, uncertainty) enters the system
2. The Chimp (limbic system) processes it first — 5x faster than the Human
3. Chimp generates emotional appraisal: threat assessment, fight-flight-freeze activation
4. Human notices seconds later
5. If Chimp has already driven action (word spoken, trade closed), Human has only damage control

**The recognition test:** "Do I want this thought, feeling, or behaviour?" If no → your Chimp is hijacking. If yes → your Human is leading.

**Critical principle:** "If you had a dog and it bit someone, you couldn't just say, 'Sorry but it was the dog, not me.' You are responsible for your Chimp and its actions."

### Gremlins, Goblins, and Autopilots

**Autopilots (Constructive — keep and strengthen)**
- Positive, adaptive, values-aligned automatic responses
- Examples: "I handle setbacks well," "I stay calm under pressure," "I follow my process"
- Built through deliberate practice until automatic (50-100+ repetitions)

**Gremlins (Destructive — removable)**
- Unhelpful beliefs created after age 8, relatively recent and removable
- Created by Chimp over-generalisation (one bad experience → universal law)
- Examples: "The market always moves against me," "I can't hold through drawdowns"
- Removal: identify → challenge with evidence → replace with Autopilot → repeat

**Goblins (Destructive — hard-wired, require management)**
- Deeply ingrained beliefs formed before age 8, extremely difficult to remove
- 30+ years of daily activation make them foundational
- Examples: "I'm not capable," "The world is dangerous," "I always fail"
- Management: develop competing Autopilots, use Human to question the Goblin, build contradicting evidence, create new experiences

**Key distinction:** Gremlins are removable; Goblins require management.

### The Stone of Life

The bedrock of psychological architecture — three components:

**1. Truths of Life** — beliefs about how the world works
- "Life is unfair," "Hard work pays off," "People are generally trustworthy"
- Filter through which you interpret events

**2. Beliefs** — explanations of cause and effect
- "I succeed when I do thorough research," "Drawdowns mean I was wrong"
- Direct emotional response to events

**3. Values** — moral and ethical principles
- "Integrity matters above returns," "Process before outcome"
- Guide decisions and define who you want to be

**The values exercise:** "If you wrote a list of all the things you would like to be — calm, disciplined, analytical, confident, patient — then this is who you really are. Any deviation from this is a hijacking by the Chimp."

This reframing is powerful: Chimp hijackings are deviations from your true self, not expressions of it.

### The Troop

Your immediate social circle — 5-15 people. The Chimp has a fundamental need for belonging. Quality over quantity.

**Upgrade protocol:** Identify people who make you feel valued (not criticised), support your goals (not undermine them), communicate honestly, and give more than they take. Actively reduce exposure to energy-draining relationships.

**Four communication channels:**
- Chimp-to-Chimp: both emotional, reactive → lowest quality (escalating conflict)
- Human-to-Human: both rational, evidence-based → highest quality (problem-solving)
- Human-to-Chimp or Chimp-to-Human: mixed → medium quality (miscommunication)

### Managing the Chimp: Three Core Techniques

**1. Exercise (Long-term regulation)**
Physical activity processes stress hormones, shifts the Chimp to a satisfied state, builds confidence, and regulates the nervous system. Not gentle walks — challenging activity.

**2. Box (Short-to-medium term: logic and evidence)**
Use facts and truth to contradict the Chimp's catastrophic narrative: "What's the evidence for this? What's the evidence against? What would I tell a friend?"

**3. Bananas (Immediate distraction)**
Deep breathing, change of environment, cold water, music, physical movement — immediate interventions to interrupt an acute Chimp state. Bridge tactics while the Chimp calms.

### The NEAT Framework

- **Normalise:** It's normal to have Chimp outbursts
- **Expect:** Expect them to happen (you have a limbic system)
- **Accept:** Accept that this is how your brain is wired
- **Take care:** When they happen, manage the consequences, including self-forgiveness

## Application to Professional Investing

**Chimp hijacking in position management:** The most common investing Chimp hijack: a position draws down significantly, the Chimp generates catastrophic threat signals ("This is a disaster, sell everything"), and the Human's slower, evidence-based assessment ("The thesis is intact, the business hasn't changed") arrives too late. Training: recognise the hijack ("Do I want to sell, or is my Chimp panicking?"), then Box it with evidence.

**Gremlins in idea generation:** Common investing Gremlins: "I always miss the best ideas," "My sizing is never right," "The market moves against me." These are Chimp over-generalisations from specific experiences. Identify them, challenge with evidence (your track record shows otherwise), and replace with Autopilots: "My IG process surfaces strong ideas," "My sizing improves with each iteration."

**Stone of Life for investment philosophy:** Explicitly articulate the Stone of Life for your investing: Truths ("Markets are mostly efficient but periodically mispriced"), Beliefs ("Concentrated positions with genuine edge outperform over time"), Values ("Process integrity, intellectual honesty, patience"). When decisions align with the Stone, confidence and consistency follow.

**The Troop for an investor:** Who are your 5-15 people? Mentors, thought partners, trusted analysts, Steve Ward. Are there relationships or information sources that consistently trigger your Chimp (market noise, social media, anxious peers)? Upgrade the Troop.

**Autopilot installation for process discipline:** Identify the key Autopilots you want running automatically: "When I encounter a stock that meets my screening criteria, I open a case file." "When a position drops 10%, I review the thesis before taking any action." "When I feel urgency to act, I take a centering breath first." Build each through 50-100+ deliberate repetitions.

**Exercise as non-negotiable:** Peters' framework reinforces that physical exercise is not optional for cognitive performance — it's the primary Chimp management tool. For a solo investor making concentrated bets, physical conditioning directly supports decision quality.

## Coaching Prompts

1. **Hijack recognition:** "Think about your last impulsive investment decision. Was that your Human (evidence-based, values-aligned, deliberate) or your Chimp (reactive, emotional, survival-driven)? How do you know?"

2. **Gremlin hunt:** "What Gremlins are running in your Computer about your investing? 'I always...' 'I never...' 'The market always...' Write them down. Now: what's the actual evidence for and against each one?"

3. **Stone of Life for investing:** "Can you articulate your Truths (how markets work), Beliefs (what causes success), and Values (what matters beyond returns) explicitly? Are your recent decisions aligned with them?"

4. **Autopilot design:** "What is the single most important Autopilot you want installed? Describe the trigger, the automatic response, and the outcome. How many deliberate repetitions have you done this week?"

5. **Chimp management plan:** "When your highest-conviction position drops 15% in a week, what's your plan? Which technique — Exercise, Box, or Bananas — do you deploy first? What does the Boxing script look like?"
