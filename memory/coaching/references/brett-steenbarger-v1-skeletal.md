## Core Philosophy

Brett Steenbarger, PhD, is a clinical psychologist and active trader who reframes trading as a performance discipline — comparable to elite athletics, surgery, or military operations. His central thesis: "Trading is a microcosm of life — in both trading and life, we pursue values and manage the risks of those pursuits." Trading performance is inseparable from personal psychology, and the path to expertise follows the same deliberate practice principles that produce excellence in any domain.

His primary goal is not to become a trader's coach for life, but to "teach traders tools for coaching themselves for life." Self-coaching — structured self-observation, honest feedback, and iterative refinement — is the endgame.

## Key Frameworks & Concepts

### From Best Practices to Best Processes

The critical distinction from *Trading Psychology 2.0*: what makes traders good are **best practices** (sound methods). What makes traders great are **best processes** — detailed routines that turn best practices into consistent habits. A practice is a method; a process is a habit. Most traders fail at this transition.

Seven essential activities requiring best processes:
1. Research
2. Idea generation
3. Trade structuring
4. Position risk management
5. Portfolio risk management
6. Self-management
7. Performance management

### The ABCD Framework

- **Adapting** to market changes: "Rules that work in one kind of market environment fall down when trends, correlations, and volatility change. No rules work ideally in all market conditions."
- **Building** social, emotional, cognitive, and personality strengths
- **Cultivating** creativity and adaptive flexibility
- **Developing** best practices across each facet of trading

### Solution-Focused Approach

Rather than dwelling on weaknesses, the inquiry shifts: "When have I solved this problem before?" or "When have I NOT had this problem?"

- Problem focus: "Why do I take losses?" → endless rumination and self-criticism
- Solution focus: "When do I take profits quickly?" → systematic replication of winning conditions

Reverse-engineer personal success: track occasions when you trade well, catalog the conditions, thought patterns, emotional states, and decisions accompanying best trades. Build a profile of "best-self trading" and design deliberate practice around making it more frequent.

### The Learning Loop (Deliberate Practice)

**Performance → Feedback → Renewed Effort → Improved Performance**

Operating on three timescales:
1. **Immediate** (within a position): Does it behave as expected?
2. **Trade-level** (after execution): Did I follow the plan? Where did I deviate?
3. **System-level** (across periods): Which conditions favour my approach? Which expose weaknesses?

### Cognitive-Behavioural Foundations

**Emotions as data, not distractions:** Fear often precedes reversals; greed precedes euphoric tops; frustration indicates strategy-regime misalignment. The emotional experience contains actionable intelligence.

**Thought-Emotion-Action connection:**
- What we think determines what we feel
- What we feel influences how we act
- How we act generates feedback that shapes what we think

Intervention can occur at any point: restructure beliefs (cognitive), develop regulation techniques (emotional), or design practice protocols (behavioural).

### Three Performance Metrics

1. **Mechanics:** Entry/exit execution, position sizing discipline, risk management adherence
2. **Strategy:** Idea quality, thesis validation, market regime alignment, trade selection
3. **Tactics:** Timing precision, price execution, speed of adaptation

Each tracked independently to identify specific improvement domains.

### Trading as Expertise Development

Stages: **Beginner → Competent → Proficient → Expert**

Each stage requires different mental models, practice protocols, and feedback mechanisms. Expertise requires mastery of two parallel domains:
1. **Pattern recognition** — identifying risk/reward patterns in markets
2. **Execution skills** — sizing, timing, entry/exit accuracy

Most investors focus on pattern recognition alone; expertise requires both.

### Positive Psychology Application

"Each of us is already the person we wish to become. The aim of our development is to discover who we are at our best and to be that person more and more consistently." — The first trading psychology text to apply positive psychology: building on strengths rather than fixing weaknesses.

## Application to Professional Investing

**Self-coaching for a solo investor:** Steenbarger's framework is uniquely valuable for Richard's setup. Without a team, the self-coaching methodology provides the structured feedback loop that institutional PMs get from risk managers and CIOs. The learning loop — performance, feedback, renewed effort — must be deliberately designed and maintained.

**Idea generation as best process:** IG is where Steenbarger's "best practices to best processes" transition matters most. Having good screening criteria (practice) is insufficient; having a repeatable, time-blocked, documented IG routine that produces consistent output (process) is the goal.

**Regime awareness:** "Robustness lives in breadth of conditional edges, not a single swiss-army knife." For a GARP + event-driven investor, this means different opportunity sets in different market regimes. The current risk-off environment (Iran, Q1-2026) demands explicit regime assessment before stock-level analysis.

**Journaling as performance tool:** Structured journaling (plan, emotion, outcome, learning) for each major decision. Regular analysis reveals patterns invisible in real-time — holding losers too long, sizing inconsistency, regime misalignment.

**The "best trades come to you" principle:** "If I begin my market day with a definite idea and a desire to trade that idea, I'm likely to lose money. If I enter the day having done research but then adopting an open mind, I'm much more likely to place winning trades." For a concentrated investor, this means: do the research, build the watchlist, but let the market present the entry — don't force timing.

**SWOT for career management:** Strengths (deep UK/Europe knowledge, concentrated approach, event-driven instinct), Weaknesses (solo coverage limitations, potential confirmation bias), Opportunities (mid-cap inefficiency, European special situations), Threats (regime shifts, liquidity constraints in smaller names).

## Integration with Richard's System

### Mapping to IAJA

Steenbarger's learning loop (Performance → Feedback → Renewed Effort → Improved Performance) is the execution layer of IAJA:
- **Information:** Steenbarger emphasises structured research processes — not ad hoc, but repeatable and documented.
- **Analysis:** Apply three performance metrics (mechanics, strategy, tactics) to each decision. Where was the analysis strong? Where did it break?
- **Judgement:** Solution-focused: "When have I made this type of judgement well before? What conditions were present?"
- **Action (+2 downstream):** Best practices → best processes. The action must be processised, not improvised. The two downstream actions should include a self-coaching review and a regime check.

### The Solo Investor's Self-Coaching Challenge

Without a CIO, risk manager, or team to provide feedback, Richard must build Steenbarger's learning loop himself. This requires:
- A structured decision journal (plan, emotion, outcome, learning for each major decision)
- Regular self-review (weekly "done well / do differently" — Steenbarger's protocol)
- Quarterly pattern analysis: what recurring strengths and weaknesses emerge from the journal?
- Watson as feedback partner: Richard can use Watson to conduct structured reviews, challenge assumptions, and identify patterns

### Regime Adaptation for GARP + Event-Driven

Steenbarger's "metarules" concept is critical: before applying stock-level edges, assess the regime. Current risk-off (Iran, Q1-2026) changes which edges work. GARP valuation screens produce different results in risk-off vs risk-on. Event-driven catalysts have different payoff profiles under geopolitical stress. The metarule: "What regime are we in, and which of my edges are regime-appropriate?"

### Deliberate Practice Protocol for Investing

Steenbarger's 30-day development cycle, adapted:
- **Days 1-10:** Baseline. Journal every major decision with full protocol (plan, thesis, emotion, outcome). No changes to process — just observe.
- **Days 11-20:** Identify 3 recurring strengths from the journal. Design one micro-practice per day to amplify them (e.g., if you're strongest when you write the bear case first, make that a permanent process step).
- **Days 21-30:** Integrate the micro-practices into standard process. Measure: has decision quality improved? Repeat the cycle.

## Coaching Prompts

1. **Best-self audit:** "Describe your last three best investment decisions in detail. What was your emotional state? What process did you follow? What conditions were present? Now: how do you replicate those conditions more consistently?"

2. **Process vs practice gap:** "You have a good triaging checklist (practice). But is it a reliable process? Do you execute it identically every time, or does it vary with mood and market conditions?"

3. **Regime check:** "What market regime are we in right now? Which of your edges work in this regime? Which don't? Are you adapting, or applying the same playbook regardless?"

4. **Emotion as signal:** "What emotion did you feel most strongly this week in relation to your portfolio? What is that emotion telling you about your positioning, your conviction, or your process?"

5. **30-day development cycle:** "For the next 10 days, journal every major decision with plan, emotion, and outcome. Then identify three recurring strengths. Then design one micro-practice per day to amplify those strengths."
