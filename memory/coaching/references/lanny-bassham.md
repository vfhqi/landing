## Core Philosophy

Lanny Bassham is an Olympic gold medallist (1976, rifle shooting) who reverse-engineered mental performance after a crushing silver medal failure in 1972. Unable to find existing courses in mental performance under pressure, he created one from first principles — interviewing gold medallists, studying what actually works under competitive pressure, and building the Mental Management System.

His central thesis: **performance is governed by the alignment of three mental systems — Conscious Mind, Subconscious Mind, and Self-Image.** When balanced and working together, peak performance is not accidental but designed. The system is "100% based on competition, not psychology" — built from practical experience, not theory.

Mental Management is defined as "the process of improving the probability of having a consistent mental performance, under pressure, on demand."

## Key Frameworks & Concepts

### The Triad: Conscious / Subconscious / Self-Image

**1. The Conscious Mind (Thinking Brain)**
- Responsible for thought, visualisation, imagery, and analysis of sensory information
- Can only concentrate on one thing at a time — this is both its strength (deep focus) and vulnerability (crowded under pressure)
- Primary power is directional: you choose what you think about
- "When the conscious mind has a positive thought, it cannot at the same time have a negative thought"

**2. The Subconscious Mind (Source of Power)**
- Source of all mental power and automated performance
- Can do many things simultaneously (unlike the conscious mind)
- Skill = doing something consciously long enough for the subconscious to automate it
- Where preparation becomes power — the flow state emerges when the subconscious runs sophisticated programmes without conscious interference

**3. Self-Image (The Governor)**
- "What makes you act like you" — the total of your habits, attitudes, and beliefs about your capability
- **"Your performance and your Self-Image are always equal."** — Bassham's most powerful assertion
- Self-Image acts as a throttle: governs how much of your mental and physical engine you actually deploy
- You will not, on any sustained basis, perform above your self-image
- Self-Image is always changing — the question is whether you direct that change proactively or allow your environment to direct it

### The Principle of Balance

When all three systems are aligned and working together:
- Work becomes smooth, efficient, seemingly effortless
- Performance flows without conscious strain
- Confidence is authentic (grounded in self-image, not forced)
- Power is maximised because all three systems row in the same direction

When misaligned:
- Conscious-Subconscious mismatch → internal conflict, hesitation, choked execution
- Conscious-Self-Image mismatch → self-sabotage despite conscious effort
- Subconscious-Self-Image mismatch → anxiety, ability without conviction

### Self-Image Controls Performance: The Mechanism

1. Self-Image generates expectations about what you're capable of
2. Your nervous system organises behaviour to match those expectations
3. Every action reinforces the self-image (success confirms capability; failure confirms doubt)
4. Over time, performance and self-image converge

**The reversibility principle:** Because self-image controls performance, and self-image can be changed, performance can be changed. Raise your self-image and performance follows.

Self-image is built through **imprinting:** positive imprints (successes, praise, evidence of capability, association with capable people) expand it; negative imprints (failures, criticism, self-criticism, limiting associations) contract it.

### Directive Affirmations: Programming Self-Image

Not positive thinking or wishful affirmations — a structured, written, multi-sensory rehearsal of the identity and capability you want to develop.

**Six-step construction process:**

1. **Define the goal** — specific, not vague. "Execute a concentrated portfolio of 7 high-conviction European positions" not "be a better investor."
2. **Set a time limit** — 21 days active, then 9 days off for integration.
3. **List personal pay-value** — 3-5 reasons why this goal matters to you personally. Real reasons: autonomy, pride, legacy, impact.
4. **Outline the plan** — concrete actions, behaviours, habits.
5. **Write the directive affirmation** — first person, present tense, as if already true. Include references to plan and pay-value.
6. **Handwrite five copies** — place at Key Points: bathroom mirror, desk, computer monitor, notebook, kitchen.

**The ritual:** Each time you encounter a Key Point, read the affirmation, visualise it, feel the competence, sense the outcomes. 5+ times daily for 21 days overwrites the old self-image with the new one.

**Example for an investor:**
> "I am a disciplined equity investor who executes a concentrated portfolio of 7 high-conviction positions in European mid-cap companies. I hold positions through earnings surprises and volatility because I have done the fundamental research and trust my thesis. This approach allows me to compound capital and build the financial independence I want. My investment journal tracks each decision. I execute without fear or greed."

### Mental Rehearsal: "Running the Programme"

**Principle:** Your nervous system doesn't sharply distinguish between vividly imagined experience and actual experience. Mental rehearsal multiplies reps.

**Vividness requirement:** "Be vivid in your rehearsal. See it, hear it, taste it, smell it, and feel it."

For investing decisions:
- **See:** The analysis on screen, the thesis summary, the position sizing calculation
- **Hear:** Your own inner voice — calm, certain, analytical
- **Feel:** The sensation of executing the decision, the calm conviction, the absence of doubt
- **Smell/taste:** The environment — sensory details that ground it in reality

**Protocol:**
1. Set the scenario (exact context, pressure level, stakes)
2. Run through the performance start to finish
3. Experience the success (feel the emotion — pride, relief, validation)
4. Reset and repeat (5-10 complete rehearsals before high-stakes events)

**Critical warning:** "When we worry that bad things might happen to us, we are actually rehearsing them." The investor who lies awake worrying about losses is mentally rehearsing losses. Control what you picture.

### Running a Mental Programme

A pre-designed mental system executed under pressure — treating your mind like a programme that can be coded, tested, and run reliably.

Components:
1. **Pre-performance ritual** — what you do in the 30 minutes before to enter the right state
2. **Arousal management** — controlling energy level (under-aroused = sluggish; over-aroused = tight, reactive)
3. **Attention control** — process focus ("execute my thesis") not outcome focus ("don't lose money")
4. **Error recovery** — pre-designed reset routine: deep breaths, reset phrase, refocus on next decision
5. **Reinforcement loop** — did the programme work? Track and adjust

## Application to Professional Investing

**Self-image as investment performance governor:** If Richard's self-image includes "I sometimes lose conviction under drawdown pressure," his nervous system will arrange for conviction to waver. Raising the self-image to "I hold high-conviction positions through volatility because my research is thorough" changes the nervous system's expectations and therefore the behaviour.

**Directive affirmations for process goals:** Write a specific affirmation for the current quarter's key behavioural goal. If the goal is "maintain IG discipline during risk-off environments," construct a full 6-step affirmation and run the 21/9 cycle.

**Mental rehearsal before position entry:** Before committing to a new position (especially a large one), mentally rehearse the scenario: entry, the first drawdown, the thesis challenge, the hold decision, the eventual outcome. Rehearse the calm, the conviction, the process discipline. Make the subconscious familiar with the experience before it happens live.

**Attention control during earnings season:** When multiple positions report simultaneously, the conscious mind can only focus on one thing. Run a mental programme: which position to monitor first, what the decision criteria are, what "good" and "bad" look like, and what you'll do in each case. Pre-programme the decisions.

**Error recovery protocol:** When a position breaks thesis (e.g., a catalyst fails to materialise), have a pre-designed reset: deep breath, reset phrase ("that's information, not failure"), refocus on the next analytical step. Don't spiral.

## Coaching Prompts

1. **Self-image audit:** "Complete this sentence honestly: 'I am the kind of investor who...' Now: is that self-image consistent with the performance level you want? What would the sentence look like if your self-image matched your ambition?"

2. **Imprinting inventory:** "Over the past week, what has your self-image been exposed to? Positive imprints (successes, evidence of capability, association with strong thinkers) or negative imprints (criticism, self-criticism, comparison, doubt)? Are you controlling the imprinting, or is your environment controlling it?"

3. **Mental rehearsal challenge:** "Before your next major position decision, spend 60 seconds vividly rehearsing the entire process — from analysis through execution through the first drawdown through the hold decision. What did you see? What did you feel?"

4. **Directive affirmation design:** "What is your single most important behavioural goal this quarter? Let's construct a full directive affirmation using the 6-step process. What's the goal, the deadline, the pay-value, the plan?"

5. **What are you rehearsing?** "When you think about your portfolio before bed, what are you picturing? Success and conviction? Or failure and loss? Remember: you get what you rehearse."
