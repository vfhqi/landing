## The 4Cs Framework

Four interdependent pillars of high performance:

### 1. Commitment
- Commitment is different from motivation. Motivation fluctuates; commitment is a structural decision.
- "I am committed to executing my process consistently" is more durable than "I feel motivated today"
- Commitment is tested in adversity — when positions go against you, when the market is confusing, when you're tired
- Practical: Define non-negotiable daily actions. Execute them regardless of emotional state.

### 2. Concentration
- Ability to focus on the relevant information and screen out noise
- In investing: focus on the drivers that matter for 18M EPS, not the daily newsflow
- Concentration degrades under stress, fatigue, and information overload
- Practical: Limit information inputs. Track specific drivers. Use checklists to maintain focus.

### 3. Confidence
- Self-belief based on evidence, not bravado
- Built by: successful execution of process (not by P&L outcomes), preparation, reviewing past good decisions
- Undermined by: outcome-based self-assessment, comparison to others, catastrophising
- Key reframing: "I am on the path to becoming a great investor" — progressive, not binary

### 4. Control
- Managing what you can control, releasing what you can't
- Can control: preparation, process execution, reaction to events, position sizing
- Cannot control: market moves, macro events, management behaviour, timing
- The "unbung" concept: identify what's blocking execution and address it directly

## Thought Management

### The Performance Cycle
Thoughts → Feelings → Behaviour → Results → Thoughts (loop)

Intervene at the thought level. If the thought is "this position is going wrong, I'm going to lose my job," the feeling is fear, the behaviour is paralysis or panic selling, and the result reinforces the negative thought.

Replace with: "The data shows X, my process says Y, the action is Z." Evidence-based, not emotional.

### Cognitive Biases in Investing (from coaching)
- **Endowment bias:** No objectivity due to ownership and identity attachment
- **Normalcy bias:** Extrapolating current trends (works until regime change)
- **Cockroach theory:** Bad news is always first suppressed by actors in the system — including management who lie to themselves before they lie to you
- **Recency bias:** Over-weighting the most recent information
- **Sunk cost:** Holding because of past investment in time/analysis

### Practical Interventions
- Pre-commitment: Define actions in advance of triggers (if X, then I do Y)
- Process journaling: Record decisions and reasoning, not just outcomes
- Recovery protocols: Structured routine to reset after a bad day/week
- Self-image work: Build identity around process execution, not P&L

## "Never Split the Difference" — Negotiation Framework (Chris Voss)

Applies to: capital calls, manager calls, team conversations, strategic discussions, partnership negotiations.

### Core Insight
**People want to be understood and accepted.** Listening intensively is the cheapest and most powerful concession. When someone feels heard, they're more flexible, more collaborative, more generous.

### Key Techniques

#### 1. **Slow It Down**
- Avoid going fast. Speed undermines rapport and risks miscommunication.
- **Richard's CFR:** He is "HIGHLY at risk here" — tendency to talk fast, move fast, get impatient. Conscious slow-down is critical.
- Build in pauses. Let silence sit for 4+ seconds. Creates space for the other person to elaborate or reconsider.
- Slow speech signals calm, control, and thoughtfulness. Fast speech signals anxiety and pressure.

#### 2. **Voice/Tone — Disarm the Lizard Brain**
People's nervous systems respond to tone before words. Three voices, deployed strategically:

- **Positive/playful (normal, bouncy):** Default mode for rapport-building. Smile while talking (the listener hears it). Bouncy energy signals "we're in this together, this is solvable."
- **Late night DJ voice (finishes low, warm):** Most powerful for calming, connection, and leadership. Lowers your pitch slightly at the end of sentences. Signals warmth, certainty, comfort. Use when you need to de-escalate or deepen trust.
- **Directive/assertive (sharp, direct):** Use rarely. Only when clarity/boundary-setting is the goal. Overuse signals aggression and shuts dialogue.

**Implementation for Richard:** Smile before big calls. Consciously drop pitch at end of sentences. Match their energy initially, then gently lead toward calmer tone.

#### 3. **Mirroring**
- **The technique:** Say their last 3 words back to them in question tone. Forces them to elaborate and creates unconscious liking.
- **Why it works:** Similarity creates liking. Repetition creates familiarity. Questions create engagement.
- **Example:** They say "We're concerned about the capital intensity of this upgrade." You mirror: "Capital intensity of the upgrade?" They elaborate: "Right, the free cash flow impact could be material for 2-3 years." You've learned more + they feel heard.
- **Silence after mirror:** 4+ seconds. Let them fill the void. This is where the real conversation happens.

#### 4. **Labelling Feelings**
- **The principle:** Don't skirt over negativity (it festers underground). Bring it to the surface and neuter it with empathy.
- **The technique:** Name the emotion without judgment. "It seems like you don't want to sell / It sounds like you're worried about / It appears you're frustrated."
- **Softening language:** Use "perhaps," "maybe," "it seems," "sorry." Remove "I" statements (they sound accusatory). Lead with their perspective.
- **Example:** Instead of "You're being difficult," try "It seems like this structure doesn't work for your constraints. What would make it workable?"
- **Why it works:** Naming the feeling defangs it. The person feels seen, not attacked. They're now willing to problem-solve instead of defend.

### Implementation for Richard (Negotiation)
- **Pre-call:** Prepare what you want to SAY but also prepare to LISTEN. Anticipate their constraints/concerns. Bring genuine curiosity.
- **During call:** Lead with questions (at least 60% listen, 40% speak). Slow down. Use mirroring. Label feelings if resistance arises.
- **Post-call:** Recap what you heard. Next steps. Keep the door open for dialogue, not just transaction.

## Steve Peters / Chimp Paradox — Recognising Primal vs Observer Modes

The nervous system has two operating modes:

### Chimp (Primal) Mode
- Reactive, emotional, threat-focused
- Hyperactive when: stressed, fatigued, triggered by past trauma/loss
- Decisions made in Chimp mode are rarely optimal
- Signs: rapid heart rate, tunnel vision, "fight/flight/freeze" impulse, catastrophising

### Observer (Rational) Mode
- Thoughtful, process-oriented, solution-focused
- Built on evidence, not fear
- This is where good investing decisions are made
- Signs: calm breathing, big-picture thinking, curiosity about facts, acceptance of uncertainty

### The Skill: Moving from Chimp to Observer
- **Recognise the trigger.** "I'm in Chimp mode right now" (non-judgement, just observation)
- **Pause. Breathe.** Even 10 deep breaths shifts nervous system. Creates space.
- **Move to neutral, non-judging observation.** "What are the facts? What can I control? What's the process?"
- **Then act from Observer mode.** The decision quality is radically better.

### For Richard
- **Pre-trigger identification:** What situations flip you into Chimp? (Loss of control, pressure on time/results, disagreement with respected person, market dislocations)
- **Pre-commitment:** When triggered, pause before speaking. Breathe. Reorient to Observer.
- **Recovery after:** If you've been in Chimp mode for a while (stress, volatility), intentional recovery is needed. Don't go straight back to work.

## Managing Overwhelm

### Root Causes
Feeling overwhelmed is **situational-specific**, not character-flaw-specific. It's solved by addressing the underlying condition:
- **Low sleep** → Restore sleep. 8 hours non-negotiable.
- **Low energy** → Movement, nutrition, sunlight. 20-minute walk in daylight resets nervous system.
- **Unclear plan** → Write it down. Break it into steps. Clarity alone reduces overwhelm.
- **Decision fatigue** → Make decisions and move forward. "Stuck" creates overwhelm; motion dissolves it.
- **Emotional residue** → Name it, process it (with a trusted person or coach), move on.

### The Mechanism
When overwhelmed, you're in Chimp mode (scattered, threat-focused, indecisive). The antidote is **action + clarity**, not more thinking.

### Tony Robbins + Schwarzkopf Principle
"Making decisions and moving forward is more important than making the perfect decision."
- **Decide quickly on things within your control.** Once decided, execute, not ruminate.
- **Release things outside your control.** Don't waste energy worrying about macro, market timing, others' behaviour.
- **Movement is good.** Standing, walking, exercising while deciding helps — it signals the nervous system that you're safe and capable.

### For Richard (When Overwhelmed)
1. **Sleep check:** Getting 7.5-8 hours? If not, that's 80% of the problem.
2. **Energy check:** Moved today? Eaten well? Sunlight? These are foundational.
3. **Plan clarity:** Write down the 3-5 things that matter most this week. Done. Other things are secondary.
4. **Decide and move:** On every open loop, make a call. No more "let me think." Decide and act.
5. **If still stuck:** Talk to Steve, Richard's wife, trusted advisor. Don't ruminate alone.

## "Trading in the Zone" Five Fears (Nov 2023)

From Mark Douglas's work on trading psychology, adapted to investing:

**Fear 1: Fear of being wrong.** Leads to indecision, over-analysis, delayed exits. Antidote: "Strong views, weakly held." Be willing to be wrong; the framework handles it.

**Fear 2: Fear of loss.** Causes either FOFR (Fear of Future Regret) or panic selling. Antidote: disprove-or-exit framework. Exit first; re-attack on clear evidence of recovery.

**Fear 3: Fear of missing out.** Drives FOMO entries and oversized conviction. Antidote: hell-yeah-or-no standard. If not 9-10/10 convinced, pass.

**Fear 4: Fear of being inadequate.** Perfectionism, imposter syndrome, "gold medallist platform." Antidote: "I am on the path to becoming a great investor." Progressive framing removes the pressure of being there now.

**Fear 5: Fear of chaos/loss of control.** Leads to over-complication, over-monitoring, stress. Antidote: Mission Command framework. Clear intent + constraints; release the need to control execution details.

Name the fear explicitly when it arises. "I'm at fear #2 (loss) on this position. Here's the disprove-or-exit call."

## Psychological Safety: Airlines vs Hospitals Culture (2023)

Framework from safety/aviation research:

**Airline culture (high psychological safety):** Pilots, flight attendants, mechanics speak up immediately if they see a problem. The system is designed to listen. Result: aviation is one of the safest industries.

**Hospital culture (low psychological safety):** Hierarchies suppress reporting. Junior doctors don't speak up if they see a senior making a mistake. Result: preventable medical errors.

**Investing parallel:** Build a culture (with advisors, with Watson, with self) where bad news is reported immediately, not suppressed. If a position is deteriorating, say it. If an assumption is breaking, surface it. Suppressing bad news kills portfolios.

## Negotiation Lesson (16-Mar-26)

### Context
Richard prepared well for what he wanted to **say** but poorly for what he didn't want to **hear.** He went into the call intending to convince, not to listen. Result: misalignment and friction.

### The Mistake
- **Preparation imbalance:** Heavy on "here's my view" → light on "what's their world, constraints, concerns?"
- **Selling vs. listening:** Trying to persuade someone to adopt a view, instead of listening to their problems.
- **Missed the real leverage:** The frameworks and solutions already exist; the constraint is understanding THEIR specific situation.

### The Reframe
**Never sell people on a view.** Listen to their problems; frameworks already available.

- **Step 1:** Deep listen. Ask questions until you understand their constraints, risks, success metrics, politics, bandwidth.
- **Step 2:** Match framework to their constraints. "Given what you're dealing with, here's what I'm seeing..."
- **Step 3:** Co-create next steps. They're now invested, because the solution addresses *their* world, not your template.

### Implementation for Richard
- **Pre-call checklist:** What are their 3 likely concerns/constraints? What would make this work for them? What's their success metric?
- **During call:** Spend 60%+ listening. Ask clarifying questions. Mirror back what you hear.
- **Avoid:** Launching into your prepared remarks without permission. Selling before listening.
- **Better:** "Here's what I'm picking up on... is that accurate? What am I missing?"
