APM Mini-Memo. Bonesupport. The sixth of November 2026.

Bonesupport is new this week at rank thirteen on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. The one-line view. Bonesupport is a one-product Swedish medtech whose antibiotic-releasing bone graft is becoming the standard of care for bone infection in the United States. The case depends on American surgeons and payers adopting the product fast enough to keep estimates rising.

First, what the company does. Bonesupport is a Swedish orthobiologics company worth about one point three billion euros at the price of the sixth of November. Its product is an injectable bone graft substitute. The surgeon injects it into a bone void, and it releases an antibiotic as new bone replaces it. So one operation treats the infection and rebuilds the bone. My own reading is that the product is sold as CERAMENT, and that bone infection, trauma and infected joint replacements are its main uses. The United States is the growth market. American penetration is below ten per cent, and United States sales grew thirty-one per cent in the second quarter of this year.

Second, the thesis. Two events this autumn turned a product surgeons liked into one hospitals are paid to use. The SOLARIO trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine on the seventeenth of September, showed that the product cuts the course of antibiotics after bone infection surgery from weeks to days. From the first of October, Medicare moved those cases into its highest-paying groups. So an American hospital now earns more on the operation that uses the product. The route to earnings per share is short. The gross margin is about ninety-one per cent, so almost every extra dollar of American sales reaches profit. Consensus already expects earnings to compound at about forty per cent a year. Our Sector digest's rule is that revenue lags a payment decision by roughly a year. So the Medicare change should feed United States growth through 2027, and the market's story is that adoption makes the product a standard of care. There are three drivers to track. The growth rate of United States sales each quarter. The direction of consensus estimates. And whether the gross margin holds as volume rises. Our note of the twenty-eighth of October found that in this Nordic medtech cohort the shares follow the direction of estimates more than the reported quarter. Bonesupport rose twenty-four per cent on the twenty-second of October, on a beat with rising estimates.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up or Stock-driven ratings, and this read is my own. The Target Setup is Demand-driven EPSU. A clinical result and a payment change are lifting demand for an existing product. The earnings step-up comes from volume at a very high gross margin, not from a cleared worry or a corporate change. A memo would decide whether the American adoption curve is steep enough to make that fit crisp.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The case is simple but fragile. It has one product, one market that matters, and one question: how fast American surgeons and payers adopt the product. What needs to go right is that United States sales accelerate through 2027 as the Medicare change works through, that estimates keep rising, and that the gross margin holds. The main risk is the multiple. The Investment Analyst's note of the thirty-first of October found that small, one-product medtech stocks have about four times as many days with a ten per cent move as large medtech. Their multiple breaks on the first doubt about the growth path, whether that is a miss or a beat with a weaker outlook. At thirty-three and a half times earnings, one slower American quarter would cost the shares a large part of the recent rise. Relative strength over eighteen months is only thirty-seven, which shows how recently the shares were out of favour. The weekly Lists judgement also recorded that a small single-product medtech name is the shape the book already has too much of. You hold Xvivo, and our market-regime reading argues for less fragility. So position size, not the case, is the second question.

Fifth, business quality. High margin, narrow base. A gross margin of about ninety-one per cent is the mark of a consumable with clinical evidence behind it and pricing power, which our Industry digest notes is rare in regulated healthcare. The published trial and the Medicare payment groups are a lead that competitors cannot copy quickly. My own reading is that no rival antibiotic-releasing injectable graft is approved in the United States. Against that, the company depends on one product and on American payers. Operating margin, cash and revenue are not in our files.

Sixth, returns and valuation. Cheap for its growth, not cheap in its sector. At thirty-three and a half times earnings, Bonesupport sits at the nineteenth percentile of its own ten-year range, and has the cheapest valuation reading on the Bench. The medtech consumables Sector median is seventeen times, but at the second percentile of its own range. The weekly TSR Valuation rating of the sixth of November is A. The plausible return reading of the twenty-ninth of October is seventy-nine per cent a year, but that assumes the multiple doubles to sixty-seven times, and I would not lean on it. Consensus earnings growth of about forty per cent a year on an unchanged multiple would give about forty per cent a year, an A against your bar. My own arithmetic says that even a fall in the multiple to twenty-five times over three years would leave about twenty-seven per cent a year, still an A. The other weekly ratings support the chart. Technical Momentum is A on the sixth of November, with stock relative strength at ninety-eight and the price about six per cent below its fifty-two week high. Sell-side Momentum is B on the fifth of November. Thematic Fit Momentum is B on the sixth of November, from the environment only.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros. Our Sector digest's story of a small maker's consumable becoming the standard of care is rated early, and Bonesupport and Xvivo are its purest plays. Who pays decides who can raise prices, and Medicare has just decided to pay more. Leadership in the Nordic medtech cohort rotated on the twenty-second of October to the estimate risers, and Bonesupport is one of them. The cons. The company waits on American payers and hospital purchasing committees. The revenue lag of about a year means the next two quarters may show more promise than numbers. The Sector is cheap as a whole, so a buyer of medtech has many cheaper worry-clearing franchises to choose from. Small growers' multiples halved after the boom of 2020 and 2021, and some of that was normal maturing rather than the group's wave.
