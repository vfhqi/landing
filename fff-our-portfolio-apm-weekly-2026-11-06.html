The gaps

Gap 1: short of the regime's winners

The target Overweights six rows (Energy downstream at Double Overweight, Energy upstream and services, Insurance, Pharma/biotech, Consumer staples and its sectors, Utilities integrated); the book holds 11.93 points in staples and nothing in the other five, in the week the regime those rows were set for arrived in Europe's own prices. Richard's European test fired for the first time and defensives lead cyclicals on every window from one week to twelve months.

Counter-case: the market has already paid. Healthcare's relative strength went to A, Energy's slipped to B with services at D, the one oil headline says dipping on peace hopes, and the tilt scorecard shows the Overweights lagging the Underweights on every table since September; closing this gap now buys a two-month-old rotation.

Gap 2: over-exposed to small, fragile cases, and now to one country

Twelve of the sixteen holdings, 50.44 points and 77% of invested weight, sit at or below the 41st size percentile while Fragility is Double Underweight (84% last week; Avanza, at the 63rd percentile, is the whole improvement). Sweden is 30% of the book in four names, three at or below the 8th percentile. The fragility adjustment already charges for size; it does not charge for four small caps sharing one currency, one rate path and one investor base.

Counter-case: the Fragility evidence (euro high yield 90 basis points wider, the cost of debt at 1.85% of sales) is a week old and could not be re-read; breadth recovering for a second week says small and mid caps are participating; and the Swedish names include the book's best-scored industrial (Sdiptech, Fitness 73.0).

Gap 3: over-exposed to leveraged, long-duration and capex-cycle cases the regime is selling

22.01 points, a third of the book, sit in the pattern the Environment note names twice this week: multiples compressed while estimates hold, leverage and duration sold, now reaching telecoms after defence. Helios Towers (7.21%, the largest position, from 5.17%), Theon (3.46%), D'Ieteren (2.59%) and Bally's Intralot (1.87%) sit in rows the target holds at Underweight or Double Underweight: 15.13 points, 23% of the book, from 13.72, the Helios Towers addition being the whole widening. Alfen (3.18%) and Arteche (3.70%) sit in the grid-equipment row whose relative strength fell to F and whose tripwire is half fired.

Counter-case: Helios Towers' row shares the telecoms rows' trigger, the United States ten-year under 4.5%, and these rows rally hardest if a dated source confirms the "Fed and Bank of England cutting" headlines; Helios Towers' own case is rated B by the Investment Analyst; and the grid-equipment demand read of 28 October is intact.

Proposed amendments to portfolio-level risk management

Twelve amendments are carried from last week with a status each, and one is new; the five that changed come first, the rest in a table.

3. Duration and leverage limits. Status: strengthened on the cap, unchanged on the floor

A cap on exposure to the Underweight and Double Underweight rows valued off a yield or financed at the margin, and a floor on the yield-beneficiary rows. The capped set is unchanged (Real assets, Towers, Renewable generators, Homebuilders, Building technology, Alternative finance, Automotive retail), and the book's exposure to it rose to 11.67 points from 9.67 (Helios Towers 7.21, D'Ieteren 2.59, Bally's Intralot 1.87) without any letter changing: the addition went to the row the target most wants less of, and nothing in the process asked the question. The cap would set a ceiling on these rows together, at a level Richard sets, and make any addition above it an explicit decision that the case outruns its row. The floor rows (Energy, Insurance, Staples, Pharma/biotech, Tobacco, Utilities integrated) are untouched at 11.93 points, all staples: Gap 1.

Counter-case: the yield headlines point the other way from the rates read, undated and unreasoned; if they are confirmed, this is the limit a relief rally punishes most, and the floor buys the crowded trade.

10. A country concentration cap at the book level, Sweden first. Status: strengthened

Sweden is 30% of invested weight from 28%, and the week's largest addition (Avanza, to 4.16% from 0.50%) was Swedish, so the concentration grew by choice, not drift. The cap would apply to any one country's share of invested weight, at a level Richard sets, by listing and, where a memo gives it, by revenue.

Counter-case: no Swedish or Nordic source was read this week, so there is no new Swedish reason either way; Avanza is the one Swedish holding above the size floor and reads 74.7 on Fitness; and a cap by listing penalises exporters whose revenue is global.

12. A cap on the book's total weight in rows the target holds at Underweight or Double Underweight. Status: strengthened

That weight is 15.13 points from 13.72, widened by an addition to the book (Helios Towers) rather than by a tilt change: the limit would have fired on a trade, not on a judgement, the reverse of the case it was written for last week. Set above today's level so it binds on additions.

Counter-case, unchanged: it double-counts amendment 3 on three of the four names, and a holding whose own case is rated B (Helios Towers) is exactly the exception a cap should allow with a written reason.

6. A cap on any one sector's share of the Ideas Bench and Shortlist. Status: weakened

Eight of the top 40 on this week's starting score are energy names, from 15, as Neste and Galp left the MI Pool and the services row's relative strength fell to D; healthcare now leads the top 40 with eleven. The limit is kept because the Energy rows are still Overweight and Double Overweight and will crowd the top again when the tape turns back, but it does not bind this week.

13. New: a cap on the share of invested weight in holdings graded Technical Momentum D or F

35.29 points, 54% of the book (28.65 at D, 6.64 at F), sit in weak charts, on a measure the holdings file has carried since last week (amendment 11, delivered). The Sell Criteria act holding by holding and nothing acts on the aggregate, so a book can be more than half weak charts without any rule firing; in a regime where strength is punished but weak charts do not thereby become strong, that shape underperforms a rally and a correction alike. The cap would bind on additions to D and F charts above it and queue the F charts (Alfen, Theon) for the review below.

Counter-case: the Momentum row is Underweight, so the target itself says not to chase strength, and a cap that forces sales of weak charts in a mean-reverting tape sells bottoms; it should bind entries and additions, never force exits, and Richard's Ruling 8 already makes a weak Stage reading a ranking input rather than a gate.

Endnotes: how this note is made

  • Where the rating comes from. The measured reading is the holdings file's position-weighted mean Fitness across 16 of 16 holdings, computed on the 30-Oct-26 target table carried forward at a tilt of 0.625; the scorer re-runs on this week's table after the judgement, and this week's two telecoms changes touch no holding, so the re-run should move the book only through the universe curve. Size percentile and fragility are populated for all 16 holdings and the fragility adjustment is inside the measured reading, so size is not deducted again; the two-point deduction is for country and position concentration only.
  • How the move was decomposed, and its limits. The book effect re-weights this week's Fitness readings at last week's sizes. Last week's sizes for CVS Group and IMCD are not in this week's files (their combined weight was 4.50 points), so they are split in today's proportion, and the decomposition is approximate to about half a point. The ledger does not distinguish trades from price moves, and it does not say what the remaining weight beyond the 65.60 invested points is.
  • How the gaps are counted. Gap sizes are points of invested weight from the holdings file. The count of rows the target holds at Underweight or Double Underweight uses the holding's sector tilt, or its industry tilt where the sector is Neutral (Theon: Defence Underweight at the industry, Non-primes Neutral).
  • What could not be read this week, and where it bears. No inflation, rates, yields, credit or consumer source; no Swedish, French or Nordic source, so the country-level counter-cases rest on last week's reads; no memo congruence line, and the memo verdicts file was empty again; Rheinmetall's 5-Nov-26 print. Small-cap growth against large-cap yield in Europe, Richard's own risk proxy, is still unmeasured (amendment 7, retired as a build). The evidence in full is in "FfF - Environment Inputs - Researcher Weekly AL Info Note of Input Information Sources on Macro, Themes, Industries, Sectors and Geographies".
  • How the tilt scorecard is used. It is equal-weighted on today's universe (survivors) and at most eight weeks old; it is cited as a warning only, and nothing in this note moves on it.
  • Lists of names that carry no judgement. The MI Pool names under each gap are candidates for the Lists note to rank, not recommendations; the holdings table sets review questions, not conclusions.
  • Who decides. This note proposes amendments, reviews and a tilt; Richard decides capital.
