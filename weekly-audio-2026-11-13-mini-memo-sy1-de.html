APM Mini-Memo. Symrise. The thirteenth of November 2026.

Symrise is rank nine on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The one-line view. Symrise is a steady flavour and fragrance house priced near the bottom of its own ten-year valuation range. The story depends on returning volume reaching profit before dearer raw materials absorb it.

First, what the company does.

Symrise is one of the large flavour and fragrance houses. The company is worth about twelve billion euros, at a share price of ninety-seven euros. Symrise designs the tastes and scents that go into food, drink, household products and personal care products. Symrise also sells the ingredients that make pet food appetising. That description is my own reading, because our files hold no research on the business.

Customers' volumes set most of Symrise's growth. Our sector work found that these houses pass on only about half of a rise in raw material costs. So their gains come from volume, not from price.

Second, the thesis.

Real demand is returning, and Symrise is the purest way to own that return. Our sector note of late October judged that food and drink volumes grew this year on real demand, not only on customers restocking. That note named Symrise first among the beneficiaries. Jefferies adds a second source of volume, which is pet food ingredients. Nestlé's pet care business grew about four per cent, and Jefferies reads that growth as confirming the turn at Symrise.

Volume reaches earnings per share with leverage. Jefferies says each extra unit of volume earns about twice a company's average margin, because the cost base is largely fixed. Consensus expects Symrise's earnings to grow about eight per cent a year. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating is C, so brokers' forecasts are not yet moving up with the story. Sustained volume growth would therefore arrive as upgrades.

Three drivers carry the thesis. The first is organic volume growth in each division. The second is the gap between raw material costs and contract prices. The third is pet food ingredient volumes. Symrise reported third-quarter sales on the twenty-eighth of October, and that result is not in our files. The full-year results in early 2027 are the next test.

Third, the Target Setup.

The Target Setup is CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal. That read is my own, because no Investment Analyst memo exists for Symrise. The cause for concern was weak volume that restocking flattered. That concern is clearing as real demand returns. Symrise's multiple sits in the lowest quarter of its own history. So a return to normal growth could bring a return to a normal multiple. The high-quality label is my assumption until a memo tests it.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

The case is simple to state, and nobody has rated it yet. With no Investment Analyst memo, there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings. One thing needs to go right. Volume growth must turn into profit growth through 2027.

The main risk is that costs and a fragrance hangover absorb the volume gain. Oil rose above a hundred and six dollars a barrel in October. Contract prices catch up with raw material costs only after six to twelve months. In 2022, the same lag cut Givaudan's gross margin by almost four percentage points, and Givaudan's share price fell thirty-nine per cent. There is a second pressure. Household and personal care customers bought fragrance early, and that early buying unwinds in the first half of 2027. The third pressure is China. Chinese producers are cutting the prices of commodity aroma molecules. Prices in Symrise's scent and care division were three per cent lower than a year earlier in the second quarter. Fragrance ingredients are about a quarter of that division's sales, against less than a tenth at Givaudan.

So track three things. Track Symrise's gross margin. Track pricing in the scent and care division. And track fragrance volumes in the first half of 2027.

Fifth, business quality.

Business quality is probably good, and unproven in our files. Testing a new recipe takes twelve to eighteen months, so the supplier that wins a recipe tends to keep it. The market values these houses as steady growers. My own reading is that a flavour or scent is a small part of a customer's cost and defines the product. That makes customers slow to switch. Commodity aroma molecules are the weaker part of the franchise. Symrise's margins, returns on capital and balance sheet are not in our files, and a memo should establish them.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

The valuation is the strongest part of the case. Symrise trades on nineteen times earnings. That multiple is at the fourth percentile of Symrise's own ten-year range. Givaudan, by contrast, stood on twenty-nine times earnings in late October, well up its own range. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating is A, and that rating reads valuation only.

The plausible return clears your bar only if the multiple recovers. The weekly routine puts the plausible return at about forty-five per cent a year over eighteen months. Over three years the figure is about twenty-six per cent a year. Both figures are above the twenty-five per cent that marks A. Both assume earnings growth of about eight per cent a year, plus dividends, and a multiple moving from nineteen to twenty-nine times. Earnings growth of eight per cent is below the twelve per cent that marks D. So the rerating carries the return.

The share price supports the timing. The weekly Technical Momentum rating is A. The share is about five per cent below its high for the year, after a pullback inside an uptrend. The flavour and fragrance houses rose thirty-seven per cent in the six months to late October. Their multiples were still near the bottom of their own ranges. Our sector judgement is that a story pays most where the multiple has not yet moved.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

Symrise sits behind the strongest theme in its sector. Our themes summary of the first of November rated the sector's net force B, with real demand returning as the strongest theme. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating is B, and that rating reflects the sector's environment only. Fragrances and ingredients rose about twenty-seven per cent over twelve months. That makes the sector one of the few rising groups in Materials.

The cons sit on both sides of the chain. Oil-linked raw materials press from behind. Food companies press their suppliers on price from in front. American recipe changes favour suppliers that sell a whole package of ingredients, and Kerry is the purest play there. Symrise's position in that recipe work is not in our files. A cartel investigation into the fragrance industry is also open.

The one-line view. Symrise is a steady flavour and fragrance house priced near the bottom of its own ten-year valuation range. The story depends on returning volume reaching profit before dearer raw materials absorb it.
