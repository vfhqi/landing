APM Mini-Memo. SCHOTT Pharma. The sixth of November 2026.

SCHOTT Pharma is rank nine on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. The one-line view. A locked-in maker of drug vials, syringes and cartridges whose revenue has turned up again, at under eighteen times earnings. The case depends on obesity-drug cartridges filling new capacity faster than idle polymer syringe lines dilute the margin.

First, what the company does. SCHOTT Pharma makes the containers that injectable drugs are sold in. Vials and ampoules are about three quarters of revenue. Prefillable syringes and cartridges are the other quarter. In the year to September 2025 the company earned two hundred and eighty million euros of operating profit before depreciation, a margin of twenty-eight per cent, on revenue of just under a billion euros. The market values the company at three point four billion euros. The business is entrenched. Each container is registered in the customer's drug approval, so switching supplier takes six to nine months. The container costs under one per cent of the drug. So SCHOTT Pharma charges ten to thirty per cent more than its peers, and ninety-seven per cent of its revenue repeats. The company leads polymer syringes with seventy per cent of the market. In glass syringes it is third, behind Becton Dickinson and Stevanato. The Carl Zeiss foundation owns seventy-seven per cent through the parent SCHOTT AG, which also supplies the glass tubing, so the shares are thinly traded.

Second, the thesis. The story is a two-year air pocket passing in a business that is otherwise an annuity. Vaccine work fell from ten per cent of sales to about five, and left the polymer syringe lines idle. One large glass syringe customer cut its orders. In December 2025 management cut its medium-term targets, to revenue growth of six to eight per cent and a margin approaching thirty per cent by the 2029 financial year. Since then the picture has turned. The customer dispute has been settled. In July, guidance for 2026 was raised to five to six per cent growth with a margin of twenty-seven to twenty-eight per cent. The third quarter grew eight per cent at constant currency, against expectations of about three and a half, led by eleven per cent growth in vials. Beneath the recovery runs the shift from bulk glass to sterile ready-to-fill formats. These sell for five to ten times the price of bulk, and they are already fifty-seven per cent of revenue, against a target above sixty. The largest volume driver is cartridges for obesity and diabetes injections. Over one billion euros is contracted through 2030 with the two leading developers. That adds up to five points a year to growth, at an estimated peak margin of thirty-five per cent. This reaches earnings per share through both lines. Consensus has earnings growing about twelve and a half per cent a year, as revenue grows six to eight per cent and the margin climbs back towards thirty. Cash is already better. Nine-month free cash flow rose nearly fifty per cent, to fifty-nine million euros, and net debt is only about a hundred million euros, so the balance sheet does not constrain the capacity programme. The drivers to track are the syringe division's margin, the high-value share of revenue, and how fast the new cartridge capacity fills.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists for SCHOTT Pharma. So there are no Bottom-up, Stock-driven ratings, and the Target Setup read is my own. The Target Setup is CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1 or Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal. The cause for concern was idle polymer lines, a customer dispute and repeated guidance cuts. The business is high quality and barely cyclical. And the multiple sits at the fortieth percentile of its own range. A Demand-driven EPSU combines with it if the cartridge contracts fill the new lines. The case does not yet match that setup crisply, because revenue has turned and the margin has not. The stock was promoted from the Longlist this week on the broadest improvement in the Pool. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of the sixth of November is A, on a base from which the longer uptrend has now proven itself. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of the fifth of November is A. The valuation reading is B. The stock sits ninth, and not higher, because the Pool's weekly screen marks it as leaving, in the first of two weeks before it drops out. The judgement recorded that a stock being ejected is not where new capital should start.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The case is of medium simplicity. Two things must go right. Obesity-drug cartridge volumes must arrive on contract and fill the new lines. And the polymer syringe lines must find new work, or at least stop shrinking. The main risk is this. The contracts protect against a customer switching supplier. They do not protect against a customer ordering less, which is what happened in the first half of 2026. Only about thirty-five million euros of prepayments stand behind the billion euros contracted, and take-or-pay terms have already been renegotiated. Obesity pills threaten the injection volumes beneath the cartridges. Bernstein expects pills to take half the market by 2028, although injections were still ninety per cent of American prescriptions in September. And Stevanato wins the largest new syringe contracts. What to watch. The syringe division's margin fell to twenty-nine per cent in the third quarter, from over thirty-six a year earlier, and the group margin fell to twenty-seven per cent from thirty-two. The case turns when that division's margin stops falling. The tests are the full-year results, the guidance for 2027 and the polymer share of sales. Management credibility is the swing. The chief executive, the finance director and the syringe division head all changed within about a year, and guidance has been cut several times since the listing. Against that, the 2025 margin beat the guide.

Fifth, business quality. The quality is high and not disputed in our files. Regulatory lock-in. An oligopoly in which the top three hold over sixty per cent of core containment. Customers who prefer zero defects to savings. And a twenty-eight per cent margin against Stevanato's twenty-five and Gerresheimer's seventeen. Demand grows with biologic drugs, which are nearly four fifths of injectable approvals, in a market expanding nine per cent a year. The weaknesses are capital intensity, with over eight hundred million euros invested from 2020 to 2025, and a parent that is both the key supplier and the controlling owner.

Sixth, returns and valuation. At twenty-one euros seventy-five on the sixth of November, the shares trade at seventeen point seven times earnings for the next two years. That is the fortieth percentile of their own range. The shares are seventeen per cent below their fifty-two week high, after rising seventy-two per cent in the six months to October. The weekly TSR Valuation rating of the sixth of November is B, a valuation reading only. The weekly routine's plausible return of the twenty-ninth of October is thirty per cent a year on average, thirty-six per cent a year over eighteen months and twenty-four per cent over three years. That comes from consensus earnings growth of twelve and a half per cent and the multiple moving from under eighteen times to over twenty-three. My own read is more cautious. Earnings growth and the small dividend alone give about thirteen per cent a year, a C against your bar. The rest needs the multiple to re-rate as the margin turns, which would lift the return to B or A. The shares stand at a thirty per cent discount to Stevanato, and the Sector Digest judged that the format shift could let SCHOTT Pharma be valued nearer a medical device maker. Broker targets run from seventeen to twenty-three euros.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. For the case. Approved capacity is the scarce asset in this Sector, and SCHOTT Pharma owns it in the format that is gaining. Ready-to-use cartridges are under five per cent penetrated. European sterile manufacturing rules push sterilisation onto the packaging supplier. The Sector Digest judged the format shift the Sector's biggest story for SCHOTT Pharma. The Pennsylvania expansion tripled high-value vial capacity with thirty-eight million dollars of federal funding, and hedges American tariffs on medical glass. The weekly Thematic Fit and Momentum rating of the sixth of November is C, on the environment only. Against the case. When a medicine reaches more patients, suppliers gain volume rather than price. The Sector Digest expects the container shortage premium to end around the close of 2027. A Researcher's report of the twenty-fifth of October warns that expansion by SCHOTT Pharma, Stevanato and Gerresheimer at the same time could leave the industry up to twenty per cent overcapacity by 2030. Bulk glass makers have already conceded price cuts of five to ten per cent.

The one-line view. A locked-in maker of drug vials, syringes and cartridges whose revenue has turned up again, at under eighteen times earnings. The case depends on obesity-drug cartridges filling new capacity faster than idle polymer syringe lines dilute the margin.
