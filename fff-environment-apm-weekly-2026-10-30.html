The week in brief

The regime: risk-off beneath a steady index, led now by credit and the cost of debt rather than by the artificial intelligence (AI) wash-out alone.

  • Credit has replaced AI financing as the main drag on risk appetite, so the Fragility Double Underweight rests on European evidence for the first time. Euro investment-grade and high-yield yields are up 30 and 90 basis points since September and the cost of debt is a measured margin drag; the case against small, complex and geared stocks is no longer a United States (US) story read across to Europe.
  • Last week's bond hedge reversed in one week. The US ten-year yield is back above 5% after a hawkish Federal Reserve hold, so the rate-sensitive rows stay at Double Underweight and duration is not yet a hedge.
  • Risk appetite fell further while the index held, and European breadth recovered sharply in the same week. Goldman Sachs' Risk Appetite Indicator is at its lowest since April 2025 while the S&P 500 sits within 1% of its high; in Europe, stocks above the 200-day average rose from 54% to 58%. One week of relief inside a risk-off is noted, not acted on, because Richard's European test still reads risk-off at four weeks and three months.
  • Four of the 122 rows change letter and 118 carry. Defence and Defence Primes fall to Underweight (the de-rating is about the multiple, not demand); Software B2B Enterprise rises to Neutral (the broadening trade has reached software); Utilities integrated rises to Overweight (the first European power-price reading in a month). The Lists are untouched directly, because no Defence name is in the Pool; Theon's Thematic Fit/Momentum falls next week.
  • Momentum is held at Underweight against half of its own written condition, which is the call Richard should challenge. 200-day breadth passed the 55% the row named for a return to Neutral, but the three-month defensives line strengthened to +3.0 points instead of falling back.
  • Both special topics point the same way. The United Kingdom (UK) Budget moves no tilt and the United Kingdom row holds Neutral; the Defence de-rating is the end of the order boom being priced before the order books show it. The shared lesson: hold row letters on the group evidence and judge each holding (Bally's Intralot in Gambling, Theon in Defence) on its own case, because in this regime relief rallies do not hold.
  • What to watch: the Bank of England on 5-Nov-26 (United Kingdom, Neutral against Underweight); Rheinmetall's print on 5-Nov-26 and the Bundestag budget vote on 27-Nov-26 (Defence); a second week of 200-day breadth above 55% with the three-month defensives line under +2 points (Momentum); and the first twelve-week tilt scorecard reading in December, since seven weeks of spreads read mostly negative and the tilts have not yet demonstrated value.

The environment

This week's changes sit in the prices rather than the causes: credit widened into the main drag on risk appetite, the bond rally of 16 October reversed, risk appetite fell while European breadth recovered, and the first European power-price reading in a month arrived. Among the causes, European macro was read for the first time in three weeks and the French premium set a record. The direction of the regime did not change.

The macro causes

Inflation is a supply-shock overshoot that central banks lean against slowly; it keeps the discount rate high for the twelve months, with near-term risk to the upside. Unchanged. US core personal consumption expenditures inflation is 3.12% against a trimmed mean of 2.27%, a gap Goldman Sachs Economics (29 October) treats as one-off (tariffs, energy, AI memory prices) and expects the Fed to wait out. Against waiting: UK consumer prices at 3.5%, an eleven-month high, Canadian inflation expectations at 4.0% and US manufacturing output prices at a post-pandemic high (Pacific, 26 October; Goldman Sachs, 23 October). I weight the upside skew for the next two quarters because oil is the channel every house names and Brent is near $115. Euro area inflation data were not read.

Economic and consumer health: the US is strong and decelerating, the euro area softening, the UK firmer than expected; no recession in the data, downside into 2027 as fiscal support fades on both sides of the Atlantic. Changed: Europe was read, and reads softer. US growth is 3.4% annualised, with Goldman Sachs (29 October) expecting 1.8% in the fourth quarter; euro area flash purchasing managers' indices missed while US ones held (Goldman Sachs' GOAL report, 26 October); the UK grew 0.7% over three months and added 102,000 jobs against 5,000 expected (Pacific, 26 October), but its Budget cut 2027 growth to 1.0% from 1.6%. US consumer sentiment is split, the Conference Board at 91.7 from 81.9 (Goldman Sachs, 27 October) against the University of Michigan at 48.0, the second lowest on record, both with inflation expectations near 4.7%. No European consumer survey was read for a fourth week, so the consumer rows carry US readings only.

Trust in fiscal and monetary leadership: France is the weak point and weakening, the Fed is credible but divided, the UK removed a fiscal tail risk. Changed: France and the UK. The French ten-year premium reached a record 150 basis points, "a lot for a core European country" (Pacific, 26 October); Goldman Sachs (25 October) puts the 2027 deficit at 5.4% of output and sees the euro area's fiscal tailwind fading to neutral in 2027; DNB Carnegie (26 October) counters that Moody's affirmation removed a sovereign tail risk. Chair Warsh was "balanced", with financial conditions tighter "but not tight" (Goldman Sachs, 28 October). The UK Budget met both fiscal rules and gilts outperformed Bunds and Treasuries on the day (special topic below). Italy was not re-read, so last week's spillover stands untested.

Geopolitics reached the pack through oil alone, and oil eased late in the week. Unchanged in kind. Goldman Sachs (25 October) names further conflict with Iran the biggest inflation risk; Brent moved towards its spring highs on re-escalation, then eased below $115 on US-Iran back-channel talks (Outset Global, 29 October; DNB Carnegie, 26 October). Lula's re-election tightened Brazilian financial conditions sharply (Goldman Sachs, 27 October). Ukraine reached the pack as web titles only, plus £10bn of UK support in the Budget. China was read at the level (growth 4.5%, retail sales up 1.1%) and through weak company reads: Kone guided its China new-equipment market down more than 10%, and AB InBev's Chinese beer volumes fell 11%.

The macro prices

Rates: three G10 central banks are hiking and the pace is slowing; the direction has not turned. Unchanged. The Fed held at 3.75 to 4.00% with two dissents for a hike, which Goldman Sachs Economics (28 October) reads as a hike every other meeting with the last in December, calling the market's four priced hikes too many. The UK and Canada are priced above 90% for November hikes (Pacific, 26 October). The European Central Bank and Bank of Japan decisions were due this week and no outcome was reported, so the euro area rate path is unread.

Bond yields: the US ten-year is back above 5%, last week's rally is undone and duration is not yet a hedge. Changed: the hedge reversed. Chair Warsh gave no forward guidance and the ten-year held above 5% "even as equity volatility capitulated" (Outset Global, 29 October); the "exhaustion" read of 16 October lasted one week, which is why the rate-sensitive rows stay where they are. The five-year fell 15 basis points in a day during the AI sell-off, which Goldman Sachs' desk reads as slower AI capital spending rather than relief. Bund and other euro area yields were not read in their own right.

Credit: widening, and now the main drag on risk appetite. Changed, and the week's most important change for the Fragility axis. Goldman Sachs' Europe Kickstart (23 October) puts corporate interest expense at 1.85% of sales from 1.25% in 2022, a 60 basis point margin drag worth more than 5% of earnings, with weak balance sheets exposed, Quality the beneficiary and no systemic credit event expected. Goldman Sachs' GOAL report (26 October) records a second "multi-standard-deviation" sell-off in credit excess returns; Jefferies (28 October) says debt markets have "almost entirely shut off" for AI data-centre borrowers without independent revenue. The widening is still high-yield-led and no default wave was read, so the Banks and Insurance tripwires have not fired; their European leg is measured for the first time, and both rows say so.

Commodities: oil near $115 and choppy is the swing variable for inflation and the Energy rows, with upside skew by Goldman Sachs' own framing; European power is firmer. Changed: power. Brent rose through the month and eased late on de-escalation reports. Nordic power forwards have risen and Fortum signed a 22-year nuclear power purchase agreement with Google, Europe's first lifetime-extension nuclear deal (Danske Bank, 28 October): the first European power-price reading in a month, and the reading that moves the Utilities integrated row. Copper, industrial metals and gold were not read beyond one-day moves.

Thematics

The market has stopped paying for AI capital spending without the demand fading, and the money is moving to quality, defensives and growth that does not depend on AI: the right side of the trade for a European book, the wrong side for our semiconductor and electrification exposure. Changed: the theme lost its pricing power.

  • Demand is intact: SK Hynix memory is sold out through 2029 and cloud capital spending is modelled at $1.43 trillion in 2027 (Jefferies, 25 and 28 October)
  • Pricing power is not: Microsoft's capital spending came in $11 billion below consensus and Alphabet did not raise its guide, so the hyperscaler prints "add no upside" (Jefferies, 29 October); corporates treat token spend as a cost (Goldman Sachs' desk, 26 October); the market sold ABB 6% on 40% order growth because the strength was data centres (Danske Bank, 25 October)
  • Inside the theme the sub-sector news was positive (ASM International's 2027 guide 5% above consensus, Aixtron's power electronics orders turning, STMicroelectronics' optical business): a stock-by-stock matter for the per-stock review, not a row reason
  • The broadening trade strengthened: Nordic software is up 9% in a month as AI-disruption fears faded while hardware gave back gains (Danske Bank's technology, media and telecoms team, 27 October); the software-against-semiconductors factor pair is reversing (Goldman Sachs' desk); Hexagon was rewarded for 13% organic growth "with nothing to do with" data centres; Quality is favoured as debt costs bite
  • Defence as an equity theme, re-shoring and European consumer demand were not spoken to by any strategist; defence is covered by its special topic

Risk appetite

Risk-off beneath a steady index, and Richard's European test still reads risk-off at the windows that matter. Changed: appetite fell further and breadth recovered, in the same week. Goldman Sachs' Risk Appetite Indicator fell to minus 0.5, its lowest since April 2025; the Nasdaq is down more than 6% in two weeks with momentum down about 15% and rotation into defensives and low leverage (Goldman Sachs' desk, 26 October), while the S&P 500 sits within 1% of its high (Outset Global, 29 October). Goldman Sachs' GOAL report stays modestly pro-risk on earnings and lighter positioning. Risk-on-ness is off: de-grossing, rotation to defensives, and one late-week volatility crush on Oracle's cash-flow guidance that the desk reads as positioning, not a bottom.

Richard's European test, on the universe's own prices to 29 October, reads a risk-off regime with a one-week relief rally inside it, not a turn, because the four-week and three-month defensives lines strengthened while the week went the other way. The breadth charts and their data are on the page below this note.

  • The STOXX Europe 600 is 1.7% below its 52-week high, so the index-near-its-high-while-breadth-is-weak combination is not present on the test's own rule (within 1%), though it is close: had the index been within 1%, today's 52% above the 100-day average would have been matched on only 4 of 1,262 such days since 2006
  • Breadth recovered in one week to 54%, 52%, 55% and 58% above the 50, 100, 150 and 200-day averages (from 44% and 54% on the 50 and 200-day), still only the 40th, 23rd, 23rd and 25th percentiles of three years
  • Defensives against cyclicals read minus 1.0 point at one week, +4.3 at four weeks, +3.0 at three months (from +2.0), +0.9 at six months and minus 1.1 at twelve, with the ratio above its 50 and 200-day averages
  • Small-cap growth against large-cap yield was again not read in Europe; the only large-against-small read (DNB Carnegie, Nordics) found no divergence

What would change the view

I am bearish on the regime with high conviction on direction; what is most likely wrong, as last week, is how long the squeeze lasts.

  • Yields: if the ten-year falls under 4.5% with the December hike priced out, the Real assets, Towers, Homebuilders, Building technology and Renewable generators rows cost most, and I would move each one notch before the data confirmed it. This week argued the other way
  • Credit: if European credit widening becomes a default cycle, Banks go to Underweight and Insurance to Neutral. The European leg is now measured, but no default wave was read and Goldman Sachs expects no systemic event: the tripwire is half-armed, not fired
  • Momentum: if 200-day breadth holds above 55% for a second week with the three-month defensives line back under +2 points, the Momentum row returns to Neutral. This week met the breadth leg (58%) and not the defensives leg (+3.0, stronger)
  • Consumer: a European consumer reading that is not falling would lift the consumer rows held at Underweight on US surveys. None arrived for a fourth week, and the UK Budget's own forecast cut 2027 growth to 1.0%
  • Semiconductors: if the AI credit channel stabilises and a second house joins Jefferies' European semiconductor call, Semiconductors go to Neutral. One condition is met (Jefferies' European team expects the sector to "trade up nicely"); the other is not (data-centre debt markets "almost entirely shut off")
  • Defence: if German approvals convert (Rheinmetall 5-Nov-26, Bundestag budget vote 27-Nov-26) and Defence relative strength leaves F, the Defence rows return to Neutral
  • Oil: if oil falls under $85, the Energy rows and the Norway row lose their reason; Brent near $115 into the December Fed meeting hardens the inflation read instead
  • The bottom-up test: no memo carried a congruence line this week, so the test of the regime against the memos is still last week's 30 verdicts (8 Fights it, 20 Mixed, almost all on the Fragility axis)
  • The tilt scorecard (prices to 29 October, on the page below): the seven target tables to date read minus 4.47, minus 0.17, minus 0.61, minus 0.30, +0.19, +0.31 and minus 1.15 points (Overweights against Underweights); the 23-Oct-26 table's first week is negative because cyclicals won the week. Seven weeks is noise and no tilt moves on it, but the tilts have not yet demonstrated value, and the first twelve-week reading arrives in December

Special topics commissioned by Richard

The UK Budget of 28-Oct-26: investment implications at a high level and by industry and sector

Judgement: the Budget moves no tilt, and the United Kingdom row holds Neutral with moderate conviction. It was written for the bond market and paid for by a known list of repeat payers (banks, gambling, landlords and savers, online marketplaces) on behalf of households and the high street. It removed a UK-specific risk to the discount rate without lowering the rate, because gilt yields are set by the Iran-war inflation shock, the Fed and the Bank of England. The decisions it postponed (defence at 3% of output, departmental spending) are bigger than the ones it took and wait for the 2027 Spending Review. For a European long-only book the UK is a place to hold at market weight and pick stocks in, not a country to tilt.

Why, ranked by evidence

  • A Budget for the bond market (best supported). Headroom £15bn in 2029-30 (£23.6bn in March), borrowing 4.2% of output this year, both fiscal rules met; the ten-year gilt closed about 5.32%, 3 basis points lower and its lowest in over a month, outperforming Bunds and Treasuries by about 5 basis points. Against: Deutsche Bank sees a £25bn to £40bn hole "to fill in the new year" and the Office for Budget Responsibility gives a 56% chance of meeting the rules, so the tail risk is reduced, not removed
  • Consolidation through repeat payers (well supported). Bank surcharge 3% to 6% from January 2027 for 15 months (£2.6bn), Machine Games Duty 20% to 25% from April 2027, income tax on property and savings income up a further 2 points, marketplaces liable for sellers' value added tax. The market priced each as less than feared: UK domestic banks closed up 1.6% to 2.0% (about 4.2% of post-tax profit a year for a wholly UK bank, against the 6.9% feared), though RBC doubts the surcharge will be temporary
  • The commission's inflation hypothesis fails the test and is withdrawn. The energy-bill measures do not take tightening out of the Bank of England's path: the Office for Budget Responsibility's path, as reported, still peaks near 3.75% in early 2027 with Bank Rate about 4.25% by mid-2027, and the zero value added tax on electricity and the fuel duty cut run only to July 2027, moving a step rather than removing it
  • Growth was moved, not raised (least important for the twelve months). 2027 cut to 1.0% from 1.6%, 2029 and 2030 raised; about 7.5% cumulative growth from 2026 to 2030 either way

The counter-case: the United Kingdom row should be Underweight. 2027 growth of 1.0%, unemployment 5.1% in 2027, the energy price cap up about 16% in January, a Bank of England rise to 4.00% on 5-Nov-26 almost fully priced, and banks now first payer in the next shock. What would refute Neutral: hawkish guidance on 5-Nov-26, a January price cap above the 16% forecast, or an announced spring fiscal event with broad taxes. For the counter to win the consumer leg has to break rather than slow, and this week's UK data (growth, employment and purchasing managers' indices all beating) say the opposite.

What it means for how we invest: six rows confirmed, none moved, the Lists unmoved, and no conflict with the regime. 22 of the 144 Pool stocks are UK-listed and a Neutral country carries no weight in Thematic Fit/Momentum; Greggs sits at the Bench cut (rank 21) and the small-business rates relief does not reach a company of its size. A Budget for the bond market inside a credit-led risk-off is congruent with it.

Why the Defence industry de-rated from July to October 2026 while Theon's forecasts rose

Judgement: the de-rating is the end of the order boom being priced before the order books show it, so Defence and Defence Primes move to Underweight and the Non-primes hold Neutral, with moderate conviction. Multiples were cut while estimates held, the industry's premium over the STOXX 600 fell from 60% to 12% (Bernstein, 9-Oct-26), and short-cycle land and ammunition were hit hardest while electronics and optronics held up. Richard's hunch is right in direction: ING's cut is not only following the price, it reflects a market concern, and the concern is the exit multiple and the slow conversion of budgets into contracts, not demand. Conviction is moderate because estimates have not fallen and the catalysts that could reverse the de-rating are dated and near.

Why, ranked by evidence

  • A phase change from order boom to execution (best supported). Exit multiples are moving towards 13 times operating profit; Jefferies (28-Oct-26) calls the industry "Defense Likely at Peak Fundamentals vs. Aero"; Bernstein (29-Sep-26) makes the case on the short-cycle names. For: the industry and both sectors read F on relative strength for a third reading (30 October against 28 August: industry F from D, Primes F from C, Non-primes F from D). Against: Theon's own forecasts rose through the period
  • Slow conversion of budgets into contracts (well supported). The German spending run-rate and Boxer approvals lag the headlines; the UK Budget funded the existing Defence Investment Plan (closing a gap of nearly £5bn) and added £10bn of Ukraine support, but postponed the 3%-of-output decision to the 2027 Spending Review
  • Ceasefire headlines (least supported as a cause). They move shares for days, not the trend
  • Richard's hypothesis, tested: supported. Price targets cut industry-wide while estimates hold is the market re-pricing the multiple, which is what the two explanations above describe

The counter-case: demand is structural, estimates have not fallen, and the premium compression to 12% may already be the exit multiple. German approvals could catch up (Rheinmetall 5-Nov-26, Bundestag budget vote 27-Nov-26, Boxer options early December 2026). What would refute the view: relative strength leaving F as approvals convert, or a signed Ukraine settlement followed by no defence fund outflows. For the counter to win, the fourth-quarter approvals must turn into contracts and cash while the multiple stops falling; until then, a government-contracted revenue line with a falling multiple is a Fragility case in a risk-off market, not a defensive one.

What it means for how we invest: the letters move at industry and Primes level, the Non-primes hold, the Lists are untouched, and there is no conflict with the regime. The Non-primes carry no second penalty because the industry letter now carries the group's Underweight and the Fragility axis already scores the small names' size and case risk.
