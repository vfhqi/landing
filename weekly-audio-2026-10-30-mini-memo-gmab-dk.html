APM Mini-Memo. Genmab. The thirtieth of October 2026.

Genmab sits at rank seven on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, promoted this week from the Shortlist. The one-line view. Genmab is a royalty-rich antibody company spending its franchise to buy its own medicines before the Darzalex royalties expire from twenty twenty-nine, and the case turns on three trial results due before the end of this year.

First, what the company does. Genmab is a Danish antibody company. Its proprietary platforms power eight approved medicines, a footprint far larger than its mid-sized biotech peers. Sales grew from about eight hundred million dollars in twenty nineteen to three point seven billion in twenty twenty-five. But nearly eighty percent of that revenue is royalties on partners' sales, chiefly Johnson and Johnson's myeloma medicine Darzalex, worth about two point eight billion dollars of this year's revenue. The United States patents on Darzalex expire in twenty twenty-nine, and the royalties are modelled to stop altogether from twenty thirty-two. Genmab's answer is to become a commercial drug company in its own right. In December twenty twenty-five Genmab bought Merus for eight billion dollars, funded with five and a half billion of new debt. The purchase brought a wholly owned head and neck cancer antibody called petosemtamab. Beside that sit the wholly owned ovarian cancer medicine Rina-S and the lymphoma medicine Epkinly, sold with AbbVie.

Second, the thesis. The story is a dated race, and the market has priced the loser. Bulls and bears agree that the share price values the legacy royalties and the cash, and gives the late-stage pipeline almost nothing. Three trial results due in the fourth quarter of this year could change that. Epkinly in front-line large B-cell lymphoma. Petosemtamab with Keytruda in first-line head and neck cancer. And Rina-S in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer. Management has raised its peak sales ambition for the three franchises to over eight and a half billion dollars combined, about three times today's Darzalex royalty. The route to earnings per share runs through revenue, not margin. Consensus already has earnings growing about fifty-eight percent a year, while operating margins are projected to slip from thirty-one percent to twenty-seven percent by twenty twenty-eight, as wholly owned launches replace near-costless royalties. Positive readouts turn that consensus into firm numbers, and give the pipeline a value the market now denies. The drivers to track are few. The three readouts. The petosemtamab monotherapy trial due in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven. And operating cash flow, which fell to fifty-four million dollars in the first half of this year from three hundred and forty-nine million a year earlier, because spending has run ahead of proof. The business itself is going well. Second-quarter revenue rose twenty-five percent to about one point one six billion dollars, beat consensus by five percent, and full-year guidance was raised.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up or Stock-driven ratings, and this read is my own. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. The Merus acquisition and the shift from royalty collector to integrated cancer company are the change, and the step-up in earnings comes as the wholly owned medicines are approved and launched from twenty twenty-seven. The match is imperfect, because the step-up depends on trial outcomes rather than on actions already taken. That is why the stock sits seventh rather than higher.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The case is not simple. It depends on three independent binary events in one quarter. The working assumptions are seventy percent for Epkinly, fifty percent for petosemtamab and seventy-five percent for Rina-S, which gives a joint clean-sweep probability of about twenty-six percent. One miss would undo the chart. Jefferies puts a petosemtamab failure at about seven hundred kroner a share, and a front-line Epkinly failure at about two hundred and sixty. Petosemtamab matters most. That medicine carries the largest positive weight at the lowest probability, in a crowded field where rivals from Bicara and Johnson and Johnson have posted competitive response rates. What needs to go right is at least two of the three, with petosemtamab among them, while the balance sheet holds. Operating costs of about two point nine billion dollars, more than three thousand staff and five and a half billion of debt were all committed before the trials resolved. The sell-side's ninety-three percent Buy ratings sit beside a scenario ladder running from fifteen to fifty-one dollars. That tells you the distribution is wide and lumpy, not a smooth expectation. In plain words, Genmab is an event-driven case, not a low-risk one.

Fifth, business quality. The platform is excellent and the economics show it. Gross margins run near ninety-four percent. Capital spending was one percent of sales last year, and free cash flow reached about one point two six billion dollars. The antibody platforms are embedded in partners' regulatory filings, so switching away would take years of revalidation. Novartis's Kesimpta and Johnson and Johnson's Darzalex are the proof. But the moat and the cliff are the same asset. The switching costs protect each molecule, not the company, and they expire with the molecules' patents. The adjusted margin of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation has already fallen from fifty-two percent in twenty nineteen to thirty-one percent last year, and return on invested capital dropped from forty percent to twelve percent after the Merus deal. Management is a conservative tracker on financial guidance, with three years of over-delivery, so the guidance can be trusted. The clinical record is mixed, so the trial odds cannot be.

Sixth, returns and valuation. Genmab trades at about twenty-five times earnings two years out, the forty-third percentile of its ten-year range. The price of three thousand nine hundred and twelve kroner on the thirtieth of October sits within one percent of the fifty-two week high, after a thirty-nine percent rise over three months. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of the thirtieth of October is C on the valuation reading alone. The routine's plausible return of over eighty percent a year rests on the multiple doubling to fifty-one times, which I would not lean on. Holding today's multiple on consensus earnings growth of fifty-eight percent a year would clear your twenty-five percent bar comfortably, an A, if the readouts hold consensus. A miss removes that at a stroke. The Sell-side Momentum rating of the twenty-eighth of October is A, and the weekly Technical Momentum rating of the thirtieth is B, up from D a week earlier.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros first. Epkinly demand accelerated in United States community practices after the twenty-four hour hospital monitoring requirement was removed from its label. Rival bispecifics and cell therapies have hit regulatory setbacks. The pipeline is insulated from United States drug price negotiation through its biologic status. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of the thirtieth of October is B. My Sector Digest of the twenty-second of October named Genmab's three trials as a single-company story to look at, in a Sector whose median stock trades at the twenty-second percentile of its ten-year range. Now the cons. Head and neck cancer is crowded. Roche's Polivy already holds thirty-nine percent of United States front-line lymphoma, and Roche's Lunsumio competes abroad. European reimbursement is slow. Supply relies on single-site contract manufacturing. And the Sector's net thematic force on the twenty-third of October was C, weak, with failed trials the main listed threat.

The one-line view. Genmab is a royalty-rich antibody company spending its franchise to buy its own medicines before the Darzalex royalties expire from twenty twenty-nine, and the case turns on three trial results due before the end of this year.
