APM Mini-Memo. Attendo. The thirteenth of November 2026.

Attendo is rank seventeen on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The one-line view. Attendo is lifting earnings per share from six Swedish kronor towards nine by 2028, through fuller care homes and buybacks. The story depends on staffing those homes at wages that municipal prices repay.

First, what the company does.

Attendo is the largest private care provider in the Nordic region. Attendo runs about seven hundred and seventy local units for about twenty-eight thousand customers. Nearly three quarters of sales is care for older people, and most of the rest is disability care. Municipalities and Finnish welfare regions pay for the care. Attendo leases almost all of its buildings.

Think of Attendo as two businesses. Finland is nearly six tenths of sales, and earned an operating margin of nine per cent after rent costs in 2025. Scandinavia, which is almost all Sweden, earned four and a half per cent. Group sales were flat in 2025, at about nineteen billion kronor. Sales were flat because Attendo has been handing back low-margin outsourcing contracts. Homes that Attendo operates itself are now over nine tenths of revenue.

Second, the thesis.

The Attendo story is arithmetic rather than sales growth. Earnings are occupancy, multiplied by the share of own-operated homes, multiplied by price indexation. Those earnings sit on leased buildings and a falling share count. In a care home, rent and staff are largely fixed. So most of the revenue from each extra resident becomes profit. Our research puts that gearing at about seven per cent of earnings per share for each half point of margin.

Scandinavia is what is improving now. The Scandinavian margin rose to five point four per cent in the second quarter of 2026, from three point three a year before. The historical Scandinavian margin was seven to eight per cent. DNB Carnegie models only five and a half per cent for 2027. Group occupancy is eighty-eight per cent, and the target is at least ninety-two. So the recovery has room to run over the next six to twelve months and beyond.

The record makes the target believable. Adjusted earnings per share doubled from three kronor in 2023 to six in 2025. The 2026 target was reached a year early. The company now targets at least nine kronor by 2028. DNB Carnegie forecast just under nine for 2027 and nearly ten for 2028, in early October. Projected buybacks of one point six billion kronor through 2028 add to the step-up. Attendo can afford those buybacks, because debt is about one times profit after rent costs, below the company's own target range.

Three drivers carry the case. Group occupancy each quarter, the Scandinavian margin, and the pace of buybacks.

Third, the Target Setup.

No Investment Analyst memo exists for Attendo. So there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings, and the Target Setup read is my own. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. The step-up in earnings per share comes from the company's own actions. Those actions are the exit from outsourcing, the repair of the Scandinavian margin, and the buybacks.

Demand does not yet drive the numbers. Organic growth was only about one per cent in the second quarter of 2026. Demand-driven EPSU would join only if the new beds fill quickly. Attendo has twelve hundred and fifty beds under construction, adding two to three per cent to capacity each year through 2027. None of the cause-for-concern setups applies, because the shares stand about one per cent below their fifty-two week high.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

The Attendo case is simple. The case is one mechanism with a handful of things to observe. No Case Riskiness rating exists, because there is no memo. What needs to go right is plain. Attendo must hire enough permanent care staff to fill its beds. And municipal prices must catch up with wages.

The main risk is that a staff shortage and slow indexation arrive together. Vacancies force Attendo onto expensive agency staff. Meanwhile, municipal contracts reprice about twelve months after costs rise. The Finnish wage agreement adds three and a half per cent from September 2026, and nearly three per cent more from September 2027. Our research has a case with no margin improvement at all. That case gives earnings per share of about six kronor, and a value of seventy kronor a share. The share price today is about one hundred and thirty-three kronor.

Four things show early whether the staffing risk is biting. Occupancy moving towards ninety-two per cent. The share of agency staff. Finnish prices against the wage rise. And the new chief executive. Virpi Holmqvist was appointed from the fourteenth of September, and has committed to the strategy. Martin Tivéus, who led the Finnish turnaround, leaves by the turn of the year. Her first results are the test of continuity.

Fifth, business quality.

Business quality is good, for a business paid by the state. Residents rent their rooms directly from Attendo, so a municipality cannot easily cancel a home. Private operators run care about ten per cent cheaper than public provision in Sweden, and close to twenty per cent cheaper in Finland. Capital spending is about one per cent of sales. Free cash flow in 2025 was just over one billion kronor, and paid for the buybacks and the dividend.

The limits on quality are a thin margin and large leases. Attendo's group margin after rent costs was six point seven per cent in 2025, against eight per cent at Ambea, the closest peer. Counting the leases as debt, borrowing is nearly four times reported profit. Finland shows the resilience. The Finnish margin rose by two points in the first half of 2026, even after regions cut prices.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

Attendo is fairly valued, not cheap. The shares trade on about fourteen times earnings two years forward. That multiple is a little above the middle of Attendo's own history. The market value is about one point six billion euros. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of the thirteenth of November is C, and that rating reads valuation alone.

The plausible return clears your bar only with a higher multiple. The weekly routine's figure is about twenty-seven per cent a year, which is an A against your anchors. That figure assumes consensus earnings growth of about twelve per cent a year. It also assumes the multiple rises from about fourteen times to about eighteen. Earnings growth and the dividend alone would sit nearer your C anchor, in my reading.

The market has reasons to pay more. Our Sector note judges that investors are starting to see these companies as owners of scarce care beds, rather than low-margin contractors. A Scandinavian margin above forecast would also lift estimates. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of the twelfth of November is A. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of the thirteenth of November is B. The chart's uptrend reading has switched on and off twice in recent months, so the chart adds little yet.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

The pros are assured demand and scarce supply. Scandinavia's population over eighty grows by about half between 2020 and 2030. Sweden needs over four hundred new nursing homes by 2032, and municipalities cannot build fast enough. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of the thirteenth of November is A, read from the Sector's environment only. Finland's reform of January 2026 lets regions buy care privately. That Finnish reform is an option for 2027 and later.

The cons are that the theme is largely known, and the customer is short of money. The Nordic care operators rose about sixty-five per cent over the twelve months to late October. Our Sector note judges that only two things are not yet recognised. Those two things are Attendo's Scandinavian margin and the Finnish reform. Finnish regions may take care back in house, and Sweden debates limits on welfare profits. The outcome of September's Swedish election is not in our files.

The one-line view. Attendo is lifting earnings per share from six Swedish kronor towards nine by 2028, through fuller care homes and buybacks. The story depends on staffing those homes at wages that municipal prices repay.
