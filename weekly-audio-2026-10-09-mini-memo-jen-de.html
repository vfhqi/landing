APM Mini-Memo. Jenoptik. The ninth of October 2026.

Jenoptik comes to you at rank sixteen on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The view is bullish, held as a working hypothesis. A single-source optics supplier removed its capacity constraint just as the lithography cycle turned, two quarters have evidenced it, and at thirty-nine euros forty-two the plausible return reading of twenty-three point six per cent a year sits near your bar. Conviction stops there because the earnings engine and the failure mode are the same object, ASML's capital cycle, and our own files disagree about what you would be paying.

First, what the company does. Jenoptik is a German photonics group, reorganised in January twenty twenty-five into four Strategic Business Units. It is a tier-two supplier of micro-optical subsystems designed into other people's machines: semiconductor lithography and inspection tools, medical and defence optics, metrology and traffic enforcement. Sales in twenty twenty-five fell six point three per cent to just over one billion euros, at an eighteen point four per cent margin on earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Semiconductor and Advanced Manufacturing is the engine at a twenty-five point seven per cent margin; Metrology and Production Solutions is the drag at seven point eight.

Second, the thesis. The mechanism to earnings per share is fixed-cost leverage on a capacity step that is already paid for. The one hundred million euro Dresden cleanroom opened in May twenty twenty-five, doubled micro-optics capacity and was funded out of operating cash flow. The second quarter of twenty twenty-six is the proof: revenue up three per cent, group earnings up twenty-eight per cent, the group margin up four points to twenty point eight per cent and the semiconductor divisional margin at thirty-three point four. The demand side is dated rather than asserted. ASML booked thirteen point two billion euros of orders in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-five, global semiconductor sales grew one hundred and thirty-five per cent in June, and consensus earnings growth is eighteen point three per cent a year as at the eighth of October. The drivers to track mostly land on the November third-quarter print: group order intake above three hundred million euros, second-half margin above nineteen point five per cent, working capital back below thirty per cent of revenue, and a decision on Prodomax at the year-end strategy update.

Third, the Target Setup. The Target Setup is Demand-driven EPSU, combined with Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. That read is mine, mapped from the memo's own setup work: its primary fit is the big demand change-driven earnings-per-share upgrade at B, with strong corporate change second, also at B. What matters more is that the clearing setups are closed. The huge clearing event in solid quality grades F and the trough-on-trough turn grades D, the memo's own view being that the clearing event happened in the first half of twenty twenty-five and has been harvested. Setups' Fit and Crispness in the store is C, set by the Investment Analyst as at the seventeenth of August, mapped from the memo and flagged as not re-judged.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. This is the weakest part of the case. Case Riskiness is D, owner the Investment Analyst, as at the seventeenth of August twenty twenty-six, mapped from the memo; on that scale A is simple and low-risk. The zero headwinds test fails at F across at least seven vectors, and change-from-current density grades D with five live items against a limit of two. The Risks report models a thirty-five per cent probability of failure over twelve to twenty-four months. Two offsets are real: a narrow five-unit perimeter, and conservative guidance, management itself flagging that the strong first-half order intake would normalise.

Fifth, business quality. Good franchise, but not compounder returns. Foundations' Robustness is C and Thesis's Change Forces' Strength is B, both set by the Investment Analyst as at the seventeenth of August on that same mapped basis. The moat is qualification lock-in: parts designed in for ASML or KLA cannot be swapped without years of re-qualification, and sole-source arrangements earn margins above thirty per cent. Against that, return on capital employed fell to eight point four per cent in twenty twenty-five, against ABB at twenty-seven point two. Leverage of one point four times buys time.

Sixth, returns and valuation. TSR and Valuation in the store is C, owner the script, provenance the dashboard, as at the ninth of October, graded on the valuation pin and flagged a valuation reading only. The separate plausible return work of the eighth of October reads twenty-three point six per cent a year on average, dividends included, and carries a B, which on your anchors is about twenty per cent a year. The problem is this. FactSet on the ninth shows fifteen point one times forward earnings at the forty-second percentile, while the memo argued its case from twenty point nine times at the ninety-second percentile and flagged its own valuation file as stale. Treat the cheap reading as unconfirmed.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. Thematic Fit and Momentum is B, owner the script, as at the ninth of October, but flagged environment only, so it is an environment score and not a judgement on this company. The memo had it at C, strong on artificial-intelligence disruption because Jenoptik sits inside the lithography tool. Sell-side Momentum is B and Technical Momentum is A, both owner the script, as at the ninth of October. The pros are demand and disruption: extreme-ultraviolet layer counts in memory should double over three years. The cons sit at both ends of the chain. The supplier side grades D, with no upstream position in substrates, while downstream ASML enforces dual sourcing with Carl Zeiss, so dominant share is not available at any level of demand.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. At its strongest, this is a tier-two supplier whose earnings are a derivative of one customer's capital cycle, resting on a memo two months old that adopted rather than exceeded its Risks report. ASML is roughly twenty per cent of sales and up to forty per cent of group earnings. The winning failure narrative is the false friend: a surging backlog masking margin erosion as input and labour inflation outpaces contract pricing. Two quarters of conversion falsified half of that, but working capital at thirty-two point three per cent of revenue, against a peer median near twenty-five, means the cash absorption leg is still building. Jefferies models a twenty-five euro downside against its own forty-five euro base case.

The one-line view. Bullish, working hypothesis: the capacity step is paid for, two quarters have evidenced the operating leverage, and the plausible return reading of twenty-three point six per cent a year sits near your bar, with the November print and a defensible answer on the multiple the two things that would move it.
