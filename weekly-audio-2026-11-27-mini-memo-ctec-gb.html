APM Mini-Memo. Convatec. The twenty-seventh of November 2026.

Convatec is new this week at rank four on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The one-line view. Convatec is a reliable chronic-care consumables compounder on a bottom-fifth multiple. Its return depends on the market coming to trust a thirteen percent earnings path as the payer worries clear on a dated calendar.

First, what the company does.

Convatec sells recurring consumables for chronic conditions. Wound dressings, ostomy bags, catheters and infusion sets. The Investment Analyst's memo of the eighteenth of November splits revenue four ways. Advanced Wound Care is thirty-one percent, Ostomy twenty-eight, Continence twenty-two, including the American home-delivery distributor 180 Medical, and Infusion nineteen. Over ninety percent of revenue recurs, because patients reorder for years and nurses rarely switch a settled patient. Revenue was about two point four billion dollars in 2025. The company reports in dollars and the shares trade in pence in London. Convatec leads only in infusion sets, where it makes about two-thirds of the world's supply, and in American catheter home delivery. In ostomy it is number three behind Coloplast and Hollister. In wound care it is number four.

Second, the thesis.

Delivery first, then re-rating. The memo's case is a good non-cyclical business doing what it said it would, at a multiple in the bottom quarter of its ten-year range. Earnings per share should compound at thirteen to fourteen percent a year to 2030. That comes from revenue growth of about six percent, an operating margin rising from twenty-two to about twenty-five percent, and a share count falling almost four percent a year through buybacks. Consensus already sits on that path. So the extra return has to come from the multiple recovering as a stack of worries clears one by one.

The worries are clearing on a calendar. The ten-month update of the eighteenth of November raised organic growth guidance and showed growth of about nine percent in July to October. It also held the margin floor of at least twenty-three percent while absorbing more currency headwind. The memo reads that as a hidden raise. Underlying margin expansion of about two and a quarter points, against seventy basis points as reported. The first half of 2027 laps a depressed first half of 2026, so earnings per share should rise twenty to twenty-five percent in that half. Reported earnings roughly double as the spin-out amortisation ends. And a bipartisan bill of the ninth of October would keep ostomy and continence out of Medicare competitive bidding for five years.

Four drivers to track. The full-year guidance on the twenty-third of February 2027, which should say six to eight percent growth and a margin of at least twenty-four percent. The segment table on the same day, which shows whether Ostomy accelerated alongside Infusion. The American launch of Natura Body in mid-2027. And Medicare's payment amounts in late summer 2027.

Third, the Target Setup.

The Target Setup is CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal. In plain words, a cause for concern clearing at a high-quality, non-cyclical business on a bottom-half multiple. The Rater, our scoring routine, rated the memo's Setups' Fit A on the eighteenth of November, sixth of eighty-three rated stocks. The memo text on the card does not name the setup, so the match is my own read. That fit is clean. Demand is non-cyclical. The shares sit at the eighteenth percentile of their own price-to-earnings range. And the causes for concern, Medicare bidding, the InnovaMatrix price cut and an open FDA warning letter, are dated and clearing rather than structural. If you judge the franchise medium quality rather than high, the match becomes Major CfC Clearing in MQ non-cyclical or better business on Q1 multiple, and the case reads the same.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

Simple. The Rater rated Case Riskiness A on the eighteenth of November, meaning simple and low-risk, sixth of eighty-three. What needs to go right is modest. The margin floors are met, twenty-three percent this year and twenty-four next. Organic growth reaches about six and a half percent, led by Infusion. And surplus cash goes to buybacks. Each sensitivity is small. A half-point margin miss costs about two and a half percent of earnings. One point less growth costs two to three percent. No buyback in 2027 costs about one and a half percent. Even at a trough multiple with a miss, the memo puts the three-year return at about nil rather than a loss.

The main risk is the payer. About nine percent of group revenue goes through Medicare. Competitive bidding from the first of January 2028 is expected to cut retail prices twenty to thirty percent. The forecasts already assume bidding proceeds at the low end of management's range. So a harsh outcome costs about five and a half percent of 2028 earnings, and a deferral adds about three and a half. The payer has form. One Medicare rule cut InnovaMatrix's price by eighty-five percent and removed sixty-four million dollars of revenue in a year. Germany and France are both tightening reimbursement in 2027. Track three things. Medicare's payment amounts in late summer 2027. Committee action on the bill. And adjusted gross margin, where price concessions, polymer inflation and automation slippage would all show first. Two lesser risks. Sterling at one thirty-nine would cut pence earnings about five percent. And growth now leans on Infusion, where new capacity is being added at a plant under an FDA warning letter, while a new chief financial officer starts on the first of March 2027.

Fifth, business quality.

Above average, not top quality. The Rater rated Foundations' Robustness B on the eighteenth of November. About seventy percent of revenue sits in concentrated oligopolies, where rivals compete on evidence and service rather than list price. Churn at 180 Medical is under one percent. Return on invested capital has risen from seven percent in 2019 to between thirteen and sixteen percent. The operating margin has risen from under eighteen percent in 2021 to over twenty-two percent in 2025. What holds the grade down is the absence of pricing power, a challenger position in ostomy and wound care, a gross margin seven points below Coloplast's, and a hundred and forty-one million of about five hundred million dollars of acquisitions written off. Cash lags earnings this year. Conversion was cut to about ninety percent as capital spending peaks near nine percent of sales, so reported cash flatters the picture for now.

They sit within one percent of the fifty-two week high, up about twenty-three percent since the September low on about one percent of consensus upgrades. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of the twenty-seventh of November is A. The plausible return averages twenty-seven percent a year. Thirty-one and a half percent a year over eighteen months, and twenty-two percent over three years. That comes from consensus earnings growth of twelve percent and the multiple moving from fourteen to seventeen times, the sixtieth percentile of the ten-year history, dividends included. The memo's own arithmetic at two hundred and sixty-eight pence says that to the end of 2027 about half the return is re-rating. To mid-2029 most of it is earnings. At an unchanged multiple the return is still about sixteen percent a year, a C against your bar. The sell-side applies fourteen and a half to eighteen times for targets of two hundred and eighty to three hundred and fifteen pence. So brokers see little left at today's price. The case for more rests on the multiple reaching seventeen times, not on upgrades, which the memo expects at only one to two percent a year.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

The pros. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of the twenty-seventh of November is A, on the environment alone. Healthcare and the United Kingdom both moved to Overweight this week. The Industry Digest ranks worries clearing at Coloplast, Convatec, Smith and Nephew and Straumann second of twenty-seven healthcare stories for share-price effect within two years. The Sector Digest's rule is that at the bottom of its range a franchise's multiple can rise without an earnings upgrade as each worry clears. Convatec showed it, rising nine percent on the eighteenth of November on a raise that did not lift dollar earnings. And Coloplast has reset to five to seven percent growth with a flat margin, a rival lowering its own bar.

The cons. Where a public payer sets the price, the maker waits on the payer. Convatec bills more of its revenue directly to Medicare than Coloplast does. The chart is the other con. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of the twenty-seventh of November is C. The shares are read as still basing rather than in an established long-term uptrend. That is the shape the Pool's own rules distrust, and the reason Convatec sits fourth rather than second. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of the twenty-fifth of November is C. And the Thesis's Change Forces grade is C from the Rater, down from B last week, because the forces sustain growth that consensus already holds rather than step above it.

The one-line view. Convatec is a reliable chronic-care consumables compounder on a bottom-fifth multiple. Its return depends on the market coming to trust a thirteen percent earnings path as the payer worries clear on a dated calendar.
