APM Mini-Memo. AIB Group. The second of October 2026.

AIB Group is new this week at rank five on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. My view is neutral, held as a working hypothesis. This is a bank the market is already paying for. The script's three top-down readings are all A, but the script's plausible return of the first of October is about twelve percent a year, half of what you built the book to find, and there is no bottom-up work of ours behind it.

First, what the company does. Our files classify AIB Group as Financials, Banks, and the list-maker calls it a yield-beneficiary bank. Beyond that the business is not described in our files. It is one of Ireland's two large domestic banks, taking deposits and lending to households and businesses, so the earnings engine is net interest income, the gap between what it earns on loans and what it pays on deposits. Market value is just under twenty three billion euros at a price of eleven euros and five cents on the second of October, which puts the shares about seven percent below their fifty two week high. Your recorded interest level is none.

Second, the thesis. The thesis the list-maker implies is a yield story: a bank whose net interest income benefits from rates staying higher for longer, with a market that keeps paying for it while the trend holds. The mechanism to earnings per share is direct. Rates stay above where deposits reprice, the margin holds, and loan growth adds a little. Consensus, in the script's reading of the first of October, has earnings growing nine point eight percent a year, with earnings per share twenty four months forward at one euro and seven cents. The drivers to track: European policy rates and the shape of the curve, deposit pricing and the migration of deposits from current accounts to term, loan growth, and the cost of credit. The list-maker flagged that credit spreads widened outside this year's ranges for the first time this week. That is the first driver to watch.

Third, the Target Setup. There is no Investment Analyst memo, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings, and this read is mine. On my read the Target Setup is closest to Demand-driven EPSU, and it is not crisp. A step-up in earnings per share needs a lever, and consensus at nine point eight percent a year, with the multiple moving from ten point three times towards nine point seven times, describes steady growth the market is already discounting, not a step-up. None of the three cause-for-concern clearing setups applies. The shares sit about seven percent below their high in a proven Stage 2, so there is nothing to clear and no trough. Nothing in our files supports the serial acquirer setup. A crisp setup would need bottom-up work beyond consensus, and there is none.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. No Case Riskiness rating exists, because no memo exists. On what the card shows, the case is simple to describe and hard to make predictable. Simple, because one line, net interest income, drives it. Hard, because that line is set by the central bank and the deposit market, neither of which the company controls, and because bank earnings carry a credit cycle the card is already signalling. The Banks tripwire was touched this week, not tripped. A bank's earnings are predictable over eighteen months to three years only while the rate and credit backdrop stays still.

Fifth, business quality. Nothing bottom-up is in our files: no return on tangible equity, no cost-to-income ratio. What the card offers is market evidence. Stock Relative Strength Composite is eighty four against a sector reading of eighty five, the eighteen-month Relative Strength is just under ninety one, and the list-maker calls it a proven Stage 2 bank. That is a quality of the share, not yet of the business.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of the second of October is D, on the valuation reading only, with no Modal return. The script's plausible return of the first of October averages eleven point nine percent a year: eleven point two percent a year over eighteen months and twelve point six percent over three years, built from consensus earnings growth of nine point eight percent a year, a multiple of ten point three times moving towards nine point seven times, and dividends included. On your anchors, about twelve percent a year is D. The multiple of ten point three times current earnings sits at the sixty fourth percentile of its own range on FactSet's data of the second of October. A yield bank in the upper third of its multiple returns roughly its earnings growth plus the dividend, less a little multiple drift, which is what the eleven point nine percent says. To reach twenty five percent a year, something outside consensus has to happen.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros first. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of the second of October is A, environment only, with an environment score of sixty nine and a half. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the first of October is A, and its Technical Momentum reading of the second of October is A, with the stock eligible for the weekly Pool of the second of October. The Banks row is one the target holds Overweight, and the book holds none of the yield-beneficiary banks, which the list-maker calls Gap 2. The chart setup is a pullback within a medium and long-term uptrend. The cons. The thematic A is the environment's grade, not the stock's; the stock layer adds nothing. Credit spreads widened outside this year's ranges this week, and AIB Group is one of thirteen banks in the top forty sharing that single risk, so this is a sector call wearing a stock's name.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. Everything attractive here is top-down and already priced, and the one thing not priced is the thing that hurts. Rates are the whole story, consensus already has the multiple compressing towards nine point seven times, and if rates fall from here the margin goes with them. If this week's widening of credit spreads is the start of a credit turn, a bank's earnings per share fall faster than any nine point eight percent growth path, and the twelve percent plausible return becomes a loss. Filling Gap 2 with the first of thirteen banks that share one risk fills the gap with the gap's own risk.

The one-line view. Neutral, as a working hypothesis. AIB Group is a bank the market is already paying for: top-down readings all A, a plausible return of about twelve percent a year that is D on your anchors, and no bottom-up work of ours yet to make the case the list has placed.
