The rule I used, so Richard can check it

To keep relative strength from deciding rather than informing, I set myself a mechanical threshold before looking at any row, and then applied it:

  • Industry rows depart from Neutral by one notch only where the industry is at the same extreme (A or F) in both readings and that extreme is broad across its sectors by stock count. P and H qualify. L qualifies trivially. I (Transportation) and J (Technology) reach the extreme but fail the breadth test, and stay Neutral
  • Sector rows depart from Neutral only where the parent industry has been left Neutral because it is split, and the sector has 20 or more stocks at the same extreme in both readings. G.13 Banks and J.4 Software qualify. Nothing else does. Inside P and H, the industry row already carries the lean and I have not stacked sector rows on top of it
  • Geography rows are all Neutral. Relative strength is not computed by country, no macro input was read, and geography in the universe is derived from the ticker suffix only. Nothing in the folder distinguishes one market from another this week
  • The two factor axes are set from the finding of component 1 itself, as argued above

The 20-stock threshold is mine and is arbitrary at the margin. Its purpose is to stop a lean on a handful of stocks masquerading as a view on the market.

How to read next week's move

The rating can change for two different reasons, and the instruction is right that conflating them is the main way it could mislead:

  1. The portfolio changed. Richard bought or sold something, or a holding's own momentum, size or case reading moved. This is the book getting fitter or less fit against a fixed yardstick
  2. The target moved beneath it. Component 2 changed a tilt, so the same book now scores differently. This is the yardstick moving, not the book

Next week's page should say which of the two produced the move, and by roughly how much each. The cleanest way to do that is for the script to score the unchanged book against both last week's target and this week's, and report the difference as "target effect" and the remainder as "portfolio effect." I propose that as a standing feature of the component, not a one-off.

This week the target moved from nothing to something, so 100 per cent of any measured rating would be "target effect." That is another reason the honest first reading is the midpoint.

Component 6: the Lists' Fitness-for-Fighting Tilt

Gap class: small, on the evidence that can be shown.

The reasoning

The rule is that the larger the gap between the book we hold and the book the environment calls for, the harder the lists should chase stocks that close it; the smaller the gap, the more they should simply chase good businesses. Applying it this week:

  • The environment is unread (component 1). No macro input, no view from Richard, and a sector table that is consistent with two regimes calling for two different books. A gap is measured against the book the environment calls for, and this week I can only half-describe that book
  • The target that stands in for the environment is Neutral on 122 of 129 rows (component 2). Even a perfectly measured book would find little to be far from. The seven departures are all one notch
  • The book cannot be measured against the target (component 4). Sixteen tickers, no exposure fields in the folder, and the per-stock scores were not among the files I could read
  • The gaps that would matter rest on recollection, and the gap I can prove is the one that matters least (component 5). Gap 1 (long fragility) and gap 2 (no energy, commodities, mining or banks) are what I remember about the holdings, flagged for Richard to confirm. Gap 3 (no Germany, France, Italy or Switzerland) is provable from ticker suffixes and universe counts, and I rank it last because a Neutral geography target is not a call for index weights

A research queue that chases a shape I cannot verify is a queue chasing my memory. At three quarters of a million tokens a memo, that is the wrong thing to commission on. So this week the lists should chase good businesses, and the fitness component, at 0.375, should do no more than lean the queue gently toward the robust and the strong, which is what the two factor tilts (Fragility D, Momentum B) will do at that weight.

Why not the neighbours

Why not 0.25, Double Underweighting. Because the gap is not "very small." It is unmeasured, and there is a specific, plausible and serious gap (fragility) that I can describe even if I cannot prove it. Setting the lists to ignore fitness entirely would mean that if the recollection is right, the lists spend a week finding more of what the book already has too much of.

Why not 0.5, Neutral. Because "unknown" is not the same as "moderate," and averaging a provable small gap with a suspected large one would launder recollection into the number. The one gap I can show is small. I set the tilt on what I can show and say plainly what would move it.

Why not 0.625 or 0.75. Because those say the gap is large or very large, and I cannot show that from anything in this folder. If the holdings join proposed in component 5 confirms gap 1, this number moves to 0.625 next week, and the reasoning for that is already written: a book long fragility against a Fragility Underweight, in an environment that cannot be read, is a large gap and the lists should chase it.
