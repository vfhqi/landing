The environment

What changed this week and why

Last week's regime (16-Oct-26) was "a discount-rate shock with the first credit crack inside the AI chain". Three readings moved it to risk-off confirmed:

  • The crack became the market's main subject. Goldman's Risk Appetite Indicator turned negative for the first time since March 2026 and technology saw its heaviest net selling since January 2025.
  • The bond market started to price the end of the squeeze. Yields fell on firm data, which Goldman (16 Oct) calls exhaustion and a break in trend, and its allocators upgraded bonds to overweight.
  • The European defensives-against-cyclicals line turned positive at three months (+2.0 percentage points, from -1.0 last week), which fired the condition written on the Momentum row.

What did not change: the US growth data did not weaken, the Fed still hikes in December, and oil rose. One new fact bears on a country row: French sovereign stress has spilled into Italy.

The macro causes

Inflation is firm with pipeline pressure, and I weight Jefferies' Tom Simons over Goldman for the next two quarters. US core personal consumption expenditures inflation was 3.1% in September against a trimmed mean of 2.2% (Goldman, 21 Oct). Goldman holds that tariff, energy and AI effects explain about three quarters of the gap and that inflation "is not broadening out"; Simons (17 Oct) and Fed President Hammack see a 1.1% rise in import prices and freight pass-through as pipeline pressure spreading into core goods. I weight Simons because oil moved from about $104 to $111 inside the week and energy is the channel every house names. Euro area, UK and Japanese inflation were not read this week.

US activity is strong and the data show no recession, with Chris Wood's K-shaped economy underneath. Third-quarter growth is tracking 3.3%, unemployment is 4.2% with the labour market "stabilized", and a global activity indicator is "notably above potential" (Goldman, 16 and 19 Oct). The consumer surveys point the other way: the University of Michigan preliminary October reading fell 1.8 points to 46.3 and the Conference Board fell 6.7 points to 81.9 in September (Goldman, 19 Oct), which is Wood's K-shape (via Jefferies, 17 Oct) in the numbers. European growth data and every non-US consumer survey were absent, so the consumer rows carry US readings only.

Trust in fiscal leadership is worsening in France and now spreading to Italy; trust in the Fed is intact. French spreads are at decade highs with "tentative spillover to Italy", France's fiscal challenge looks "harder now than in 2011-15", and Goldman (16 Oct) names worrying debt dynamics in Belgium, Austria and Finland too, while judging disorderly escalation less likely than then because growth, banks and European Central Bank (ECB) tools are stronger; its fragmentation index (19 Oct) says risk "remains limited". The Italian spillover is the new fact and bears on the Italy row. At the Fed there is near-unanimity that more tightening is needed, and Chair Warsh says the world "has broadly conformed" to expectations.

Geopolitics is worsening and reached the pack through one channel, oil, which is the channel that matters most to a European book. Brent reached $111 on mixed Middle East headlines (Jefferies, 22 Oct) after a US move to block a Russian diesel deal (17 Oct); Goldman's allocators add the US midterms as a volatility source. A US plan to fund Ukraine through tariffs on China appeared only as a web headline that no strategist assessed; Ukraine and China's economy were otherwise unread.

The macro prices

Rates: the Fed consensus is a rise every other meeting, no move in October and a rise in December (Goldman's Fed Chatterbox, 21 Oct), with Jefferies putting December odds above 90%. ECB, Bank of England and Nordic paths were not read, so the European rate path is carried from last week, not re-read.

Bond yields: the week's most important single change for the rate-sensitive rows, a hedge working rather than a peak. The US 10-year is near 5.10%, but yields fell this week after a sharp sell-off; Goldman's trader commentary (16 Oct) reads the failure to sell off on firm data as "a degree of exhaustion", and its allocators upgraded bonds to overweight over twelve months, "moving from headwind to hedge". It is one week's rally with the policy rate still rising, but it is the first time in this cycle that duration worked as a hedge. Bund and gilt yields were not read.

Credit: the widening is concentrated in AI financing and emerging-market sovereigns, so the Insurance and Banks tripwires have not fired on what was read. The CCC-to-BB spread gap, hyperscaler credit default swaps and neocloud spreads are all wider, neoclouds "the epicentre of the funding cost debate" (Goldman, 19 Oct), and emerging-market sovereign spreads widen with US rate volatility (Goldman, 22 Oct); Goldman stays underweight credit. European credit spreads were not read, so the two tripwires (a default cycle, a general European widening) cannot be tested.

Commodities: rising, led by oil. Brent is $111 (Jefferies, 22 Oct), above $100 and rising, which Goldman attributes partly to restocking; soft commodities are up 10 to 40% since the second quarter (Danske, 18 Oct); DNB Carnegie (22 Oct) reads Sandvik and Metso's results as a new mining cycle. Gold, copper and gas were not read.

Risk appetite: negative and turned this week, and on Richard's own European test the reading is risk-off confirmed, no longer a crack. Goldman's sentiment and positioning indicator is at its lowest since April; the VIX reached 22.3, the highest since late July; technology saw $5.5 billion of net selling while energy, utilities and staples led and value beat growth beat momentum (Jefferies, 17 Oct). The houses split on what follows: Goldman went tactically overweight equities on earnings and lighter positioning ("momentum's pendulum has swung too far"), while Jefferies' technician Kirsche sees the market "vulnerable" near term.

  • Richard's European test (the weekly breadth and defensives reading, prices to 22 Oct): the STOXX Europe 600 is 3.4% below its 52-week high, so the index-near-its-high-while-breadth-is-weak combination is not present in Europe this week. Breadth itself is weak on all four averages, with 43%, 46%, 52% and 54% of stocks above the 50, 100, 150 and 200-day averages, the 16th, 13th, 16th and 17th percentiles of three years, flat to slightly lower on the week. Defensives beat cyclicals over every window (+2.5 points at one week, +4.7 at four weeks, +2.0 at three months, +0.5 at six months and +1.3 at twelve), with the ratio above its 50 and 200-day averages.
  • Small-cap growth against large-cap yield, Richard's risk proxy, was again unread in Europe; the only reading is Kirsche's US view that small caps regain footing three to six months after the first hike.

Thematics and risk appetite

The AI theme was sold hard on financing fears while every house that looked at demand found it intact, and Europe's semiconductor weight sits on the wrong side of the trade. Goldman (16 Oct) sees no evidence of slowing compute demand; Jefferies (21 and 22 Oct) notes Meta raising its capital spending guide and Supermicro ahead. Goldman's trader expects leadership to flip from AI suppliers to the biggest AI investors as they show returns, and Wood says "the easy money in semis is made". Our semiconductor weight is in suppliers, and Jefferies reads analogue (Infineon, STMicroelectronics) up and equipment ("ASML - still not excited") down inside the same sector.

Outside AI the sources carry a rotation to quality, a non-AI cyclical case and a defensive case. Goldman's strategists see "Global Quality gaining ground" on strong balance sheets and resilient margins. The non-AI cyclical case rests on mining equipment, heavy trucks, pulp and paper. The defensive case comes from Jefferies' desk ("pricing-power defensives such as staples and utilities") and Danske (discount food retail "the place to be in times with rising inflation"), with Danske's warning that most cyclical exposure sits "more or less at all-time highs" with "cost inflation emerging". Defence, re-shoring and the energy transition were not spoken to.

Risk appetite is off. The only sectors bought were financials and real estate investment trusts, and Europe's relative strength this week rested on low AI exposure rather than on any European growth view, which no source offered.

What would change the view

I am bearish with high conviction on the regime; the thing most likely to be wrong is the duration of the squeeze, not its direction.

  • If the bond rally Goldman calls exhaustion becomes a trend (US 10-year under 4.5% with the December hike priced out), the Real assets, Towers, Homebuilders, Building technology and Renewable generators rows are where my tilts cost most, and I would move them one notch each before the data confirmed it. This week's evidence is one rally in yields with the policy rate still rising and oil at $111: a hedge working, not a peak.
  • A general European credit widening, or a default wave, turns Banks to Underweight and Insurance to Neutral. European spreads were not read, so the tripwires stand untested; the widening read this week is confined to AI credits and emerging-market sovereigns.
  • A European consumer reading that is not falling lifts the consumer rows held at Underweight on US surveys (Automotive retail, Online retail, Retail property). None arrived, and the US surveys fell again.
  • If a second house repeats the non-AI cyclical case (mining equipment, trucks, pulp and paper) with order data, the Industrials Neutral is too low, and Mining equipment at Neutral against relative strength F is the row to revisit first.
  • Oil in either direction: under $85, the Energy rows and the Norway row lose their reason; Brent above $100 into the December Fed meeting hardens the inflation and rates read, and the squeeze lasts longer than the bond market now says.
  • The memos' verdicts give no new bottom-up test. No memo carried a congruence line this week, so the test of this regime is last week's 30 memos (8 Fights it, 20 Mixed, almost all on the Fragility axis), unchanged.
  • The tilt scorecard neither confirms nor refutes the tilts, and no tilt moves on it. The 16-Oct-26 table (prices to 22 Oct) is the first with a positive spread, +0.66 points (Overweights -0.21% against Underweights -0.87%, the index -1.09%); the earlier tables read -6.9, -0.86, -1.39, -1.59 and -0.52 points. Six weeks is noise.
