Key Themes of the Middle Innings Lists in the Week of the sixteenth of October 2026.

This is Watson's second script on this week's lists: what the mix of the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection and of the three lists tells us about portfolio construction, read against this week's Portfolio Fitness for Fighting judgement, and including what is absent. It runs about fifteen minutes: the headline, seven themes each tested against the mix tables, the strongest counter-case to my reading and my reply, a recap and two next actions.

The headline.

The Pool and the lists describe a portfolio that is the mirror image of the one you hold. The lists are large, dear, energy-led and bank-heavy. The book is small, cheap, industrial and consumer-led, and it holds none of the environment's hedge. Only four of your sixteen holdings pass the Pool's own test. That is the most important fact in the mix tables, and the seven themes are seven ways of reading it.

Theme one. Energy is the leadership of the Pool and of the lists, the book has none of it, and only half of the lean is a case.

Judgement first: the energy lean is real information about the regime and the book should hold some of the hedge, but the record-margin half is paid for. The data. Energy, commodities and metals mining is six percent of our universe of one thousand three hundred and sixty-three stocks. It is twenty-two percent of the Pool, thirty-six names; thirty-four percent of the eighty list places; and forty percent of the Ideas Bench, eight names. The live book holds none. That is nearly fourfold over-representation at Pool level and nearly sevenfold on the Ideas Bench, and it is the environment's doing: energy sets inflation, inflation sets the hiking path, forward crude sets long yields, so the target table holds downstream distribution at Double Overweight and upstream and services at Overweight. The counter inside the data is the memos. Seven energy memos this week read bearish or park on a negative return, Galp at minus six point three percent a year, and not one disputes the asset; each says a record margin is being capitalised as a run rate. So the direction: enter the hedge through the services and scarcity shape, Tenaris, SBM Offshore, Subsea Seven and Saipem, and through the yield shape, Shell, TotalEnergies, Eni and Aker BP, not through the record-margin producers. So, theme one: the lists are forty percent energy and the book is zero, and the way to close that is scarcity and yield, not margin.

Theme two. The lists lean large, the book is small, and Fragility decides which is right.

Judgement: the size gap between the lists and the book is the gap that would cost the most if component one's sequence runs, and the lists are already closing it by behaviour without a rule. The data. The universe's median market value is about three point three billion euros. The Pool's is about four point seven billion, the eighty list places about five point nine billion, and the Ideas Bench about eight point three billion. The book's median is about one point five billion. Ten of your sixteen holdings are under two billion euros; one of the twenty on the Ideas Bench is. Four of the twenty on the Ideas Bench are above fifty billion; none of yours is. Fragility stays Double Underweight, confirmed from the bottom up: of thirty memo verdicts, eight fight the environment and twenty are Mixed, almost all naming this axis. The counter: DNB Carnegie find size a non-event in the Nordics, European breadth bounced this week, and a fragile book is what a relief rally rewards most. Where this is your decision: the size floor for new positions while Fragility is Double Underweight, proposed for three weeks and still unappliable because size is blank for every holding. I would propose a floor at about the Pool's median, four point seven billion euros, for Core Investments, with Early Innings Probing Bets exempt below it. So, theme two: the lists are already a large-cap screen by behaviour, the floor would make it a rule, and it is yours to set.

Theme three. The Pool is dear, the book is cheap, and the Ideas Bench sits between them by judgement.

Judgement: the valuation mix is the clearest sign that this regime refuses re-ratings, and the opportunity in it is the pullback that turns a park into a buy. The data, on the ratings store's valuation reading. In the universe, A is the largest group at twenty-nine percent and F is eighteen. In the Pool the order inverts: F is thirty-nine percent and A six. The eighty list places mirror the Pool, F thirty-six percent and A four. The Ideas Bench is different: C forty percent, B twenty-five, A ten and F five, so the judgement call pulled the top twenty toward the middle. The book is at the other end: sixty-three percent on the two cheapest readings against twenty percent in the Pool. Why: the Pool is built on stock relative strength of eighty or more, and strong relative strength in this regime buys the dear. Twenty of the thirty memo verdicts are Mixed, and the recurring sentence is parked on price, not on quality. The triggers are dated and near: Kainos below about one thousand one hundred and sixty pence with the ninth of November print, Clariant at about nine francs thirty-five with the third of November, Diploma on the seventeenth of November. What would make me wrong: the pullback arriving because the case changed, which shows as Sell-side Momentum falling before the price reaches the level; Bufab is this week's warning. So, theme three: the lists find quality at fair prices that the regime will not re-rate, which makes them the instrument for seeing the pullback arrive.

Theme four. Banks are a fifth of the list places, mostly without a memo, and the book holds none, rightly for now.

Judgement: the earnings leg works, the price leg does not, the loop that matters is sovereign, and the bank cluster is where the ranking's conviction is lowest. The data. Financials are seventeen percent of the universe and fourteen percent of the Pool, so banks are not over-selected at Pool level. They are over-selected on the lists: twenty-one percent of the list places, seventeen names, all but one of them banks. Of the thirteen banks on the Ideas Shortlist and the Ideas Longlist, ten have no memo. The book holds one financial, Avanza, which is not a bank, and the gap note counts no banks as an Underweight respected. The Banks row is Neutral, and the four bank memos split along the strategists' line: supportive earnings and dividends, material multiple compression, the catalyst being eventual rate relief, and this week the hiking path was pulled forward again. Poland is the country row most likely to move down: six list places, five of them banks carrying the same sovereign fact. So, theme four: the lists are telling you where rate relief would pay, the Dutch mechanism first and the Polish tape second, and they are not telling you to own a bank before the trigger.

Theme five. What is absent: the Overweight rows the book lacks are not in the Pool, and neither are the two largest continental markets.

Where this is yours: whether a Pool built on relative strength, which excludes the regime's defensive Overweights by construction, is the Pool you want in a defensive regime. I would propose a standing line each week naming the Overweight rows with no Pool representation, so the absence is counted rather than assumed. So, theme five: the lists cannot offer what the Pool does not hold, and this week that is insurance, pharma, tobacco, Germany and France.

The strongest counter-case to my reading, and my reply.

The counter-case is that the mix tables describe a screen, not a portfolio, and that the book is right to look nothing like it. The Pool is built on relative strength; relative strength in a risk-off, energy-led regime buys large, dear energy and banks on the environment's grades; eighteen of the top forty carry the Thin mark, a fitness score of seventy or better with no memo; and the memos that exist on the leaders say the hedge is paid for. Meanwhile the book is small, cheap and quality-rated, which is what a relief rally pays for. On that reading the lists are a crowded trade and the book is the contrarian position.

My reply has three parts. First, the tilt is the statement's instruction when the gap is Large, and component six says why it is not higher: a Double Overweight on fitness would hand the lists to the names we know least about. Second, the book's cheapness is not costless: fourteen point seven two points sit in rows the regime punishes or has just stopped rewarding, three holdings read Sell-side Momentum F, and the Fragility axis is named by twenty-eight of the thirty memo verdicts. Third, nothing here is a trade. The lists are research priorities, and the construction rules on the table, gross exposure, the size floor, the duration cap and the review trigger, are proposals for you to decide.

Recap.

The lists are large, dear, energy-led and bank-heavy; the book is small, cheap, industrial and consumer-led; four holdings pass the Pool's test; the hedge the book lacks is only half a case, and the half to use is scarcity and yield; the defensive Overweights the book lacks are not in the Pool; Switzerland is up and Sweden is down, with the book on the wrong side of the second.

Two next actions. For you: decide the size floor and the sector cap, in that order, because the size floor is the one the Fragility read makes urgent; my proposal is a floor at about the Pool's median for Core Investments, with Early Innings Probing Bets exempt. For me: close the measurement gap, size and fragility for every holding and the sector tilt join for the twelve of sixteen where it is blank, so that next week's mix tables place the book on the same footing as the lists.
