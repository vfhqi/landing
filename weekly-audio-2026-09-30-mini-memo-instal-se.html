APM Mini-Memo. Instalco. The first of October 2026.

Instalco sits at rank seven on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, promoted since last Friday.

My view is neutral, with medium conviction. This is a founder-led margin turnaround that is real in the profit and loss account but not yet in the cash, and at thirteen point one times forward earnings, with the shares about three percent off their high, the likely return looks short of your twenty-five percent a year.

First, what the company does. Instalco is a Nordic technical installation group: more than one hundred and fifty local electrical, heating, plumbing, ventilation and industrial businesses that keep their own names and full profit responsibility, with a tiny team at the centre. Sweden is seventy-one percent of sales; Norway and Finland are the rest. Revenue last year was about thirteen point six billion Swedish kronor, third in the Nordics behind Bravida and Assemblin Caverion, in a market so fragmented that the top three hold only twenty-three percent. About thirty-five percent of sales is service and forty-three percent fixed-price projects.

Second, the thesis. The founder, Per Sjöstrand, came back as chief executive in August twenty twenty-five with a mandate he summed up as margin, margin and margin, and a new country-based structure called Instalco two point oh. The second quarter of twenty twenty-six was the proof point. Organic growth was eight point four percent after six quarters of contraction. The margin, meaning operating profit before amortisation, reached seven point one percent, against five point nine for twenty twenty-five and above Bravida's five point four. The order backlog rose seventeen percent to almost eleven billion kronor. The Investment Analyst's memo of the third of August rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength B, and the script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the twenty-ninth of September is A, after DNB Carnegie raised its target to forty-seven kronor from forty-two.

The route to earnings per share is margin. Each fifty basis points of margin moves adjusted earnings per share by about nine percent, so getting from seven point one percent to the eight percent target is worth roughly sixteen percent, by my arithmetic. DNB Carnegie has adjusted earnings per share of two kronor forty-seven this year and three kronor next year. The German business, Fabri, now fifty-one percent owned and consolidated since August, runs at a twelve point seven percent margin and should add a mid-single-digit percentage to twenty twenty-seven profit. The things to track are the Swedish margin, cash conversion back above one hundred percent, leverage falling from two point six times to the two and a half times target, and what Fabri costs to finance.

Third, the Target Setup. The Investment Analyst's memo graded its setup fit B, carried into Setups' Fit/Crispness and not re-judged since. Translating that into your six Case Setups is my read, and my read is that the Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. A founder's return, a reorganisation, a named margin programme and a German platform are corporate change, and they are what moves earnings. The clearing setups fail on quality: a return on capital employed of nine point six percent and goodwill at one hundred and forty-five percent of equity do not make a high-quality business. Nor is it a delivering serial acquirer today; the balance sheet is the constraint.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The Investment Analyst's memo of the third of August rated Case Riskiness C, and I agree this is not simple. Three internal changes are running at once, the German step is financed on credit lines, and the research digest judges the chance of every driver executing over eighteen months as low. The tripwires are clear: leverage above three times including earn-outs, or a Swedish margin below six percent.

Fifth, business quality. The Investment Analyst rated Foundations' Robustness B, again mapped and not re-judged, though the same memo left operator quality and competitive advantage untested. The moat is local relationships: repeat customers are eighty percent of revenue, and margins ran above eight percent from twenty seventeen until the downturn, against about five and a half for peers. Against that, leverage is two point six times against a peer average of one point one, and twenty twenty-five missed every target, with organic growth of minus one percent and a margin of five point nine. Cash conversion was one hundred and eight percent last year, and the founder owns about eight and a half percent.

Sixth, returns and valuation. At forty-two point nine on the thirtieth of September, FactSet puts the shares on thirteen point one times twenty-four-month forward earnings, the forty-sixth percentile of their history. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of the thirtieth of September is D, on valuation alone. DNB Carnegie's target is about ten percent above the price, and the market has noticed: relative strength of ninety-two against fifty-five for the sector, and the script's Technical Momentum reading of the thirtieth is B. On a flat multiple and the sell-side earnings path, my rough modal return is in the low to mid teens a year. More needs eight percent margins by late this year, while the bear case of no growth and six and a half percent margins on nine times operating profit would hurt.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. On the plus side are the European Union renovation directive, public work such as prisons and hospitals, a Norwegian backlog up forty-one percent, a rising service share, and a German market five times the size of the Nordics. On the minus side, tenders often draw fifteen bidders against a historical five, and the forty-three percent of fixed-price work is exposed to wage and material inflation, which the memo flagged with a D for oil-driven inflation. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading is C, and provisional. And the Portfolio Fitness for Fighting reading of fifty-six point nine reflects the Fragility axis: a mid-cap serial acquirer is what this regime punishes first.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. The digest's own matrix picks the false friend profile. Second-quarter operating cash flow fell by almost half, to one hundred and four million kronor, while the backlog grew seventeen percent. A seventy-five million kronor receivables impairment came in the first quarter. The fall in leverage from three point three times came mainly from releasing working capital, not from profit. Financing already eats fourteen to twenty percent of operating profit, Fabri adds debt, goodwill exceeds equity, and Instalco has issued shares for deals before. If inflation returns, a fixed-price backlog won in a crowded market locks in thin margins just as the shares sit near their high.

The one-line view. Neutral, medium conviction: a real founder-led margin turnaround, not yet confirmed in cash, priced for a return in the low to mid teens rather than twenty-five percent. Watch cash conversion and leverage over the next two quarters.
