APM Mini-Memo. Bavarian Nordic. The twenty-seventh of November 2026.

Bavarian Nordic sits at rank twenty on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, promoted this week from the Longlist.

The one-line view. A net-cash vaccine maker whose travel franchise is growing at more than twenty percent a year, with margins rising because it now makes its own vaccines. The story depends on Travel Health outgrowing a lull in government stockpile orders next year, and the American renewal is the swing factor.

First, what the company does.

Bavarian Nordic is a Danish specialty vaccine maker with two businesses of very different character. The first, Public Preparedness, sells JYNNEOS, the only approved non-replicating smallpox and mpox vaccine in the world. The buyers are governments that stockpile it, chiefly the United States, France, the European Commission and Canada. The second, Travel Health, sells rabies, tick-borne encephalitis, chikungunya, typhoid and cholera vaccines. Travellers in America and Europe mostly pay for those themselves. The portfolio was bought from GSK in twenty nineteen and from Emergent in twenty twenty-three.

Last year's revenue was about six point two billion Danish kroner, split almost evenly between the two businesses. Travel Health grew thirty percent. The company makes everything itself, from bulk vaccine at its Kvistgaard plant in Denmark through to filling and direct sales. That integration is where the margin comes from.

Second, the thesis.

The interesting part is Travel Health. Over the first nine months of this year Travel Health revenue grew twenty-four percent, or thirty-four percent once you strip out discontinued partner agreements. The rabies vaccines led, up thirty-nine percent to about two billion kroner, on an American rabies market growing thirty-six percent. Management has lifted this year's Travel Health target by four hundred million kroner to three point four billion. Each extra krone of sales carries an operating margin, before depreciation and amortisation, of about sixty-eight percent. The rabies position is dominant, with seventy-nine percent share in America and ninety-six percent in Germany. Pricing power holds because the traveller pays, not an insurer.

The margin story is a manufacturing story. Bringing production of the former GSK vaccines in-house lifted the second-quarter gross margin to sixty-one percent. The research expects those transfers to add fifteen to twenty points of gross margin and to cut production cost by up to thirty percent by next year. That gain is largely already paid for, because the GSK and Emergent milestone payments are complete.

Now the earnings. Guidance for this year is revenue of about six point one billion kroner at a thirty-two percent operating margin before depreciation. Management raised that guidance in October, after a third quarter that beat consensus on revenue, on Travel Health, on operating profit and on the chikungunya vaccine Vimkunya. The broker DNB Carnegie has this year's adjusted earnings per share at fifteen kroner forty. The catch is next year. Only nine hundred million kroner of stockpile orders are secured for twenty twenty-seven, against about two point four billion for this year. So the same broker has next year's earnings at eleven kroner ten, a fall of twenty-eight percent. The upside case is that Travel Health keeps compounding and a renewed American contract fills the gap. Then next year's earnings land above that low bar, and the market stops valuing the company on its episodic engine.

The drivers to track are five. Quarterly Travel Health growth, especially rabies. The American stockpile renewal, now under negotiation. New government orders for next year. Vimkunya's American reimbursement. And execution of the buyback.

Third, the Target Setup.

There is no Investment Analyst memo on Bavarian Nordic. So there are no Bottom-up or Stock-driven ratings, and this read is my own. The Target Setup is Demand-driven EPSU. The step-up comes from travel vaccine volumes and the margin they carry, helped by in-sourced manufacturing that reads as corporate change. That fit is loose, because consensus has group earnings per share falling next year while the government engine rests. The setup becomes crisp only if Travel Health and a renewed stockpile contract together make next year a year of growth rather than a plateau.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

The case is not simple. Two engines pull in opposite directions, and almost every forward number depends on which one you weight. Three things need to go right. Travel Health keeps growing at twenty percent or more against tough comparisons. The United States renews its stockpile contract next year, the original eleven and a half million dose order having been completed in May. And Kvistgaard runs both product lines without conflict.

The main risk is government order flow through one factory. The United States alone was eighteen percent of last year's revenue and France eleven percent. A failed renewal would strip out half of group revenue. The research puts the joint success rate of its five drivers at only thirty-five percent, and that assumes Kvistgaard avoids bottlenecks. Two newer hazards sit alongside. Sanofi's next-generation rabies vaccine won European approval on the nineteenth of November, with an American filing planned for late this year, aimed straight at the franchise driving the beats. And the third-quarter beat rests on a thirty-six percent jump in American rabies demand that could normalise. What to track is clear. American budget allocations and the renewal announcement. Quarterly rabies sales and market share. And any production slip at Kvistgaard. Leadership is also new. Tarja Stenvall became chief executive on the fifteenth of October, the finance director leaves in April, and a strategy update is due in the first half of next year.

Fifth, business quality.

Good franchise, poor predictability. The moats are genuine. The only approved non-replicating smallpox vaccine. Regulatory and manufacturing barriers that take years to cross. Dominant rabies shares. And an adjusted operating margin before depreciation of nearly forty-one percent last year. The balance sheet is strong, with nearly three billion kroner of cash despite a five hundred million buyback completed in July and a further seven hundred and fifty million launched in September.

Against that, Public Preparedness revenue swung from five billion kroner in twenty twenty-three to three point one billion last year. That places the company in the bottom five percent for earnings predictability. Cash conversion this year is weak. Nine-month operating cash flow was under five hundred million kroner, against two point two billion a year earlier, on higher receivables and the final GSK payment. Our Sector Digest of the twenty-second of October lists Bavarian Nordic among beats built on stocking, accruals and one-offs.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

Cheap on this year, fair on next. At two hundred and fifty-two kroner, the shares trade at about eighteen times earnings two years forward, in the middle of their ten-year range, and two percent below the fifty-two week high. On DNB Carnegie's figures that is about sixteen times this year's earnings and twenty-three times next year's. The weekly TSR Valuation rating is C, on the valuation reading alone. The weekly routine's plausible return of the nineteenth of November is fifty-four percent a year. But that figure rests on the multiple moving from eighteen to thirty times, which I would not lean on while consensus earnings fall next year.

Broker targets of two hundred and eighty and two hundred and ninety kroner imply eleven to fifteen percent, a D against your bar. The shares trade at a twenty percent discount to European vaccine peers, and the private equity bid withdrawn last year was two hundred and fifty per share. A B return needs next year's earnings above eleven kroner ten, which the renewal and Travel Health could deliver. Carnegie's bear target is one hundred and sixty.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

The pros. The weekly Thematic Fit rating is A, from the pharma environment alone. The Sector Digest of the twenty-second of October puts the pharma sector at the twenty-second percentile of its ten-year multiple range, shows Bavarian Nordic up forty percent over three months and rising after a fall, and names its travel vaccines as a single-company story worth a look. Weekly Sell-side Momentum rose to A this week, and weekly Technical Momentum is B. The Industry Digest's rule applies. Where the patient pays, pricing power survives.

The cons. Half the revenue is set by government budgets, with price caps in European joint procurement. Longer-dated, smallpox vaccines on the newer messenger RNA platform, from Moderna and from CSPC, threaten the stockpile franchise. And the two-dose JYNNEOS regimen loses about a quarter of patients between doses.

The one-line view. A net-cash vaccine maker whose travel franchise is growing at more than twenty percent a year, with margins rising because it now makes its own vaccines. The story depends on Travel Health outgrowing a lull in government stockpile orders next year, and the American renewal is the swing factor.
