APM Mini-Memo. Azelis. The first of October 2026.

Azelis is rank six on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

My view is neutral, as a working hypothesis. This is a real cause-for-concern clearing in a solid but over-borrowed specialty chemicals distributor. The market has already paid for the end of customer destocking, and the rest of the return waits on leverage falling back inside three times.

First, what the company does. Azelis is the second-largest pure distributor of specialty chemicals and food ingredients, behind IMCD. It acts as the outsourced sales and technical arm for more than two thousand eight hundred manufacturers and over sixty-five thousand customers, across sixty-four countries, with more than seventy application laboratories. Revenue in 2025 was about four point one billion euros, with adjusted operating profit of four hundred and eleven million, a ten percent margin. Roughly two thirds is Life Sciences, the rest Industrial Chemicals. The market value is about two point nine billion euros at twelve euros nine cents on the thirtieth of September.

Second, the thesis. Three concerns hit at once. Organic revenue fell for three years. Leverage overshot to three point four times net debt to cash earnings, against a target of two and a half to three, and that stopped an acquisition engine which had done sixty-four deals in eight and a half years. And 2025 was the third straight year of downgrades. Now customers have finished destocking. Organic growth was plus three point nine percent in the second quarter against one point eight expected, and IMCD grew seven and a half, so this is a sector fact. Supplier price rises lifted gross margin by a full percentage point to twenty-four point seven. Twenty million euros of cost is out, and guidance went up on the thirtieth of July. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twenty-ninth of September rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength C, because the forces are mostly external and partly temporary. The path to earnings per share is modest: from eighty-three cents in 2025 to ninety cents, then one euro and one cent, then one euro and eight cents by 2028. That is about nine percent a year, below the twelve to twenty percent band. The biggest single contributor is lower interest cost, not the business. What you track is the third-quarter update in late October, for volume against price, and year-end leverage, heading toward three point one times or stuck above three point four.

Third, the Target Setup. The memo says the Target Setup is Major CfC Clearing in MQ non-cyclical or better business on Q1 multiple, leading to reversion to normal, with CfC in quality cyclical at trough-on-trough as a secondary reading of the same facts. The memo rated Setups' Fit/Crispness C. All five conditions pass, but two only at the boundary. The multiple sits around the twenty-fifth to twenty-ninth percentile rather than clearly in the bottom quarter, and capitulation showed in the price but never in the brokers' ratings. I agree with that. You are seeing this setup from the middle, nine months on and seventy-seven percent off the low.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The memo rated Case Riskiness C, combining Riskiness at C with Simplicity at D. Simplicity is poor. There are seven company-specific causes for concern, guidance is only qualitative, it operates in sixty-four countries, and the market reads its earnings on two different bases. What holds it at C is that the case turns on three dated things, and a covenant at four and a half times against three point four bounds the fundamental downside. Even so, a fall back to a trough multiple would cost eighteen to thirty-six percent with earnings unchanged.

Fifth, business quality. Solid, not strong. Foundations' Robustness is C from the Investment Analyst. The supplier franchise is the standout: a thirty-year average tenure with the top ten principals, no losses among the top twenty in ten years, and at least eighty-five percent of top-ten revenue exclusive. Cash conversion averaged ninety-three percent from 2019 to 2025. Against that, return on invested capital is seven point three percent against IMCD's ten point four, leverage is the highest among the peers, and organic growth has trailed IMCD for six quarters running. Management is competent and defensive rather than value-creating.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The memo's modal return, rebased to twelve euros nine cents, is twenty point two percent a year over eighteen months and fourteen point five percent a year over three years. The average sits between the C and B anchors, and TSR/Valuation is C. More than half of the eighteen-month return comes from re-rating, at near-even odds that the balance sheet clears by March 2028. On the consensus median earnings path it drops to seven point nine percent a year. The shares trade at thirteen point one times forward earnings, the twenty-eighth percentile of their history, against a ten-year median of about fifteen times and IMCD at fourteen point nine. The memo puts the price that clears both horizons at about ten euros forty. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the twenty-ninth of September is A, and its Technical Momentum reading of the thirtieth is B.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. On the plus side, producers route only a fifth of their volume through distributors, who serve eighty-five percent of customers. The market grows about three and a half percent a year to 2030, the top ten hold under fifteen percent, and downturns push more accounts to distributors. On the minus side, non-exclusive Chinese supply is pressing the semi-specialty end, the European small-customer tail is shrinking, and the pricing tailwind rests partly on Middle East disruption, where Azelis has six to seven percent direct exposure against under one percent at IMCD. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of the thirtieth of September is C, and provisional.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. This is a false friend: a pricing-led re-rating without volume, in Morgan Stanley's phrase. Estimates were cut by a fifth, then raised on one price-assisted quarter. Management itself expects the Middle East pricing and cheap inventory to ease in the second half. Cash conversion was flattered by running down inventory, and it reverses when stock is rebuilt. Leverage rose to three point four times in June rather than falling. On the lower of the two broker earnings clusters the stock is on about sixteen times and the re-rating is already spent. The price rose thirty-nine percent in six months while estimates rose six. On that reading you drop it rather than park it, and Morgan Stanley's bear case is six euros eighty.

The one-line view. Neutral, as a working hypothesis: a real clearing in a solid but over-borrowed distributor, where the market has paid for the end of destocking and the rest of the return waits on leverage falling back inside three times.
