APM Mini-Memo. HELLENiQ Energy. The twenty-seventh of November 2026.

HELLENiQ Energy sits at rank eight on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, promoted this week from the Longlist.

The one-line view. Greece's dominant refiner is earning record diesel and jet fuel margins, has paid down debt fast and now trades on six times consensus earnings. The story depends on Mediterranean refining margins holding through 2027, when every published base case has them falling back.

First, what the company does.

HELLENiQ Energy runs three coastal refineries in Greece, at Aspropyrgos, Elefsina and Thessaloniki. Together they hold sixty-five percent of the country's refining capacity. Refining earned seventy-two percent of the group's adjusted operating profit before depreciation of about one point one billion euros in 2025. More than half of refining sales are exports, so you should think of the group as a Mediterranean exporter rather than a Greek utility. Around the refineries sit a third of Greek fuel retail through about fifteen hundred stations, a polypropylene plant, and a power and renewables arm. That arm had five hundred and ten megawatts running in 2025 against a target of one and a half gigawatts by 2028.

The company is closely held. Paneuropean Oil and Industrial Holdings owns forty percent and the Greek State thirty-one percent, leaving a free float of twenty-eight percent. The state is also customer, supplier and tax-setter. A thirty-three percent solidarity contribution under a 2024 law cost the company about one hundred and seventy-four million euros.

Second, the thesis.

The story is a windfall being banked. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz in late February 2026, Russian refinery outages and two decades of European underinvestment took HELLENiQ Energy's realised refining margin from under fourteen dollars a barrel in the second quarter of 2025 to twenty-two and a half dollars a year later. Each dollar a barrel is worth about one hundred and six million euros of pre-tax profit. So adjusted operating profit before depreciation doubled in the second quarter of 2026, and net debt fell by seven hundred million euros in a single quarter, to just under two billion euros. Debt repaid in a windfall does not come back when margins normalise. The equity keeps a larger share of whatever the business earns next.

The route to earnings per share runs through one number. Refining margins set about sixty-five percent of forward earnings per share. The euro against the dollar sets about fifteen percent, and the renewables build about ten percent. Consensus has followed the margin. Forward earnings per share rose about forty percent between early October and now, while the price fell from about eighteen euros to sixteen euros twenty. So the forward multiple dropped from nine and a half times to six times, by my own arithmetic from our two readings. That swing moved the valuation reading from F to A and lifted the stock onto the Ideas Bench.

Why might the market pay more? The specialists now call the European distillate shortage structural. Management expects strong margins through 2027. The sell-side base case still has the margin falling back to historic levels by 2027. The gap between those views is where further upgrades come from. The drivers to track are four. The realised margin against eighteen dollars a barrel after the fourth-quarter print. The direction of 2027 consensus. Brent against the eighty-five dollar level that would remove the downstream energy row's reason. And the year-end dividend and buyback decisions.

Third, the Target Setup.

The Target Setup is Demand-driven EPSU. The Investment Analyst's memo of the third of October rated Setups' Fit at D, because that fit is loose. The inflection is in the margin rather than in demand. Greek fuel demand fell six percent in the second quarter while the margin doubled, and the earnings step-up had already happened. The memo named the trough-on-trough setup as the one this stock will fit when the margin normalises, estimates are cut and the multiple sits between six and seven and a half times. My own reading is that the multiple has arrived there first, at six times, while earnings are still at the peak. So the setup is half-formed. The price is at trough. The earnings are not.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

The case is simple to state and not low-risk. The Investment Analyst's memo of the third of October rated Case Riskiness D, where A is simple and low-risk. The one driver that makes the case is the one that unmakes it. The memo's own path has adjusted earnings per share peaking near two euros forty in 2026 and falling to one euro fifty by 2028, as the realised margin returns to thirteen or fourteen dollars a barrel. Even the sell-side median has 2027 earnings falling twenty-two percent.

Here is what needs to go right. The Mediterranean middle-distillate margin must stay above about eighteen dollars a barrel for two consecutive quarters after the fourth-quarter print, with no sign of Hormuz normalising. And 2027 consensus must rise rather than fall. The Investment Analyst put that branch at about one in three. The dated risks beside the margin are a repeat windfall levy, the Thessaloniki turnaround in 2027, and an unhedged currency exposure, where a five percent move in the euro shifts earnings per share about nine percent. The constructive point is that the leading indicators are daily. The margin is published, the arithmetic is public, and a wrong call shows quickly.

Fifth, business quality.

HELLENiQ Energy is an excellent operator with ordinary returns. The largest turnaround in the company's history finished on schedule. Utilisation reached one hundred and three percent. The Geneva trading desk replaced the lost Hormuz crude with Norwegian and American grades without a slowdown, and that desk's premium of about seven dollars a barrel over the Mediterranean benchmark has held across cycles. Against that, normalised return on invested capital of seven point four percent sits below a cost of capital of about eight percent. The renewables build has delivered about half of what was promised, and management publishes no guidance. The Investment Analyst's memo of the third of October rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength B and Foundations' Robustness C. The duopoly and the distillate deficit are robust. The business beneath them does not survive a change of environment.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

Two readings disagree, and the gap between them is the case. The weekly routine's plausible return of the nineteenth of November is about thirty-one percent a year on average, an A against your bar. That figure rests on the multiple moving from six to nine and a half times while consensus earnings fall thirty-two percent a year. The Investment Analyst's memo of the third of October, at a price of eighteen euros eighteen, gave a modal return of about minus eighteen percent a year to April 2028. The memo applied eight times to its own lower earnings path, and the four to five percent dividend was the only positive leg. My own reading is that at sixteen euros twenty the answer sits between. The memo's bull branch of twenty-one to twenty-five euros plus dividends by 2029 is roughly a C-grade return from here. The memo's central path still gives a negative one. So the return is a bet on which earnings path is right, rather than on the multiple. One caution. The Investment Analyst grades the valuation swing below the data until its cause is known, and the consensus mean target of early October sits below today's price.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

The pros. HELLENiQ Energy carries two of the Sector's five live stories at full strength: record diesel and jet margins, and the windfall wiping out debt and buying a utility. The Greek market is a two-player duopoly with over ninety percent of wholesale fuel and no volume war. The Romanian and Bulgarian renewables yield ten to twelve percent against lower single digits at home. All four top-down readings are A this week: Sell-side Momentum, Technical Momentum, Thematic Fit and Valuation. And downstream energy is the row where your target holds Double Overweight and the book holds nothing.

The cons. The Sector digest marks the margin story as one fed by the Strait of Hormuz staying shut. A reopening that coincides with new capacity is the downturn case. My note of the sixth of November found no refiner or product tanker yet a capital case while the memos call the upswing fully priced. The state's levy precedent and retail price caps run one way. And polypropylene spreads at record lows are a drag.
