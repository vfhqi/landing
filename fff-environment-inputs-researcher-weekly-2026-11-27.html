Report 1
G10 and European macroeconomic inputs (source #1)
Executive summary
  • The week's read: United States (US) activity is running hot (third-quarter gross domestic product (GDP) revised up to 4.3% annualised) while core inflation sits at 3.3%, and the houses split on whether the Federal Reserve (Fed) must hike
  • Strongest view: Goldman Sachs Economics (25 Nov) argues trimmed-mean inflation near 2.4% is the true signal and expects inflation to fall across developed markets by end-2027, despite raising 2027 core forecasts for commodity and artificial intelligence (AI) cost pressure
  • Sharpest disagreement: Pivotus Partners (R. Farr, 25 Nov) expects the Fed to hike; MacroStrategy Partnership (Michael Wilson, 26 Nov) reads falling breakevens and a 1.9% GDP deflator as inflation rolling over
  • What changed: Conference Board consumer confidence jumped 11.4 points to 103.3, while France's forward-looking purchasing managers' index (PMI) fell to just above its historic low (Goldman Sachs, 24 Nov)
  • Watch: the Section 301 tariff hearing on 25 January (Goldman Sachs, 25 Nov); Iran escalation; the French election run-in

1. Inflation: hot headline, disputed core

Goldman Sachs Economics (25 Nov) says exclusion-based core inflation is still rising year on year in the US (3.3%, personal consumption expenditures (PCE) basis) and euro area (2.6%), and flat in the UK (2.8%). Its reason for calm: monthly core slowed everywhere in October, and trimmed measures (Dallas Fed 2.4%) strip out one-off effects. Its caution: euro area core services accelerated to 3.7%. Pivotus (Farr, 25 Nov) reads it the other way: core PCE at 3.26% is the 78th consecutive monthly rise, so "we think the Fed hikes". MacroStrategy (Wilson, 26 Nov) sides with forward indicators: breakevens down to 2.1% and 2.08%.

Houses disagree on the next Fed move, not the level. For a European book, Goldman's view that commodity spillovers into European core will be "more delayed" keeps the European Central Bank (ECB) boxed in through winter.

StatusCore 2.6% euro area, 2.8% UK, 3.3% US; headline US 3.8%
TrendStill rising year on year in the US and euro area; monthly pace slowing
Change in trendGoldman raised 2027 forecasts; market breakevens fell
SkewGoldman and MacroStrategy lean lower by end-2027; Pivotus leans higher near term

2. Economic health and consumer sentiment

Goldman Sachs Economics (25 Nov) stresses "the resilience of the US consumer" but expects fourth-quarter consumption to soften. Jan Hatzius (Goldman, relayed 22 Nov) forecasts 2.8% global growth in 2027 with risks "tilted to the downside" and "early signs that AI is weighing on the job market".

Europe is two-speed. Pivotus (23 Nov) relays S&P Global: Germany 55.0 at a 56-month high, France 46.2, "a deepening downturn" (Chris Williamson, S&P Global). Jari Stehn (Goldman, 22 Nov) expects high energy prices to slow euro area consumption over winter and France to stagnate until its elections.

StatusUS strong, Germany improving, France contracting, UK resilient (Goldman, 20 Nov)
TrendUS and German momentum up; euro area services and France down
Change in trendUS confidence broke higher after the midterms; developed-market services PMIs fell
SkewHatzius and Stehn tilt downside into 2027; Pivotus tilts upside for the US

Mark Wilson (Goldman Sachs sales, 20 Nov) says the Fed "feels meaningfully behind the curve" and is compounding a tightening in financial conditions. Pivotus (Farr, 23 and 24 Nov) frames Washington as unstable: Nvidia's chief executive removed and reinstated "with senior US government input". Farr's conclusion: "you must account for this volatility with lower multiples". In France, Pivotus (23 Nov) reports Macron considering a constitutional "golden rule" if markets stay unstable in January, with Le Pen leading polls. Greece is the counter-example, with a 3.2% primary surplus (Jefferies, D. Farr, 26 Nov).

Executive summary

  • Strongest view: Julien Garran (MacroStrategy, 20 Nov) says Nvidia's 700 billion dollar ecosystem financing is 200 times Cisco's vendor financing in 2000 and expects orders to swing from up 70% to "heavily down" within a year, most neoclouds to fail and hyperscalers to write down data centres
  • Sharpest disagreement: Mark Wilson (Goldman Sachs, 20 Nov) says adoption, not building, is the durable earnings source, with Europe and the UK well placed; Jefferies' Janardan Menon (26 Nov) says early estimate cuts would be good news because the semiconductor bottom follows the first cut
  • Change: evidence of surplus arrived this week: SpaceX cut graphics processing unit (GPU) rental prices by a third, banks are selling AI loans (MacroStrategy, 26 Nov) and Nvidia halted South East Asian shipments (Jefferies, 26 Nov)

1. AI capital spending: from boom to financing stress

Garran (MacroStrategy, 20 Nov) rests his case on Nvidia's own disclosures: vendor financing up 1,600% in three years to 63 billion dollars, and off-balance-sheet obligations up from 184 to 530 billion dollars in a quarter. His conclusion: "the great AI circular financing circus is now over". Mark Wilson (Goldman, 20 Nov) reaches a softer version from the same data: the pace "is likely unsustainable" and investors must separate "durable earnings growth versus a pull forward of future demand". Menon (Jefferies, 26 Nov) adds the mechanism: when GPU availability eases in a market that over-ordered, order books get cut far more than the marginal volume warrants. Goldman's Jan Hatzius (22 Nov) still expects strong AI capital spending to support 2.8% global growth, listing disappointment as a downside risk.

In Europe, Danske's Carl-Johan Kristell (22 Nov) calls Bravida "the last man standing" among Nordic data-centre plays and says peak orders are behind it; Danske's technology, media and telecoms (TMT) team (26 Nov) says the "AI hardware rewind" has been overdone in HMS Networks; Kepler's Tom Donneky (26 Nov) notes Prysmian's data-centre work is already 12% of sales and still winning hyperscaler deals.

Why it matters: the theme that led European industrials, cables, installers and semiconductor equipment for two years is now a source of estimate risk, and the houses split on whether second-order names (cables, grid) hold up while first-order names (GPUs, installers) fall.

StatusCapital spending still growing in the data; orders and financing turning
TrendDecelerating, with surplus signs
Change in trendThis week's shift from "slowing" to "GPU prices falling"
SkewDownside for builders (Garran, Menon, Kristell); mixed for second-order beneficiaries (Donneky, Danske TMT)

2. AI adoption and productivity: the second-leg thesis

Mark Wilson (Goldman, 20 Nov) argues "the true possibilities of AI come from the technology's deployment, not its build", that Goldman's "AI productivity champions" have diverged from capital-spending winners over six weeks, and that Europe and the UK, with ageing populations and services-heavy economies, stand to gain most. Hatzius (22 Nov) adds the darker half: "early signs that AI is weighing on the job market". Kepler's Donneky (26 Nov) gives Roche as "the clear AI leader in pharma".

StatusNascent; a thesis more than a set of earnings
TrendGaining airtime as the capital-spending theme fades
Change in trendGoldman has made it a formal 2027 strategy paper
SkewUpside for adopters, by the houses' telling; nobody quantified it

1. The relative call: Cyclicals against Defensives

Jaisson (Goldman, 20 Nov) observes Defensives have beaten Cyclicals by 14% since early October "despite a resilient macro backdrop". His reason to lean back: past rotations lasted four months and delivered 10% to 15%, so this one is mature, and positioning, stretched Defensive valuations, the artificial intelligence (AI) unwind and wider credit spreads have all reset. Peter Oppenheimer (Goldman, 22 Nov) frames 2027 as "Growth balanced with selective Defensives and beneficiaries of lower bond yields". No non-Goldman house made the opposite call in writing, but Jefferies' Marc Kofler (21 Nov) describes resources trading "as treacherous as I can remember".

Why it matters: for a European long-only book the Goldman call says the defensive crowding is the risk now, not the cyclical exposure.

StatusDefensives have led by 14% over seven weeks
TrendRotation into Defensives, now of typical size
Change in trendGoldman flipped to favour selected Cyclicals this week
SkewGoldman says Cyclical upside; no source argued Defensives extend further

Technology. His twist: early cuts to 2027 earnings per share are good news because stocks bottom soon after the first cut, and semiconductor equipment re-rates first.
