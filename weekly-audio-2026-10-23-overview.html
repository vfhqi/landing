Overview (including process/structure) of the Middle Innings Lists in the Week of the twenty-third of October 2026.

This is Watson's overview of this week's Ideas Bench, Ideas Shortlist and Ideas Longlist: how they were made, what they say, and what changed since last Friday. It runs about fifteen minutes, covering the process, the headline judgement, the Ideas Bench name by name, the four things this routine flags every week, the Shortlist and Longlist in brief, the changes, how Portfolio Fitness for Fighting shaped the lists, the caveats, and two next actions.

Part one, the process and the structure.

The judgement first. These are research priorities built from a Pool the environment has narrowed hard toward energy and strength, scored with a blend that overweights Portfolio Fitness for Fighting, then re-ordered by judgement where a memo or the research said the score was wrong. The result is a Bench whose top is memo-backed and dated, and whose middle is carried by the environment more than by what we know.

The mechanics. The Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection was read on Friday's close and holds one hundred and fifty-one stocks out of our universe of one thousand three hundred and sixty-three. Twenty-nine are marked leaving, week one of two. Two live holdings sit inside the Pool, Sdiptech and Volex; all sixteen live holdings are excluded, leaving one hundred and forty-nine candidates, of which sixty carry a published memo from the Investment Analyst and eighty-nine do not.

The scoring blends the bottom-up case with the Portfolio Fitness for Fighting read at a tilt of point six two five toward Portfolio Fitness for Fighting, unchanged from last week. The Eight Master Ratings feed it: Thesis's Change Forces' Strength, Foundations' Robustness, Setups' Fit/Crispness and Case Riskiness on the case side, Sell-side Momentum, Thematic Fit/Momentum, Technical Momentum and TSR/Valuation on the environment side. TSR/Valuation ranks and never gates. For every no-memo name all four case ratings are averages, and that applies to forty-five names on the lists. No Thematic Fit/Momentum override was applied and the overrides file was empty: you spoke no additions or moves.

The judgement call then sits on top of the score. Its checks: every data layer was fresh, no quick-judgement pitch existed, and the turning and inflecting note carried nothing, the normal case. Judgement moves of more than five places are recorded one by one. Which model the session ran on is not in our files for this week, so I will not guess. The output is the usual three lists, all full length, compared against the lists of the sixteenth of October.

Bearish on the regime, selective on the lists, high conviction on the first and medium on the second. The week moved from a crack to a confirmed risk-off on your own European test, defensives beating cyclicals by two percentage points at three months, and the lists lean on the one thing the environment pays for, energy, with fourteen of the top forty starting-score names in it. Where I would spend your ten minutes is not the energy lean but the five memo-backed cases with a dated test inside four weeks: ING Groep between the twenty-ninth and thirty-first of October, WPP in late October, Clariant on the third of November, Kainos on the ninth of November, Diploma on the seventeenth of November.

Nine stocks carry a majority of A and B grades and all nine have memos, so all nine are put to you. Only one memo contemplates buying near today's price, Kainos, where the memo of the second of October says accumulate below about one thousand one hundred and sixty pence and do not chase above one thousand two hundred and fifty. Two rows changed letter. Energy upstream stays B rather than A because thirteen of the eighteen upstream Pool names read F on TSR/Valuation.

The gap report names four gaps: no energy, insurance, pharma or tobacco, the regime's hedges; over-exposure to fragility and the capped rows; concentration in Sweden; and, new, weak momentum, with Technical Momentum D on ten of sixteen holdings. The Bench answers the first gap and nothing else.

The strongest counter-case: the hedge is paid for. Thirteen of eighteen upstream names read F on valuation, every one of the nine upstream memos reads the return as paid for or negative, and chasing the regime's leader two months in is where reversals begin. If the bond rally becomes a trend, with the ten-year under four and a half percent and the December hike priced out, the broken charts, ASML first, are where relief lands and the parked compounders re-rate before their memos re-open. My reply is that the counter argues for services and downstream over upstream, not for no energy, and that is where the Bench's conviction sits.

Part eight, the caveats.

The Pool is two-thirds unmemoed and eighteen of the top forty by score are thin, scoring well on the environment with nothing known about the business. Six Bench names could leave the Pool next week. Component one did not read European inflation, growth, central bank paths or credit spreads, and your own view on file is five weeks old. The size floor amendment is still unappliable because size is blank for every holding.
