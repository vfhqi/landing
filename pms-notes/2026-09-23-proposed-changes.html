What "more than 5% above" means, and why it is a problem. It means the price is more than 5% above the level where the stop rule starts to work: in other words, nothing in that group asks anything of you today. It is the "nothing to do" group. But the group is defined only by distance, and distance measures risk (how much room is left), not health (whether the trend is any good). So today it puts Hilton Food, 14.2% above its stop with every average stacked and rising, in the same line as Helios Towers' probing bet, 6.3% above its stop but with its 50-day average below its 150-day and 200-day, and Arteche's main tranche, 16.5% above its stop but in a seventh base, which is late in Minervini's counting. Those three situations call for three different things: add on a pullback; hold and watch; trim into strength.

The logic that got us here. The table was built to answer the rule's question, "what must I do today", so it is sorted by nearness to action. Your own question when you read the book is different, and you wrote it down on 22 September: "The key question (One Thing) I need to keep asking of all stocks: Is this a strong looking Stage 2?" Those two questions collide exactly in this row: nothing to do today, and yet this is the group where the adds and the trims come from. Splitting it makes the table answer both questions.

6. The Position Management System page, main view: why it confuses, specifically
