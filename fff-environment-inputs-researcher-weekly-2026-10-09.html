Report 1
G10 and European macroeconomic inputs (source #1)

Contents: Executive summary; Inflation; Economic health and consumer surveys; Leadership trust and geopolitics; What no source spoke to this week

Executive summary
  • The read: activity is strong in the United States (US) and firm in Europe, inflation sits above target on energy and artificial intelligence (AI) effects, and trust in European fiscal leadership, France above all, is the input that worsened
  • Strongest view: Goldman Sachs (7 Oct) finds most of the US core inflation overshoot one-off or mismeasured, so the underlying trend has not accelerated
  • Sharpest disagreement: Federal Reserve (Fed) Governor Waller (8 Oct) reads the same drivers as persistent and expects two more hikes
  • What changed: Goldman Sachs moved its next Fed hike from October to December (5 Oct); the French 10-year premium over Germany reached 152 basis points, the widest since 2011 (R. Marshall, 2 Oct)
  • Watch: the University of Michigan October sentiment reading, due 9 Oct

Inflation

Goldman Sachs (US Daily, 7 Oct) traces August core personal consumption expenditures (PCE) inflation of 3.0%, 0.7 points above its forecast, to AI-driven electronics prices, jet fuel from the war with Iran and tariffs. It judges three quarters of this temporary or mismeasured and keeps 2.2% by December 2027. Waller (via Goldman Sachs, 8 Oct) says those same forces "undermined his faith" in disinflation; the September Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) minutes (7 Oct) show "almost all" participants saw upside risk. vperception (6 Oct) sides with Goldman: oil and Iran drive the narrative, but inflation breadth is muted. Swiss core inflation of 0.5% was "a relief" to the Swiss National Bank (R. Marshall, Pacific Asset Management internal note, 2 Oct).

Why it matters: if Waller is right, policy rates stay higher for longer and the discount rate on long-duration European earnings stays elevated.

Status: above target in the US, low in Switzerland

Trend: US core easing slowly, on Goldman's numbers

Change in trend: none in the data; the Fed's tone turned more hawkish

Skew: upside, per the FOMC and Waller

  • Goldman Sachs, US Daily, 7 Oct: overshoot mostly one-off
  • Goldman Sachs on Waller and the FOMC minutes, 7 and 8 Oct: risks "tilted to the upside"
  • vperception, 6 Oct: inflation breadth muted

Economic health and consumer surveys

Goldman Sachs reads the US as strong: third-quarter gross domestic product (GDP) tracking of 3.1% (6 Oct), the lowest four-week average of jobless claims since September 2022 (8 Oct) and services purchasing managers' index (PMI) at a post-pandemic high of 58.8 (5 Oct). The soft spot is September payrolls of +29,000 with unemployment at 4.2% (2 Oct), which Goldman puts near breakeven. Euro area PMIs rose to 52.9 in manufacturing and 53.0 in services (5 Oct). Goldman (5 Oct) estimates global AI spending adds about 0.1 points to European growth this year, but its Macro Roadmap (2 Oct) still puts European growth at "just ~1%".

Consumer sentiment and expectations: the only long-running survey in the evidence is the University of Michigan (US), last at 48.1 for September, with Goldman expecting a lower 47.0 for October and 5-to-10-year inflation expectations steady at 3.4% (4 Oct). Web searches returned headlines without readings. No Conference Board, GfK, European Commission or national statistics office reading appeared. No reading this week: Canada, the United Kingdom (UK), Germany, France, Italy, Japan, Switzerland, Sweden, Belgium, the Netherlands, and the US itself.

Why it matters: strong US demand keeps pressure on global yields, which Europe pays without the same growth.

Status: US strong, Europe firm but slow

Trend: PMIs improving

Change in trend: US payrolls softening

Skew: downside for Europe, through rates

  • Goldman Sachs, 2 to 8 Oct: GDP tracking 3.1%, payrolls +29,000, global PMI 54.3
  • Goldman Sachs, European Economics Analyst, 5 Oct: AI adds about 0.1 points to European growth

Leadership trust and geopolitics

R. Marshall (2 Oct) reports France planning a record €340 billion of bond sales in 2027 and blames growth revisions colliding with "the French desire to spend more". Goldman Sachs (4 Oct) says France needs "a fiscal rather than an ECB [European Central Bank] solution". Goldman's Mark (2 Oct) judges Europe more vulnerable than the US: complex decision-making, French elections next year and leadership that "looks less obvious". Goldman (5 Oct) adds Italy's revised deficit and Spain's snap election.

DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) reports a "sharp re-escalation" of Middle East risk; Wood & Company (8 Oct) counters that more oil is passing the Strait of Hormuz. SemiAnalysis (8 Oct) judges China's AI policy "speed-based, not safety-based" after the Trump-Xi summit opened a US-China AI dialogue.

Why it matters: fiscal doubt in France raises the risk premium on all European assets.

Status: French credibility at its weakest since 2011 to 2012

Trend: deteriorating, spreading to Italy

Change in trend: yes, sharply since late September

Skew: downside until a fiscal response

  • R. Marshall, 2 Oct: record French issuance, 152 basis point spread
  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: Middle East re-escalation

What no source spoke to this week

  • Consumer surveys outside the US
  • Japanese, Canadian or UK inflation
  • Bank of England and Bank of Japan credibility
  • Ukraine, beyond a preview title (Comment is Freed, "Operation Vivaldi", 4 Oct)
Report 2
G10 and European macroeconomic outputs (source #2)

Contents: Executive summary; The discount rate: policy rates and bond yields; Credit spreads and commodities; Risk appetite and the diagnostic questions; What no source spoke to this week

Executive summary
  • The read: the discount rate is still rising, led by long-end real yields and European sovereign spreads, and the stress has moved from rates into credit, but equities sit near their highs
  • Strongest view: Goldman Sachs's Macro Roadmap (2 Oct) calls the rising cost of capital during an artificial intelligence (AI) capital spending cycle "the structural story", with Europe paying the price without the growth
  • Sharpest disagreement: Goldman's Mark (2 Oct) sees US yields at "near-term exhaustion" and hedge funds already defensive; Alpine Macro (8 Oct) warns "a shakeout in stocks could happen at any time"
  • What changed: Goldman Sachs (5 Oct) says the widening broadened from rates into credit, with one of its credit indicator's sharpest weekly falls since 2000
  • Watch: whether the European Central Bank (ECB) leans against French spreads

The discount rate: policy rates and bond yields

Goldman Sachs expects one more Federal Reserve (Fed) hike in December, to 4.00-4.25%, then cuts from late 2027 (8 Oct), while Governor Waller wants two more and Dallas Fed President Logan "50 basis points or more" (4 Oct). Goldman (5 Oct) sees the US 10-year yield easing from 5.3% to 4.4% by end-2027. For the ECB, Goldman (4 Oct) keeps a third and last hike in December, arguing euro area financial conditions have tightened by the equivalent of one 25 basis point hike. The Macro Roadmap (2 Oct) notes 30-year real yields up about 70 basis points this year to above 3%, and says "rallies stay shallow" unless the AI outlook or the macro shifts. R. Marshall (Pacific Asset Management internal note, 2 Oct) calls it a regime shift to a higher neutral rate and term premium.

Why it matters: a higher, stickier discount rate weighs most on long-duration European growth shares.

Status: US 10-year near 5.33% (DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct), multi-decade highs

Trend: rising

Change in trend: possible US exhaustion, per Mark; none in Europe

Skew: upside for yields, per the Macro Roadmap

  • Goldman Sachs, 4 to 8 Oct: Fed and ECB December hikes
  • Goldman Sachs, Macro Roadmap, 2 Oct: cost of capital structural

Credit spreads and commodities

Goldman Sachs's GOAL Kickstart (5 Oct) says spread widening was sharpest in French sovereign debt but broad across corporate and emerging market credit, and that credit stress has spilled into European, not US, equities. Mark (2 Oct) records high yield's largest one-day fall in 18 months. Goldman stays underweight euro investment grade and high yield credit over 12 months.

DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) puts Brent near $104 after Middle East re-escalation. Wood & Company (8 Oct) notes oil above $100 and record refining margins, yet asks whether the worst has passed as Gulf flows recover. Bisoninvest's "Buy Oil" (7 Oct) arrived as a preview only.

Why it matters: European equities, not US ones, now trade with credit, and energy costs hit European margins first.

Status: spreads widening, oil above $100

Trend: wider; energy firm

Change in trend: credit joined rates this week

Skew: wider spreads until a policy response, per Goldman

  • Goldman Sachs, GOAL Kickstart, 5 Oct: broad credit stress
  • Wood & Company, 8 Oct: record refining margins

Risk appetite and the diagnostic questions

  • Are equities ignoring the discount rate: yes, says the Macro Roadmap (2 Oct): a near two standard deviation move in long rates with equities "a whisker off the highs", credited to the AI story; Mark (2 Oct) says that threshold has now been hit
  • Cracks underneath: credit is the crack; Daily Chartbook (3 Oct) finds rate-sensitive sectors the most oversold in five years
  • Defensives outperforming: no source spoke to this
  • Large over small: DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) found size "a non-event" in the Nordics
  • Momentum spreads: no source spoke to this
  • Cash yield over growth: DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) says value led and growth lagged as rates compressed long-duration multiples
  • Small-cap growth against large-cap yield: Russell 2000 short interest is at the 99.8th percentile (JPMorgan, via Daily Chartbook, 3 Oct)
  • Is this 2007 into 2008: no source drew that parallel; vperception (6 Oct) sees "an equity wobble" but "no immediate recession"
  • Modal case: Goldman (5 Oct) still expects equities up slightly over 10% by end-2027, though stable rates could limit upside
  • Cone of uncertainty: wider, per Alpine Macro (8 Oct)
  • Skew: vperception (6 Oct) sees "lower margin of safety"; the Macro Roadmap sees "a rip higher" if the macro calms

Goldman (5 Oct) is overweight equities over twelve months; Mark notes hedge fund net exposure in the 5th percentile.

Status: risk-on but fragile

Trend: weakening breadth, per vperception

Change in trend: credit stress this week

Skew: two-way, with downside from rates

  • Goldman Sachs, Macro Roadmap and Mark, 2 Oct: equities resilient
  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: value over growth
  • vperception, 6 Oct: lower margin of safety

What no source spoke to this week

  • Gold, copper and industrial metals beyond web headlines
  • Momentum and defensive factor performance
  • Japanese, UK and Swiss bond yields
Report 3
Stock-market and business/demand-level thematic narratives and developments (source #3)

Contents: Executive summary; Artificial intelligence: spending, broadening and its sceptics; Energy and the Middle East; Defence, robotics and the physical build-out; What no source spoke to this week

Executive summary
  • The read: artificial intelligence (AI) spending still drives demand and leadership, and the debate has moved from whether it lasts to who pays for it through higher rates
  • Strongest view: Goldman Sachs's Mark (2 Oct) says leadership is broadening from AI capital spending beneficiaries to companies that enable AI use, and that narrowness is "a feature (not a bug)" of the next move higher
  • Sharpest disagreement: Alpine Macro (8 Oct) and vperception (6 Oct) warn of extreme bifurcation and weakening breadth, citing dot-com era analogies
  • What changed: Goldman Sachs (5 Oct) found the first measurable AI spillover into European growth, about 0.1 points
  • Watch: third-quarter results, which Goldman's GOAL Kickstart (5 Oct) names as possible support for risk appetite

Artificial intelligence: spending, broadening and its sceptics

Goldman Sachs is the most constructive house. Its European economists (5 Oct) estimate global AI investment above $1 trillion this year, with data centre hosts such as Scandinavia and Iberia, and supply chain economies such as Germany, gaining most. Its emerging market strategists (8 Oct) argue concentration in the "AI trio" of TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix reflects "exceptional profit growth, rather than speculative exuberance". Jefferies (2 Oct) sees AI optical transceiver demand near 180 million units in 2027 and AI agents lifting central processing unit (CPU) demand, though its three regional sector heads "did not hide their disagreements" on the AI trade. DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) reports TSMC revenue up 51% year on year.

The sceptics: Alpine Macro (8 Oct) says "a shakeout in stocks could happen at any time"; vperception (6 Oct) watches the Cisco and Juniper analogies. Goldman's Macro Roadmap (2 Oct) names an AI capital spending slowdown, through power bottlenecks or doubts about return on investment, as the main catalyst for a reversal. SemiAnalysis (8 Oct) judges China's AI regime "speed-first", so competition will not pause.

Why it matters: Europe owns the supply chain, not the platforms, so European exposure rides capital spending and suffers first if it slows.

Status: AI spending accelerating

Trend: broadening to AI use

Change in trend: none

Skew: downside from concentration and rates, per the sceptics

  • Goldman Sachs, European Economics Analyst, 5 Oct: AI adds 0.05 to 0.15 points to European growth
  • Goldman Sachs, Mark, 2 Oct: broadening to AI facilitators
  • Jefferies, 2 Oct: optical and CPU demand
  • Alpine Macro, 8 Oct: shakeout risk

Energy and the Middle East

Daily Chartbook (3 Oct, citing Rob Anderson) finds "the Energy sector has never mattered more for stock market returns": the S&P 500 lost 5.5% on energy up days over the past year. Wood & Company (8 Oct) sees record refining margins, helped by rumours of a US diesel export ban, but notes Gulf production "roaring back". DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) reports value leading on oil-linked Norwegian names as Brent reached about $104. Bisoninvest's "Buy Oil" (7 Oct) was a preview only.

Why it matters: energy is the hedge inside a European book against the shock that hurts everything else.

Status: oil above $100, refining margins at records

Trend: firm

Change in trend: renewed escalation on 8 Oct

Skew: two-way: escalation against recovering flows

  • Daily Chartbook, 3 Oct: equities inversely tied to energy
  • Wood & Company, 8 Oct: refining strength, Gulf recovery

Defence, robotics and the physical build-out

Barclays (6 Oct), after its first European Robotics, Defence and Automation Forum, frames robotics, defence and automation as "converging" into one AI-led theme, possibly "the decade of the robot". DNB Carnegie (6 Oct) calls defence demand "stronger for longer", driven by air defence, radar and missiles. DNB Carnegie also reports data centres at about 30% of one Nordic engine maker's order backlog (6 Oct), a strong mining capital spending cycle (8 Oct), and China building inland data centres on cheap power (8 Oct). No source was sceptical of defence this week.

Why it matters: these are the channels through which European industrials capture global AI and security spending.

Status: strong order books

Trend: rising

Change in trend: none

Skew: upside on orders, downside on rates for construction-linked demand

  • Barclays, 6 Oct: robotics and defence convergence
  • DNB Carnegie, 6 and 8 Oct: defence, data centres, mining

What no source spoke to this week

  • Re-shoring
  • The energy transition and renewables
  • Consumer-facing themes such as luxury demand

Industry-level commentary, developments and news (source #4)

Contents: Executive summary; Rate-sensitive industries against energy; Banks and financials; Industrials and consumer staples; What no source spoke to this week

Executive summary
  • The read: rates set the industry ranking this week; energy led, while real estate, technology and financials lagged as government bond stress built
  • Strongest view: DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) says higher-for-longer rates are widening discount rates on real estate asset values and compressing technology multiples
  • Sharpest disagreement: Goldman Sachs (5 Oct) sees rate-sensitive industries as the cheapest way to own a rates relief, and Daily Chartbook (3 Oct) finds them the most oversold in five years
  • What changed: financials, a key European gainer this year, lagged as sovereign stress rose (DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct)
  • Watch: third-quarter results across Nordic banks and industrials

Rate-sensitive industries against energy

DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) reports real estate and technology as the heaviest Nordic sectors, with Oslo outperforming on its energy weight. Its reasoning: the United States (US) 10-year yield near multi-decade highs widens discount rates on net asset values and compresses multiples on long-duration earnings. Goldman Sachs's GOAL Kickstart (5 Oct) takes the other side of the trade: it names calls on real estate, utilities and homebuilders as attractive exposure to rates relief. Daily Chartbook (3 Oct, citing Real Investment Advice) finds rate-sensitive sectors the most oversold in five years. Daily Chartbook also notes US equities have moved inversely to energy over the past year.

Why it matters: a European book long duration-sensitive industries is exposed to the same rate move that is lifting energy.

Status: energy leading, real estate and technology lagging

Trend: rate-driven rotation

Change in trend: none

Skew: upside for rate-sensitive industries if rates ease, per Goldman

  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: real estate and technology hit by rates
  • Goldman Sachs, GOAL Kickstart, 5 Oct: rate-sensitive industries as relief exposure
  • Daily Chartbook, 3 Oct: most oversold in five years

Banks and financials

DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) relays Bank of America's expectation that Nordic bank third-quarter earnings per share will be about 1% above consensus on higher rates and lending, though Bank of America stays cautious on valuation. The same note says financials lagged as government bond stress built. DNB Carnegie (6 Oct) sees upside from interest rates for Nordic online brokers. Norne Securities (via AlphaSense, 8 Oct) flags artificial intelligence agents lowering switching barriers for insurers.

Why it matters: banks have been a European leader this year; the question is whether sovereign stress now outweighs the earnings benefit of higher rates.

Status: earnings strong, shares lagging

Trend: earnings upgrades

Change in trend: share price leadership faltered

Skew: downside from sovereign stress

  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: Bank of America Nordic banks view
  • AlphaSense summary, 8 Oct: insurer switching risk

Industrials and consumer staples

DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) argues higher fuel prices and interest rates make logistics and construction customers postpone investment, citing weaker US housing starts and European construction pressure. Goldman Sachs (5 Oct) agrees that residential investment takes the heaviest hit from higher rates. Against that, DNB Carnegie sees strong capital goods demand from defence, data centres and mining (6 and 8 Oct). In consumer staples, Barclays's Laurence Whyatt (2 Oct) flags destocking clouding beverage results, and DNB Carnegie (6 Oct) reports wet Nordic weather hurting brewers.

Why it matters: industrials split between rate-hit cyclicals and order-backed capital goods; the split matters more than the industry label.

Status: mixed

Trend: construction weakening, capital goods strong

Change in trend: construction caution growing

Skew: downside for construction-linked demand

  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: construction and logistics caution
  • Goldman Sachs, 5 Oct: housing most rate-sensitive
  • Barclays, 2 Oct: beverage destocking

Sector-level commentary, developments and news (source #5)

Contents: Executive summary; Semiconductors and artificial intelligence hardware; Oil refining and energy services; Capital goods niches: defence, engines, mining equipment; What no source spoke to this week

Executive summary
  • The read: inside industries that rates hurt, sector news was strong; semiconductor demand contradicts the technology sell-off, and refining contradicts concern over oil services
  • Strongest view: Jefferies (2 Oct) sees optical and processor demand from artificial intelligence (AI) outrunning capacity, with memory supply disciplined through 2028

Semiconductors and artificial intelligence hardware

Jefferies (2 Oct) says the 1.6 terabit optical ramp is running faster than expected, AI transceiver demand nears 180 million units in 2027, and AI agents are adding to central processing unit (CPU) demand. It sees NAND memory supply disciplined through 2028, with a Chinese entrant only a long-term risk. Goldman Sachs (8 Oct) expects persistent memory tightness to support Asian earnings. DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) reports TSMC revenue up 51% year on year, reinforcing "the broader AI demand read".

The contradiction: the same DNB Carnegie note shows technology among the weakest Nordic sectors, as rates compress long-duration multiples. The sector view is strong; the industry view is rate-driven.

Why it matters: European semiconductor equipment and component makers carry the demand, but trade with the rate move.

Status: demand strong

Trend: accelerating in optical and processors

Change in trend: none

Skew: upside on demand, downside on valuation from rates

  • Jefferies, 2 Oct: optical, CPU and NAND views
  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: TSMC beat; Nordic technology weak
  • Goldman Sachs, 8 Oct: memory tightness

Oil refining and energy services

Wood & Company (8 Oct) reports refining margins "yet again breaking records" at $57.1 per barrel on its benchmark, helped by rumours of a United States (US) diesel export ban, and sees a winter gas spike favouring integrated producers. It expects normalising Gulf activity to help drillers. DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) shows the other side: one Norwegian seismic company's vessel use fell to 75% against 85% guided, suggesting contracts slipped.

Why it matters: within energy, refining and gas are the beneficiaries of disruption, while services depend on activity holding up.

Status: refining at records; services mixed

Trend: refining rising

Change in trend: none

Skew: downside for refining if product flows normalise, per Wood & Company

  • Wood & Company, 8 Oct: refining records, gas winter risk
  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: seismic weakness

Capital goods niches: defence, engines, mining equipment

DNB Carnegie calls defence "stronger for longer", with demand increasingly from air defence, surveillance, radar and missiles and book-to-bill near three times (6 Oct). It sees marine and power engines lifted by data centres, now about 30% of one maker's backlog (6 Oct), and mining equipment "exceptionally well positioned" in the mining capital spending cycle (8 Oct). The contrast is truck-mounted equipment, which DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) downgraded on weak North American demand and higher rates. Danske Bank (4 Oct) sees slower order growth ahead for one Nordic flow-technology group.

Why it matters: within industrials, order-backed niches are holding up while rate-sensitive equipment weakens.

Status: defence, engines and mining strong; mobile equipment weak

Trend: diverging

Change in trend: none

Skew: upside on orders, per DNB Carnegie

  • DNB Carnegie, 6 Oct: defence and data centre engines
  • DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct: mining equipment strong, truck equipment downgraded
  • Danske Bank, 4 Oct: slower flow-technology orders

Executive summary

  • Strongest view: Goldman Sachs's GOAL Kickstart (5 Oct) stays underweight European equities and euro credit, citing France, Italy, Spain and winter gas risk
  • Sharpest disagreement: Goldman's Mark (2 Oct) calls himself a "constructive contrarian" on European stocks, yet finds US technology "hard to resist"

Europe against the United States

Goldman's Macro Roadmap (2 Oct) asks whether Europe, growing "just ~1%", can carry a global cost of capital set by US artificial intelligence (AI) spending, and suggests Europe "might not be able to sustain these levels of rates". Goldman's GOAL Kickstart (5 Oct) finds credit stress spilling into European but not US equities. Mark (2 Oct) adds that Europe's complex decision-making and single-mandate central bank leave it more exposed to market forces. On the other side, Goldman (5 Oct) sees calls on the STOXX 600 paying off if European risk eases.

Why it matters: a European book is carrying a risk premium the US is not.

Status: Europe underperforming on risk

Trend: widening gap

Change in trend: yes, credit contagion this week

Skew: upside for Europe if spreads calm, per Goldman

  • Goldman Sachs, Macro Roadmap and Mark, 2 Oct: Europe vulnerable
  • Goldman Sachs, GOAL Kickstart, 5 Oct: underweight Europe

European sovereign bonds and the European Central Bank

Goldman Sachs (4 Oct) says French spreads are the widest since 2012 with spillover; its Macro Roadmap (2 Oct) calls the start deleveraging, with German bonds catching a flight-to-quality bid. Goldman sees the ECB's last hike in December and an October move priced at only 4 basis points. Intervention through the Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI) would need "significantly more intense and broad-based" stress; the bar is lower for "innocent bystanders" such as Spain than for France. R. Marshall (2 Oct) adds Italy's premium above 100 basis points.

Why it matters: French stress feeds into European bank and equity risk premia.

Status: French and Italian spreads at multi-year wides

Trend: widening

Change in trend: sharp acceleration in late September

Skew: wider until fiscal action, per Goldman

  • Goldman Sachs, 4 Oct: TPI bar high
  • R. Marshall, 2 Oct: French and Italian premia

The United Kingdom, the Nordics and Switzerland

Marshall (2 Oct) criticises Labour's unfunded spending pledges and its move towards rejoining the European Union; Sam Freedman's "The route back into the EU" (6 Oct) was a preview only. In the Nordics, DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) reports Oslo outperforming on energy, and Goldman Sachs (5 Oct) sees Scandinavia gaining from data centre investment. Marshall notes strong Swedish retail sales and Swiss inflation low enough to relieve the Swiss National Bank.

Asia, China and emerging markets

Its emerging market team (8 Oct) argues concentration reflects profits, not exuberance, and stays overweight Brazil, South Africa, Greece and Hungary. For China, Marshall (2 Oct) notes a first-home mortgage subsidy, and DNB Carnegie (8 Oct) reports inland data centre building.

Why it matters: Asia captures the AI earnings that Europe largely does not.

Status: Asian earnings strong

Trend: rising
