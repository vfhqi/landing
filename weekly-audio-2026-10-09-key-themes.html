Key Themes of the Middle Innings Lists in the Week of the ninth of October 2026.

This is the themes reading rather than the list reading, and it runs about fifteen minutes. It looks at the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection and this week's three lists through portfolio construction and Portfolio Fitness for Fighting, using the mix tables on industry, listing country, valuation reading and size, and it gives as much weight to what is absent as to what is there. Seven themes, then the strongest counter-case and my reply, a recap and two next actions. Nothing here is a trading decision.

The headline.

The real construction decision this week was not the ranking. It was the Pool, and the Pool is a narrower instrument than the universe it comes from. One hundred and seventy-five stocks out of one thousand three hundred and sixty-three, and that gate has already decided what Portfolio Fitness for Fighting can be chased with. Every theme below is a consequence of that, read against a target table whose only Double Overweight is energy and whose Fragility row is Double Underweight.

Theme one, and it is the one I would put first. Judgement, high conviction: the Pool, not the lists, is where your opportunity set is being set, and six of the sixteen industries in the universe have no member in it at all. Utilities, real assets, telecoms, defence, infrastructure and media are absent, and that is eighty-nine real asset names and sixty-six utilities gone before any scoring happens. What is left is concentrated: industrials are twenty percent of the Pool against twelve percent of the universe, energy nineteen percent against six, technology seventeen against eight, while consumer discretionary falls from twelve percent of the universe to two percent of the Pool and staples from five percent to one. Financials are the one large industry whose weight barely moves, seventeen percent to nineteen. So the Pool is already an industrials, energy, technology and financials machine, and when the target table asks for the energy hedge it is asking of a Pool that happens to be able to supply it, and when it asks for insurance it is asking of a Pool that holds no insurer. So, theme one: the gate does more of the construction than the ranking does, and it should be read as a portfolio decision, not plumbing.

Theme two. Judgement, high conviction: the Pool is where cheapness is not, and that single fact explains the park epidemic better than any memo does. On the valuation reading, the universe is thirty-one percent A and seventeen percent F. The Pool inverts it: forty percent F, three percent A, six names out of one hundred and seventy-five with the top letter. The eighty list places land at thirty-one percent F and two percent A, and the Ideas Bench is the one place where judgement pulled back toward the middle, thirty-five percent C, twenty-five percent B, twenty-five percent D and only two names at F. Twenty-seven of the thirty-eight listed memos say park, and they park on price, not on the gate. Those two readings are the same fact seen from two ends: the gate selects for trend and change, trend and change have been paid for, and the Investment Analyst keeps finding good businesses and full prices. So, theme two: the Pool systematically selects expensive stocks, so a memo level matters more than a rank, and the lists should be read as a trigger sheet.

Theme three, and this is the one I would act on first in construction terms. Judgement, high conviction: the Ideas Bench and your book are opposite shapes on size, in the week when Fragility is Double Underweight. Not one of the twenty Ideas Bench names has a market value below two billion euros; eleven are between two and ten billion, five between ten and fifty, four above fifty, and the median is eight and a half billion. The live holdings are the mirror image: a median of one point five billion, ten of the sixteen below two billion, one above ten billion. The whole list set reads the same way as the Bench, a median of eight point four billion and seven names below two billion out of seventy-nine. So the lists have already answered the Fragility row and the book has not, and the fourth component's own gap note says the size column is blank for all sixteen holdings, so the exposure cannot even be measured. This is yours to decide, and what I would propose is a two-part rule: a size floor as a hard limit on new Middle Innings positions while Fragility is Double Underweight, applied on market value because the percentile is unavailable, and no retrospective application to Core Investments or Early Innings Probing Bets, which were never bought on this axis. The counter I would hold alongside it is that a size floor turns the lists into a large-capitalisation screen for the no-memo stocks, and one house found size a non-event in the Nordics this week, where most of your book is listed. So, theme three: the lists are now large and the book is small, and only you can rule on whether that gap closes from the lists or from the book.

Theme four. Judgement, medium conviction: Portfolio Fitness for Fighting is visibly steering the lists, and the mix tables prove it rather than assert it. On the target sector weight letter, the universe is thirteen percent A and forty-one percent B. The list places are forty percent A and the Ideas Bench forty-five percent, nine of twenty. Your live holdings are fifty-six percent B and nineteen percent A. So the lists are built out of the rows the environment favours and the book sits in the rows it is indifferent to, which is exactly what the sixth component asks for when it keeps the tilt Overweighting Portfolio Fitness for Fighting. There is a sharper version of the same point in the holdings table. Only four of your sixteen live holdings are in the Pool at all, Arteche, Hexatronic, Sdiptech and Volex, which means twelve of the positions you own would not be in the candidate set this machine selects from today. Some of that is right by design, because the gate tests Middle Innings eligibility and your Core Investments and Early Innings Probing Bets were never required to pass it. But it does mean the lists and the book are being judged on different tests, and that the lists can only ever add a shape, never defend the one you hold. The honest qualification on the letters is that the store and this week's judgement disagree in places, and where they do the second component is the authority, which is why two names carry Thematic Fit/Momentum overrides this week. So, theme four: the lists are now an instrument of the gap rather than a ranking of quality, and the energy block is the clearest evidence.

Theme five. Judgement, medium conviction: the country axis is doing real work, and one leg of it is doing work it has not earned. France is eleven percent of the universe, five percent of the Pool and two names in eighty list places, which is the Double Underweight arriving in full. Norway goes the other way, four percent of the universe to nine percent of list places, on an Overweight earned by where energy is listed. The Netherlands is the quiet winner, four percent of the universe and twelve percent of list places with three Ideas Bench seats. Greece is the anomaly: two percent of the universe, five percent of the Pool, eight percent of list places, and every one of those names is low down the Ideas Longlist, because the Greece letter was written as a second bank weight and the Banks row has gone to Neutral. The warning on this axis is written into the mix table itself: listing country is the ticker suffix, not where revenue is earned. A country letter on one hundred and ten French stocks fits domestic banks, property and consumer names, and it does not fit a monopoly licensor on liquefied natural gas containment design or a cost-advantaged global producer, which is why Gaztransport and Technigaz is eighth on the Ideas Bench and TotalEnergies second on the Ideas Shortlist rather than both further down. So, theme five: the country letters are the sharpest tool in the table and the bluntest in their application, and the override route is the only place that gets fixed.

Theme six, which is mostly an absence. Judgement, high conviction: financials keep their weight on the lists and lose their leadership, and the row the environment actually wants cannot be bought at all. Sixteen of the eighty list places are financials, almost the Pool's own weight, but twenty of last week's top forty by score were banks against eight this week, and three banks hold Ideas Bench places on their own ratings rather than on a tilt. Seven of the thirteen banks on the Ideas Longlist carry the leaving mark, so the tape is saying what the tilt says. Beside that, insurance stays Overweight, your book holds nothing in it, and the Pool contains no insurer, so the one yield-beneficiary row still favoured by the table cannot be closed from these lists in any week. That is a structural limit, not a ranking choice, and it is the second absence after the six empty industries. So, theme six: the banks on the lists are now cases rather than a theme, and the insurance gap needs a route that does not run through the Pool.

Theme seven. Judgement, medium conviction and this is where the lists are weakest: there is nothing defensive to offer you in the week the defensive turn began. Defensives have beaten cyclicals by one point three percentage points over one week and one point one over four, the first positive readings after minus two point six over three months, so the turn is nascent. The lists have one consumer staples name, Kesko, at Ideas Longlist thirty-two on a memo finding one point seven percent a year with an equity issue ahead; three healthcare names, all of them carrying the leaving mark; one professional services name, WPP, which is a one-day technical trigger with no memo; and no consumer discretionary name at all, from four in the Pool, three of which meet a Sell Criterion. Meanwhile four of the defensives you do hold read Sell-side Momentum D or F, worth thirteen and a half points of the book. So the book's defensive leg has estimates falling and the lists cannot replace it. So, theme seven: the defensive turn is the one regime change these lists cannot express, and that is a gate problem to raise rather than a ranking to fix.

The strongest counter-case to all of this, and my reply. The counter is that I am reading a positioning washout as a regime. Hedge fund net exposure sits at the fifth percentile, small-cap short interest at the ninety-ninth point eight, and the published skew is that French spreads calming produces a rip higher led by the banks, with the rate-sensitives the cheapest exposure to relief. On that path the energy block is dear two months into its leadership, the size floor I am proposing is the most expensive rule in the book, and the Greek, Polish and Iberian banks that went down the Ideas Longlist come straight back. My reply is that none of it changes the three readings that do not depend on the regime: the Pool is empty in six industries and in insurance, it selects for expensive stocks whatever happens next, and your book is small while the lists are large. Those are construction facts, and the conditions that reverse the regime are written down, a French premium under one hundred basis points with the credit indicator recovering, or a benchmark refining margin below forty dollars.

To recap: the gate decides more than the ranking, the Pool is structurally expensive, the lists are large and the book is small, the target table is steering visibly, the country letters are sharp and bluntly applied, financials keep weight and lose leadership while insurance cannot be reached, and the defensive turn has almost no expression available.

Two next actions. Yours: rule on the size floor for new Middle Innings positions, and tell me whether the insurance gap is to be closed outside the Pool or left open. Mine: bring you a one-page read on what the Pool's sector test is doing to healthcare and consumer names, since nine healthcare candidates with leaving marks and four consumer discretionary names producing no listing look like a gate artefact rather than a judgement, and commission the memo on ASM International, one of the two Ideas Bench names with nothing on file and the first target in the commissioning order.

That is the end of the summary.
