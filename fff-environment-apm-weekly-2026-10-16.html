The regime, in one line

A late-cycle rates squeeze has become a discount-rate shock with the first credit crack inside the artificial intelligence (AI) chain: United States inflation is re-accelerating on energy, the hiking path has been pulled forward again, the US 10-year yield is about 5.1% (the highest since 2007), equities have stopped ignoring it (the US joined the sell-off, Asia is in washout, defensives are bid), and Europe is cheap and narrow, with breadth off its three-year lows but still in the bottom quarter. Risk-off, led by AI and Asia. Against last week's read (a European credit-and-sovereign stress episode centred on France), the centre of stress has moved from European sovereigns to the financing of the AI build-out and to Asian technology; the European sovereign leg was not re-read this week (no French spread, no ECB, no euro-area data), so it is carried as last read, not as improved.

Macro inputs, G10 and European: the causes

1. G10 inflation: read for the US and the UK, not the euro area. US September core consumer price index (CPI) rose 0.33% against Goldman Sachs's 0.24%, led by shelter, and the 15-Oct producer price index (PPI) was worse, with energy up 10.8% on the Iran re-escalation (Hatzius, 14-Oct and 15-Oct). The houses disagree on the cause: the Federal Reserve blames AI demand in its hawkish pivot, Goldman Sachs (13-Oct) say tariffs and energy explain 0.9 of the 1-point overshoot and AI only about 45 basis points, half of it mismeasurement, fading by 2027. Barclays (11-Oct) call the UK "a renewed cost-of-living shock", CPI 3.1% in 2026 and 3.0% in 2027. My read: above target, headline rising on energy, core sticky, with the upside near term and the fall deferred to 2027; this is the input that sets everything below. Euro-area inflation was not read.

2. G10 economic health: read for the US and the UK, with one consumer survey. Goldman Sachs (15-Oct) call the US "a healthy pace of economic activity but no sign of a surge" and expect consumer spending to slow in the fourth quarter; each 100 basis points of higher rates costs developed-market gross domestic product about 0.6%, worst where mortgages reset (Sweden, Australia, Canada), a 0.4 to 0.6 point drag on 2027 growth (Allen, 13-Oct). The one consumer reading, the University of Michigan preliminary October (via Goldman Sachs, 9-Oct): sentiment 46.3 from 48.1, current conditions 44.7, the lowest in the survey's history, expectations 47.3 up 1.0, one-year inflation expectations 4.7%. No European consumer survey was reported this week, so the consumer rows below for Europe read "not read" and are not inferred from Dunelm's miss or Entain's guidance. Growth at the level is firm, decelerating at the margin, and no house forecasts a recession (the IMF's Adrian sanguine, Zandi one in three).

3. G10 trust in fiscal and monetary leadership: read, strained, not broken. The rise in yields is a funding-competition regime: 98 of the 108 basis points of the US 10-year sell-off are real rates and "investors see ghosts around every corner", so Le Pen's 2% French deficit target was read as unachievable (Wilson, 9-Oct); Goldman Sachs GOAL (12-Oct) call euro-area sovereign stress "elevated", Wilson cites Ireland's turnaround as the counter. Barclays (11-Oct) have gilts in line with peers and Chancellor Healey's incentive to "let sleeping dogs lie". On the central banks: Goldman Sachs moved within the week from "an October Fed hike is unlikely" (12-Oct) to "October possible, December most likely" (15-Oct); a November Bank of England hike with less tightening than priced; Barclays see a November Riksbank hike and the Swiss National Bank (SNB) on hold. My read: the discount rate on every European equity is being set by fiscal credibility priced through real rates, and the one bank not hiking is the SNB. Not read: the ECB, the French spread itself, Italy.

4. Global geopolitics: headlines only, no house view. Gulf: Houthi strikes on Riyadh airport and Aramco headquarters (11-Oct, 13-Oct), Iran saying the US accepted some Hormuz conditions, an International Energy Agency release of 100 million barrels (15-Oct). Ukraine: an "energy ceasefire" announced by Trump (11-Oct), unconfirmed. China: the European Union capped Chinese hybrid imports and is assessing further quota sectors (chemicals, machinery, possibly pharmaceuticals) at Germany's urging. My read: two live conflicts feeding energy prices, the Gulf escalating and Ukraine talking de-escalation; the skew is downside through oil and gas, and the EU quota extension is a small, real tailwind for European chemicals, machinery and steel producers that no strategist has yet written on.

What would change my mind

  • Bullish, the rates-relief path. If energy stabilises (Brent under $85, gas falling as Goldman Sachs expect for 2027) and yields follow it down through Wilson's crude-to-yields link, the rate-sensitives I hold at Underweight and Double Underweight (property, homebuilders, towers, software, renewables) are where the relief rally costs most, and GOAL's three-month overweight equities is the right call. The tell: the US 10-year under 4.5% with the Fed path marked back down
  • Bearish, the general-credit path. If European corporate spreads (not read this week) widen in sympathy with the AI credits, or a bank funding event appears, the regime becomes the one Richard's 16-Sep view asked about and Fragility, Banks and Insurance all move down. The tell: any source reporting a default wave or general European spread widening, and defensives against cyclicals turning positive at three months
  • The breadth path. If European breadth keeps recovering (above 60% on the 200-day) with defensives against cyclicals rolling back under zero at four weeks, the risk-off read is wrong and the Pool's cyclical leaders (energy, logistics, diversified industrials) are the right place. Wilson already says Europe has "no bad breadth issues"; our file says the bottom quarter of three years, and I weight our file
  • The memos' pattern, read as evidence on the regime. Of 30 Stock-specific Investment Case A&J Memos this week, 2 are Congruent, 20 Mixed and 8 Fight the environment, and almost every Mixed or Fights verdict names the Fragility Double Underweight or the discount rate, not a sector tilt: the bottom-up cases that fail do so because they are small, levered, illiquid, or need a re-rating the market is refusing. Not one memo argues the environment is read too bearishly.   • The tilt scorecard, with its caveat. 38-report-data.json shows the Overweight-against-Underweight spread negative on every past table (-3.87, -0.16, -0.67, -0.38, -0.13 percentage points since 16-Sep, 18-Sep, 25-Sep, 2-Oct and 9-Oct), beside a universe up 1.06% and the STOXX up 1.36% since 9-Oct.
