APM Mini-Memo. Bufab. The ninth of October 2026.

Bufab comes to you at rank nine on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. The view is neutral, with medium conviction. This is a genuinely good business whose corporate change has already travelled the whole way into the share price, so the earnings case is sound and the return is not.

First, what the company does. Bufab is a Swedish-listed supply chain partner that sources and manages C-parts, mostly fasteners and similar low-value components, for industrial customers. Net sales in twenty twenty-five were eight thousand and seventy-two million Swedish kronor, at an adjusted margin on earnings before interest, tax and amortisation of thirteen point three percent, rising to fifteen point zero percent in the first half of twenty twenty-six on sales up six point three percent. What it actually sells is cost removal rather than parts. The component itself is fifteen to twenty percent of a customer's fastening cost and the rest is hidden administration, so Bufab takes out the other eighty-five percent and embeds itself through managed inventory that takes twelve to eighteen months to migrate.

Second, the thesis. The business is one to be bullish about, and the mechanism is fully visible. A new chief executive arrived in August twenty twenty-two, repriced on value delivered rather than cost, raised the margin target from twelve to fourteen percent in December twenty twenty-three, and sold the capital-hungry manufacturing units in twenty twenty-four and twenty twenty-five. Gross margin reached thirty-three point two percent in the second quarter, against twenty-nine point three at the end of twenty twenty-three, and drops through a flexible cost base to the adjusted margin. On the Investment Analyst's modal path, reported earnings per share go from three kronor thirty in twenty twenty-five to about five kronor thirty in twenty twenty-eight, a rate carried by the first year then settling at ten to twelve percent a year. Four things to track. Gross margin above about thirty-two and a half percent. Organic growth above about four percent into twenty twenty-seven, against five point three percent in the second quarter. The acquisition pace, modelled at three to four percent of sales a year against the company's ambition of six to eight. And cash conversion, which was fifty-eight percent and then ninety percent across the first two quarters of this year. The third-quarter report lands on the twenty-third of October.

Third, the Target Setup. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU, with Delivering, Serial Acquirer, Mechanical EPS Upgrader combining behind it. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twenty-fourth of September rated the major corporate change pattern B and the serial-acquirer pattern C, with the other four at D or F, and rated Setups' Fit and Crispness B. The match is crisp and the entry is late. That pattern pays at input and early output, and this case is already at full output. Consensus earnings per share for twenty twenty-eight are up twenty-three point nine percent in twelve months, and the shares up fifty-three percent.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twenty-fourth of September, re-formed by Watson on the first of October, rated Case Riskiness C, where A is simple and low-risk. Nine of the ten combination red flags are absent, so the risk that binds is the multiple. On unchanged earnings the price is one hundred and seven kronor at the five-year median multiple, and seventy-eight and a half at the ten-year conservative band. The one fundamental shock is currency: a nine hundred and thirty-eight million kronor unhedged dollar mismatch, where a thirty percent move removes more than a quarter of twenty twenty-five operating profit. Simplicity holds in the mechanism and fails in the perimeter, which is thirty-one countries reporting quarterly.

Fifth, business quality. High quality for its category, and short of the premium Swedish compounders on capital. Twelve consecutive quarters of gross margin expansion, an adjusted margin of fifteen percent against Bossard's ten and a half, and an operating profit every year since nineteen seventy-seven. Against all of that, return on capital employed is fourteen point three percent, against Lagercrantz at seventeen point four. Working capital is thirty-eight percent of sales and goodwill over seventy percent of net assets. The research digest treats that capital intensity as the price of the moat rather than weak operating, because Bossard's ratio is near-identical. Foundations' Robustness is B, set by the Investment Analyst as at the twenty-fourth of September.

Sixth, returns and valuation. This is where the case fails. TSR and Valuation reads F, set by the Investment Analyst on the ninth of October on the memo's modal return, rebased to the price of one hundred and thirty-nine kronor sixty. That return is about four point nine percent a year over three years and one point seven percent over eighteen months, against your D anchor of about twelve percent a year. The legs are one percent of cash, eight percent of earnings growth and minus four point six of de-rating. FactSet on the ninth of October has the shares at twenty-four point seven times twenty-four-month-forward earnings per share of five kronor sixty-four, the ninety-eighth percentile of their own history, against a five-year median near nineteen times. Even holding the multiple gives only about nine percent a year.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The direction is a pro and the shape is a con. Customers want fewer suppliers and outsourced logistics, the carbon border adjustment mechanism and the sustainability reporting directive raise barriers that favour the compliant scaled distributor, and defence and semiconductor demand is strong. Against that, the top five distributors hold only about fifteen percent of the market and Bufab about two percent globally, and over half of revenue is tied to European industrial production, where the purchasing managers' indices sit below fifty. Thematic Fit and Momentum reads B, set by the script on the ninth of October, where the memo had it pinned at C on the first of October.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. In its strongest form the counter-case is bullish. On that reading Bufab has joined the Swedish serial acquirers: a decade of seventeen percent compounding, three consecutive profit beats, and outer-year consensus revenue growth for twenty twenty-eight that is mechanically too low for an acquirer. If the deal pace doubles, the Analyst's own earnings path moves to about thirteen percent a year and the three-year return to about eighteen to twenty-two percent a year. Sell-side Momentum and Technical Momentum both read A on the ninth of October, set by the script, where the memo had each pinned differently on the first of October.

The one-line view. Neutral, with medium conviction: a genuinely good business whose corporate change has already travelled the whole way into the share price, so the earnings case is sound and the return is not.
