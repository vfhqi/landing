APM Mini-Memo. BP. The twenty-third of October 2026.

BP is new to the Ideas Bench this week at rank ten, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

My view is neutral, with medium conviction. The largest corporate-change agenda among the European majors sits on the weakest balance sheet and reserve base in its peer set, and at six hundred and thirty-eight and a half pence, on its fifty-two-week high, the dashboard's plausible return of thirteen point three per cent a year falls well short of your twenty-five per cent bar. The third-quarter print in November decides the case, not the price.

First, what the company does. BP is a vertically integrated oil and gas major, from exploration and production through trading to refining and marketing, in sixty-one countries. It is the sub-scale, most-geared European major. Production last year was two point three one million barrels of oil equivalent a day against Shell's two point seven million. The market value on the twenty-third of October was about a hundred and five billion euros, and gearing including hybrids and leases reached forty-five per cent last year, the highest among the supermajors.

Second, the thesis. This is self-help and deleveraging, with the balance sheet as the whole case. Meg O'Neill, the first external chief executive in a hundred and sixteen years, took office on the first of April with Elliott Management's five per cent stake behind her. Transition capital has been cut by three quarters, a twenty billion dollar disposal programme is under way, led by the sale of sixty-five per cent of Castrol to Stonepeak for about six billion dollars, and on the fourth of August the fourteen to eighteen billion dollar net debt target was pulled forward a full year to the end of this year. The mechanism to earnings per share is lower interest as total financial obligations fall from fifty-three point six billion dollars towards thirty-nine to forty-one billion, a cost-out raised to six and a half to seven and a half billion dollars by the end of next year with three and a half billion already achieved, and buyback reinstatement once net debt is inside target.

Fifth, business quality. Below peers on every metric the memo measured, and the Great Operator and Advantaged Business elements are not yet built. Proved reserve life of seven point one years is the lowest among the majors, and three-year organic reserve replacement was thirty-nine per cent. The real strengths are trading, which adds four points to return on capital employed, and the bpx onshore business at five hundred and forty-five thousand barrels a day, up sixteen per cent.
