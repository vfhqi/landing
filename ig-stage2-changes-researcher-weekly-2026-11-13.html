Executive summary
  • 85 stocks turned in the week of 6 November to 12 November: 16 are settled and have a note below, and 69 are unsettled and appear in the table only; 60 of the 85 were reported in this note in an earlier week, 6 of them among the 16 settled
  • The turns concentrated in Financials (22 stocks), Industrials and capital goods (14), Consumer discretionary (13) and Consumer staples (11, the highest share of any industry at 15%); Technology and Healthcare lost more stocks from the Stage 2 set than they gained in turns

Stock notes: the settled stocks only

Each of the 16 stocks below is settled: its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back, which runs from the anchor day, 12 October, to 12 November. Each stock is numbered by its row in the table near the end of this note.

For every one of the 16 stocks, five of the nine Stage 2 tests are not computed this week, which is a gap in our data and not a fail: the price above the 200-day moving average, the price above the 150-day moving average, the price within 25% of its 52-week high, and the two moving-average tests. The one gate that is computed, the 150-day moving average above the 200-day, passes for all 16. Each note therefore reports only the three relative-strength tests, which pass at 70 or above.

A turn is confirmed by holding: in our backtest a rise to Probable was worth more once the stock had stayed Probable for 20 trading days. A turn is undone when the rating falls back below Plausible or the 150-day moving average falls back below the 200-day. Our backtest did not test the moving-average cross on its own.

Each bullet opens with a marker for what its fact does to the stock's technical picture: [+], [++] and [+++] strengthen the picture by rising degrees, [-], [--] and [---] weaken the picture by rising degrees, and [=] does neither.

How to read this note

Terms

  • Stage 2: Richard's second stage of a stock's technical life, the sustained advance that follows a base. The rating is the master dashboard's own read on how firmly a stock qualifies, on a ladder that runs None, Possible, Plausible, Probable
  • A turn, and the two kinds of trigger: a stock turned when one of two triggers fired. Either the rating is Plausible or Probable now and was lower on the anchor day; or the 150-day moving average crossed from below the 200-day moving average to above it inside the look-back and is still above it now. A move up to Possible alone does not count. A stock is in this week's note when its first or latest trigger fell inside the week
  • The look-back and the anchor day: the screen compares each stock's rating on the anchor day, 12 October, with its rating on each of the dashboard's last 20 weekday builds, 13 October to 12 November. This note calls that span the look-back
  • Settled and unsettled: a stock is settled when its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back. A stock is unsettled when its rating did fall back and recover, one or more times, each time being a round trip, so the current reading of an unsettled stock may be a flicker on a threshold
  • The Stage 2 set: the stocks whose trigger inside the look-back still stands, 209 now. A stock leaves the set when its trigger no longer stands, or when the trigger is older than the look-back although the stock may still be Plausible or Probable
  • Reported here before: the stock appeared in this note in an earlier week, according to this project's record of what each week reported
  • The four Stage 2 gates: the price above the 200-day moving average; the price above the 150-day moving average; the 150-day moving average above the 200-day; and the price within 25% of its 52-week high
  • The two moving-average tests: the 50-day moving average above the 150-day and above the 200-day
  • The three relative-strength tests: the stock's industry at 70 or above in relative strength; its sector at 70 or above; and the stock itself at 70 or above against its industry. Relative strength here is the composite measure the master dashboard uses. A test the data marks as not computed is a gap in our data, not a fail
  • The nine Stage 2 tests: the four gates, the two moving-average tests and the three relative-strength tests together
  • Our tilt: our tilt on an industry or sector as set on 6 November 2026, shown with its letter, for example Neutral (C) or Double Underweight (F)

What a turn has been worth in our own backtest

  • The backtest covers triggers from January 2002 to 22 September 2026, on price returns, using the Stage 2 definition of 1 October 2026
  • A stock whose rating rose to Probable and stayed Probable for 20 trading days then beat the average stock of the same day by 1.4 percentage points over three months and 2.2 over six; a hold of 40 days did better, at 1.8 and 3.3 points
  • A rise to Probable on its first day beat the average stock by only 0.5 points over three months, and only 16% of rises to Probable stay Probable for 20 trading days
  • The gain comes more from fewer large losses than from more winners: 4.9% of the 20-day triggers ended three months down more than 20%, against 7.1% of all stocks
  • The gain is mainly a small-company effect: for companies worth EUR 5 to 50 billion the 20-day hold was worth 0.5 points over three months and 0.9 over six against stocks of the same size, which is only borderline reliable
  • A day at Plausible beat the average stock by only 0.2 points over three months, and the moving-average cross on its own was not tested
  • A turn is therefore a prompt to look, not a forecast

The group-move bar

A sector qualifies as a group move when at least 3 of its stocks triggered in the week, and a cohort when at least 2 of its stocks did, AND those triggers are at least a quarter (25%) of all that sector's or cohort's stocks in 11-universe-lookup.md (1364 stocks this week). Every trigger counts, settled or not. A cohort move whose stocks all sit inside a sector move is listed under that sector move and not on its own.
