What this picture says

  • Bullish, high conviction: Technology's 33 positive qualifiers of 105 are two different stories at two different stages, and only one of them is a turning/inflecting story
    • The twelve semiconductor qualifiers of 21 are all MI MI Pool members, with the four materials and test consumables makers four of four and the equipment makers three of five, which is a mature, broadening move that the Lists own
    • The seventeen qualifiers in software, IT services and online platforms (six of 21, six of 18 and five of 14) are almost all outside the MI Pool, with Stock Relative Strength readings between 6 and 79, which is the start of a move and not the middle of one
    • The specialists talk about the semiconductor cycle peaking in the fourth quarter of 2026 and more than 80% of the equipment makers' gain since September 2025 coming from the multiple, so the next big, long move in Technology is more likely to come from the de-rated half than from the re-rated half
  • Bullish, high conviction: Healthcare's 21 positive and 10 negative qualifiers of 114 split cleanly by business model, and the split is the signal
    • Every positive qualifier sells to a consumer, a hospital or a drug maker on volumes (hearing aids and their retail, pharmacies, medical supplies, niche medtech, consumables for drug makers, animal health, allergy and travel vaccines), while every negative one is a clinical-stage biotech, a care home operator or a research services firm waiting on a trial or a tariff
    • Healthcare distribution and retail is five of eight, the pharmacy cohort three of three and the hearing aid cohort two of two, which is as broad as a Sector turn gets at this stage
    • Sector Relative Strength readings of 20 (medtech consumables), 35 (hospitals), 44 (pharma services) and 45 (healthcare retail) mean the MI Pool is some way off for most of these stocks, and that is the point: they are turning cases precisely because the Sector has not yet been believed
  • Bullish, medium conviction: Consumer discretionary's 22 positive and 29 negative qualifiers of 166 are a rotation inside the Industry, not a recovery of all of it, and rotations of this shape run for quarters
    • Rising: sportswear and footwear (three of five), apparel retail (five of 14), food-to-go, value retail, pubs, cruise and travel, with Stock Relative Strength readings mostly below 50 and prices 15% to 40% below their highs
    • Falling: luxury (Hermes International, Salvatore Ferragamo), premium carmakers (three of four), vehicle dealers, online gambling (Entain, Flutter), homebuilders (four of six) and residential landlords
    • The market is moving from the consumer stocks that were expensive and crowded to the ones that were cheap and abandoned, which is how a cause for concern clears at group level: the cheap end gets re-rated first on nothing more than the absence of further bad news
  • Bullish, but for the Lists: Energy's 17 positive qualifiers of 76 are the most mature group on the screen, so the turning/inflecting value is at its edges
    • Thirteen of the seventeen are MI MI Pool members, the product and gas tanker cohort is three of three, the regional refiners three of three, and most have qualified since 28 August or 4 September
    • The edges are where the new money is: oilfield services and equipment (Tenaris entering the MI Pool, Saipem's Stage 2 Probing Bet, Nekkar new), storage (Vopak new), the North Sea independents (Harbour Energy entering) and the Romanian gas chain (OMV Petrom new inside the MI Pool, Transgaz and Nuclearelectrica new outside it)
    • The AlphaSense answer on energy says discretionary investor positioning in energy has only returned to neutral, the sixty-ninth percentile, after a strong run, which says the group is believed but not crowded
  • Bullish, medium conviction: Utilities' nine positive qualifiers of 64 are the generators and the networks, and the underperforming utilities are the developers, so the energy price shock is being read as a windfall for existing assets
    • PGE rose 50% relative over thirty days and Fortum 20%, both entering the MI Pool, Energa rose 47% (a print to check by eye), Enea 15%, and Iberdrola and Naturgy triggered the Stage 2 Probing Bet test on 6 and 8 October
    • Grenergy, Voltalia and Nordex are negative qualifiers, Pennon is collapsing, so the market is paying for generation and regulated returns it can already see, not for projects it cannot
    • Naturgy's digest shows the Spanish regulator raising the electricity distribution return to 6.58% for 2026 to 2031 from 5.58%, which is a regulated step-up arriving at the same time as a power price step-up, a combination the market tends to extrapolate
  • Judgement on what is absent: the market is not buying capital spending, defence or financials, and that narrows where the next bigger, longer moves can come from
    • Defence is nil of 29 positive and nine negative, financials eight of 223 positive and 25 negative, real assets one of 89, and industrials and capital goods only 13 of 166
    • Last week's grid, cable and electrical equipment group has paused: Prysmian is not a qualifier this week, Schneider Electric, Kempower and R&S Group are negative qualifiers, and only Arteche remains inside the MI Pool, so the physical build-out story is resting while the specialists still describe its contracted visibility
    • The steel makers are falling (six of 17) while Vesuvius, their consumables supplier, rose 25% relative over ten days, and the carmakers are falling while the engineering consultancies rise, so the market is separating the supplier with pricing from the price-taker, which is where the Investment Analyst's time should go
  • Where Richard's two to three Probing Bets should come from, on the Industry view: the de-rated quality names in Technology's software and services half, in consumer-facing healthcare and in the cheap end of the consumer, because those are the groups at the start of a move; Energy and semiconductors are better left to the Lists
