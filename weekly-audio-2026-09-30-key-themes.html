Key Themes of the Middle Innings Lists in the Week of the thirtieth of September 2026.

This is the key themes script for this week's lists, read from the point of view of portfolio construction and Portfolio Fitness for Fighting. It runs about fifteen minutes. It sets the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, the lists, the Ideas Bench and your live holdings against the universe, covers seven themes including what is absent, then the strongest counter-case, a recap and two next actions.

The headline. The lists pull hard toward the shape Portfolio Fitness for Fighting asks for on size and on energy, and they cannot reach it on defensives or on price. Your live holdings are small, lean to staples and industrials, and are cheaper than what you are being shown. The Ideas Bench is large, heavy in energy and technology, and expensive. The gap the target names first is now visible in the mix tables, and this month's Pool can close only part of it.

The push is plain in one number. The store's sector weight letter is A for thirteen per cent of the universe, thirty-three per cent of the Pool, forty-one per cent of all list places and seventy per cent of the Ideas Bench, against twelve per cent of your holdings. A caution on that letter: it runs on a different scale from this week's top-down table, where no row is at A, and where the two disagree the top-down table is the authority. The direction still holds: each step toward the Bench is a step further into the sectors the target favours.

Theme one, size. The judgement: size is the clearest construction gap, and the lists close it. The median market value across the universe is about three point four billion euros. The Pool's is about four point one billion, all the list places together about six point six billion, and the Ideas Bench about eight point eight billion. Your live holdings have a median of about one point five billion euros. Ten of your sixteen are below two billion, and none is above fifty billion. On the Bench, only two names are below two billion, seven sit between ten and fifty billion, and two are above fifty billion.

That is Gap One in the top-down call: Fragility at Double Underweight, the book needing to be large, simple and proven, and a tape that has started to punish small caps and growth. Until now the gap could not be measured, because the holdings were tickers only. The size column now measures one part of it, and it says the book is where the target says it should not be.

The counter: for stocks without case ratings the fragility reading is size alone, and large caps were where the twenty twenty-two break finally landed once small caps had already fallen. A book that is merely large is not thereby fit.

The construction rule is yours to decide. The top-down call proposes a minimum size for new positions while Fragility sits at Double Underweight, and a review of any holding below it. I would propose the floor apply to new Core Investments only, with smaller names still eligible as Early Innings Probing Bets, and with the review of existing holdings being a review, not a sale list. That keeps the process able to find businesses while they are small.

So, theme one: your book is small and the Bench is large, and that is the first gap the target names.

Theme two, energy. The judgement: energy is the lists' largest bet and their least researched. Energy, commodities and metals mining is five per cent of the universe, twenty per cent of the Pool, twenty-six per cent of the list places and thirty-five per cent of the Ideas Bench, seven of the twenty names. Twenty-one of the Pool's twenty-three energy and commodity names made the lists. Your live holdings contain none.

This is the lists acting on Gap Two: the book under-exposed to yield-and-inflation beneficiaries, with oil above a hundred dollars and Energy held at Overweight. But not one of the seven energy names on the Bench has a memo. Galp, MOL, Var Energi and the miners on the Ideas Shortlist are marked Thin, which means the score is the sector tilt and nothing read about the company.

The counter is the one the top-down call wrote itself: the skew on oil is two-sided, and a phased Hormuz deal takes twenty-five dollars off in one move. Energy is also among the momentum leaders that are first casualties if the trigger arrives.

The rule here is yours. I would propose that no energy name moves from commission to capital until its memo fills the three blank ratings, and that the order of commissions start with Galp and TotalEnergies, the largest and simplest of the shape.

So, theme two: the lists close Gap Two through energy, on the tilt, and not yet on research.

Theme three, price. The judgement: the Pool hands you uptrends at full prices, so valuation cannot separate candidates this month, and entry discipline will have to do that work. Across the universe the valuation reading is spread evenly: F, D and C at twenty-three per cent each, B at fourteen and A at nine. In the Pool, F is forty-one per cent and D thirty-three per cent, and only three of one hundred and fourteen stocks read A or B. On the Ideas Bench nine names read F, eight read D and three read C; there is no A or B at all. Your live holdings read A or B for a quarter of the book and F for twelve per cent.

So the Bench is markedly more expensive than what you already own. That is what a relative strength filter produces. The counter is that momentum persists for longer than valuation predicts, and a twelve-month uptrend at F is not by itself a warning. My reading is narrower: when every candidate is expensive, the place to protect price is the size and timing of entry, not the choice of name.

So, theme three: everything on the Bench is priced for its uptrend, and your book is cheaper than the names it is being shown.

Theme four, what is absent: the defensives. The judgement: the defensive half of Gap Two cannot be closed from this month's Pool, and the staples you already hold are doing that job. Utilities, real assets, telecoms, defence, media and infrastructure together make up about eighteen per cent of the universe and none of the Pool. Consumer staples has one Pool name, Inter Cars, an automotive parts distributor. There is no Pharma name. There is one insurer, Beazley, at Shortlist eight. The nearest thing to a hospitals name is Attendo, a care home operator at Longlist thirty-one.

The top-down call holds Staples, Pharma, Hospitals, Insurance, Banks, Energy and Grid at Overweight. Of those, the Pool supplies energy, grid and banks in depth, and almost nothing else. Last week's defensive large names, National Grid, Danone and Axfood among them, left with the Pool.

Some absences are the target agreeing with the tape. Consumer discretionary is twelve per cent of the universe, two names in the Pool and none on the lists, and the target holds it at Double Underweight. Software for businesses is at F, and every one of those names was left out. Real assets and homebuilders are at F and absent. Those are not gaps.

Your live holdings are a quarter consumer staples, four names, which is where the defensive exposure the target wants already sits. The counter: staples' relative strength reads D against a target of B, so the market is not yet paying for defence, and a momentum Pool will keep excluding it until it does.

So, theme four: the Pool cannot supply the defensives the target asks for, and the book's own staples are carrying that part of the shape.

Theme five, two benches. The judgement: conviction lives in industrials and technology, the top-down push lives in energy, and the two have not yet met. The ten memo-backed names on the Bench are five industrials, Cenergy, Diploma, Renishaw, Instalco and Bodycote; four technology names, ASML, VAT Group, Technoprobe and AT and S; and one healthcare name, Demant. All six rare grade sets in the Pool sit in industrials and technology. The ten commissions are seven energy names, two materials names and Maersk.

The top-down call is cautious exactly where the research is strongest. Semiconductors are at Neutral with a tripwire to Underweight. Factory equipment is at Underweight, which bears on Renishaw and Bodycote. Only grid, Cenergy's sector, is an Overweight. Your holdings are thirty-one per cent industrials and hold no technology at all, so the technology half of the Bench is new ground for the book.

The counter: if the semiconductor tripwire fires, three memo-backed Bench names, ASML, VAT Group and Technoprobe, weaken together. That is a correlation risk to take into account when more than one of them is pursued.

So, theme five: the memos point to industrials and technology, the top-down call points to energy, and the Bench holds both without reconciling them.

Theme six, banks. The judgement: banks are an Overweight with no stock-level evidence, and the lists are right to hold them in a queue rather than on the Bench. Financials are seventeen per cent of the universe, nine per cent of the Pool, eleven per cent of the list places, nine names, and none of the Ideas Bench. Eight of the nine are banks, every one without a memo and with four of six ratings blank. The top-down call holds Banks at Overweight on the net interest margin leg, but its own tripwire, credit spreads widening, cannot be tested this week, because no spread index was read. It also names a new leg: euro-area banks holding French government bonds carry the French sovereign risk. Your own question, whether this is two thousand and seven into two thousand and eight, points at rate-sensitive financials as where a break starts. Your book holds one financial, Avanza.

So, theme six: banks are a top-down Overweight that the evidence cannot yet support at the level of any single bank.

Theme seven, geography. The judgement: the France Underweight costs the lists little, the book is concentrated in three markets, and the Bench spreads toward Europe's periphery and its oil exporters. France is eleven per cent of the universe and five per cent of the Pool, and it has four list places and two Bench names, TotalEnergies and Gaztransport and Technigaz. None of your holdings is French-listed.

Your book is three quarters in Britain, Sweden and the Netherlands: thirty-one, twenty-five and nineteen per cent. The Bench is spread across thirteen listing countries, with Britain at three names and Greece, Sweden, Norway, France and Denmark at two each, plus single names from Hungary, Portugal and others. Two caveats. A listing suffix is not where revenue is earned, so the French question is about French-domestic earnings, which the suffix cannot show. And the top-down call did not read the Nordics or Switzerland this week, so no tilt either way rests on evidence there.

The construction proposal in the top-down call is to measure French-domestic exposure by revenue each week until the October catalyst passes, with no limit until it has been measured once. I agree, and that decision is yours.

So, theme seven: France matters less to these lists than to the target, and the book's real geographic fact is its concentration in three markets.

Themes I tested and dropped: healthcare, because the Pool's seven names include no Pharma and no source spoke to it; and transportation, because only Maersk reaches the Bench.

The strongest counter-case to my overall reading. My reading treats the lists as the place to close Gaps One and Two. The case against: the Pool is a relative strength filter, so everything it supplies is a recent winner. Building toward its shape means buying the momentum leaders the top-down call names as first casualties, at F and D prices, in large caps, just as the tape is starting to turn. If hiking pauses, the long-duration names rally hardest and the yield beneficiaries give back their gains, and a book rebuilt from this Pool would be positioned for the regime just after it ends.

My reply: that is why the lists are research priorities and not trades. The construction answer is to research the energy half before owning any of it, to use the size rule for new Core Investments only, to let the book's own staples carry the defensive half, and to protect price through entry rather than selection. The gap the target names is real, and the mix tables now show it. The Pool is a source of candidates for closing it, not a template for the book.

The recap. Your book is small and the Bench is large. Energy is the lists' largest bet and their least researched. Everything on the Bench is priced for its uptrend. The defensives the target asks for are absent from the Pool and present in your staples. The memo-backed conviction sits in industrials and technology, where the top-down call is cautious. Banks are an untested Overweight. France matters less than the book's concentration in three markets.

Two next actions. For you: decide the size rule, whether a minimum size applies to new Core Investments while Fragility stays at Double Underweight, and whether any energy exposure waits for memos. For me, Watson: have the live holdings carry industry, sector, size and case ratings every week, so that the book can be set against the Bench on every theme here and the gaps measured rather than named.

That is the end of the summary.
