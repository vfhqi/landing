Regime in one line

Late-cycle rates squeeze turning into a European credit-and-sovereign stress episode: the crack is in credit and France, breadth has broken, the index has started to follow, defensives are turning but unconfirmed, and growth is still firm at the level.

Macro inputs, G10 and European: the causes

1. G10 inflation. Above target in the United States and the Federal Reserve's tone has turned more hawkish: August core personal consumption expenditures inflation of 3.0 percent, 0.7 points above Goldman Sachs's forecast, traced to artificial-intelligence-driven electronics prices, jet fuel from the war with Iran and tariffs (Goldman Sachs US Daily, 7 Oct). Goldman judges three quarters of it one-off or mismeasured; Governor Waller (8 Oct) reads the same forces as persistent and the September Federal Open Market Committee minutes show "almost all" participants seeing upside risk. Swiss core inflation of 0.5 percent was "a relief" to the Swiss National Bank (R. Marshall, 2 Oct). I weight Waller over Goldman for a twelve-month European book: whether or not the overshoot is one-off, the policy reaction is what sets the discount rate, and the reaction has hardened. Japanese, Canadian and United Kingdom inflation were not read.

2. G10 economic health and consumer surveys. Strong in the United States and firm but slow in Europe: gross domestic product tracking 3.1 percent, the lowest four-week average of jobless claims since September 2022, services purchasing managers' index at 58.8, against payrolls of plus 29,000 and unemployment of 4.2 percent (Goldman Sachs, 2 to 8 Oct). Euro area purchasing managers' indices rose to 52.9 in manufacturing and 53.0 in services (5 Oct), while Goldman's Macro Roadmap (2 Oct) still has European growth at "just ~1%" and global artificial intelligence spending adding about 0.1 points to it. Consumer sentiment: the only reading is the University of Michigan at 48.1 for September with Goldman expecting 47.0 for October and five-to-ten-year inflation expectations steady at 3.4 percent (4 Oct); no European, United Kingdom, Nordic or Swiss consumer survey appeared, so the consumer rows below are United States-only and the European consumer is not read this week. Swedish retail sales were strong (Marshall, 2 Oct), which is spending, not sentiment, and is not used for the consumer row.

3. G10 trust in fiscal and monetary leadership. This is the input that worsened most and it is European. France's 10-year premium over Germany at 152 basis points, the widest since 2011, a record EUR 340 billion of 2027 bond sales, "the French desire to spend more" colliding with growth revisions (Marshall, 2 Oct); Goldman Sachs (4 Oct) says France needs "a fiscal rather than an ECB solution" and that Transmission Protection Instrument intervention needs "significantly more intense and broad-based" stress, with a lower bar for "innocent bystanders" such as Spain than for France. Italy's premium is above 100 basis points with a revised deficit, Spain has a snap election, and Goldman's Mark (2 Oct) judges Europe more vulnerable than the United States on complex decision-making, French elections next year and leadership that "looks less obvious". The United Kingdom carries fiscal doubt from one source (Marshall on unfunded Labour pledges). Bank of England and Bank of Japan credibility were not read. Status: French credibility at its weakest since 2011 to 2012, deteriorating and spreading to Italy.

4. Global geopolitics. The Middle East re-escalated sharply (DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct) and Brent is near 104 dollars, against Wood & Company (8 Oct) reporting more oil passing the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf production "roaring back". China's artificial intelligence policy is "speed-based, not safety-based" after the Trump-Xi summit opened a United States-China artificial intelligence dialogue (SemiAnalysis, 8 Oct). Ukraine was not read beyond a preview title. Skew: two-way on oil, which is why the energy tilt rises one notch at the refining end and not at the producer end.

Thematics, and risk-on-ness

  • Artificial intelligence: spending accelerating, leadership broadening to companies that enable artificial intelligence use, and the debate is now who pays for it through higher rates. Global investment above 1 trillion dollars this year, with Scandinavia and Iberia as data centre hosts and Germany in the supply chain (Goldman, 5 Oct); TSMC revenue up 51 percent year on year; Jefferies (2 Oct) sees optical transceiver demand near 180 million units in 2027 and NAND supply disciplined through 2028. Sceptics: Alpine Macro and vperception on bifurcation and dot-com analogies; the Macro Roadmap names a capital-spending slowdown as the main reversal catalyst. For Europe the thematics report's own line holds: Europe owns the supply chain, not the platforms, so it rides capital spending and suffers first if it slows
  • Energy and the Middle East: the hedge inside a European book. The S&P 500 lost 5.5 percent on energy up days over the past year (Daily Chartbook citing Rob Anderson); refining at records; Oslo outperforming on its energy weight. The turning note's fourth hypothesis extends this to the second tier (subsea vessels at 85 to 90 percent utilisation, ageing tankers, generators owning existing capacity), which shaped the Energy services row below
  • Defence, robotics and automation: converging into one artificial-intelligence-led theme (Barclays, 6 Oct), demand "stronger for longer" with book-to-bill near three times (DNB Carnegie, 6 Oct), and no source sceptical. Against that, the turning note's falling side shows defence electronics derating from a high (Theon minus 16 points relative over 30 days, Renk minus 26, Rheinmetall minus 16) on ceasefire talk and budget timing. Orders up, shares down: the Defence rows stay Neutral as a judgement, as last week
  • The turning note's other hypotheses, weighed as one input each: the artificial-intelligence de-rating clearing from the top (information providers, classifieds, software, consultancies), consumer-paid healthcare turning from the bottom (hearing aids, pharmacies, niche medtech), the de-rated consumer rotating from expensive to cheap, and the chip restock reaching manufacturers and distributors. The note contradicts itself on classifieds (Rightmove turning, Hemnet and Vend collapsing), which is a reason to hold that row where it is. None of these moves a tilt alone; where a hypothesis coincided with a relative strength change and a source, it is cited on the row
  • Industries and sectors from the sources: rates set the ranking (energy led; real estate, technology and financials lagged); banks' earnings strong (Bank of America expects Nordic bank third-quarter earnings per share about 1 percent above consensus) but shares lagging as sovereign stress built; construction and logistics customers postponing investment (DNB Carnegie, 8 Oct), residential investment hit hardest by rates (Goldman, 5 Oct); capital goods niches strong (defence, data-centre engines at about 30 percent of one maker's backlog, mining equipment), truck-mounted equipment downgraded; beverage destocking (Barclays, 2 Oct) and wet Nordic weather for brewers (DNB Carnegie, 6 Oct)
