How to read this note

Stage 2. Richard's second stage of a stock's technical life: the sustained advance that follows a base. The rating is the master dashboard's own read on how firmly a stock qualifies. The ladder runs None, Possible, Plausible, Probable.

The two kinds of trigger. Either the rating is Plausible or Probable now and was lower on the anchor day, or the 150-day moving average crossed from below the 200-day moving average to above it inside the look-back and is still above it now. A move up to Possible alone does not count (Richard's ruling of 10 August 2026). A stock is in this week's note when its first or latest trigger fell inside the week.

The look-back and the anchor day. The screen compares each stock's rating on the anchor day, 28 September, with its rating on each of the dashboard's last 20 weekday builds, 29 September to 29 October. This note calls that span the look-back.

Settled and unsettled. A stock is settled when its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back; unsettled when it did, one or more times. Each time is a round trip, so an unsettled stock's current reading may be a flicker on a threshold.

The Stage 2 set. The stocks whose trigger inside the look-back still stands: 259 now. A stock leaves the set when its trigger no longer stands, or when the trigger is older than the look-back although the stock may still be Plausible or Probable.

Reported here before. The stock appeared in this note in an earlier week, from this project's record of what each week reported.

The nine Stage 2 tests. The four gates: the price above the 200-day moving average; the price above the 150-day moving average; the 150-day above the 200-day; and the price within 25% of its 52-week high. The two moving-average tests: the 50-day moving average above the 150-day and above the 200-day. The three relative-strength tests: the stock's industry at 70 or above in relative strength; its sector at 70 or above; and the stock itself at 70 or above against its industry. Relative strength here is the composite measure the master dashboard uses. A test the data marks as not computed is a gap in our data, not a fail.

Our tilt. The fund's tilt on each industry and sector, as set on 23 October 2026, graded from Overweight (B) through Neutral (C) and Underweight (D) to Double Underweight (F).

The signpost markers. Each bullet in a stock note opens with a marker from [+++] to [---] showing whether that fact strengthens or weakens the stock's technical picture, and [=] where it does neither.

What a turn has been worth in our own backtest. From the project "APM - Data Analytics - Backtesting" (the answer of 2 October 2026 and its addendum of 3 October 2026, which wins where they differ; triggers from January 2002 to 22 September 2026, price returns, the Stage 2 definition of 1 October 2026). A stock whose rating rose to Probable and stayed Probable for 20 trading days then beat the average stock of the same day by 1.4 percentage points over three months and 2.2 over six. A rise to Probable on its first day beat it by only 0.5 points over three months, and only 16% of rises to Probable stay Probable for 20 trading days; longer holds did better (40 days: 1.8 and 3.3 points). The gain comes more from fewer large losses than from more winners: 4.9% of the 20-day triggers ended three months down more than 20%, against 7.1% of all stocks. It is mainly a small-company effect: for companies worth EUR 5 to 50 billion it was 0.5 points over three months and 0.9 over six against stocks of the same size, only borderline reliable. A day at Plausible beat the average stock by only 0.2 points over three months, and the moving-average cross on its own was not tested. So a turn is a prompt to look, not a forecast.

The group-move bar. A sector qualifies as a group move when at least 3 of its stocks triggered in the week, and a cohort when at least 2 of its stocks did, AND those triggers are at least a quarter (25%) of all that sector's or cohort's stocks in 11-universe-lookup.md (1363 stocks this week). Every trigger counts, settled or not. Where a cohort's triggered stocks are exactly a sector's triggered stocks, the two are one group, reported once under the cohort. The rule is Richard's of 28 October 2026: 3 for a sector and 2 for a cohort, because cohorts are smaller. A cohort's code, such as 01.008, is its identifier in our universe database.
