APM Mini-Memo. Subsea 7. The first of October 2026.

Subsea Seven is new this week at rank eight on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

My view is neutral, as a working hypothesis. This is a best-in-class offshore contractor delivering record margins on unusually full order books, but the shares you would own from late this year are a nil-premium merger with lower-margin Saipem, arriving near what may be the top of the offshore margin cycle. Earnings per share beyond twenty twenty-six are less predictable than the standalone numbers suggest.

First, what the company does. Subsea Seven designs, builds and installs the seabed pipework that connects offshore oil and gas wells to production facilities, using its own fleet of thirty-five vessels. The deepwater subsea business is eighty-two per cent of revenue, and offshore wind installation, under the Seaway Seven brand, is about sixteen per cent. Most work is fixed-price, so Subsea Seven carries cost overruns. Brazil is twenty-four per cent of revenue and over thirty per cent of the order book. Revenue last year was seven point one billion dollars, and the market value is about eight point six billion euros at three hundred and twenty-five kroner.

Second, the thesis. Contracts won at higher prices since twenty twenty-four are replacing old low-margin work, in a market with only twenty high-specification pipelay vessels across nine operators and none being built. Cash profit, meaning earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, rose thirty-seven per cent last year to one point four nine billion dollars, a twenty-one per cent margin against sixteen per cent the year before. Management has raised this year's margin guidance twice, from twenty-two to about twenty-four per cent, and has beaten its initial guidance in each of the last two years. The route to earnings per share is margin, not volume. Revenue guidance is flat at seven to seven point four billion dollars, so on my arithmetic a twenty-four per cent margin lifts cash profit by about sixteen per cent this year. After that, the merger with Saipem, owned fifty-fifty and targeting three hundred million euros of annual synergies by twenty twenty-nine, replaces the standalone earnings stream. The things to track are the European Commission decision, due by the twenty-sixth of November, fleet utilisation, which was eighty-seven per cent in the second quarter, and order book coverage for the next two years, now seventy-two and thirty-eight per cent.

Third, the Target Setup. There is no Investment Analyst memo, so there are no Bottom-up, Stock-driven ratings, and this read is mine. The Target Setup is Demand-driven EPSU: the offshore spending upcycle and tight vessel supply lift pricing, and the better-priced order book lifts margins as it is executed. Once the merger closes, Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU could combine with it through synergies, but until the regulators clear the deal that leg is a risk rather than a driver. It is not a reversion-to-normal setup. There is no cause for concern to clear, margins are at records, and the shares sit about nine per cent below their fifty-two-week high.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. No memo rating exists, so again this is my read: near-term delivery is low-risk, but the case is not simple. The order book covers ninety-seven per cent of this year's revenue, and management habitually under-promises. Against that, fixed-price projects book most of their profit in the final fifth of the work, so any delay defers earnings. The merger faces in-depth reviews in Europe and Australia, with a right to walk away if required disposals exceed five hundred million euros.

Fifth, business quality. Strong for a cyclical, but cyclical. The foundations report of the twenty-third of September calls foundation quality strong. The barriers are real: a top vessel costs up to nine hundred million dollars with half paid up front, and deepwater installation is effectively a three-player market. Free cash flow more than doubled last year to one point two billion dollars. But return on capital employed was only nine per cent last year, below the cost of capital, and net operating income was just one hundred and five million dollars at the twenty twenty-three low. This business earns well at this point in the cycle, not through it.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of the thirtieth of September is C, a valuation reading only, with no Modal return behind it. The price to earnings ratio is twelve point three, at the thirty-second percentile. DNB Carnegie puts the free cash flow to equity yield at ten to twelve per cent over the next two years, and the ordinary dividend is thirteen kroner a share, about four hundred million dollars. Broker targets in our files are three hundred and forty kroner from DNB Carnegie and four hundred and ten from Rothschild and Co Redburn, both Buy. My read is a modal return nearer thirteen to sixteen per cent a year than your twenty-five.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of the thirtieth of September is C, built on the environment only and provisional. On the plus side, offshore oil and gas spending is in a multi-year expansion, with spending decisions projected to peak around twenty twenty-seven, and no new pipelay vessels are coming. On the minus side, that peak is close, offshore wind outlooks have weakened outside the United Kingdom, and Brazil's weight makes Petrobras both the key customer and an objector to the merger. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the twenty-ninth of September is B, and its Technical Momentum reading of the thirtieth is B, though twelve-month relative strength of eighty-six trails the sector's ninety.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. You would be buying a cyclical at peak margins, just before it turns into a different company. DNB Carnegie warns margins may peak by twenty twenty-seven or twenty twenty-eight, offshore spending decisions peak around twenty twenty-seven, and the second quarter's strong cash came partly from a seasonal working capital release that reverses this half. A price to earnings ratio of twelve point three looks moderate, but cyclicals always look cheapest at the top. Then the merger blends Subsea Seven's twenty-one per cent margin with Saipem's eleven per cent, at no premium, with synergies not complete until twenty twenty-nine and regulators able to force disposals. For someone who wants predictable earnings per share over eighteen months to three years, the predictable part is mostly this year.

The one-line view. Neutral, as a working hypothesis: superb standalone execution and a full order book, but what you would own beyond this year is a merged, lower-margin company near the top of the offshore cycle, and the European Commission decision by the twenty-sixth of November is the test.
