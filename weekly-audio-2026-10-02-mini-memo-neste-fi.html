APM Mini-Memo. Neste. The second of October 2026.

Neste is rank one on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. My view is neutral, held as a working hypothesis. Neste is the strongest momentum name in the Pool on all three Top Down, Exogenously-driven readings, but its earnings ride on distillate cracks, renewable fuel margins and American credit prices, consensus has those earnings falling thirteen percent a year, and the plausible return of about twelve percent a year is a D anchor. It is a stock the environment likes, not the predictable earnings per share compounder your book is built on.

First, what the company does. Neste is a Finnish refiner with two engines. Renewable Products makes renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel from waste and residue feedstocks at Porvoo, Rotterdam and Singapore. Oil Products runs the conventional refinery at Porvoo, and Marketing and Services sells fuel. The market value is about twenty four billion euros at a price of thirty one euros forty seven on the second of October. The card places it in energy, downstream distribution, where your interest level is D, nonplussed.

Second, the thesis. This is self-help plus mandated demand. A new executive team under Heikki Malinen, appointed in October 2024, has pulled Neste back from speculative ventures and refocused on the two core businesses. The performance improvement programme reached a run-rate of five hundred and ninety four million euros by June, beating its target a year early. The change-forces digest of the twentieth of September calls it a shift from chronic over-promiser to honest tracker. The mechanism to earnings per share has three channels. Cost taken out of fixed operating expense, which reaches the bottom line whatever commodities do. Physical demand from the European renewable energy directive, where the end of double counting from January this year and Germany's transposition add one and a half million tonnes of annual demand. And the Rotterdam expansion, two and a half billion euros lifting renewable capacity to six point eight million tonnes, due to start in 2027. The things to track are the Renewable Products sales margin, a record twelve hundred dollars a tonne in the second quarter, more than three times a year earlier, utilisation at seventy five percent against an eighty percent target, Rotterdam's start date, mandate enforcement, American D four credit prices, and leverage.

Third, the Target Setup. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up, Stock-driven ratings, and this read is mine. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU combined with Demand-driven EPSU: new management converting a cost programme, deleveraging and a 2027 capacity step into earnings, with regulatory demand behind it. The match is uneasy. A step-up should show in consensus, and the plausible-return note of the first of October has consensus earnings falling thirteen percent a year from a record base. What the card describes today looks more like a quality cyclical at peak-on-peak than any of the Clearing setups, and that is the wrong end of the cycle for your book.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. Not rated, since no memo exists. On the evidence the case is not simple. The change-forces digest puts the compound probability of all six drivers delivering at roughly eleven percent and calls Neste a leveraged bet on regulatory tightening, lasting geopolitical disruption and flawless execution during maximum organisational stress. Around forty percent of the margin expansion is attributed to Middle East conflict pushing up distillate cracks. The second half carries three long turnarounds, eight weeks at Porvoo, eight weeks at Rotterdam and eleven weeks on one Singapore line, with capital expenditure of about one point two billion euros.

Fifth, business quality. Mixed. The positives are real: record group comparable EBITDA of about one point two billion euros in the second quarter, three and a half times a year earlier, Renewable Products beating consensus by nine percent, net debt to EBITDA down to one point two times from two point seven at the end of last year, and Moody's affirming A three with a stable outlook. Against that, management called seventy five percent utilisation unsatisfactory, sustainable aviation fuel production fell by more than half, Oil Products missed consensus by seventeen percent on volume, and working capital days rose. This is a good operator in a commodity business, not a franchise with pricing power.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The script's TSR, Valuation reading of the second of October is B, and the card flags it as a valuation reading only, not an expected return. The shares trade on twelve times earnings, at the twenty first percentile of their own history. The script's plausible-return note of the first of October gives an average of eleven point nine percent a year, dividends included, built on consensus earnings falling thirteen percent a year while the multiple moves from twelve times towards fifteen point three. That is a D anchor, and the return comes from re-rating, not from earnings growth. The stock sits about twelve percent below its fifty two week high with relative strength in the nineties.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The script's Thematic Fit, Momentum reading of the second of October is A, but flagged environment only. Sell-side Momentum of the first of October is A and Technical Momentum of the second of October is A, with a proven Stage two. The pros are mandated European demand, record American D four credit prices above two hundred and forty cents, and a waste-feedstock position that earns a premium over vegetable oils. The cons are that Asian biodiesel capacity threatens feedstock inflation, that American small refinery exemptions could collapse credit prices retroactively, and that political pushback on energy affordability could dilute mandate enforcement.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. The record quarter is a snapshot of extraordinary conditions, not a run-rate. Strip out the Middle East distillate premium, add three long turnarounds and heavy capital expenditure, and Neste's margin vulnerability shows while the shares are priced on Stage two enthusiasm. Consensus already has earnings falling thirteen percent a year. If Rotterdam slips past 2027 while Asian capacity compresses industry margins, earnings fall further and the re-rating the plausible return depends on never arrives. For a book that prizes predictable earnings per share over eighteen months to three years, the honest objection is not that Neste will do badly but that nobody can say with confidence what it will earn in 2028.

The one-line view. Neutral, as a working hypothesis. The environment loves Neste, but its earnings ride on cracks, margins and credit prices, consensus has them falling thirteen percent a year, and the plausible return of about twelve percent a year is a D anchor. It is a stock the environment likes, not the predictable earnings per share compounder your book is built on.
