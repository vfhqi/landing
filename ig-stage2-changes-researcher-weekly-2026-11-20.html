Stock notes: the settled stocks only

Every stock in this section is settled: its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back, which runs from the anchor day, 22 October, to 20 November. Each stock is numbered by its row in the table near the end of the note.

For every stock in this section, six of the nine Stage 2 tests were not computed this week: the price above the 200-day moving average, the price above the 150-day moving average, the price within 25% of the 52-week high, and the three relative-strength tests. A test that was not computed is a gap in our data, not a fail. The test that the 150-day moving average is above the 200-day passes for every stock here. Both of the other moving-average tests, the 50-day moving average above the 150-day and above the 200-day, also pass unless the stock's note says otherwise.

In general, a turn is confirmed when the rating reaches Probable and stays there for weeks: our backtest measures the benefit from 20 trading days at Probable. A turn is undone when the rating falls back below Plausible or the 150-day moving average falls back below the 200-day.

The marker at the start of each bullet runs from [+++], for the fact that most strengthens the stock's technical picture, to [---], for the fact that most weakens it; [=] marks a fact that does neither.

How to read this note

  • Stage 2: Richard's second stage of a stock's technical life, the sustained advance that follows a base. The rating is the master dashboard's own read on how firmly a stock qualifies, on a ladder that runs None, Possible, Plausible, Probable
  • A turn, and the two kinds of trigger: a stock turns when one of two triggers fires. The rating trigger: the rating is Plausible or Probable now and was lower on the anchor day. The moving-average cross: the 150-day moving average crossed from below the 200-day moving average to above it inside the look-back and is still above it now. A move up to Possible alone does not count. A stock is in this week's note when its first or latest trigger fell inside the week
  • The look-back and the anchor day: the screen compares each stock's rating on the anchor day, 22 October, with its rating on each of the dashboard's last 20 weekday builds, 23 October to 20 November. This note calls that span the look-back
  • Settled and unsettled: a stock is settled when its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back. A stock is unsettled when its rating did so one or more times, each time being a round trip, so the current reading of an unsettled stock may be a flicker on a threshold
  • The Stage 2 set: the stocks whose trigger inside the look-back still stands, 155 now. A stock leaves the set when its trigger no longer stands, or when the trigger is older than the look-back although the stock may still be Plausible or Probable
  • Reported here before: the stock appeared in this note in an earlier week, according to this project's record of what each week reported
  • The nine Stage 2 tests: four gates, two moving-average tests and three relative-strength tests
  • The four Stage 2 gates: the price above the 200-day moving average; the price above the 150-day moving average; the 150-day moving average above the 200-day; and the price within 25% of its 52-week high
  • The two moving-average tests: the 50-day moving average above the 150-day and above the 200-day
  • The three relative-strength tests: the stock's industry at 70 or above in relative strength; its sector at 70 or above; and the stock itself at 70 or above against its industry. Relative strength is the composite measure the master dashboard uses. A test the data marks as not computed is a gap in our data, not a fail
  • Our tilt: our weighting stance on an industry or sector, as set on 13 November 2026, from Overweight (B) through Neutral (C) and Underweight (D) to Double Underweight (F)
  • What a turn has been worth in our own backtest: the backtest covers triggers from January 2002 to 22 September 2026, on price returns. A stock whose rating rose to Probable and stayed Probable for 20 trading days then beat the average stock of the same day by 1.4 percentage points over three months and 2.2 over six. A rise to Probable on its first day beat the average stock by only 0.5 points over three months, and only 16% of rises to Probable stay Probable for 20 trading days. Longer holds did better: 1.8 and 3.3 points after 40 days. The gain comes more from fewer large losses than from more winners: 4.9% of the 20-day triggers ended three months down more than 20%, against 7.1% of all stocks. The effect is mainly a small-company effect: for companies worth EUR 5 to 50 billion the gain was 0.5 points over three months and 0.9 over six against stocks of the same size, only borderline reliable. A day at Plausible beat the average stock by only 0.2 points over three months, and the moving-average cross on its own was not tested. So a turn is a prompt to look, not a forecast
