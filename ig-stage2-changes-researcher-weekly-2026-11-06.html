Stock notes: the settled stocks only

Every stock in this section is settled: its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back, 5 October to 5 November. Each note is numbered by the stock's row in the table near the end of this note. Where a note says nothing about earlier weeks, the stock is in this note for the first time.

What would confirm a turn, for any stock here: the rating holding its rung through the coming weeks (our backtest counts 20 trading days at Probable as the useful mark), a stock rated None or Possible rising to Plausible or Probable, and the tests that now fail coming to pass. What would undo a turn: the rating falling back a rung, the price dropping below the 150-day or the 200-day moving average, or the 150-day moving average falling back below the 200-day. A stock rated None whose only trigger is the moving-average cross has the weakest kind of turn, and a rung reached on 5 November, the screen's last day, has not yet been held for a day. Where the facts mark a test as not computed, that is a gap in our data, not a fail.

How to read this note

  • Stage 2: Richard's second stage of a stock's technical life, the sustained advance that follows a base. The rating is the master dashboard's own read on how firmly a stock qualifies. The ladder runs None, Possible, Plausible, Probable
  • The two kinds of trigger: the rating is Plausible or Probable now and was lower on the anchor day; or the 150-day moving average crossed from below the 200-day moving average to above it inside the look-back and is still above it now. A move up to Possible alone does not count (Richard's ruling of 10 August 2026). A stock is in this week's note when its first or latest trigger fell inside the week
  • The look-back and the anchor day: the screen compares each stock's rating on the anchor day, 5 October, with its rating on each of the dashboard's last 20 weekday builds, 6 October to 5 November. This note calls that span the look-back
  • Settled and unsettled: a stock is settled when its rating did not fall back and recover inside the look-back; unsettled when it did, one or more times (each time is a round trip), so its current reading may be a flicker on a threshold
  • The Stage 2 set: the stocks whose trigger inside the look-back still stands (272 now). A stock leaves the set when its trigger no longer stands, or when the trigger is older than the look-back although the stock may still be Plausible or Probable
  • Reported here before: the stock appeared in this note in an earlier week, from this project's record of what each week reported
  • The four Stage 2 gates: the price above the 200-day moving average; the price above the 150-day moving average; the 150-day above the 200-day; and the price within 25% of its 52-week high
  • The two moving-average tests: the 50-day moving average above the 150-day and above the 200-day
  • The three relative-strength tests: the stock's industry at 70 or above in relative strength; its sector at 70 or above; and the stock itself at 70 or above against its industry. Relative strength here is the composite measure the master dashboard uses. A test the data marks as not computed is a gap in our data, not a fail
  • The nine tests: the four gates, the two moving-average tests and the three relative-strength tests together. The stock notes count how many of the nine pass
  • What a turn has been worth in our own backtest (triggers from January 2002 to 22 September 2026, price returns, the Stage 2 definition of 1 October 2026): a stock whose rating rose to Probable and stayed Probable for 20 trading days then beat the average stock of the same day by 1.4 percentage points over three months and 2.2 over six. A rise to Probable on its first day beat the average stock by only 0.5 points over three months, and only 16% of rises to Probable stay Probable for 20 trading days; longer holds did better (40 days: 1.8 and 3.3 points). The gain comes more from fewer large losses than from more winners: 4.9% of the 20-day triggers ended three months down more than 20%, against 7.1% of all stocks.
