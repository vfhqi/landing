APM Mini-Memo. MOL Hungarian Oil & Gas. The first of October 2026.

MOL Hungarian Oil & Gas sits at rank ten on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, promoted this week.

My view is bearish, as a working hypothesis. The momentum is strong and the headline multiple looks cheap, but both rest on peak refining margins that management itself expects to normalise. So earnings per share over the next eighteen months to three years look more likely to step down than up, and that fails your predictability test.

First, what the company does. MOL is Central Europe's integrated energy group. It produces oil and gas in eight countries, runs three landlocked refineries and two petrochemical plants, owns more than two thousand three hundred service stations, and operates Hungary's only high-pressure gas transmission network. It has forty-five per cent of Hungarian retail fuel, forty-six per cent in Slovakia and sixty per cent in Croatia through its subsidiary INA. At four thousand nine hundred and seventy-four forints on the thirtieth of September, it is worth about twelve billion euros.

Second, the thesis. The bull thesis is that disrupted European diesel supply keeps refining margins high for longer, and MOL's diesel-heavy system turns that straight into cash. Each extra dollar a barrel on the complex refining margin adds about one hundred and seventeen million dollars a year to clean operating profit. The second quarter of this year showed the gearing. Clean operating profit was about one point three billion dollars, up eighty-nine per cent and thirty-eight per cent above consensus, on a complex margin of twenty-one dollars seventy a barrel against six dollars a year earlier. The self-help leg is the Tomorrow Downstream programme, aiming at five hundred million dollars of annual savings by twenty twenty-seven. But the earnings review of the twenty-third of September makes the opposite call: consensus earnings per share for twenty twenty-seven and twenty twenty-eight cut by fifteen to twenty per cent as margins normalise. The things to track are the refining margin, flows on the Druzhba pipeline, the unsigned contract with the Croatian pipeline operator JANAF, Hungary's ninety-five per cent windfall tax on the Brent-Urals spread, and petrochemical margins, which already fell from five hundred and forty-eight euros a tonne in the second quarter to one hundred and nineteen in July.

Third, the Target Setup. There is no Investment Analyst memo, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings, and this read is mine. Nothing fits cleanly. The closest is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU, resting on Tomorrow Downstream, the new holding structure and the Aphrodite gas field purchase off Cyprus. None of those lifts earnings per share inside our window, and Aphrodite's first gas is not expected until twenty thirty-one. The live driver is a margin peak, which is the mirror image of CfC in quality cyclical at trough-on-trough, leading to reversion to normal. Here, reversion to normal points down.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. This is not a simple case. There are six drivers and they move together: falling Brent and falling refining margins at the same time could cut cash generation by more than three hundred million dollars. Governments, with price caps in ten of twelve markets, Slovak levies and the windfall tax, and a transit monopoly charging more than three times the Transalpine pipeline's rates, capture much of the rent. In July MOL went to court over one hundred and twenty-six billion forints of carbon dioxide quota taxes, which our files describe both as a refund claim and as a disputed liability of about three hundred and ninety-five million dollars. And a restructuring called Project HIVE doubled staff turnover to thirteen per cent in the first half, just as the savings programme needs delivering. The July pre-mortem directed a firm Park.

Fifth, business quality. A good franchise with poor rent capture. Landlocked isolation, integrated feedstock and an oligopolistic retail market, where the top five distributors hold over eighty per cent of regional volumes, are real advantages, and non-fuel sales reached a record forty-one and a half per cent of retail margin. But the earnings record is erratic: a miss in the second quarter of last year, a refinery fire that October, and a ten-week Druzhba stoppage that sank the first quarter of this year. Net debt was a comfortable nought point four nine times operating profit at the end of June, but it touched one times in the first quarter after a one point four billion dollar working capital build. Minority holders have little leverage, with a thirty per cent foundation stake, a voting cap and a state veto share.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of the thirtieth of September is F, a valuation reading only, with no Modal return. FactSet puts the stock on nine point two times twenty-four-month forward earnings per share, and ranks that multiple at the ninety-eighth percentile. On my arithmetic, the August consensus target of five thousand three hundred and fifty forints is eight per cent up, the bull case of six thousand three hundred and seven is twenty-seven per cent up, and the bear case of three thousand six hundred and ninety is twenty-six per cent down. The dividend of about three hundred forints a share yields about six per cent. The earnings review puts three-year returns at mid-single digits a year, below the D anchor of about twelve per cent.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. On the plus side, the script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of the thirtieth of September is B, on the energy environment only and provisional. Regional fuel demand grows steadily, and Aphrodite adds a large block of contingent gas. On the minus side, European petrochemicals made from naphtha are in structural decline against gas-fed rivals, and a May explosion at the Tiszaújváros plant cut petrochemical volumes by a third. Carbon prices of one hundred to one hundred and forty euros a tonne by twenty thirty would cost real money, and Hungary's deficit of around seven per cent of national output invites more taxation of MOL.

Eighth, the strongest counter-case. Momentum is the counter-case, and it is strong. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of the twenty-ninth of September is A and its Technical Momentum reading of the thirtieth is B. Relative strength over twelve months is ninety-seven. Management budgets on sixty-five dollar Brent against ninety-two dollars realised, the savings programme does not depend on the cycle, and a signed JANAF contract or the end of the windfall tax would lift earnings for good. If refining stays tight into twenty twenty-eight, nine point two times is cheap and my bear case is simply early.

The one-line view. Bearish, as a working hypothesis: a cyclical refining windfall dressed as a cheap stock, with governments and a pipeline monopoly taking much of the rent, rather than the predictable earnings per share step-up you look for.
