Glossary

Every acronym and technical term used in this note, defined in plain English.

  • APM (Assistant Portfolio Manager) — the analytical role generating this note; a human analyst's (Richard's) aide, not an autonomous decision-maker
  • SLQ (Short-List Qualified) — the pool of stocks that pass all three eligibility gates (Sector Weight ≠ F; Timeliness A or B; Stage Signal active). The re-run produced 303 stocks passing this filter from a universe of 985 European equities
  • Universe — 985 European listed equities tracked by Viewforth Partners' APM system. Each has technical price data, sell-side estimate data, and valuation history
  • Stage 2 (Stan Weinstein framework) — a confirmed price uptrend: the stock is above its rising 30-week moving average, with price action making higher highs and higher lows. Named for market technician Stan Weinstein's four-stage cycle model
  • Stage 1 — a basing phase before Stage 2 begins: price is no longer in a downtrend but has not yet broken to new highs. A Stage 1 pick is a bet on the entry into Stage 2
  • Persistence Window — the 12 trailing calendar month-ends, each scored as to whether the stock qualified for Stage 2 at that point (Probable, Plausible, Possible, or None)
  • Proven Stage 2 (Bucket A) — ≥4 qualifying months in the persistence window. The trend has been confirmed on multiple occasions; it is not a one-month fluke
  • Early Stage 2 (Bucket B) — 1–3 qualifying months. A young signal: real, but short track record. Higher false-positive rate than Proven
  • Stage 1 Transition (Bucket C) — Stage 1 signal only (no Stage 2 qualifying months yet), with Valuation grade A or B. The entry bet relies on valuation cheapness as the catalyst trigger
  • Record Length — months since the first qualifying month in the persistence window. Fresh = ≤7 months; Mid = 8–11 months; Extended = 12 months (the maximum — the signal first appeared a full year ago)
  • SSEM (Sell-Side Estimate Momentum) — a composite score from −15 to +15 measuring analyst estimate revisions for a stock. +1 for each estimate raised, −1 for each cut, across six metrics (EPS, EBITDA, Revenue, Target Price, % Buy rating) over three windows (L1M=last month, L3M=last 3 months, L6M=last 6 months), giving 18 data points per stock. Graded A–F on a bell-curve relative to all 985 stocks
  • EPS revisions — analysts changing their earnings-per-share forecasts up (positive) or down (negative); the most important SSEM component because it directly tracks profit momentum
  • Component deterioration — a specific SSEM flag meaning profit-line estimates (EPS, EBITDA, or Revenue) were cut in the most recent period even if the composite SSEM score is positive. This was the key re-run correction: three stocks previously included (ARIS, ALC, CARM) had positive composite scores but deteriorating profit lines — falling knives in disguise
  • Valuation Grade — how cheap or expensive the stock is relative to all 985 stocks in the universe, ranked on pe_percentile (the stock's current P/E ratio as a percentile of its own 10-year history). A = cheapest 10% of the universe; B = next 15%; C = middle 25%; D = next 25%; F = most expensive 25%. Critical correction: this is a cross-sectional ranking, not an absolute level — a Val Grade A stock is cheaper than 90% of the universe, not necessarily cheap in isolation
  • pe_percentile — where the stock's current P/E ratio sits in its own 10-year history (0% = at its all-time cheapest, 100% = at its all-time most expensive). Used as the raw input to the cross-sectional valuation grade
  • SW (Sector Weight) — Richard's discretionary stance on each sector: SOW = Strong Overweight, OW = Overweight, NW = Neutral Weight, UW = Underweight, SUW = Strong Underweight. F = excluded entirely (sector fails eligibility). Stocks in F-sectors do not appear in the SLQ
  • Timeliness — a qualitative grade (A, B, or C) for the quality of the current entry setup. A = ideal setup (e.g., volatility contraction pattern on verge of breakout, or healthy first retest after a clean breakout); B = acceptable setup (stock qualifies but entry is not textbook); C = poor setup (disqualifies from SLQ)
  • VCP (Volatility Contraction Pattern) — a specific Timeliness A setup: a series of smaller and smaller price consolidations, each with lower volatility, on declining volume, just before a breakout. Coined by Mark Minervini
  • Currently qualifying (cur=True/False) — whether the stock still meets Stage 2 criteria as of the most recent data point (June 2026). Several portfolio picks have cur=False because they lapsed at the May month-end — this requires a June month-end re-confirmation before position entry
  • Deteriorating — the ssem_comp.deteriorating flag; True means profit-line estimates (EPS/EBITDA/Sales) were cut in the most recent period. A red-flag even on otherwise high-scoring stocks
  • VSL (Very Short-List) — the reserve bench: the best non-portfolio names per industry, held ready for rotation into the portfolio if a portfolio stock is exited or if a better opportunity arises
  • Bell curve grading — all grades (SSEM, Valuation) are assigned by ranking all 985 stocks and applying fixed percentile cuts. A grade of A does not mean "objectively good" — it means "in the top 10% of this universe". A grade of F means bottom 25% of universe, not failing on an absolute basis. Context matters
  • Stage-1-only names — stocks with no Stage 2 history but a valid Stage 1 base + cheap valuation. These are turnaround bets: the thesis is that the stock is poised to enter Stage 2, not that it has already done so. Higher uncertainty, potentially higher upside
  • J→A→I — the preferred communication order: Judgement (conclusion, conviction, action) first; Analysis (the reasoning and evidence) second; Information (the raw data and background) last. This note follows this ordering throughout
  • BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front) — the most important conclusion of a section, stated at the top before any supporting detail
