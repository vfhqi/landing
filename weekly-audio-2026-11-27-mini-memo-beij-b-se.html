APM Mini-Memo. Beijer Ref. The twenty-seventh of November 2026.

Beijer Ref is new this week at rank one on the Ideas Bench, in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. The one-line view. Beijer Ref is the world's largest refrigeration and air-conditioning wholesaler. Replacement demand is dated by law, and a re-lit acquisition engine compounds earnings per share at about twelve percent a year. The case depends on the market holding or raising its multiple through the hard summer comparison of 2027.

First, what the company does. Beijer Ref sells refrigeration, air-conditioning and heating parts and equipment to professional installers. The network is about four hundred and fifty to five hundred branches in forty-five countries. Sales grew from under fifteen billion kronor in 2019 to thirty-seven billion in 2025. Roughly sixty percent of sales are in Europe, where Beijer Ref is number one. About a quarter are in North America, where the company holds one to one and a half percent of a market that Watsco leads. Between eighty and ninety percent of sales are repair and maintenance. Eight to ten percent come from the company's own manufacture of natural-refrigerant systems. Demand is partly written into law. European refrigerant rules halve the fluorinated-gas quota on the first of January 2027, and ban most new split air-conditioning units by 2029. The United States phases down the old gases by 2036. Old systems cannot run the new gases, so installers must replace equipment. The Investment Analyst's memo of the ninth of November finds that regulation already touches forty to forty-five percent of sales directly.

Second, the thesis. Four forces moved together in five months. Organic growth turned from minus one point four percent to plus eight point three percent in the quarter to September 2026. Acquisitions adding about three point seven billion kronor of annual sales, around ten percent of revenue, were signed in nine months. Jonas Steen, who has run Europe since 2010, became chief executive on the first of November. And Melker Schörling took control from EQT in June, which removed both a control overhang and a seller of stock. The route to earnings per share is mechanical. The memo's model has earnings per share rising about thirteen percent in 2026 and twelve percent in 2027, to six kronor fifty-one. Growth then runs at about twelve percent a year to 2030. Over half of the growth to 2027 is already signed in deals. Brokers count acquisitions only on completion, so a running deal engine is a stream of upgrades. Why could the market pay more? Beijer Ref trades at a twenty to thirty percent discount to the Swedish serial acquirers because its returns on capital are lower. The new owner's other companies run to return and cash targets. The memo shows that taking five points of revenue out of working capital would release about two point three billion kronor, enough to fund two billion kronor of acquired sales without new debt. A numerical return or working-capital target, possibly at the results on the twenty-ninth of January, would start to close the discount. The drivers to track are four. Organic growth in the two weather-free quarters to December and March, where above three percent confirms the turn. The acquisition pace, against two to two and a half billion kronor of sales a year. The January report, for a new return or cash target. And the North American margin.

Third, the Target Setup. The Target Setup is Delivering, Serial Acquirer, Mechanical EPS Upgrader. The earnings step comes from signed deals and a dated replacement cycle, not from a turn in sentiment, and consensus is still catching up with the deal flow. The fit itself is middling. The memo of the twenty-fourth of September graded the serial-acquirer setup C, and the Rater's Setups' Fit and Crispness of the ninth of November is B. The weekly Lists judgement placed Beijer Ref first because five of the Eight Master Ratings read A or B and the chart is read as probably in an early uptrend. Your interest level rose to B this week.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The Rater's Case Riskiness of the ninth of November is B, B plus on the fine scale, meaning fairly simple and fairly low-risk. The earnings are the simple part. For 2027, eight outcomes in ten sit within about six percent of the model, and no single operating assumption moves the year by more than three percent. The main risk is the multiple, not the earnings. About half of the acceleration in the September quarter was the warmest Western European summer on record, worth about four hundred and fifty million kronor of sales. So the July and October reports of 2027 will show organic growth near zero against that comparison. No broker carries that print. The model sits four to six percent below 2027 consensus. And climate wholesalers de-rate together when bond yields rise. The weekly Lists judgement adds that the Industry relative strength Sell Criterion is already met for industrials, and that a distributor priced for a mandated cycle has little cushion if cooling demand softens with construction. What needs to go right is three things. Organic growth above three percent in the quarters to December and March, proving the turn is more than weather. Acquisitions that keep adding two to two and a half billion kronor of sales a year within the leverage ceiling of two and a half times. That ceiling is tight at the seasonal peak in the second quarter, and leverage is about two point eight times once three point four billion kronor of option liabilities are counted. And a January report that reaffirms or sharpens the targets. The memo expects estimate revisions to trace a V through 2027, so timing matters as much as direction.

Fifth, business quality. Well-moated, with ordinary returns. Customers are fragmented, and prices held while volumes fell in 2023 to 2025. The moats are branch density, training for flammable refrigerants, private label at a quarter or more of European climate sales, and own green manufacturing. Each widens with every regulatory step. The operating margin held near ten point seven percent through the organic stall, and fell by less than a point when revenue fell five percent in 2020. The financial profile lags the cohort. The operating margin of about eleven percent compares with fourteen to twenty-three percent for the Swedish serial acquirers. Return on operating capital of eleven point two percent sits below the company's own twelve percent target. Working capital is about forty percent of sales, and new deals earn only seven to nine percent after tax. Governance trails too, with dual-class shares, no central internal audit across more than one hundred and seventy subsidiaries, and incentives that reward earnings growth alone. The Rater's Foundations' Robustness and Thesis's Change Forces' Strength of the ninth of November are both C.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The shares are priced for delivery. They closed at one hundred and sixty-four and a half kronor on the twenty-seventh of November, under one percent below their fifty-two week high, on twenty-two times current earnings. That multiple is at the thirty-first percentile of the stock's own history. The weekly TSR and Valuation rating of the twenty-seventh of November is B. The weekly plausible return of the nineteenth of November averages nearly forty percent a year, grade A, but assumes the multiple rises from twenty-two to thirty-four times. The Investment Analyst does not believe that multiple. At twenty-four to twenty-six times, the memo gets a return of twenty-eight to forty percent to March 2028. On an unchanged multiple, twenty-two percent. Over three years, about fifteen to eighteen percent a year, which is C against your bar. The entry multiple where earnings growth alone carries the return is nineteen to twenty times, and the shares have risen five percent since the memo. Sell-side Momentum is A and Technical Momentum is B on the weekly readings, with Jefferies at one hundred and ninety kronor and DNB Carnegie at one hundred and seventy.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros. The replacement cycle is dated by regulation rather than by construction. European air-conditioning penetration of twenty-three percent, against ninety-three percent in the United States, gives a long runway. Cheap Chinese propane units hurt European manufacturers far more than the channel, and they give Beijer Ref private-label supply. The weekly Thematic Fit and Momentum of the twenty-seventh of November is B. The cons. Relative strength for industrials and capital goods read twenty-six on the twenty-seventh of November, below the Sell Criterion line of thirty for a second week, so the stock must be bought against the tape. Rising yields press on every compounder's multiple. Ninety-eight percent of sales are outside the krona and unhedged. And the North American platform, bought at twelve times earnings against six to eight for European deals, carries the growth story with about one percent market share.
