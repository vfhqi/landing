APM Mini-Memo. Azelis. The twenty-seventh of November 2026.

Azelis is new on the Ideas Bench this week, at rank nine in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection.

The one-line view. Azelis is a sticky specialty-chemicals distributor whose recovery is now in the numbers, and the case depends on leverage falling far enough to restart acquisitions and earn back the multiple.

First, what the company does.

Azelis sells specialty chemicals and food ingredients on behalf of about two thousand eight hundred manufacturers. It serves some sixty-five thousand customers in sixty-four countries, with more than seventy laboratories that help customers formulate their products. Life sciences, meaning pharmaceuticals, food, personal care and home care, is about sixty-three percent of sales. Industrial chemicals is the other thirty-seven percent. Europe, the Middle East and Africa is forty-five percent of revenue, the Americas thirty-five and Asia-Pacific twenty. Revenue in 2025 was four point one billion euros at an adjusted operating margin of ten percent. That makes Azelis the third largest listed European distributor, behind Brenntag and IMCD, in a market where the top ten hold under fifteen percent.

The lock is in the formulation. Once an ingredient is specified into a customer's recipe, and validated by regulators in pharmaceuticals, replacing the distributor is hard. Logistics are outsourced and capital spending is below half a percent of sales. That cash historically funded a buy-and-build engine of sixty-four deals in eight and a half years.

Second, the thesis.

This is a recovery the market has half paid for. Three things went wrong at once. Organic sales fell for three years, to the first quarter of 2026. Net debt overshot to three point four times cash profit, against a target of two and a half to three times, and that froze acquisitions. And 2025 brought a third year of downgrades. The shares fell to six euros seventy-two and an all-time low multiple in January 2026.

The turn is now visible and broadening. Customers finished destocking. Organic growth went from minus three point nine percent in the first quarter of 2026, to plus three point nine in the second, to plus eight point two in the third, reported on the twenty-second of October. Every region grew, and adjusted operating profit beat consensus in both quarters. A twenty million euro cost programme landed early. A refinancing in March 2026 saves six and a half million euros a year of interest. So profit grows faster than sales.

The route to earnings per share has three legs. Organic growth of three to four percent and a held gross margin give operating profit growth of about five percent a year, and falling interest adds more. The Investment Analyst's path is ninety cents in 2026, one euro one in 2027 and one euro eight in 2028, about nine percent a year. The third leg is the re-rating. Once leverage is back inside three times, the market restores the multiple it pays a consolidator that can consolidate. Management already speaks of ten to fifteen bolt-on deals from the first half of 2027. The drivers to track are the split of organic growth between volume and price, year-end leverage, heading toward three point one times or stuck above three point four, and the earnings for 2027.

Third, the Target Setup.

The Target Setup is Major CfC Clearing in MQ non-cyclical or better business on Q1 multiple, leading to reversion to normal. The Investment Analyst's memo of the twenty-ninth of September rated Setups' Fit/Crispness C. The mechanism fits exactly and all five conditions pass, but two pass only at the boundary. The multiple sat at the twenty-fifth to twenty-ninth percentile rather than below it, and the sell-side never capitulated. Today the shares trade at twelve point three times forward earnings, the twenty-fourth percentile of their own history, so they still sit just inside the bottom quarter. A trough-on-trough reading of the same facts is the secondary fit.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity.

The Investment Analyst's memo of the twenty-ninth of September rated Case Riskiness C. Riskiness is average. Simplicity is poor, for a sixty-four country distributor without quantitative guidance. But the case turns on three dated things, so it resolves rather than drifts.

The main risk is that the recovery is a false friend: price without volume, and cash that does not reach the balance sheet. The third quarter's eight point two percent organic growth leaned on industrial chemicals, up twenty-one percent, and on pricing lifted by Middle East disruption, which Deutsche Bank expects to fade. Life sciences grew only one and a half percent. Meanwhile free cash flow halved, cash conversion fell to fifty-nine percent, and leverage rose to three point four six times. A three hundred and fifty million euro goodwill write-down landed in the Americas. The shares fell thirteen percent in a fortnight.

What needs to go right is simple to state. Volume-led growth holding above four percent. Leverage falling toward three times by year-end. And no top-twenty principal leaving, because that would re-open the whole case. The downside is bounded. The covenant is four and a half times, liquidity is seven hundred and forty-five million euros, and no bond matures before September 2029. The memo's mark-to-market downside to a trough multiple is eighteen to thirty-six percent.

Fifth, business quality.

Solid rather than strong. The supplier franchise is the exceptional fact. Thirty years' average tenure with the top ten principals. Zero attrition among the top twenty over ten years. At least eighty-five percent of top-ten revenue exclusive. That is why the business came through three years of decline intact. Cash conversion averaged ninety-three percent from 2019 to 2025, and return on tangible capital is about sixty-three percent. Against that, reported return on invested capital is seven point three percent after acquisition goodwill, leverage is the highest in the peer set, and IMCD out-executes on gross margin, twenty-five point one against twenty-three point four percent, and on European growth. The Investment Analyst's memo rated Foundations' Robustness C and Thesis's Change Forces' Strength C on the twenty-ninth of September.

Sixth, returns and valuation.

About a B return, resting on the re-rating. At twelve euros five the shares trade at twelve point three times forward earnings of ninety-eight cents, the twenty-fourth percentile of their own history. They stand at a twenty-one to twenty-five percent discount to IMCD on enterprise value to operating profit. The Investment Analyst's modal return, rebased to today's price, is twenty-three percent a year over eighteen months and fifteen and a half percent over three years, averaging just over nineteen percent, and the TSR/Valuation rating in our store is B. In the memo's own figure of twenty percent a year, seventeen points of the eighteen-month return came from the re-rating and eleven from earnings. At the consensus earnings path the return falls to eight percent a year. The memo put the price at which the case clears both horizons at about ten euros forty. The market is confirming the case. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of the twenty-seventh of November is A, with three grade-A chart setups. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating rose to B on the twenty-fifth of November, with a median target of fourteen euros seventy.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics.

The pros. Europe's chemical producers are shrinking and handing small customers to specialty distributors, which adds up to a point a year of organic growth. The restock with inflation is the one phase when a distributor wins on volume, price and its own shelves at once. And a newly lit acquisition engine is a stream of upgrades, because consensus counts only completed deals. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of the twenty-seventh of November is B.

The cons. The restock story lasts six to eighteen months, and the chemical price spike from panic-buying is expected to reverse. Chinese oversupply shrinks the spread at the semi-specialty end through 2027. In a risk-off market this group pays for cash conversion and balance sheet first, which is where Azelis is weakest. And Industry relative strength for industrials read twenty-six on the twenty-seventh of November, below the Sell Criterion line of thirty for a second Friday.

In short. Azelis is interesting because a sticky franchise is recovering in plain sight, with the shares still in the bottom quarter of their own multiple. The return is about a B, but most of it is a re-rating that waits on leverage. The hinge is the balance sheet: leverage must fall from three point four six times toward three by year-end for bolt-ons to resume in 2027. Watch the volume-versus-price split and year-end leverage. A top-twenty principal loss or leverage above three point eight times re-opens the case. The Investment Analyst's memo of the first of October said park at eleven euros ninety. The Lists judgement asks for a full memo now that momentum has turned A.

The one-line view. Azelis is a sticky specialty-chemicals distributor whose recovery is now in the numbers, and the case depends on leverage falling far enough to restart acquisitions and earn back the multiple.
