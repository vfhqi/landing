Stock notes: the settled stocks only

Every stock in this section is settled: its rating did not fall back and recover inside the screen's window, so a rating that climbed, climbed cleanly, and a rating that sat still while the moving averages crossed sat still throughout. The data's list of stocks already reported in an earlier week is empty, so every stock here is new to these reports. Each heading carries the stock's row number in the table near the end of this note. In general, a turn is confirmed when the rating holds on the next readings, the price stays above both the 150-day and the 200-day moving averages, the 150-day stays above the 200-day, the price stays within 25% of its 52-week high and the relative-strength tests come to be met; it is undone when the price drops back below either average, the 150-day falls back under the 200-day or the rating falls a rung. Each bullet opens with a signpost: [+++], [++] and [+] mark a fact that strengthens the stock's technical picture, [=] one that does neither, and [-], [--] and [---] one that weakens it. Three shorthands recur, set out in full under 'How to read this note': 'all four gates pass' means the price is above the 200-day moving average and above the 150-day, the 150-day is above the 200-day, and the price is within 25% of its 52-week high; 'both moving-average tests pass' means the 50-day moving average is above the 150-day and above the 200-day; and the three relative-strength tests ask whether the stock's industry, its sector and the stock against its industry each score 70 or above.

How to read this note

Stage 2 and the rungs. Stage 2 is Richard's second stage of a stock's technical life, the sustained advance that follows a base. The rating is the master dashboard's own read on how firmly a stock qualifies, and its ladder runs None, Possible, Plausible, Probable.

The two kinds of trigger. Either the rating is Plausible or Probable now and was lower at the start of the window, or the 150-day moving average crossed from below the 200-day moving average to above it inside the window and is still above it now. A move up to Possible alone does not count (Richard's ruling of 10 August 2026).

Settled and unsettled. A stock is settled when its rating did not fall back and recover inside the window. It is unsettled when it did, one or more times; each time is a round trip, and the current reading may be a flicker on a threshold.

The four Stage 2 gates. The price above the 200-day moving average; the price above the 150-day moving average; the 150-day above the 200-day; and the price within 25% of its 52-week high.

The two moving-average tests. The 50-day moving average above the 150-day, and the 50-day above the 200-day.

The three relative-strength tests. The stock's industry at 70 or above in relative strength; its sector at 70 or above; and the stock itself at 70 or above against its industry. Relative strength here is the composite measure the master dashboard uses. A test the data marks as not computed is a gap, not a fail.

The window and the anchor day. The window is 2026-10-16 to 2026-10-22, the screen's own window; the screen's header does not report a shorter one. The anchor day for the 'rating on anchor day' column is 2026-09-22, one trading day before the script's 20-day window, and where a stock was not measured that day its baseline is 'not measured', never invented. Settled and unsettled are read over that 20-trading-day span, so a round trip counted against a stock can predate the week.

The group-move bar. A sector qualifies as a group move when at least 3 of its stocks triggered in the week, and a cohort when at least 2 of its stocks did, AND those triggers are at least a quarter (25%) of all that sector's or cohort's stocks in 11-universe-lookup.md (1363 stocks this week). Every trigger counts, settled or not. Where a cohort's triggered stocks are exactly a sector's triggered stocks, the two are one group, reported once under the cohort. Richard's rule of 28 October 2026: 3 for a sector and 2 for a cohort, because cohorts are smaller.
