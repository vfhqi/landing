The gaps

Gap 1: a book that leans neither way inside a target that leans hard, holding nothing in the rows the target Overweights most. Of 50.73 points invested, 13.30 (26%) sit in Overweight rows (Carlsberg and Fevertree in Beverages, Hilton Food in Food production, CVS Group in Hospitals), 13.35 (26%) in Underweight rows (Helios Towers in Towers, Arteche in Electrical equipment, D'Ieteren in Automotive retail), and 48% in Neutral rows. The target has 66 non-Neutral rows, and the rows it Overweights most strongly hold nothing: Downstream energy at Double Overweight; Upstream energy, Energy services, Insurance, Pharma/biotech, Integrated utilities, Food retail, Tobacco, Household products and Testing at Overweight; Switzerland, Norway and Hungary among the countries. The reasoning: the environment (risk-off in the open, defensives leading on all five windows, the squeeze running through credit) pays large, proven, cash-yielding defensives and energy's disruption beneficiaries, and the book's Overweight-row exposure is three staples and one care-home operator, all small or mid caps.

  • Counter-case: the energy rows are five dollars from their $85 trigger with the consensus oil path pointing through it during 2027, so filling that gap now buys the end of its reason; the tilt scorecard has not shown the Overweights add value (three of ten tables positive after ten weeks, noise); and a book 50.7% invested in a correction has already done the defensive work the Overweights are meant to do.

Gap 2: fragile and concentrated where the environment punishes both. Eight of twelve holdings (34.12 points, 67% of invested weight) are at or below the 41st size percentile, and four (Hilton Food, Hexatronic, Sdiptech, CVS Group) are below the 15th; Sweden is 39% of invested weight in four names, three of them small; the two largest positions are 26% and Helios Towers alone 15%. Fragility is Double Underweight on fresh evidence this week: credit widened with the stress in AI-linked names, European breadth fell to the bottom third of its history since 2006, and Danske reads Nordic small and mid caps as de-rating. Richard's own line is the reason: in these markets investors sell small, niche, unproven and complex first.

  • Counter-case: the holdings' own charts are strong (66% of invested weight at Technical Momentum A or B); the fragility adjustment is already inside each reading and nets to +0.4 points across the book, so the measure says size is not hurting the book on average; and three of the four small Swedish names have simple, proven cases on their memos. The gap is the shape, not the individual names.

Gap 3: over-exposed, in size, to the rate-and-AI-sensitive Underweights. 13.35 points (26% of invested weight) sit in Underweight rows today, 18.24 (36%) once Hardware's move reaches Hexatronic, and the largest position in the book is in one of them. These holdings are carried by their charts and cases (Arteche's Bottom-up 91.4 and Sell-side Momentum A; Helios Towers' Bottom-up 80.0 and Technical Momentum A; Hexatronic's Sell-side A and Technical A), not by the environment, and the environment moved against two of them this week: the AI capital-spending theme flipped to liability with hardware at the epicentre, while the grid order case survived only at the second order.

  • Counter-case, strong this week: these are the rows where a growth-fear fall in yields pays first (Towers and Real assets led the week's rebound), the ten-year is 33 basis points from the trigger that lifts them, and the signed Lists statement's principle holds for the book too: a top-down letter never excludes a strong case, it ranks it. The right response is a limit on how much of the book may sit in such rows, not an exit.

Endnotes: how this note is made

The rating. The published score is the measured position-weighted mean Fitness less a judgement deduction for concentration. The grade follows the scale every prior reading has implied (A 80 to 100, B 65 to 79, C 50 to 64, D 35 to 49, F below 35), stated here because no file gives it; the per-holding grades above use the same scale. The rating's track week by week and the book against the target, holding by holding, are on the page beneath this note. The track rows of 2 to 23 October carry a correction note (a double-counted size deduction), so they are compared on their measured starts, as here.

What the scores were computed on. The scores were computed on last week's carried-forward table at a tilt of 0.625, so this week's eight tilt changes reach the reading next week; where a judgement above anticipates them (Hexatronic's Hardware row, the United Kingdom-listed holdings, CVS Group and Elekta, D'Ieteren), it says so. Last week's per-holding readings are not in the pack, so the split between the book changing and the holdings' own ratings moving is a judgement from the exits' last known readings. Elekta's and D'Ieteren's four Top Down/Exogenously-driven grades sit in the part of the ratings table that was not read; their Fitness readings and older ratings columns carry the judgements above.

How the exposures were measured. Invested weight is the sum of the twelve position sizes in the holdings file (50.73 points); shares of invested weight are computed on it. Overweight-row and Underweight-row exposure take each holding's sector tilt, or its industry tilt where the sector is Neutral, from last week's table. No European size-factor series exists beside the breadth file (amendment 7, retired), so Gap 2 rests on size percentiles, breadth and one house's Nordic read. The French premium in basis points was unread for a fourth week.
