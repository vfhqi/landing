How fit the portfolio is

A measured 57 of 100, grade C, with the book unchanged: the measure was corrected and the book stood still while the environment moved against two of its holdings.

Where the number comes from, and why it moved

The position-weighted mean Fitness across all 16 holdings is 57.2, and I publish 57 with no adjustment. The measure is the Top Down/Exogenously-driven composite with each holding's fragility adjustment inside it (book-weighted minus 1.1 points), computed against last week's carried-forward target table at a tilt of 0.625. For four weeks the published figure took 4 points off the measured start for blank size and fragility columns; the correction note of 29-Oct-26 found that this double-counted the fragility adjustment, and both columns are now populated for every holding, so the deduction goes. The scores cannot yet see this week's own target change: Defence to Underweight lowers Theon's Thematic Fit/Momentum, worth a fraction of a point on a 4.05-point holding, which arrives next week as target effect rather than being guessed now.

Like with like, the measured start rose from 56.8 to 57.2, which is flat, and the three sources separate as follows:

  • The book did not change: the same 16 holdings and 55.35 points in the holdings ledger of 28-Oct-26
  • The target moved beneath it last week, as forecast, and lifted the reading: Momentum to Underweight under a book with 34.00 points at Technical Momentum D and 4.05 at F; the other change, Renewable equipment to Neutral, touches no holding
  • The holdings' own weekly readings weakened enough to take most of that back: Carlsberg's Sell-side Momentum at D, Theon's Technical Momentum at F, Xvivo's and D'Ieteren's Sell-side Momentum at F, against Hexatronic's Technical Momentum improving to B. Last week's per-holding readings are not carried, so this split is read from the direction of the ratings, not measured

The comparable track is lower than the published one and drifting down. Each of the last four published figures (54, 58, 55, 53) sits 4 points below its measured start, so the comparable series reads 57.7, 61.9, 58.7, 56.8 and now 57.2: down 4.5 points over five measured weeks. The week-by-week track is on the page below this note.

The gaps

The book and the target disagree in three places that matter over the next twelve months; this week the environment widened one (Defence) and confirmed the other two on European evidence for the first time.

Gap 1. Short of the yield beneficiaries and defensives the target overweights

The target holds Energy downstream at Double Overweight, and Energy upstream, Energy services, Insurance, Pharma/biotech and now Utilities integrated at Overweight; the book holds none of them. Its only overweight row is staples (Carlsberg, Hilton Food and Fevertree, 9.28 points), plus CVS Group in the Hospitals Overweight. With the discount rate back above 5%, credit the main drag and Brent near $115, these are the rows that earn more as yields rise (insurance reinvestment income), hedge the inflation channel (energy) or carry the energy shock into contracted prices (power).

  • Counter-case: energy is the crowded trade in this system (15 of the top 40 on the starting score) and oil eased late in the week on Iran talks, so the gap would be closed by buying what the Lists already own most; insurance's asset books are the first casualty if the credit widening becomes a default cycle
  • What would change the view: Brent under $85 or a default wave, either of which retires the overweights and the gap with them

Gap 2. Too fragile and too momentum-weak for a Fragility Double Underweight and a Momentum Underweight

12 of the 16 holdings, 46.37 of the 55.35 points, sit at or below the 41st market-capitalisation percentile (Alfen 2nd, Hilton Food and Xvivo 6th, Hexatronic 7th, Sdiptech 8th), and 38.05 points sit at Technical Momentum D or F, so both tilts are judgements against most of the book by construction. This week the Fragility case moved from American to European evidence: the cost of debt is a measured 60 basis point margin drag, euro high yield is 90 basis points wider since September, and the market sold beats that carried complexity (ABB, the Nordic capital goods names) while paying for simple growth (Hexagon). Richard's own words are the reasoning: in risk-off markets investors "aggressively sell small, niche, weird, unproven, risky, complex".

  • Counter-case: the book is built this way on purpose, as a Middle Innings portfolio of small growth cases, and most of its bottom-up composites read above 60; the one large-against-small read this week (DNB Carnegie, Nordics) found no divergence; and small caps regain footing three to six months after the first hike
  • What would change the view: credit stabilising with a European small-against-large reading turning up, which would take Fragility to Underweight

Gap 3. Holding rows the target underweights, and the exposure widened this week

13.72 points, a quarter of the invested weight, sit in rows the top-down read says to be short of: Helios Towers (5.17) in Telecom towers at Underweight, D'Ieteren (2.50) in Automotive retail at Underweight inside the Consumer discretionary Double Underweight, Bally's Intralot (2.00) in Gambling at Underweight, and Theon (4.05) in a Defence industry moved to Underweight this week. The reasoning differs by row (duration and debt for towers, a split consumer for automotive retail, a repeat-payer policy premium for gambling, multiple compression for defence) and is made in the Environment note.

  • Counter-case: each holding has a bottom-up case the row does not describe. Helios Towers reads 82.0 on the Bottom-up composite with TSR/Valuation A, the Budget removed a tail risk for Bally's Intralot, and Theon's fall is mostly its own leverage with electronics holding up; the rows say what the environment pays for, the cases say whether the company can outrun it
  • What would change the view: the ten-year under 4.5% (towers), a European consumer reading not falling (automotive retail), German defence approvals converting (defence)

Proposed amendments to portfolio-level risk management

Last week's eleven amendments carry forward, each with a status, and one is new. The four that changed or are new are argued in full; the rest are in the table. All are proposals.

2. A size floor as a risk limit for new positions while Fragility is Double Underweight, with a review of any holding below it. Status: strengthened, and now appliable

The floor can be applied for the first time, and its footing is stronger. For four weeks it could not be applied because size was blank; the holdings ledger now carries the percentile for every holding and shows the 12 of 16 in Gap 2, and the Fragility evidence is now European as well as American. In the book, the floor would sit under new positions at a percentile Richard sets, and would queue the 12 for a review that asks one question each: is the case simple and proven enough to be the exception?

  • Counter-case: a floor turns the Lists into a large-cap screen for the two thirds of the Pool without case ratings, and the one large-against-small read this week found no divergence; the floor should bind entries, not force exits

3. Duration and leverage limits: a cap on exposure to Underweight and Double Underweight rows valued off a yield or financed at the margin, and a floor on the yield-beneficiary rows. Status: strengthened on the cap, unchanged on the floor

The cap's counter-case weakened in a week: last week's bond rally, which made this the limit a relief rally would punish, reversed, with the ten-year back above 5% and rate-sensitives lagging. The capped rows read as last week (Real assets, Towers, Renewable generators, Homebuilders, Building technology, Alternative finance, Automotive retail; Software B2B Enterprise leaves the list at Neutral), and the book's capped exposure is Helios Towers, D'Ieteren and Bally's Intralot, 9.67 points. The floor rows now read Energy, Insurance, Staples, Pharma/biotech, Tobacco and Utilities integrated; the book holds staples and nothing in the other five, which is Gap 1.

  • Counter-case on the floor: it buys the crowded trade

10. A country concentration cap at the book level, Sweden first. Status: strengthened, and recast from a line to a limit

Sweden is 15.55 of 55.35 points, 28% of the invested weight, in four names (Hexatronic, Sdiptech, Xvivo, Avanza), and the concentration is also a fragility concentration: the Sweden row is Neutral and three of the four sit at or below the 8th size percentile. The amendment becomes a proposed cap on any one country's share of invested weight, at a level Richard sets, applied by listing and, where a memo gives it, by revenue.

  • Counter-case: the Nordic reads this week lean positive (Nordic equities ahead of Europe, software working, insurance constructive), and a cap by listing penalises Swedish exporters whose revenue is global

12. New: a cap on the book's total weight in rows the target holds at Underweight or Double Underweight. Status: new, from Gap 3

That weight is 13.72 points today and widened this week on the Defence move without any holding changing. Amendment 3 caps only the rate-financed rows; this one caps the whole set, so that a tilt change beneath the book shows up as a limit test rather than as a quiet fall in the rating. In the book it would force the question, for each name over the cap, of whether the case outruns its row.

  • Counter-case: it double-counts amendment 3 on the three rate-financed names, and a cap that fires on a tilt change punishes the book for the judgement, not the holding; the answer is to set it above today's 13.72 points, so that it binds on additions and not on this week's move

The Lists' tilt

The Lists' tilt stays at 0.625, published as Overweighting Fitness for Fighting, as last week. The gap is large and unchanged in kind, sized on the three gaps above and on the measured reading of 57; a large gap means the Lists should keep chasing the stocks that close it ahead of good businesses that do not.

  • Not the step up to 0.75: the gap is not very large. Three holdings already fit the environment at grade B, the like-for-like reading is flat rather than falling, and the Lists' top 40 is already 15 energy names, so a Double Overweight would narrow the Lists to one trade the tilt scorecard has not yet shown to work
  • Not the step down to 0.5: nothing closed. The book did not change, the environment confirmed the Fragility and credit case on European evidence, and the Underweight rows beneath the book grew by one
  • The counter-case: the tilt scorecard's seven tables read minus 4.47, minus 0.17, minus 0.61, minus 0.30, +0.19, +0.31 and minus 1.15 points, Overweights against Underweights, so the tilts have not yet demonstrated value and a Lists tilt that leans on them leans on an unproven instrument. Seven weeks is noise and the first twelve-week reading arrives in December, which is when this argument gets its test; if the scorecard is still negative then with the gap unchanged, the right move is down to 0.5 on the instrument, not on the gap
