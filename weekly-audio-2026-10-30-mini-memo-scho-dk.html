APM Mini-Memo. Schouw & Co. The thirtieth of October 2026.

Schouw & Co is rank eleven on the Ideas Bench in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. The one-line view. Schouw & Co owns a high-return fish-feed business with a public price, and five smaller industrial companies the market values at about four times operating profit. The case is upgraded earnings now and a narrowing discount later. The case depends on BioMar's feed margins holding.

First, what the company does. Schouw & Co is a Danish industrial holding company listed in Copenhagen. At eight hundred and twenty-seven kroner on the thirtieth of October it is worth about two point seven billion euros. The group owns seven business-to-business platforms across thirty-five countries, with over fifteen thousand employees. In 2025 the group earned operating profit before depreciation of two point nine billion kroner on revenue of thirty-four billion. BioMar is the engine. BioMar makes fish and shrimp feed at sixteen plants and holds about a fifth of global salmon feed volume. BioMar earns a return on invested capital of about twenty-four percent. BioMar provides nearly half of group revenue, over half of group operating profit and roughly three quarters of the group's asset value. Schouw listed BioMar separately in Copenhagen in May 2026, kept seventy-four percent, and received two point seven billion kroner. The rest are European niche leaders. GPV is Europe's second-largest contract electronics manufacturer. HydraSpecma builds hydraulic and cooling systems for wind turbine makers. Borg Automotive remanufactures car parts. Two Fibertex companies make nonwoven fabrics. Spectre, a maker of technical outdoor apparel bought in September, is the newest platform.

Second, the thesis. The story is a discount with a new yardstick. Since BioMar's listing, the market can price Schouw's stake directly. What is left over trades at about four to four and a half times this year's operating profit before depreciation. That is an implied discount of forty-five to fifty percent, against a normal ten to twenty percent for holding companies. The market's story is that the discount closes as the parts are marked. Earnings do the work while the discount waits. In the second quarter of 2026, revenue grew five percent to nine billion kroner. Operating profit before depreciation grew seventeen percent, and the margin widened to nine point two percent. Earnings per share rose thirty-nine percent. Management raised its full-year profit guidance in August, and at the capital markets day on the eighth of October set an ambition of four and a half billion kroner of operating profit before depreciation by 2030, about half as much again as 2025. The step-up has named parts. GPV is cutting its sites from nineteen to fourteen for about one hundred million kroner of savings, and targets a ten percent margin by 2030 from seven point four percent last year. Borg's programme targets another hundred million by 2027. BioMar targets eight to ten percent annual profit growth to 2030. The parent's leverage is down to nought point seven times from one point nine, so it can buy again, and it is buying back four hundred and ten million kroner of stock this year. Consensus earnings growth is about fourteen percent a year. The drivers to track are BioMar's feed volumes against its three to seven percent target for this year, GPV's order intake, the working-capital unwind in the full-year results, and any sign of an actual divestment.

Third, the Target Setup. There is no Investment Analyst memo on Schouw. So there are no Bottom-up or Stock-driven ratings, and the read here is my own. The Target Setup is Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU. The BioMar listing changed the structure, released the capital and exposed the discount, and the earnings step-up comes from margin programmes and redeployment. If management keeps buying at the Spectre multiple of about seven times, the case also takes the shape of Delivering, Serial Acquirer, Mechanical EPS Upgrader, because consensus only models the businesses already owned.

Fourth, Case Riskiness and simplicity. The case is simple to state and moderately risky to hold. What needs to go right is that BioMar's feed margins hold while the industrial units deliver their cost programmes, and that reported profits turn into cash. The main risk is a biological shock at BioMar. A disease outbreak, or an El Niño spike in marine raw materials, would cut feed volumes and could derail group guidance, because BioMar is over half of group profit. The modelled downside floor is about six hundred and fifty kroner a share, roughly twenty percent below today's price. Against that, BioMar's contracts pass raw material costs through with a one-quarter lag. A twenty percent rise in its input basket produced margin expansion, not compression, in the second quarter. The second thing to watch is cash. First-half operating cash flow fell forty-three percent while operating profit set records, because working capital swelled on raw-material buying. Net working capital above six billion kroner would be the warning. Behind both sits governance. There is a twenty-nine percent anchor shareholder, a chief executive in place since the year 2000 with no published successor, and no divestment since 2016. That is why the discount may need a seller to close.

Fifth, business quality. Quality is high at the core and mixed at the edges. BioMar's cost-plus contracts, twenty-four percent return on invested capital and twenty-four days of working capital make it an outlier. GPV's open-book pricing and Fibertex's indexed prices extend the pass-through habit across the group. Group return on invested capital excluding goodwill improved to fourteen percent in the second quarter from thirteen percent last year. Management narrows guidance rather than over-promising, and delivered 2025 inside its range. The weaker units are real. Borg's profit fell to zero in 2025 under Chinese parts pricing. GPV and HydraSpecma follow industrial capital cycles. And our research files call the Spectre purchase, a lower-return apparel maker bought with proceeds from a high-return feed business, inadequately explained to investors.

Sixth, returns and valuation. The shares are cheap on the parts and ordinary on earnings. Schouw trades at just under twelve times current earnings, the middle of its own history, and at about six times operating profit before depreciation against ten times for capital goods peers. The weekly TSR Valuation rating of the thirtieth of October is C, on the valuation reading alone. The plausible return clears your bar. The weekly calculation of the twenty-ninth of October puts the plausible return at about twenty-five percent a year, from consensus earnings growth of about fourteen percent, the multiple moving from under twelve to under fourteen times, and dividends. On the anchors that is an A, but it is a script reading, not a memo's. Momentum backs it. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of the twenty-eighth of October is A, and the weekly Technical Momentum rating of the thirtieth is A, with the shares three percent below their fifty-two week high.

Seventh, the value chain and thematics. The pros. Protein feed demand is defensive and growing two to three percent a year. BioMar has cut marine inputs from eighty percent of feed in 1990 to about twelve percent. And the Industry Digest sees the short-cycle turn reaching electronics first, which would lift GPV's order book. The cons. The Sector Digest's rule is that a listing merely prices the best part, while a trade sale closes the discount, and Schouw's discount has not moved since BioMar listed. The market paid for break-ups this year and nothing for acquisitions. Nordic industrial beats were sold this week. The weekly Thematic Fit Momentum rating of the thirtieth of October is C, on the environment only. And the stock is thinly traded, with no activist and no short interest on file to force the question.

The one-line view. Schouw & Co owns a high-return fish-feed business with a public price, and five smaller industrial companies the market values at about four times operating profit. The case is upgraded earnings now and a narrowing discount later. The case depends on BioMar's feed margins holding.
